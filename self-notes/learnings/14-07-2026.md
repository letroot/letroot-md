### Main Learnings

#### 1. We're in a uni-context
Source: [[1-a-philosophers-one-word-theory-to-explain-why-the-world-feels-so-weird]]
- Modern society is mostly lived in a uni-context: a concept describing the compression of our social experience into homognenised settings facilitated by mass-media technology — radio, television, social media.
- We used to have church-context, school-context, family-context. Now we have immediate and absolute exposure to one another in an undifferentiated way. I can inhabit a reaction thread to the war in Iran while simultaneously putting up a selfie liked by an American in germany.
- We all show up to tweet on X and are at once subject to the same moral and identity judgments that that setting has cultivated and deemed acceptable regardless of my local context.
- This gives rise to a greater regard for identity over character because inclusion becomes the defining ethical concern in a setting defined by omni-access to all. "Political disagreement then often shifts toward which identities deserve recognition or safeguarding rather than which character traits ought to be cultivated."
- It's also easier to categorise everyone into the largest buckets possible that flatten individual nuance and character than not. I show up online as a black man and that's an easy plug to fit into the moral keyholes fashioned by the uni-context.
- Character is difficult to scale because character is distilled from repeated behaviour given various contexts. The uni-context is fundamentally at odds with such nuance. It does not scale and is not abstract enough.
- Identity — gay, Jew, male — is an abstraction. they are not as real as what I am in my real-world interaction where there are real stakes and I am limited in how much I can exert on the (real)world. In the uni-context, the stakes are one-dimensional — "cancelled" vs. "idolised" — the in-between is ignored. I don't exert myself on the uni-context. the uni-context removes the need to even bother.
- Context is flattened in the uni-context. Good is context-sensitive. Negative/bad things are not. Bad is more universal than good is.
- This means the negative resonates and unites in the uni-context more than good does.
- Abstraction as the modern tendency. experiencing things in the abstract. When we read the news in the same space we interact with all the world at once, we are consuming an unreal/abstract stimuli. It rears an appetite in us to idealise, be susceptible to ideological capture(s) and more comfortable/desirous? with/of the abstract, non-local experience.
- money abstracts away the qualitative to enable quantitative equivalences.
