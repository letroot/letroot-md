# Morning Digest - 2026-11-03

## Main Thought
Richard Rainwater’s “all returns, no capital” approach shows that useful leverage does not always come from writing a check. In David Senra’s account, Rainwater earned equity through strategic insight and a valuable position in the network around a deal. The lesson is not that involvement alone deserves ownership; it is that a scarce contribution can be capital in another form.

## For You As A Founder
When you enter a partnership or opportunity, be clear about the specific value you bring: customer access, distribution, expertise, execution, or a decision that materially improves the outcome. Build that capability and prove it. Negotiating from demonstrated contribution is stronger than asking for upside based only on enthusiasm or proximity.

## For You As A Young Man
Do not measure your usefulness only by money, status, or credentials. Become someone whose judgment, reliability, and relationships help other people make progress. Then keep your promises; trust is what turns contribution into long-term leverage.
