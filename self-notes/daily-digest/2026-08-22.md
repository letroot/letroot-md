# Morning Digest - 2026-08-22

## Main Thought
Early founders do not need the perfect idea; they need a specific enough problem to test. Broad ambition creates vague feedback. A narrow customer, a concrete pain, and a clear promised outcome create a learning loop: you can find people, hear whether the language lands, and improve the offer until it becomes less wrong.

## For You As A Founder
Write your current offer in one sentence: "I help [specific person] get [specific outcome] without [specific pain]." If it sounds interchangeable with ten other tools, narrow the customer or sharpen the pain. Then take that sentence into real conversations before adding more product surface area. Specificity is not a constraint on ambition; it is how an early company earns the right to expand.

## For You As A Young Man
Clarity comes from contact with reality, not endless private deliberation. Choose a direction, say it plainly, and expose it to honest feedback. The willingness to be initially wrong is a form of courage.
