# Morning Digest - 2026-08-27

## Main Thought
The Abia power project was not a quick fix to a broken grid. Its builders identified the constraint that made every downstream effort expensive, then took responsibility for the critical path end to end. It required patience through financial shocks, legal resistance, and political uncertainty. The lesson is not to own everything; it is to own or control the dependency that determines whether your promised outcome can reliably happen.

## For You As A Founder
Map the path from your promise to customer value. Where can a weak dependency break the experience: acquisition, onboarding, data, an API, support, or payment? Do not respond by rebuilding the world. Pick the dependency with the highest consequence and create a backup, a simpler workflow, or direct ownership. Durable businesses are often built by removing one structural bottleneck others have learned to tolerate.

## For You As A Young Man
Long work asks for more than intensity. It asks you to stay useful through delay, uncertainty, and setbacks without surrendering responsibility for the next practical move. Patience is active when it keeps building the conditions for a future opening.
