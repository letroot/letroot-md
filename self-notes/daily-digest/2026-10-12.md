# Morning Digest - 2026-10-12

## Main Thought
Good judgment starts with knowing where your knowledge is reliable. Munger calls this a circle of competence: the edge is not knowing everything, but recognizing which decisions you can assess well and where you are guessing. You can widen that circle through steady work, but confidence alone does not expand it.

## For You As A Founder
Before making a consequential bet, separate what you know from what you assume. Name the customer, market, or technical questions where you lack direct experience, then gather evidence or involve someone who has it. Concentrate resources when the opportunity is understandable; keep the bet small while important parts remain guesswork.

## For You As A Young Man
Treat “I don’t know” as useful information. Build expertise patiently in areas where your effort compounds, and ask for help when a decision falls outside them. Humility protects you from expensive certainty and gives you room to learn.
