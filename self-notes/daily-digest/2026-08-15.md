# Morning Digest - 2026-08-15

## Main Thought
Keep conviction about the problem separate from attachment to your first solution. A founder's instinct can be right while the current feature, workflow, or business model is wrong. This distinction lets you discard weak implementations without losing the underlying mission. Build just enough to test the idea, protect the parts users already understand, and let evidence decide what survives.

## For You As A Founder
Write two sentences for your current bet: one stating the customer problem you are convinced matters, and one stating the solution hypothesis you are testing. Treat the first as durable but revisable; treat the second as disposable until users repeatedly choose it. Before expanding the product, isolate one new assumption so a failed test can teach you something specific instead of leaving you with a confused, expensive negative result.

## For You As A Young Man
Being committed does not require being stubborn. Strength includes the ability to admit that a route is wrong, learn cleanly, and continue without turning a failed attempt into an injury to your identity.
