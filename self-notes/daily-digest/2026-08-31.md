# Morning Digest - 2026-08-31

## Main Thought
Good judgment needs two tracks: first, examine the incentives, probabilities, economics, and constraints that actually govern a decision; second, examine the psychological forces that may be distorting your view. A conclusion can look rational while being driven by status, social proof, fear of missing out, sunk cost, or the desire to defend a past choice.

## For You As A Founder
For any consequential decision, write two short columns before acting. In the first, list the observable facts: customer evidence, costs, downside, alternatives, and what must be true for the bet to work. In the second, list possible distortions: ego, urgency, imitation, attachment to old work, or wanting approval. This does not remove uncertainty, but it separates the business case from the story you may be telling yourself.

## For You As A Young Man
Self-command begins with noticing when your mind is trying to protect comfort or image instead of seeking truth. You do not need to distrust yourself constantly; you need enough honesty to ask what influence is moving you before it becomes a decision.
