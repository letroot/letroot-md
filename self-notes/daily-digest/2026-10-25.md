# Morning Digest - 2026-10-25

## Main Thought
When a co-founder relationship is clearly over, the goal changes: stop trying to win the history of the conflict and work toward a fair, professional ending. Dalton Caldwell and Michael Seibel emphasize that a drawn-out fight can consume time, money, and trust long after the partnership has stopped helping the company. A clean separation protects the possibility of moving forward.

## For You As A Founder
If a partnership reaches that point, keep the circle small, document the practical decisions, and agree on roles, assets, and communication before resentment takes over. Get qualified legal advice where needed, but do not turn every disagreement into litigation. Spend effort on a workable transition rather than proving who was more right.

## For You As A Young Man
Being right is not always worth the price of making an enemy. You can hold a boundary and still be fair, direct, and restrained. Handle conflict in a way you can respect later, especially when you are frustrated and other people are watching.
