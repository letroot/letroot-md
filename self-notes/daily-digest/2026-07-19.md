# Morning Digest - 2026-07-19

## Main Thought
Good judgment starts with admitting how much of life and business is shaped by randomness. People regularly tell neat stories about why something worked, but many outcomes are noisier than they look after the fact. If you forget that, you start copying winners too literally and trusting your own explanations too much.

The practical lesson is not cynicism. It is to build methods that stay sane under uncertainty. You prepare well, you act, and then you judge results with more humility than your ego naturally wants.

## For You As A Founder
Do not read every good result as proof that your process was excellent, and do not read every bad result as proof that your approach was broken. In early-stage work especially, some things will work later than expected, some will fail for reasons outside your control, and some apparent wins will not repeat. Your job is to improve the quality of the bet, not to pretend you control every outcome.

That means reviewing decisions separately from outcomes. Ask: was the reasoning sound, was the downside acceptable, and did I learn something that improves the next move? Founders get stronger when they stop worshipping short-term outcomes and start designing better decision processes.

## For You As A Young Man
This should make you steadier. You do not need to turn every setback into a verdict on your worth, and you should not let every success inflate your self-image either. A mature man learns to work hard, prepare well, and stay modest about what he can actually control.

Humility here is practical. It keeps you from despair when things go badly and from foolish pride when things go well. Both errors make a man easier to shake.
