# Morning Digest - 2026-10-11

## Main Thought
Advice is more trustworthy when the person giving it bears some consequence if it is wrong. Credentials and confidence are easy to display; exposure to customers, money, reputation, or operational results is harder to fake. Risk ownership does not guarantee good judgment, but it puts advice in contact with reality.

## For You As A Founder
When weighing a recommendation, ask what the adviser has at stake and what evidence would show the advice working. Inside your own company, avoid arrangements where you keep the upside while customers, staff, or partners absorb the downside. Set clear expectations, own the commitments you make, and use customer behavior and cash collected as reality checks.

## For You As A Young Man
Prefer responsibility over performance. Make commitments you can stand behind, accept the cost when your judgment is wrong, and be wary of confident advice from people who can walk away untouched. Take risks that teach you and keep you accountable, not risks that only look bold.
