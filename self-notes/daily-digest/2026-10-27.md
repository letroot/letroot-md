# Morning Digest - 2026-10-27

## Main Thought
A clean-looking number can hide an important exposure. In Poor Charlie's Almanack, Munger treats accounts as a starting point, then looks for obligations, dependencies, and competitive threats that ordinary summaries can understate. The useful question is not only “What does this report say?” but “What reality might not be visible in it yet?”

## For You As A Founder
When reviewing a business, pair each encouraging metric with a search for what could make it fragile: a customer who represents too much revenue, an obligation that has not come due, a channel you do not control, or a cost pushed into the future. You do not need to predict every risk; make the material ones understandable before they surprise you.

## For You As A Young Man
Look beneath appearances before you form a confident judgment. Ask what is missing, who bears the cost, and what would change your view. That habit protects you from being impressed by a polished surface or frightened by a number without context.
