# Morning Digest - 2026-08-30

## Main Thought
Users are better at revealing friction than explaining the product they need. Their words often describe a preferred solution; their behavior shows the actual job, workaround, and point of confusion. Product judgment improves when you watch the task happen without rescuing the user, then ask why each detour made sense from their point of view.

## For You As A Founder
Schedule one observation session this week. Give a user a real task, stay quiet, and record where they hesitate, abandon a step, open another tool, or ask for help. Do not start with "what feature do you want?" Start with "what were you trying to accomplish?" Then fix the highest-cost gap between their intent and the result. That is often more valuable than another round of roadmap debate.

## For You As A Young Man
Pay attention to what people do, not only what they say they value. In work and relationships, behavior is often the clearest signal of a real constraint, fear, or need. Observation without quick judgment is a practical form of wisdom.
