# Morning Digest - 2026-07-25

## Main Thought
The second-highest priority rarely gets done. When a team lets every new request jump the queue, it slowly trades the core problem for a growing collection of smaller promises. The work that protects quality, reliability, and future speed is easy to postpone precisely because it is not loud today. Eventually, the neglected foundation becomes the thing customers feel most.

## For You As A Founder
Name the one constraint that determines whether your product deserves to scale, then protect time for it before accepting more feature work. A backlog is not strategy. Ask of every incoming request: does this make the core job work materially better, or does it merely create the appearance of progress? Give capacity, ownership, and a deadline to the former. Make the tradeoff explicit when you decline the latter.

## For You As A Young Man
Your life has second-highest priorities too: sleep, training, honest reflection, difficult conversations, and learning the basics well. They are rarely urgent, so distraction can keep outranking them indefinitely. Maturity is choosing the important work before the cost of neglect turns it into an emergency.
