# Morning Digest - 2026-10-23

## Main Thought
Mark Pincus’s reflection on Tribe.net points to a quiet product lesson: people need a reason to trust a space before they will share themselves in it. Facebook’s early .edu boundary gave its first users a legible context for who else was there. The boundary was not just a restriction; it helped make participation feel safer.

## For You As A Founder
When asking users to post, connect, or delegate something sensitive, do not treat trust as a vague brand attribute. Design the boundary: who can see this, who belongs here, and what signals that the space is appropriate? A narrower trusted starting group may create more useful participation than opening access broadly before people feel secure.

## For You As A Young Man
Trust grows when people can understand the terms of a relationship. Be clear about what you will keep private, what you will share, and what you expect in return. Do not demand openness before you have shown that you can handle it responsibly.
