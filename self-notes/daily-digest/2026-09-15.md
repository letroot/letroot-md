# Morning Digest - 2026-09-15

## Main Thought
Learning feels worse before it feels useful. Early practice is often tiring, clumsy, and unrewarding because you are gathering the data your mind needs to improve later. The mistake is to judge the practice session by how smooth it felt. A better standard is whether you showed up at the right dose, focused on the basics, and returned the next day.

## For You As A Founder
Treat founder skills the same way: sales, user research, writing, hiring, and product judgment improve through deliberately repeated exposure, not through consuming more theory. Pick one skill that would materially change your company, practice it in small real-world reps, and stop before fatigue turns practice into careless repetition. The compounding comes between sessions, provided you return.

## For You As A Young Man
Do not interpret beginner discomfort as a verdict on your ability. Build the patience to be awkward, learn the basics, sleep, and come back. That patience becomes confidence you actually earned.
