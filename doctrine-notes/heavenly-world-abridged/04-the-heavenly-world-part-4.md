There were four axes in the categorization of heavenly experiences: internal versus external, overlap versus immersive, executive control present or absent, and faith imagination versus autonomous generation.

You must maintain a faith stand for executive control. Do not enter spiritual experiences in which executive control is absent. Executive control means you can end the experience. It does not mean you are generating it. There can be autonomous generation, with executive control still very much present.

“The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.” “If anything is revealed”—that is autonomous. They are observing and reasoning within the spiritual world: “Father, you are here. What do you have?” While listening and observing, something can hit them autonomously and they begin to see it, but they can end it at any time. Executive control must be present.

Yielding does not mean not having control. You are observing and listening to allow autonomous content, but even while having the experience you can end it. It is your right.

# Faith, Asking, and Autonomous Experiences

Faith-stand imagination, or self-generated content, leads to autonomous experiences under executive control.

First, you have a faith stand on the reality of the Father and Son making their home with you and being available to give what you ask. In John 14, he says that in that day, when he sends the Spirit to be in us and the Father and Son make their home with us, we can ask things and they will answer us.

You stand in faith: “Father and Son, you are here. You said that if I ask, I will receive.” That includes supernatural things: “The works I do, you will do also.” I can receive a Mount-of-Transfiguration-type experience or whatever it may be. I am asking, “What do you have?”

John 14:12: “Most assuredly, I say to you, he who believes in me, the works that I do he will do also; and greater works than these he will do, because I go to my Father.”

For a long time, John 14:13–14 was explained to me as the believer's authority in the name of Jesus. I never thought to see it as asking and receiving in an open-ended Luke 11 context.

After speaking of greater works, he says, ‘And whatever you ask in my name, that I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.’ Who said it was not a word of knowledge, a word of wisdom, or interpretation of tongues?

Verse 14: “If you ask anything in my name, I will do it.” “In the name” and “by the Spirit” are parallels. In that situation, when the Spirit is in you, ask in his name and he will do it.

Can this mean that, relative to the gifts of the Spirit, when we ask we receive, as in Luke 11? Yes. “Ask, and it shall be given; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you.” Does this show our initiative concerning these gifts and yet God's discretion? Yes. It shows the interaction between our initiative, God's impulses, and our initiative to act on them.

This leads to autonomous experiences while the faith stand for executive control is never lost. Conversion and compression are fully normalized: “I know I will receive it in my own language, in my own way, in the way I receive. Give it to me; I will receive.”

Do not let people's criticism stop you. You have received from God as you are, in your state. If you find that you were not completely accurate, understand that your conversion or compression may have affected it. Do not let that stop you from receiving from God.

# Our Right to Ask and Receive

For spiritual growth, you stand on God's record. For fellowship, you get to fellowship. When we talk about the gifts, experiences of God's glory, and answers for ministry, ask, knowing you have a right to be answered.

‘Whatever you ask, I will give to you.’ This is within the context of what is righteous and according to God's word. You are ready for the application of natural and discretionary impulses.

We can gather in a Christian meeting and ask. ‘Let him who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.’ Let him ask. It is the same thought: ‘Whatever you ask, I will give.’

“I want to have this to bless somebody.” If he does not have it, fine; keep quiet and speak to yourself and to God. But he can ask, assured that he has a right to receive. If for some reason he is not receiving—whether on his end or for a discretionary reason—then let him leave it. There is no big deal. He had the right to ask.

Before asking, we often say, “Can it really happen? What about God's discretion?” Just ask. Leave the discretion to him. There is a faith-stand confession or imagination of where we are and the fact that we ask and receive. As we observe and listen in that realm, standing in faith, something can hit us.

I ask and I receive. If you ask, stand on your watch, and have not received, ask again later. It happens. I talk to God about many things. If I do not receive then, I know he will answer me; I will still get it. I postpone it to a later time, unless the matter is really bothering me at that moment.

Ask something for yourself, then for someone else and minister to them. It may concern your spiritual assignment, your natural assignment, or experiences: ‘What do you have to say about this issue? Concerning this person, what can bless him most? What should I minister?’

# Raise Your Standards for the Season Ahead

Raise your standards for yourself higher than you have done. The reason for your lower ambition concerning your growth is that you have lowered the standards. Raise them to where you know they ought to be, where my word says they should be.

This may not mean doing more in time, but how much is done with time. When your standards are raised, it forces you to push harder within the limited, finite time you have—to go for something greater, more complete, more perfect than what you have been settling for.

Raise your standards, because there are times and seasons. There are seasons of readiness and preparation for leadership. You must no longer see it as far away or as an option. See it as close by and as a necessity: “Yes, I must lead. I must do more.”

# Taking Initiative as a Member of the Body

It is time for you to take initiative concerning how things ought to be.

When you see how something in church should be, do not wait for someone to give direction. Take initiative. See yourself as someone who can lead the cause, called by God to do it: ‘God has given me this idea, direction, or wisdom, so I can take initiative.’

Be the kind of person who, when something is laid on your heart, can say what to do and present it. ‘I do not want to be too forward’ is the wrong spirit. We have tensions between love and discipline, between order and submission on one hand and pursuit and ambition on the other. But it is not right simply to hold back.

I saw the Lord laying different things on different people, but some were holding back. He may not be laying it on my heart; for effectiveness, it may not be my business. It could be natural or spiritual. ‘I do not want to bring that suggestion’ is not of God's Spirit.

Be careful of that inhibiting spirit that does not allow God to work fully through you or allow you ownership in a mission. You are abandoning things that are your part. ‘I can do this and make things work better.’ That is how the body works, as in 1 Corinthians 12\.

Whenever you have something, present the initiative. Push for it. It shows you understand the body of Christ: ‘I am a part. Could it be that others are not seeing this, or other things overwhelm their minds more? Perhaps this is my part.’

Whatever it is, do not say, “We need to be bigger before I do that.” That is not true. We need to be disciplined so that we always do it. If we do not do it small, we may not do it big. Start doing it so you always do it.

Do not repress what he lays on your heart. Any system where people have this inhibiting spirit hinders what God can do, including excellence and the working of the Spirit.

It is better to make a mistake and be corrected than to hold back and lose much good. We can correct and fix the bad things. As someone said, ‘I would rather a little excess than a whole house with no fire. We have enough damp cloths to put out the fire.’

Taking initiative is better than doing nothing. It applies to the supernatural, the natural, leadership, and many other things. I pray you get hold of it, because it really hit me in the room, just as we were discussing.
