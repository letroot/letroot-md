# The Heavenly World — Part 2 — Abridged Transcript

# Worship and the Nearness of the Heart

We are looking at the heavenly world in view of GCC. Whenever we study anything along these lines, we are looking at the way the Christian life is in practice. There can be a kind of Christian life and worship that is not exactly what it is meant to be in the mind of the Lord. This year, I want to put my attention on the practical details.

In Matthew 15:7, the Lord said, “Hypocrites, well did Isaiah prophesy about you.” We must not assume that their pattern no longer exists. It could be that we fall in that category and have to repent. “These people draw near to me with their mouth, they honor me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.” Are you close to God, or are you far from God? A lot of things people do in worship keep them far from God while they are very active.

One of the things I bother about is what people often call praise. I do not see anything wrong with it ordinarily. It has a testimonial use: the celebration of testimonies of what God has done. But if it becomes all that people do in what they call the worship of God, there is something wrong, because usually those activities are not interactions with God. You are excited, celebrating and thankful about what God has done. It has its place. It can become wrong when it becomes all that you do and all that you like.

It is a strange thing to want to engage with God but only want to do the things that are about you—your own joy and celebration of what he has done. You do not want to talk to him as a person, honor him or worship him.

There is the argument that their heart being far is about their doctrinal belief, and that is true. They were concerned about the washing of hands, so concerned about external things, but internally they were not close to him. The principle holds true: someone can be doing everything that looks like they are relating with God, but the part that matters most—their heart—is far. Someone can turn up for every prayer-watch session and still be far from him. Being close to him or far from him is a question of where your heart or mind is. Are you mindful of him? Is your mind present with him?

# The Different Realities Called Heaven

God did not just create an earthly world; he created a heavenly world. When it says, “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth,” we must understand what heaven God created and what heaven God did not create.

God did not create heaven, his throne. There is no separate throne from him. Heaven, his throne, is \[unclear wording\] himself. “Heaven is where you are” is not wrong in that context, even though there is a context in which it is not true, because there are wicked spirits in the heavenly. Heaven can be a spiritual world overlapping a material world, but at the height of what we call heaven, you can even say it is who he is. The highest thing in existence is God, the self-existent.

We can see arguably three or four heavens God created. In original creation, we see three: the atmospheric heavens where the birds fly, the stellar heavens where the stars are, and a spiritual world that overlaps a material or natural world. Then there is God's person or authority called heaven.

In Isaiah 66:1, he says, “Heaven is my throne, the earth is my footstool.” In that context, we ought to think of the earth as the entirety of creation. He is not saying atmospheric or stellar heaven is his throne. Very likely he is not saying the spiritual heaven is his throne either, because all kinds of activities can happen there. His throne, his authority, was functional before he created anything. Did he only become enthroned after creation? We have reasons to understand this as his self-existent authority. It is from that seat that he has done everything else he has done.

Matthew 5:34 says, “Do not swear at all: neither by heaven, for it is God's throne,” and, “Nor by the earth, for it is his footstool.” Stephen also quoted it in Acts 7\. We ought to appreciate what Scripture calls heaven: things of the high, of the beyond. “Wicked spirits in heavenly places” means places of the beyond. “Heavenly Jerusalem” is a Jerusalem not of here but of the beyond. In Matthew 6, “Our Father in heaven,” we would be wise to think of the One who is highest, who is self-existent.

There are at least five descriptions, five separate realities, called heaven in Scripture. The atmospheric and stellar heavens are natural, but they develop the concept for spiritual things. Because they are high above, anything regarded as above, beyond or high in comparison with another can be called heaven. Today people use “heaven” for anything pleasurable, but the word itself was not defined by pleasure originally. Language develops: the future reality of the new heavens and new earth is understood to be blissful, without sin and death, and that idea is then taken to other realities.

We see the term used for God's self-existent state; for a spiritual world above the natural but overlapping it—above in the sense that it influences the natural; and for the church, heavenly Jerusalem. The church has a specific reputation for being called heaven because God's own heaven, self-existent, is now taken as immanent in the church. The Father and the Son have chosen to have their dwelling there. God, who is transcendent, beyond, has chosen to be immanent, present in the church.

When I speak about the heavenly world, we are talking about the spiritual world overlapping the natural world. Different experiences in that world are sometimes compartmentalized, bounded. There can be real boundaries between experiences pertaining to human beings no longer in the body and those pertaining to people in the body.

# Access to the Father by Birth

Ephesians 2:18 says, “For through him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father.” We are not saying Jesus gave us a card called the Spirit. By the regenerative work of the Spirit, we have access to the Father. The idea is the way a child who is born has access to their parents. That kind of access is on a different level: this is their child.

That is why the Lord said, “I will not leave you orphans. I will come to you.” He says the Father and himself will manifest themselves to us. If you love him, believe him and keep his commandment, his Father will love you, and they will make their home with you. We have an ever-present parent, with ever-present parenting. Our access to God is direct by birth.

Ephesians 2:19 says, “You are no longer strangers nor foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God.” He is showing your status generally: you are part of God's family. After showing our status, he shows what we are locationally: “Having been built on...” This location is not just where you are; it is also who you are. You are part of a building, and that location is where God dwells. Access to God is access to his dwelling place. We are his home, his house. When the believer does not know this, he might not function as he is supposed to.

# Understanding Supernatural Experience

I was reading something someone wrote about a man of God known to talk about visions, revelations and supernatural experiences. People were attacking him, saying he was lying. I have no concern about whether he is lying; I do not even know the details. But the writer said, “This person may not be lying. It may be that he is sick.” My problem was the labeling: calling these experiences religious psychoses and saying some of these people ought to belong in a psychiatric ward.

He was right on one hand: these experiences are not strange to the world. You can argue that they are purely natural phenomena with no spiritual value; that is a separate thing. But I do not think it shows that you have studied the matter enough to immediately give people having these experiences the verdict of religious psychosis. When you want to say someone has psychosis, there is instability. You would have seen a breakdown somewhere.

I think God will use your mind, as he always does with everything. I cannot fault the involvement of his mind. I cannot fault the details of what he is receiving. But I am not going to label it as someone going through religious psychosis. We must be able to explain supernatural phenomena, particularly revelation. Otherwise, naturalistic positions may start shaking people, or they will just be dogmatic about it. Many experiences we read in Scripture, judged from a natural standpoint today, would be considered those kinds of things. We need clear explanations.

# Worship as Engagement Rather Than Performance

The believer's walk with God is in the heavenly world. You cannot say, “I am praying,” without being heavenly, because prayer is in the heavenly world. We should be concerned about things people do when they say they are interacting with God which do not get them engaging in that world.

I have seen a tendency while someone is leading worship: they are singing, demonstrative, with their eyes open, moving their hands, following the rhythm. I am not saying having their eyes open is a problem. But if you want to interact with God, interact with God and forget about what you are doing. You are in a conversation with God, who has made his home with you, whose house you make up. Are you actually talking to God?

Imagine I am praying, “Our Father who is in heaven, hallowed be your name,” while looking at the audience. How do we think someone is actually talking to God in worship when they are focused on the audience, not engaged with God? Perhaps you are enjoying how I am moved by talking to God, but you are not talking to God. You are supposed to be the worship leader, leading me in my worship, which means you are doing the same.

This is concerning because it is taking worship into a performance. A performer is fueled by how people are moved. When we worship the Lord, you are fueled by that engagement with him. You have gone there, engaging with him, and that is what people join you in.

That mode people call praise has its testimonial use. You are giving glory to God for the good things he has done. But if you want to interact with him, that is a different matter. It is him you are dealing with. If we are not careful, prayer can also become performative. When I am praying, nothing else concerns me. If my mind is going off, I cannot force the prayer. I bring it back: “Father, here. Here.” Then we go from there.

# Spiritual Fellowship Integrated with Natural Life

Something is wrong with the practice of the faith if our attention is always in the natural when we say we are engaging with our Father. God's purpose is sonship: children of God and heirs of God. The fundamental thing we are called to do is fellowship with God. That means engaging the supernatural, heavenly, spiritual world while still actively participating in this natural world.

We started with Navigating the Supernatural in the Natural World so you would understand the integrated nature. There are cases where people assume they are having an experience, and there is a breakdown in their engagement with the natural world. That is a problem even scripturally. You fellowship with God in the morning, then continue your day in the natural world carrying the air and atmosphere of that fellowship. Sometimes you pause and engage in that world again. There may be experiences that are internally and externally immersive, and experiences that overlap, but your groundedness in this natural world must remain.

Someone says, “Their description describes something they already had in their mind.” That is not supposed to be surprising, because compression and conversion are meant to be normalized in our participation in the supernatural world. Where I often would not trust someone's experience is the foundation of their belief system. If you do not have the word of truth, it is very likely that your engagement in the heavenly may not be by the Spirit of truth. You may have limited participation by the Spirit of truth because of the limitation of the message of truth you have.

# A Dwelling Place for God in the Spirit

Ephesians 2:20 says we have been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief cornerstone. The whole building grows into a holy temple in the Lord, “a dwelling place for God in the Spirit.” Could they have used “in the heavenly” here? Yes. I do not think “in the Spirit” here necessarily means the Spirit God has sent. He is likely saying a dwelling place of God in the spiritual world.

It can also mean a dwelling place of God in Christ, a dwelling place by the Spirit. Everyone forming it has the Spirit. You could have used “a dwelling place of God in light.” God could not dwell in the people in darkness, because they were not in the Spirit. But I think it leans more toward “in the spirit” as location, not as our ontological reality or nature, even though both are fine.

It is the same in John 4: neither in Jerusalem nor in this place will you worship the Father; those who worship him worship in spirit and in truth. I believe it is “in spirit” as location, although “in spirit” as ontological state would not be wrong. When I engage the Father, my attention cannot be more in the natural.

# Heaven Overlapping the Earth and Our Location in It

One of the core texts is Genesis 1, which is a redemptive account. God said, “Let there be a firmament between the waters,” and called the firmament heaven. He said, “Let the waters be gathered together, and let the dry land appear,” and called the dry land earth. That space upon the dry land is heaven on earth, an idea of the spiritual world overlapping the natural. Does that mean only the space overlapping this one is heaven? No. If someone goes into space, heaven is still overlapping the material world. This was their way of explaining that within this natural world there is a spiritual world overlapping it.

They still had a sense of distance: that world is higher, even though it overlaps. In Jacob's dream there was a ladder leading up to heaven, angels ascending and descending, and God above them all. The Lord Jesus said, “After this you will see the Son of Man raised up and angels ascending and descending on him.” Through humanity we find the ministry of angels happening, and that is supposed to increase from the resurrection: activities from the heavenly world in the natural world, in favor, preservation and orchestrations, in pursuit of God's purpose, natural assignment and spiritual assignment.

If man is the means by which the heavenly and earthly worlds interact, what is our location in the heavenly world? Our location is God's house. We make up God's dwelling place, the Father's house.

# The Father and the Son Make Their Home with Us

Born of God by the Spirit, I have access to the Father in his dwelling place, which is all of us as believers. Individually the Father and the Son make their home with us.

John 14:16 says, “I will pray the Father, and he will give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you forever, even the Spirit of truth.” He is with you and shall be in you. Verse 18 says, “I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.” I have seen the unfortunate interpretation that sending the Spirit is him coming. I think the interpretation goes further to say that the Father and Son making their home with us is the Spirit being sent to dwell in us. I am not sure about that last one, but I am sure about the first one.

That is bad interpretation. It is: “I will send the Spirit to be in you. But even upon that, I will still not leave you orphans. I am coming back to you again.” He continues, “A little while longer, the world will see me no more, but you will see me.” We are supposed to see Jesus. “Because I live, you will live also.” We will share the same nature. There will be fellowship with the Father and the Son, as 1 John 1:1–3 says.

Verse 21 says, “He who has my commandments and keeps them is he who loves me. He who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and I will manifest myself to him.” Judas, not Iscariot, understood that Jesus was saying, “I am going, but when I am gone, I will still be around.” He asked, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us” and not to the world?

“Manifest yourself”—emphanizo—means something is in the light, contactable by your senses. If someone brings a table into complete darkness, our sight cannot pick it up. If we touch it, it becomes manifested to us; we can feel it. “I will be manifested to you” means “I will be perceivable to your senses.” Seeing God is a scriptural reality, spoken about as though it should be common. Think of “manifest” as overall perceptibility to our senses, not only visibility.

The Lord answered, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word; my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.” There is a personal experience of the person who has believed, where the Father and Son make their home with him, perceptible to his senses and sight.

# The Reality of the Heavenly World and Our Perception of It

There are two things here. There is an ontological reality: the Father and Son make their home with you. There is also the fact that your senses can engage it. Tangibility and perceptibility ought to accompany our engagement in the heavenly world.

It is one thing that this lectern is here, and another that I can perceive it. Someone might be dead: the table is there, but the person has no perception of it. That is the idea of “Because I live, you will live also.” The person's condition does not change the reality, but their perception is gone. We must establish both: there is a real heavenly world we are in, and real, tangible and perceptible engagement with it.

“Clouds” describes something high above. Heaven upon the earth, clouds of glory: a tangible, perceptible, visible and audible heavenly world that is here. “Cloud” can also be used for God himself, the manifestation of his glory. “Clouds of glory” is heavenly reality spoken of positively. Dark clouds can sometimes be used for God in judgment, seen as God's withdrawal or the withdrawal of light.

# Natural and Discretionary Impulses

It should not be strange that your experience of that world is tangible, to whatever degree. We might speak of natural and discretionary degrees, following the way the gifts of the Spirit work, which you could call the way our spiritual senses work.

Even in our natural body there are natural impulses and discretionary impulses, which come at particular times, by occasion or situation. The impulse to look at anything is available; you can decide to look. But if a sound suddenly happens, the impulse to look there is occasion-determined. The impulse to urinate is not there all the time. In the same way, if you pray a lot, the impulse for certain supernatural things that are discretionary will be higher. You have put yourself in a position where those things are more available to you.

# Engaging the Father's House through Persistent Faith

We are in the heavenly world, and our participation ought to be tangible and perceptible, even when we minister to the sick and pray. “We will make our home with him. We will manifest ourselves to him. The world will not see me, but you will see me. Because I live, you will live also.” There is a way you will be alive that will make you perceive him.

How do we have these experiences in the Father's house become tangible? How we fellowship with him, see and perceive, is by faith standing in prayer. Any result there is by continually asking, seeking and knocking. Luke 11:2 says, “When you pray, say: Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.”

Prayer is what it means to engage God in this home they have made with us. In what manner do I go about prayer? With persistence of faith. If we are not walking in all the realities of this heavenly world, it points to something in our persistence of faith. We must be careful about a lot of teaching as opposed to a lot of praying. A lot of issues are to be addressed in prayer.

We should be able to have meetings where we can pray without having to bother about time. The end of all the preaching is faith standing. Is it not better to spend more time in prayer, standing in faith, so we can really change? As you pray, take the things that really matter from what has been passed to you and work on them. It is even better when you can hear God more precisely and address the areas that need addressing.

We must stay in the persistent engagement of faith to have the tangible, perceptible, visible and audible experiences he desires us to have, whether they happen by natural or discretionary impulse.

# Tangibility Perceptibility Visibility and Audibility

Even in a time of morning prayer watch there should be a level of tangibility, perceptibility, visibility and audibility of the heavenly world. “I will manifest myself to you”—make myself perceptible to you. I would argue that tangibility and perceptibility, at least at the lowest degree, should be a matter of natural experience. We may argue that visibility and audibility land more on the discretionary side, made possible by our being disposed to the natural experience.

When they say the glory filled the house, that lies more on the tangibility and perceptibility side. Seeing the cloud is visibility; a sound coming is audibility. People being as though in a cloud or fog should be more on the natural side. As we embrace more of these, discretionary experiences serving as signs, wonders and comforts will become more commonplace.

# As He Prayed the Glory on the Mountain

In Luke 9, he took Peter, James and John up on the mountain to pray. “And as he prayed...” Was it a discretionary experience? Yes, but it was still as he prayed. As Peter prayed, he fell into a trance. As Paul prayed, he saw Ananias coming to lay hands on him. If he had not prayed, would it likely have happened? Likely not. Could it still happen? Maybe yes. It is not necessary that because you are praying, you should see someone coming to lay hands on you. But you set yourself up for discretionary experiences by giving yourself to natural experiences with their tangibility and perceptibility.

“As he prayed, the appearance of his face was altered, and his robe became white and glistening.” Two men spoke with him, appearing in glory: visibility and audibility. Peter and those with him were heavy with sleep; when fully awake, they saw his glory and the two men. As the men were parting, Peter said they should make three tabernacles, one for Jesus, Moses and Elijah, not knowing what he said.

While he was speaking, a cloud overshadowed them. They were fearful as they entered the cloud—not that they were walking inside it, but as it overshadowed them, they were entering it. Does that experience happen in a Christian meeting, where a glory clothes you? Yes. They are meant to be tangible and perceptible experiences in the Father's house, which should then come with discretionary visible and audible experiences.

A voice came out of the cloud: audibility. Many times we have limited discretionary experiences of the heavenly world because we have not engaged the natural experience of being in that cloud tangibly and perceptibly. Sometimes people call it soaking or ascension. Those words describe the experience of the heavenly world, which should be a matter of natural experience.

# Peter's Trance and the Place of Natural Engagement

In Acts 10:9, Peter went up to the housetop to pray. He became hungry, and while they made ready, he fell into a trance. Falling into a trance is discretionary. But something must have been present naturally, which we would argue is the tangibility and perceptibility of the heavenly world, accommodating discretionary visibility or audibility.

Internally and externally immersive experiences, internal and external overlap experiences, are very likely more on the discretionary side. The basic thing is tangible and perceptible experiences accommodating those things as God wills.

People can live their lives and not know they are far from him. Sometimes people say, “I do not feel anything, but I am sure I am fine.” That is true. But sometimes being far can be felt. You know it has been a while since you tasted his glory, engaging in the holiest, in his presence, in his home with you, in his house that you make up with all the saints. “Draw nearer to me, and I will draw nearer to you.” You need to draw nearer.

Let us pray for ourselves, just talking to our Father as we ought to.
