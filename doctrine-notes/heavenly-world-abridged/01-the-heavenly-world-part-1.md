# Being Open to What God Is Saying Now

We must know that it is one thing for God to speak; it is another thing for us to be open and disposed to his speaking. One may not be disposed to God speaking generally, or to a particular kind of God speaking.

When John spoke the words of the Lord Jesus to the churches in Revelation, it was not yet Scripture. Now it is for us, by God's providence. But he was speaking by inspired revelation, by the working of the Holy Ghost. God can speak to us that way.

There are things God can only say to you by the inspiration of the Spirit because they are context-sensitive. There are things he has said in his Word that are general; they apply across the board. Then there are things relevant to a particular context. We can get tired of gifts so that we are not listening to what God has to say for this time.

Your attitude towards utterances can numb the impulse for God's Spirit to work in you, and even the impulse for God to inspire others to speak to you. # Sonship and Our Natural Assignment

God's purpose, in a summative form, is sonship. Do not take the common idea of adoption into your understanding of sonship in a way that robs you of regeneration, the new birth.

He wants us as his children and heirs. As his heirs, we have both a natural and a spiritual assignment. Consecration includes your natural assignment: you are a bondservant of Christ in it. This is not an attempt to balance anything; it is part of the truth itself.

You start from where you are, take advantage of godly opportunities, and pursue what you desire, while remaining open to the leading of the Spirit. He might say, “Do this,” or, “Do this that way.” The whole counsel of God includes what you do in the natural.

Our natural assignment is not the test of consecration because all humans have natural pursuits, whether for survival or personal ambition. Not all regard them as assignments from God. Your pursuit can be idolatrous rather than an assignment. Yahweh should be your only high place, and you carry out his assignments as his bondservant.

Whether you are a business owner, employee, career person, or student, if you do not see your natural pursuit as an assignment from God, you are treating it as an idol. In value, however, our spiritual assignment ranks highest: what God wants done in men's spiritual condition and eternal destinies. We must not lose sight of either our natural assignment or the instruction to have financial wisdom.

The primary reality of God's purpose that ought to dawn on us is our status before God. We are called to fellowship with him.

Romans 8:15–16 says we did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but the Spirit of adoption, sonship, whereby we cry, “Abba, Father.” The Spirit bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God; if children, then heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ.

Our first status is as creatures of God. Even a new creation is still a creature. The distance between creature and Creator is far. He can take away his breath, dismantle the creation, click reset. That brings awe and reverence: this self-existent being has chosen to share his very life and nature with us and be our Father. \[Unclear qualification concerning fear.\]

# Redemption, Regeneration, and Fellowship

After redemption, something happens. God saves us. His salvation does not just deal with the consequence of sin and death. He regenerates us. If we start with the fact that he deals with the consequence of sin and death, we might think about it only from a judicial standpoint: sin, death, and darkness have been dealt with, so we do not need to experience the consequence. In that sense, we say we are justified.

But that is not all he did. In that redemptive work, bearing the consequence of sin and death, he gives us his Spirit and regenerates us. That act of giving us his Spirit brings us into fellowship with him.

“Fellowship with him” here does not mean that we are relating with him. It means we are now fellows with him, fellows in a particular context. We are not fellows with God in the most fundamental and substantial context. Substantially, he is self-existent. We are not fellow self-existent people. We are fellows in the fact that he gives us his nature and life. We are fellows of that nature and life. We have his Spirit.

That is the idea of fellowship, what we can call ontological fellowship, used in 1 John 1:1–3. That is why we know 1 John 1 is not written to believers, because you cannot be inviting believers into the fellowship of having God's life and nature. That would be very strange.

“That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life.” This issue of speaking to unbelievers began to arise in some of their letters because they were concerned that some people in their midst were not believers. You see it in Revelation too: “If anyone overcomes,” he has this. They were throwing questions at how some people were acting. They saw people acting like heretics, like idolaters, in ways that concerned them. You see them throwing those posers at them, to examine themselves.

“The life was manifested, and we have seen and bear witness, and declare to you that eternal life which was with the Father and was manifested to us.” He is saying that what is self-existent, we have now witnessed in the person of the Lord Jesus. Eternal life, the self-existent, we have witnessed. He says he was with the Father, so he begins to show this dynamic within self-existence of three persons.

# Revelation and the Fellowship of the Son

“That which we have seen and heard we declare to you,” is God become man, the truth of the incarnation, “that you also may have fellowship with us.” “Our fellowship is with the Father and his Son.” We have that shared nature with the Father and his Son Jesus Christ. # Sharing the Divine Nature: Ontological Fellowship

“Fellowship” means to have in common. It can mean sharing resources: I see what I own as available for others' benefit because I am a steward. That does not remove my personal agency to manage my resources.

There is relational fellowship, as in Acts 2:42: they continued in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, breaking bread and prayers. Some understand giving or breaking bread there, but relational fellowship also qualifies. In 1 John 1, however, the fellowship is ontological: we share the same thing. All human beings share humanity, not a divided humanity, but each one hundred percent humanity.

Second Peter 1:4 says we are partakers of the divine nature: we have that nature in common. First Corinthians 1:9 says we were called into the fellowship of God's Son Jesus Christ. Salvation calls us to have in common with his Son.

Second Corinthians 13:14 says, “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Spirit is with you.” Read it this way: the grace of Jesus Christ, which is God's love and the fellowship of the Spirit, is with you. What God has given in Christ, expressing his love, is the fellowship of the Spirit. Giving us his own life and nature is the highest expression of his love.

When Paul closes with “Grace with you,” he is reminding you of the Spirit in you. Galatians 6:18 says, “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.” God's love in giving Jesus, and Jesus' grace in redemption, is that we should have his Spirit. The greetings of grace and peace at the beginning and close of the letters remind us of our status, forgiveness, and nature through redemption.

The benediction affirms who we are in Christ. Different churches can use different benedictions expressing the same truth. “Redeemed by his blood, indwelt by his Spirit” expresses grace and the fellowship of the Spirit. “Called to his purpose, mandated with his mission” adds what we have the Spirit for.

# From Shared Nature to Relationship with the Father

Ontological fellowship ought to lead to relational fellowship. We relate to fellow humans differently from a dog, however much we love it. We cannot discuss scientific or metaphysical issues with a dog. The ontological fellowship limits the relational fellowship possible.

A child of God can have a different fellowship with God from a human being who is not his child. Your status before someone affects your relationship. A president's staff relate differently from his child or wife.

Relating to God as Creator brings awe; relating to him as Father also brings warmth, love, familiarity in a positive sense, and boldness. We do not lose our regard for him, but our relationship includes more than awe. “Abba, Father.”

When he justified us and reconciled us to himself, he gave us access. That access is to him within the heavenly world.

# God's Self-Existence and Our Access to Him

We have corrected the idea that heaven is simply where God lives. Where was he living before that heaven? If heaven always existed and God found it, then heaven is God. At best, we can say God dwelt in himself. A self-existent being is self-existent.

What we experience in time is already experienced by him: he is one eternal now, an “I am.” In examining his aseity, simplicity, and pure actuality, we cannot think of a process of him wanting. We speak of his good pleasure, will, and purpose as an order, not separate instances in him. There is a philosophical necessity to that, but we cannot go into those details today.

God does not intend to relate to us that way. He relates in the motion of time, the way he made us. If you ask whether you will fulfil your purpose, or whether someone will go to hell, he knows, but that is not a question he will answer to you. His interaction and fellowship are not from that standpoint of his one pure now.

When he gave us access, access to where? Jesus himself raised the question of “where.” When the woman asked whether to worship in Jerusalem or on the mountain, he said neither. There is a place he seeks people to worship him, a “where” we must understand.

# Justification, Peace and Access by the Spirit

Romans 5:1 says, “Having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.” Judicially we are justified; relationally we are at peace. These are distinct statuses.

The enmity was not from God's side. Scripture says we were reconciled to him, not that he was reconciled to us. Our sinful state was enmity towards him, and he worked to fix it so we could be reconciled to him.

Verse 2 moves to relational activity: “Through whom also we have access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and rejoice in hope of the glory of God.” Access means we are to relate actively.

Ephesians 2:16 speaks of reconciling Jews and Gentiles to God. The law-defined enmity between Jew and Gentile was technical: redemption fulfilled the law's demands and removed its basis. Then humanity as a whole is reconciled to God, resolving the actual enmity of a sinful state repulsed against truth, light, and God.

He reconciled both “in one body through the cross,” and preached peace to those far off, Gentiles, and those near, Jews. Verse 18 says, “Through him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father.”

He did not give us an access card. By regeneration, the Spirit becoming one with our spirit and changing us into God's children, we have access by birth. A child automatically has a different, arguably highest, kind of access to a parent. By the Spirit, through redemption, we have access to the Father.

Where do we have access to? Where are you going? He had spoken about it from John 14:1: “Let not your heart be troubled; you believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father's house.” Where is he going? To the Father's house. Remember, we have already raised a serious question about the issue of a Father's house, as though God has a house. Which house? Hold on.

“In my Father's house there are many mansions.” Think of that as spaces; that is best. My margin puts “dwellings.” There are many dwellings. Some take it as rooms, but there are many spaces in my Father's house. “If it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you.” I am going to make a space available for you.

“If I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and receive you to myself; that where I am, there you may be also.” I will also be there. I am going to prepare a place for you to be there, then I will also be there. Where I am, there you may be also.

# The Father's House and the Temple

To understand “the Father's house” in John 14, look at the same expression in John 2\. The way is Jesus, redemption, but what is the house?

John 2:15–16 describes Jesus driving people, sheep, and oxen out of the temple and overturning the money changers' tables. He said, “Do not make my Father's house a house of merchandise.” He called the physical temple the Father's house.

That temple was patterned after the tabernacle God showed Moses. When David wanted to build God a house, God questioned whether he could do so, and said his son would build it. Can anyone build a house for the self-existent?

Solomon himself said in 1 Kings 8:27, “Heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain you. How much less this temple which I have built\!” He asked God to regard the prayers toward the place where his name would be, and to hear in heaven his dwelling place and forgive. The temple played a representative, not a realistic, role.

When Jesus was asked for a sign, he answered, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” He was going to build the actual temple, the third temple, in three days, not rebuild that same physical temple.

“I go to prepare a place for you” means God's house had not yet been built. “In my Father's house are many mansions” speaks of it like an architectural plan: in the house the Father desires, there are many spaces. I want to make you one of its stones.

God's desired house is his family, the church. Originally, he wanted to dwell among all humanity. Everyone was meant to be a stone in his house.

# Living Stones in God's Desired House

First Peter 2:4 explains this: “Coming to him”—to Jesus—“as to a living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God and precious, you also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.”

The house we are being built up as is a place where sacrifices will be offered. What house is that? A temple. All of us are stones that make up this temple, God's house, where we will offer spiritual sacrifices.

God's intention was that every human being would make up God's house. But because of sin, God is not going to dwell in a house of sin. Someone needs to do a work of redemption to make us people God can dwell in. We will be blocks God can dwell in. That is what the Lord was going to do: “I go to prepare a place for you.” By redemption, I will make it possible for you to be a place where God dwells. When you believe, you become part of the stones, the blocks, the spaces that make up God's house.

# The Different Uses of Heaven

God's original dwelling place is himself, his self-existent state, which is also called heaven. “Heaven is my throne; the earth is my footstool” speaks of his throne and everything he created.

Human meaning uses physical things to explain non-physical realities. Languages use the nostril and breathing to describe anger, and warmth to describe love. Likewise, the natural heaven was applied to God.

In Hebrew and Greek, where the birds fly and where the stars are both use the word heaven. What is far above us expresses what is beyond us. God, self-existent and maker of everything, must be in the highest heaven. His authority is called heaven.

God is transcendent, beyond everything he made, yet desires to dwell among his people. He is immanent generally, available to his creation, but there is a particular immanence among his people. His desired dwelling is his family, the church.

Hebrews 12:22 calls the church a heavenly reality: Mount Zion, the city of the living God, heavenly Jerusalem, the church of the firstborn and general assembly. It is a specific reality within the heavenly.

There is also the heaven where angels function. Job 38:7 calls them morning stars and sons of God. “Sons of God” there means the God-kind, not our sonship. They function neither in the birds' physical sky nor among physical stars, but in the heavenly world, the heavenly cosmos.

At the core, God creating the heavens and earth means he created a spiritual cosmos overlapping the natural cosmos. Ephesians 6:12 describes spiritual wickedness in the heavenly realm. First Corinthians 15 speaks of celestial beings naturally and spiritually; “celestial” is the adjective “heavenly.”

# Our Address in the Heavenly World

Let us go to the primary text for this, Genesis 1\. We have looked at navigating the supernatural in the natural world. The first thing we need to understand in the heavenly world is: where is your address? Where do you live in the heavenly world? What do you do in your own house there? It looks like Sims. Where is your address? How do you function from your address, doing other things there?

In Ephesians 6, he said, “We do not wrestle against flesh and blood,” because they were aware that natural forces were affecting them as believers, persecuting them. But he said it is against principalities, powers, rulers of the darkness of this age, and spiritual wickedness in heavenly places. They thought they were wrestling against natural rulers persecuting them, but these are on the other side of the world, the spiritual side overlapping the natural world.

# The Firmament and the Space Called Heaven

Genesis 1:6 says, “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters.” To understand it, look at verse 2: “The earth was without form and void; darkness was upon the face of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters.”

Imagine the heavens and earth as a box, with the top part as heaven where the lights are and the rest as earth. The whole box had become flooded and dark. “Let there be light” brought light, but there was still room for darkness.

Let me say that Genesis 1:1 is not creation. There was not a six-day creation; there was a six-day redemption. Something existed and was being restored. Dry land was not created there: “Let the waters be gathered together, and let dry land appear.” Genesis 1 and 2 are a redemption account based on God's foreknowledge: he would create a world, they would sin and mess it up, and he would redeem it until his purpose was fulfilled. That is the idea of Genesis 1, 2, and 3\.

“Firmament” is \[unclear Hebrew term\], meaning space, expanse of land. God says to form space between the waters: some water above, some beneath. Verse 7 says he made the firmament and divided the waters, and he called the firmament heaven.

The original heavens and earth, the universe and this earth, are different from this space between waters being called heaven. This is the space we are in. He gathered the waters beneath and let dry land appear, calling the dry land earth. There was heaven, this space, on earth over the water and land.

The lights in the firmament of heaven refer to the space of heaven, not this earthly space between waters. Where birds fly is under the water above, which now makes the cloud. In the author's mind, the space we are in can be called heaven on earth, different from the space where the lights are.

In this sense we see the heavenly world overlapping the earth. Jacob saw a ladder between heaven and earth, angels ascending and descending, and called it the gate of heaven: an intersection or portal. Truly, man is the means by which heaven and earth interact, for good or evil. That is God's design.

# Locations and Boundaries in the Heavenly World

Luke 16 shows locations and boundaries in the heavenly world. When people die, they are still in the heavenly world overlapping the natural world, but how we think about it matters.

The rich man lived sumptuously, while Lazarus lay at his gate full of sores, desiring crumbs. Lazarus died and was carried by angels to Abraham's bosom. “Carried” could mean he was led. If I am correct, it might be the word \[unclear Greek term\]. It might not mean they were literally carrying him. You see a journey in the spiritual world.

The rich man died, was buried, and in torment in Hades saw Abraham afar off with Lazarus. He asked that Lazarus dip his finger in water and cool his tongue. Abraham said he had received good things in his lifetime and Lazarus evil things; now Lazarus was comforted and he was tormented. Remember, Abraham also received good things. Receiving good things is not itself the issue.

Verse 26 says, “Between us and you there is a great gulf fixed.” There can be divinely fixed boundaries people cannot cross. Be careful of suggestions that the dead are simply walking around the earth trying to talk to you, and you can just go and talk to them. That is not true.

Despite the great gulf, there was interaction. Boundaries can prevent movement while allowing interaction. “Great” is “megas”; “gulf” is “chasma”; “fixed” means set stable \[Greek term unclear\].

We must study whether Scripture establishes a fixed boundary between the living and dead generally. It establishes one between the righteous and unrighteous in Hades before the Lord's resurrection. What about the living and dead within the church, on the side of light, where the spirits of just men made perfect are? These are questions for this study.

In verse 27, the rich man asks that Lazarus be sent to warn his five brothers at his father's house. Again, there are different locations. The heavenly world has movement, locations, boundaries, and the possibility of interaction despite boundaries.

# Five Uses of Heaven in Scripture

What I want to establish is that there are five ways we can see heaven used in Scripture. Heaven as where the birds fly, number one. Heaven as where the stars are, number two. Heaven as God's own authority or throne, number three. Heaven as the spiritual world God created, number four: where angels function, man functions, and God desires to be immanent in his church. Then heaven as the church itself, number five, because God's own heaven takes his residence there by his choice.

When Solomon said, “Hear in heaven your dwelling place,” he was saying, “Hear from your place as self-existent.” He was also making an insinuation: you are supposed to be dwelling here, but we know you are not. Hear from your eternal or desired dwelling place, the real one, and answer.

I could go further and argue that even at that time we can argue for his actual dwelling in the heavenly world among people of faith, as a temporary stop in view of redemption happening. I do not want to go into all those complexities now.

# Redemption and the Cleansing of the Heavenly

Why do they say Jesus went to cleanse the heavens? It is the same way they talk about cleansing the temple. When the high priest goes into the temple, he sprinkles blood in different places to cleanse the temple. The people who make up God's dwelling place, called the temple, were first in sin and are now being redeemed to form God's house. Jesus cleanses us to form the house where God dwells.

The heavenly world was subjected to death and darkness as much as this natural world is subjected to corruption. There is now a redemption of that world to accommodate the house God wants to have based on redemption. Heaven was under the bondage of death and darkness. Angels choosing the side of light become part of the elect, holy angels, God's side. Men choosing redemption become part of that. There is a cleansing by redemption to form God's house, a necessary cleansing of the heavenly for those who will be of God, from the state of death and darkness that has permeated this world.

# Functioning from Fellowship with the Father

At the core, we are functioning from fellowship with the Father. That is where we started. As children of God, in God's purpose, we have been given access to God where he desires to be: the house he has built for himself, his church. We will see that, in a personal sense, he has made his home with us. We function from that house, our starting location in the heavenly world.

Then we will see the things that can happen to us from that starting location. We can grow, be edified, and have mobile activities in that world. If someone at a distance asks you to pray for them, you can pray, trusting to go and minister there supernaturally.

I hope we get to talk about how, even with things like healing power, we have a more ontological concept of them than we presently do. Let me use the word “tangible.” Healing power is not just something on paper; it is tangible, like how we think about electricity. We are looking at how the spiritual world functions, what we can know in the heavenly world, and how we can function there.

I am in the heavenly world. My primary location is the Father's house. The Father and the Son have made their home with me. I function in the heavenly world from fellowship with the Father, to the edification of myself, and then to the supernatural workings of God.

We function from fellowship with the Father, to the edification of ourselves, spiritual growth in this house, and then to other supernatural workings of God: healings, revelation, utterances, and so on. The believer is called to function in two worlds: the heavenly world and the natural world.

# The Tangibility of God's Power

God is still in the healing business. We must not normalise the contradictions of sickness, disease, growths, or even boils.

There is a way the spiritual world can become more real to you than the natural. We will put caution on our approach to some of these things, but healing power has tangibility. Approach it not just intellectually but ontologically, as it really is.

When we say we are in God's surgery room, we are speaking about tangible things, real surgeries that will outstand even natural surgeons. His strategy room is where we receive his counsel by the supernatural gifts of the Spirit. Such is the nature of the church.

Standing in the council of the Lord, his strategy room, and functioning in his surgery room should become tangible experiences, commonplace among us because we know what belongs to us and press into it intentionally.

From our family home with the Father, we can move to his surgery room: “Father, you are healing me completely, fixing these weak bones, removing these contradictory things in sickness and disease.” In his strategy room, he gives us answers. We will address many rooms and partake of what is available by his Spirit.

The cloud describes that heavenly place as our minds understand it. It is one of the manifestations of the fact that we are describing a heavenly place.
