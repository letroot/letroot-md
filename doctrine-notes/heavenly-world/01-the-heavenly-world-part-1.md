# The Heavenly World — Part 1

## Being Open to What God Is Saying Now

We must know that it is one thing for God to speak; it is another thing for us to be open and disposed to his speaking. There is a state where one is not disposed to God speaking, and there is a case where one is not disposed to a particular kind or manner of God speaking.

In 1 Thessalonians 5:19, Paul says, “Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies. Test all things.” Then you hold fast to that which is proven good. It is possible that one may not be disposed to God speaking generally, or even to a particular kind of God speaking. There are different ways God can speak to us.

Someone says, “If it is not the Scriptures, then I am not going to listen to it.” That is completely different from, “If it does not line up with the Scriptures, I am not going to listen to it.” To say, “If you are not speaking only from the Scriptures, I am not going to listen to you,” can be a problem.

When John spoke the words of the Lord Jesus to the different churches in the book of Revelation, as we have it today, it was not yet Scripture. Now it is for us, by God's orchestration, by God's providence. But he was speaking to them by inspired revelation: revelation that he had by the working of the Holy Ghost, by the inspiration of the Spirit, by the impulse of the Spirit. God can speak to us that way.

I am saying this almost like I am prophesying, speaking by the Spirit, because it was just on my heart: there are things God can only say to you by the inspiration of the Spirit because they are context-sensitive. There are things he has said in his Word that are general. They apply across the board. Then there are things he wants to say that are context-specific, relevant to a particular context.

This is the challenge: we can get bored or tired of gifts, especially, so that we are not listening to what God has to say to us for this time. We should be concerned about the things he is saying relative to a particular context, more importantly the context of a particular time. What is he saying to me at this time? There are things the Lord will speak to us at different times. There are things he will want to address in different churches at a given time.

There are things God will be saying to us as a church, Fullness of Christ Church, that are not what he is going to be saying to another church, because that is what is relevant to us at that time. We see the case where Paul was telling the folks in Corinth that what he wanted to send Titus to do with them was to perfect the grace of giving in them. But he was telling the folks in Thessalonica, “I want to see you face to face because I need to perfect what is lacking in your faith.” We do not know whether, in that case, it is giving. It might not be at all, but he wants to perfect what is lacking in their faith.

There are things God will be speaking to us that are context-specific. That is why we cannot afford to despise prophecies. It says, “Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophesyings.” When you have a negative attitude towards prophecies, you might quench the impulse for it. You might not have the impulse or desire for it. You might not find yourself coveting it, going for it. You might not be sensitive to it. Your attitude towards utterances can numb the impulse for God's Spirit to work in you.

I would even say it this way: your attitude towards utterances can numb the impulse for God to inspire others to speak to you. Sometimes they may still speak to you, but it can affect it because that is your attitude. You do not see the workings of God's Spirit where people can just speak to you out of the blue.

I have had people speak to me out of the blue. I forget how many weeks back now, someone just texted me. He said he was praying and the Lord told him this and that. I said, “Wow, glory to God. Thank you for that,” because I am open to whatever God is saying. He could speak to me and choose to validate what he has said to me via other sources as well. Your attitude towards the things of the Spirit matters.

Despite how much we see of the things of the Spirit in our world, there are people who are almost completely shut out of supernatural occurrences. They are in circles and situations where they are completely shut out of such happenings. They have never seen unquestionable demonstrations of revelation gifts. I am saying that one first, before unquestionable demonstrations of power gifts. They are completely shut out of it. The circle does not even allow it. If you are not careful as a believer, you can progressively grow into a state where that is what your experience is like. That is something we must be careful of.

That is what was on my heart to say: you must be concerned, “Lord, what are you saying to me at this time?” One of the ways you do not despise prophecy is that you have this disposition to know the context-specific revelation of God to you at each time.

I know there is what God has already said to you. Sometimes, if we are not careful, you can have a “Word man” disposition that can be costly. Imagine someone comes to you and says, “What is God saying?” and you say, “God is saying, ‘Read your Bible.’” I know some people overdo some of these things. That may be fine if you know the person you are addressing is becoming overly spooky, overly sensational, and you are trying to get the person to set their foundation right, to keep their foundation as God's Word, as God's truth.

## Context-Specific Guidance and the Counsel of God

That may be fine in that context, but it may not be fine generally. It can create an attitude where you are despising the supernatural, the gifts and the things of the Spirit. There is what God can be saying at a particular time. The person is asking whether there are any updates you have. It could be like a friend coming to you, even in the natural, asking for updates within an industry or a circle. You might have some. If you do not, you can say, “Let us pray,” to see whether we are going to receive something the Lord is saying at this time.

We must have that disposition: what is he saying at this time? What is he saying to us as a church at this time? What is he saying to you as a person at this time? What is God saying at this time? “Lord, what are you saying now?” That means we are seeking the counsel of God. What should we turn our minds to at this instance, beyond what God says generally? There is that which he says to us specifically.

There is something you should be paying attention to now, and it may not be the same as what someone else should be paying attention to now. What makes for God's context-specific speaking can be either of two things. It can be the state of your own growth and walk. He has to speak to it at this time: “Your heart is not right in this way,” and he needs to address it. Or it can be the time-sensitive nature of some of his orchestrations, things that need to be done now.

It works both ways. As I have the privilege to lead us as a church, there are times I know this is what we are supposed to do next. We have to have this in place. This initiative needs to be taken now. That is next. By the counsel of God, you can know.

Then there are times, relative to the state of our heart, our soul, our spirit as a people being of one spirit and one soul, when God has to speak to us: “You are not as strong in your consciousness of such and such right now.” It could be love, and he is correcting us. We have to look at it. It could be righteousness. It could even be the supernatural itself. He is correcting us; we have to pay attention to it. It could be the way we treat our natural assignment. It could be that people are not walking in financial wisdom, and the way they are going about it can affect something else he is planning for the future. He is saying, “You need to talk about this now.”

Can God lead you supernaturally on financial wisdom? Yes. We know the case of his leading for the preservation of Israel. That is a lesson in itself. For the sake of the church, the preservation of the church, he can tell you that you had better take some basic things seriously now, maybe more seriously than you used to before. Save. Have some financial discipline and prudence. Do this next; do that next. This is how you should handle your finances. There are things the Lord can be saying to you. You ought to be aware.

There are things God speaks to you that are context-specific. There are things God speaks to you that are general: “This is my purpose.” There is no particular time you should know that; you should have known that. Those are foundational, general truths. “This is my purpose. This is the truth. This is how you know the truth for sure. This is how you are certain you are not being misled or manipulated. This is who you are in Christ. This is why you are here. These are your assignments. This is what you should do. This is the truth of redemption. You have this character and these abilities in Christ.” General truth.

But there are times when he speaks to you specifically. Those ones are often instructional: “Pay attention to this now. Pay attention to this area now.” Not just, “Know this,” or, “Be convinced of this,” as in the case of general or biblical truth from a doctrinal standpoint. He is speaking to you instructionally: “Be strong in righteousness.” Maybe because you are waning, or maybe because there is a temptation ahead.

Jesus said, “Let us go pray.” He said, “Pray lest you enter into temptation.” At that time they did not know there was a temptation ahead. They might think, “Entering into temptation? We have been with you. We have been fine.” He is telling them that what is ahead requires them to pray now.

Can someone speak to you by the Spirit and say, “It is not that you have not been doing well in this regard, but you need to strengthen yourself in this regard because something is ahead”? Yes, by the Spirit. Someone can speak to you about your love walk by the Spirit, and you might think, “Nothing is wrong with my love walk right now.” But there may be something ahead of you. Maybe someone is about to get you offended, and that offence can get you out of God's will, make you react in a way that is going to be a mess. If you had prepared, you would not enter into that temptation. He says, “Pray lest you enter into temptation.”

## Do Not Despise Prophecy

Do not despise the context-specific speakings of God. When we say, “Do not despise prophecies,” do not despise the context-sensitive speakings of God. This is not, “Do not despise Scriptures.” That is pretty obvious, even though it is still instructed. This is, “Do not despise the context-specific speakings of God.”

You should not despise it in two ways. You should seek to know, “Lord, what are you saying at this time? Is there something you want me to pay attention to at this time?” Also, when he has spoken to you, you should take heed to it: “This is what I ought to pay attention to at this time.”

There is a spirit we should have. We should be of one spirit, one mind. There will be times when God is calling us into a particular consciousness that we ought to share. That is the work of prophecy, especially when he tells us in 1 Corinthians 14:3, “He that prophesies speaks edification, exhortation, and comfort to men.” Particularly that word there, [unclear Greek term]: you are calling someone aside. You are calling a person's attention: pay attention to this thing and edify yourself on it; pay attention to this area. Edification, exhortation, and comfort. There will be things the Lord is calling your attention to.

We say, “Our Father who art in heaven.” What heaven are you talking about? Do we have to go and meet him? Or is he going to be there and hear us? He is far off there, but he is going to hear us? Or is God also everywhere? Is that the sense in which we fellowship with God—that he is just generally everywhere? The answer is surely no. Neither is it that we fellowship with him as though God is just there, but he can still hear us from wherever. Again, the answer is surely no. That is not the idea we get from God's Word, and that is not the truth of the matter.

We have been examining that in the teaching. Here we want to look at what just came to my heart to call “The Heavenly World,” because I want us to use it as an opportunity to take a wholesome view. I will still track back and repeat things I said last week because I think they are supposed to be part of my emphasis for today. But we are going to be looking at the heavenly world.

## True Consecration Without Abuse

Let me backtrack to things we saw last week that I believe are supposed to be part of our emphasis. There is a reason I am repeating this. One of the things I know is meant to be an emphasis at this time for us as a church, and, if I may say, many circles in the body of Christ, is consecration.

There are many reasons for that. There are people who are worldly, loose, and idolatrous, who have abused the faith. There is a need for the truth of consecration to restore things to order. But there are also people who have been on the extreme, and damaging consecration has made people disillusioned with God. It has made them go into rebellion, some into idolatry, into anger against God. Even where they have somehow found a balance, they are not doing it with consecration to God anymore. They are just doing it, God being there by the way, but they cannot say, “Lord, you know I am consecrated to you.”

It can look legitimate because they will say, “I know what I have experienced already. I know what I have seen. Out of my sincere and good intention to be consecrated to him, people have abused the privilege, abused me, misused me, hurt me, manipulated me, damaged me”—mentally, physically, emotionally, psychologically, in all these different ways. They can claim a legitimate reason for their weariness to be consecrated to the Lord.

Christianity without consecration is like salt without its flavour. If that disposition becomes normative, it is not going to end well. I believe that, even for us personally, consecration is an emphasis. For many others, as the Lord speaks to them, consecration may be a required emphasis as well. It has to be true consecration, not a consecration that brings us again into an abusive and manipulated state.

We must have a wholesome view when we talk about consecration, so that people are not hearing it as a dismissal of other vital truths of the faith. Usually, when people talk about consecration to the Lord, because one of its high points, if not the highest point, is the work of ministry, consecration can come across as a dismissal of natural pursuits, or more precisely, our natural assignment.

## Preaching the Whole Counsel of God

Because the highest point of consecration is the work of ministry, there is a tendency, when consecration is being espoused and preached, for people to get the idea that it dismisses natural pursuit. There is a way you can preach so that people think it dismisses financial wisdom, godly financial wisdom, and calls them to a vow of poverty.

There are texts you can go to: “We have laid down all and followed you. We have given up all and followed you.” “We are just consecrated to you. We are not doing anything else. We are just all about you.” You can sound well-intentioned, but the way you are speaking, the general tone you carry, can carry implications that will be costly to the way people live their lives.

That is the power of the pulpit. That is why you cannot afford not to be wholesome as a preacher. You have to preach the whole counsel of God's Word. You have to be holistic in your preaching. Your silence itself is a message. Whether you intend it or not, the fact that you did not make a commentary on something can be taken to mean it is something we should not be bothering about.

You say, “Do I have to say that?” Unfortunately, yes. It is part of your duty. You will be judged for your silence too. Not saying the things you should say, things that can form people's disposition and belief framework, can be expensive. The work of pastoring is not an easy one. Knowing what to say and what not to say, the control of the tongue James was talking about, is that important. Saying what you should say, and not saying what you should not say, is a very important task.

When we are preaching the whole counsel of God, we can read that word “counsel” as “will” as well: the whole will, the whole plan, the whole purpose of God. That word, “boule,” was used with respect to God's purpose, plan, and will in Ephesians 1, especially verse 11.

Paul said, “We have obtained an inheritance, being predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things after the counsel of his will.” We have obtained an inheritance, being predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things after the counsel of his will. I have obtained an inheritance after the counsel of his will.

## Sonship and Our Natural Assignment

The whole counsel of God is about his purpose. We have spoken about what God's purpose is. God's purpose, in a summative form, is sonship. I have always warned against taking the common or simple idea of adoption into your understanding of sonship. Sometimes that idea can rob you of the truth of regeneration, the birth we have in the faith, the new birth.

Sonship is God's purpose. He wants us as his children and as his heirs. As his heirs, we have been given both a natural and a spiritual assignment. We have commented on our natural assignment as part and parcel of God's purpose. No believer must be ignorant of that. Consecration to the Lord includes your natural assignment. You are a bondservant of Christ in your natural assignment. That is without dispute or question. It is not an attempt to balance anything. It is part of what must be said, part of the truth itself.

Not only is it part of the truth that you have a natural assignment and are supposed to carry out your desired, chosen natural assignment with maximum ambition, but financial wisdom, godly financial wisdom, is also part of biblical truth. You may be wondering why I am saying all this when I am going to teach on the heavenly world. We need to make some emphases before we step into that study.

Our natural assignment is part and parcel of God's purpose for our lives. There are things God wants you to do in the natural, and we have seen how he wants us to go about it. You start your natural assignment from where you are, through the opportunities you have, to the things you desire, while remaining open to the leading of the Spirit about anything God may want to say on it.

In this case of our natural assignment, God leaves it up to us. You start from where you are. You take advantage of opportunities that come to you, as long as they are godly. You can pursue the things you desire. But you are also open to the leading of the Spirit. He might say, “Do this,” or, “Do this that way.” The whole counsel of God includes what you do in the natural.

When we talk about consecration to the Lord, we must not assume that because its high point is the work of ministry, our spiritual assignment, it is to the neglect of our natural assignment. In our natural assignment, we are still bondservants of Christ. I have a natural assignment as a bondservant of Christ, and I have a spiritual assignment.

The reason our natural assignment, unfortunately, is not the test of consecration is that all humans have natural pursuits, and not all of them treat those pursuits as an assignment given to them by God. Even if their motivation is just survival, and later it goes beyond survival to personal ambition, they still have natural pursuits. Not all of them regard those pursuits as assignments given to them by God.

Your natural pursuit can be idolatrous rather than an assignment, and a believer can be like that. You have high places different from Yahweh, whereas Yahweh should be the only high place, and he has given you assignments that you are carrying out for him. You see yourself as a bondservant in your natural pursuit.

Whether you are a business owner, a career person, an employee, or a student, if you do not see your natural pursuit as an assignment given to you by God, you are treating it as an idol. It is just your own thing. That is why our natural pursuits are often not the test of consecration. Beyond that, they are not the highest point of consecration. In terms of value, what ranks highest is our spiritual assignment: what God wants to see done in the spiritual condition and eternal destinies of men.

But we must not lose sight of the fact that, in the whole counsel of God's Word, we have a natural assignment. Let me go further: we must not lose sight of the fact that we are instructed to have financial wisdom. We do not just have a natural assignment; we are instructed to have financial wisdom.

We are looking at the heavenly world. I am saying these things because I feel led to emphasise things we have said before, before I get back to the teaching. This teaching is going in the direction of the supernatural, our walk in the supernatural.

## Financial Wisdom Is Part of Biblical Truth

We are not just instructed on our natural assignment; we are instructed to have financial wisdom. Within the practical theology of Scripture, would you see instructions about our finances? Yes. For example, Ephesians 4:28 says, “Let him that stole steal no more, but rather let him labour, working with his hands that which is good, that he may have”—that is finances. Then he tells him what to do: “that he may have to give to him that is in need.” He is instructing him what to do about his finances.

The same thing in 2 Thessalonians 3: “He that does not work should not eat.” If someone is choosing not to be naturally responsible, do not sponsor the person. Do not be generous to the person. He is giving instructions about our finances. The whole counsel of God's Word includes godly financial wisdom.

We examined that last week. The Scriptures present different levels of financial status and the instructions that pertain to each level. We saw seven levels overall, but five of those seven were particularly given instructions.

## Financial Dependence, Sustenance, and Stability

The first level of financial status we see in Scripture receiving instructions by God's wisdom is financial dependence. We saw that in 2 Thessalonians 3. If someone is in a state of financial dependence, God has something to say about that. Scripture instructs that person to be productive, financially productive. He should work.

Now, the person who is attempting to work, who is productive, who is still dependent but working and striving—we have instructions in Scripture to be generous towards them. There is a level of financial status we have an instruction for: financial dependence. Scripture says they should be productive.

The second level Scripture gives instructions to is financial sustenance or survival. You just have enough to meet your needs. There is an instruction to you too. The first instruction is that you can give. It is only a financially dependent person who is struggling to give. If you are in a state of financial sustenance, Scripture says you can give.

But that is not all the instruction to you. You should become stable. That state is not the best. You need to be productive enough so that you can have, in a stable way, to give.

I gave you examples of people in that state in 2 Corinthians 8. The folks in Macedonia were giving to support the Jerusalem church. Paul says they were going through an affliction; they were having a difficult time themselves. But they were giving. That means they were not in the state of the people in the Jerusalem church, who did not even have, who were dependent. They still had, but it was difficult. That is why he said, “According to their ability, and beyond their ability, they were willing.”

They were giving according to their ability, but they were even willing beyond their ability to give. There was still ability. Those in Jerusalem were in a state of financial dependence. These were in a state of financial sustenance and were willing to support them. The Bible is not silent on our natural assignment. The Bible is not silent on financial wisdom.

The third level Scripture talks about is financial stability. It instructs us to get into a state of stability. That is Ephesians 4:28. When we are speaking the whole counsel of God's Word, we tell you God's purpose is sonship. He wants you to be his child and his heir. As his heir, he wants you to do his natural and spiritual assignments. In doing his natural assignment, he wants you to do it with financial wisdom, as a financial steward.

## Financial Security and Providing for Children

As a financial steward, you must know what level of financial status you are in and what Scripture instructs you to do at that level. If I am dependent, it tells me I need to work, at least to sustain myself. If I am sustaining myself, it tells me to get to the place where I am stable enough to support others, to give.

Ephesians 4:28 is on moving from sustenance to stability. Even Paul's example in Acts 20:33–35: “With my hands I have worked to meet my needs and those who were with me.” This is beyond personal sustenance; he is stable enough to meet the needs of others.

We can also presuppose stability from the next level: Scripture instructs you to save—financial security. That is entrenched in Scripture. We saw Proverbs 21:20 and Proverbs 8:18. You are instructed to save. In fact, it calls not saving unrighteousness or foolishness. If you are financially stable, there is a scriptural instruction to save, to have treasures, reserves, deposits. You are instructed to save.

Why are you saving? Can't God take care of you? Someone says, “God is my insurance policy.” Even God has an insurance policy: he says you should save. That means you should do insurance. In a world where there is sin, death, and corruption, it is righteousness and of life for you to have resources to meet different times, seasons, and difficult circumstances.

To the one who is financially secure, there is a further instruction we saw in Scripture: have a financial legacy for your children. Paul says in 2 Corinthians 12:14 that children ought not to lay up for their parents, but parents for their children. It ought to be that you leave a financial legacy for your children.

If your parents could not do that, there is no point fighting them. There is another instruction for you in 1 Timothy 5: children should repay their parents, for that is good and acceptable in the sight of God. But the natural instruction of God's Word is that, as parents, you ought to lay up for them. You are supposed to lay up for your children. Where you know your parents did their best and now it is up to you to be responsible, you ought to see it as your responsibility to repay them.

Scripture instructs us on our natural assignment. Scripture instructs us on financial wisdom. But that is not the high point of our walk in God's purpose, even though it is very important in our walk in God's purpose.

## God's Purpose and Maximum Ambition

We have said before that when you know God's purpose, that is just one step. You need to know God's ambition. I gave a crude example: imagine someone believes God's purpose or assignment for them is to be a professional footballer. That does not tell us their level of ambition as a professional footballer. Someone can say, “I do not just want to be a professional footballer; I want to be the best in the world.” That is ambition. It is different from purpose.

Does Scripture speak about ambition, not just God's purpose? It does. It tells us that God expects maximum ambition in our natural and spiritual assignments. We saw Paul talking about his spiritual assignment, reaching the world with the gospel. He said, “I want to reach all, that I may by all means save some.” He used the example of the ambition of a person competing in the natural to describe the ambition you should have in the spiritual, but also applicable to your natural assignment.

We are told that, at the end, God will judge our natural assignment. Colossians 3:24 says, “From the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance, for you serve the Lord Christ.” Your natural assignments will be judged. Let us look at Colossians 3:22, so that we will be on the same page before we proceed.

## Serving Christ in Our Natural Work

It says, “Bondservants, obey in all things your masters.” I understand that there are those who deliberately choose to read “bondservants” as slaves, similar to the eighteenth- and nineteenth-century slave trade. There are some similarities and differences. That term “bondservant” carries a wider definition than just a typical slave, although it could also mean that. In their economy at that time, human rights were very low.

It included people who owed someone something and were repaying it by working. It included people who could earn in that same sense. When you read some of the accounts of the Lord and see the vineyard owner, he was paying wages to his servants. Reading only one kind of bondservant into it, to say something that suits an agenda you have, is a personal problem. It is not for people who are looking to understand what Scripture says.

Here he is speaking about people carrying out their natural pursuits, in this case as employees. He will speak about masters, like the employers, and that is why there is a reward. They are supposed to be just and fair, knowing they have a Master in heaven. They are supposed to pay them what is right.

Ephesians 6:9 says, “Masters, do the same to them, giving up threatening, knowing that your Master is in heaven, and there is no partiality with him.” Colossians 4:1 says, “Give to your bondservants what is just and fair,” speaking about payments, “knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.” James also talks about not paying the wages that are due and curses the masters who do that.

If you think “bondservant” here is just an indentured servant, that is not complete. If you think it is just a war captive, that is also not a complete view. It is a full view of someone working for someone. It therefore carries no endorsement of whether indentured servanthood is right or being war slaves or captives is right. That is not his discussion. He is talking generally about natural pursuit.

“Bondservants, obey in all things your masters.” If you are working for someone, obey the people you work for according to the flesh, “not with eye service, as men-pleasers, but in sincerity of heart, fearing God.” “Whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men.” He is saying, take your natural pursuit as something given to you by God. Do it as to the Lord and not to men.

The core part is verse 24: “Knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance.” He is calling what they are doing an inheritance, what God has assigned to them. “For you serve the Lord Christ.” They understood the concept of inheritance. They knew Paul's teaching: God's purpose is sonship; they are heirs of God. God has allotted something to them individually to do. They will receive the reward of the inheritance from the Lord.

The masters are still going to pay them: “Give to them what is just and fair, knowing that you have a Master in heaven.” You will also be judged yourself. But he is saying our service here is in view of Jesus being our Lord and Master.

## Maximum Ambition, Not Competitive Ambition

Will our natural assignment be judged? Yes. Will it be rewarded? Yes. What should our disposition be? It is not enough that I know God's purpose or assignment for me. I must know his ambition. To what degree will our assignment be judged?

Look at 1 Corinthians 9:24–27. What kind of ambition does Scripture instruct? Maximum ambition towards our assignments. You cannot say, “I will just do my work as I like,” or, “I will do my academics as I like,” or, “I will do my business as I like.” You are not given the liberty of ambition. You are given the liberty of choosing what to do, but not the liberty of ambition, both in your natural and spiritual assignments. You are only given the liberty of what to do in your natural assignment. In spiritual assignment, that is God's orchestration.

Does the Bible have any commentary on ambition? It does. It tells you the level of ambition to have. A non-ambitious Christian life is a very questionable one. You are instructed to go for the fullness. In your spiritual growth, you are not free to choose the level of ambition. Ephesians 4:13 does not say, “Till we all come to the point that we like.” It says, “Till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.”

For your natural assignment, you have the liberty of choosing what to do. You can say, “I do not want anything risky and stressful. I want to do a very simple thing, live a simple life with my family. I do not want stress.” That is fine, except there is a leading of God's Spirit different from that. You have the right to choose what you want to do in the natural, but you do not have liberty over the ambition with which you do what you have chosen. You must do it with maximum effectiveness, with maximum ambition.

When we find believers living a non-ambitious life, and ask, “You are in school; what are you going for?” they say, “Whatever. I am just doing my own part and seeing what will happen.” That is a sin. It is going to be judged. Or someone working says, “We are just going to get by.” That is a sinful way of living.

Wherever you have found yourself, you are serving the CEO called God in his project called the earth, in its redemptive phase today, even though it will still be destroyed. In this period where his longsuffering is going on, you are serving the CEO called God in your own part. You do not have liberty over ambition. You have liberty over what you have chosen to do.

“I feel disposed to be a software engineer, or a lawyer, or to study accounting, or to do this or that.” You can choose. He told us that in his Word. But he did not give you freedom over the ambition to have once you have chosen.

First Corinthians 9:24 says, “Do you not know that those who run in a race all run, but one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may obtain it.” The same mentality people have in a competitive race, where they know only one will receive the prize, is how you should run. Run in such a way. Have that mentality.

He is not trying to teach us competitive ambition, because God's purpose is not competitive ambition: “The prize is only for one person, and I must be the only one to get it.” That is not true. Sometimes people are taught competitive ambition in school. In a school system, if you teach people competitive ambition, you are not maximising their learning.

Someone gets third position and you ask, “The person who got first, does he have two heads?” That is a nonsense way of parenting someone. You are not telling the person to learn everything they are supposed to learn. You are only concerned about them being better than someone else. If they were better and got 65 while everybody else got 60, you would say, “I am very proud of you.” Why are you proud of them for getting 65 out of 100? If your real concern were for them to do things the best way, you would have maximum ambition, not competitive ambition.

That is how God sees our pursuit of his purpose. He calls each of us to maximum ambition, not competitive ambition. You are not competing against someone.

There are areas of life where you personally can have competitive ambition. That is not God putting it on you. If you are going for a race, a sporting event, you are competing against other people. There is no sin in having competitive ambition there. In school, you do not need competitive ambition; you need maximum ambition. Be trying to get 100. If all of you get 100, you should not feel bad. With competitive ambition, you would feel bad: “Why are we all getting 100? We are not supposed to be mates.” That is an evil heart.

If you are in business, you are competing against others in your industry. You can be competitive there within your maximum ambition, knowing what your competitor is doing. That is your personal issue, not God-instructed. It is understanding how the world works. We are in a capitalist system. You look at what they are doing right and try to do it better, out-innovate them and out-market them.

But speaking about God's purpose and how he wants you to live, he wants you to live with maximum ambition. A state of life where you are not infused with ambition is not normal. You are just going through life to see what will happen. There is no ambition in your bones. That is not Christian living. He says, “Run in such a way that you may obtain it.”

Verse 25 says, “Everyone who competes for the prize is temperate in all things.” That word means disciplined or self-controlled in all things. Anyone competing for the prize is disciplined in all things. “Now they do it to obtain a perishable crown, but we for an imperishable.” Apply the mentality people use for a perishable crown to an imperishable crown.

Are we receiving an imperishable crown for our natural assignment? We saw in Colossians 3:24, “From the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance, for you serve the Lord Christ.” Do you need to bring that mentality into your natural assignment? Yes, a maximum-ambition mentality. More importantly, Paul's direct discussion here was about our spiritual assignment: “I am going to do all things to save as many people as I can get saved.” We need the mentality of maximum ambition in our natural and spiritual assignments.

## Righteous Ambition and Sinful Ambition

A selfishly ambitious Christian is living in sin. A non-ambitious Christian is living in sin. Selfishly ambitious—living in sin. Non-ambitious—living in sin.

Let me give another example. A Christian who does not pray is living in sin. The person can grow, but at that moment that is the case. A Christian who prays selfishly is living in sin. Selfish ambition is sinful; non-ambition is sinful. Selfish giving is sinful; not giving is sinful. Giving itself is not the issue; righteous giving is the issue. Righteous praying is the issue. In the same way, righteous ambition is the issue.

Ambition is not the issue in itself; it is what qualifies it: selfish, righteous, unrighteous, godly, ungodly. Righteous ambition is required of believers. If you see a believer in a non-ambitious state, you are seeing a believer already living in sin.

A believer may say, “Why are they stressing us? All these things are prosperity gospel!” [Unclear aside.] It is responsible Christian living. It is purposeful living. I understand that people can abuse the truth of God's purpose for idolatrous ends. I am aware. But that does not take away from the fact that purposeful living is living with maximum ambition.

When there is no ambition in your heart, in your bones, you need to check yourself. You are surely not doing everything God told you to do, because what God told you to do requires a maximum-ambition mentality where you are trying to obtain as though you were in a competitive race.

The best way to compete in a competitive race is to do what is maximally possible. Even if you draw, and that was what was maximally possible, both of you have gotten the prize. I saw a video one day; I think it was maybe for long jump. The two men had been doing it for a while, and I think they were at the same mark. It had come down to just two of them. They wanted to go again, then asked whether two people could share it, so both got the gold.

The mentality present in competitive ambition, if it is righteous or sensible, is maximum ambition. That is the way he says our lives ought to be in trying to obtain an imperishable crown. We do so with maximum ambition. Maximum ambition is part of Christian living. This is not the only text that talks about it. There is Luke 12, which we examined as well, but that is not my theme today.

## Purpose Requires Consecration

It is one thing to know God's purpose for your life, why you are on earth. The reason you exist is that God made you to be part of his family and to carry out the assignment he has for you. It is another thing to have the ambition you are supposed to have in his purpose. Knowing the purpose is not enough. Having the ambition is important.

But we have been saying that when we know God's purpose and have his ambition, a necessary quality that must be present, separating us from idolatry, is consecration. If your pursuit of God's purpose is without consecration, even in your spiritual assignment, you are an idolater.

There are people who do the work of ministry for self, for fame, for glory. That is the truth. There are people like that. They are users. They exploit people. Not all exploitation is financial exploitation. You can exploit people for self-aggrandisement, to position yourself as something. There are other forms of exploitation, even sexual exploitation. There are people whose leadership of people is to sexually exploit them. These are people using the noble spiritual assignment, the work of ministry, for selfish ends.

The same way some take natural pursuits in an idolatrous manner, some take spiritual pursuits in an idolatrous manner, looking for fame, glory, money, and in some cases control, power, influence.

That is why, in knowing God's purpose and his ambition, we urgently need consecration. Consecration is this: “Lord, it is not about me. Not my will, but your will be done. Not my will, but your will be done. Help me remove self from my pursuit. I am pursuing my natural assignment with maximum ambition, but it is all about you. I am pursuing my spiritual assignment with maximum ambition, but it is all about you.”

Do not let your head start thinking, “One day I will rise up and be this and that.” Be careful. In knowing God's purpose and his ambition, it is required of a steward that one has consecration. I am still trying to get to the teaching on the heavenly world, but I feel I should emphasise some of these things again: purpose, ambition, consecration. We have spoken about it over and again. Are you consecrated?

## Conviction, Consecration, Commitment, and Consistency

Consecration is quite different from conviction, as I have tried to explain. If you want a proper order, you have conviction, number one; consecration, number two; commitment, number three; consistency, number four.

Commitment is an effect of consecration. You become committed, and people ask, “You are so dedicated to this; are you okay?” Then the commitment is seen with consistency.

Many people can have conviction and not have consecration. One example is Peter. Peter was convinced about the person of the Lord Jesus: “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” “To whom shall we go? You have the words of life.” You have proven to us that you are a person of truth. But he lacked the consecration for the temptation they were going to face after Gethsemane. When the Lord was telling them to pray, they were sleeping.

Consecration is not conviction. Conviction is a mental issue: you now agree; you have seen it and accept it. But consecration is different. It is the strength to stand by what you believe, no matter the pressure. That is not conviction. That is a different package: consecration.

Why was Peter broken afterwards? He knew, after denying him three times, that he had gone against his conviction. He knew it was not right.

## Our Status Before God: Creatures and Children

To connect where we are to our teaching on the heavenly world, one of the things God's purpose makes clear to us—in fact, the primary thing we see—is that we are called to fellowship with God. When you are a child of God, God has called you to fellowship with him.

Let me use the approach I used in the podcast, “Fellowship: Our Status Before God.” The primary reality of the Christian faith, of the newness of life, of God's purpose that ought to dawn on us is our status, especially before God.

Go to Romans 8:15–16. We have already established God's purpose from Ephesians 1. His purpose is sonship. We can do the long explanation, but let us move from there.

Romans 8:15: “For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption,” sonship, “whereby we cry, ‘Abba, Father.’” Verse 16 explains what sonship means: “The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God. And if children, then heirs—heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ.”

The first reality of sonship, which is God's purpose, is that we are children of God. He says, “Whereby we cry, ‘Abba, Father.’” I am a child of God.

In the teaching on fellowship, our status before God, I said the first status we had, even as we are now children of God, was the status we had as creatures of God. Romans 1 talks about that. There are loads of details there I do not want to go back into because it could take us into bibliology-like teachings.

The first recognition is that we are creatures of God. Even if you say, “I am a new creation,” being a new creation means you are still a creature. When you are a creature, the distance between you and your Creator is far. One of the major things about someone being a creator is that he can click reset. That is a description we see particularly in the prophets: he could take away his breath, or everything could melt before him. It can be dismantled if there is a major issue. He could click reset on his creation.

He is the Creator. We are his creation, his creatures. That ought to bring awe, reverence, fear into our hearts. [Unclear qualification concerning fear.] The majesty of the Creator, the awesomeness of the Creator—a sense of awe in our hearts that this person who has chosen to be our Father is this kind of person. A self-existent being choosing to share with us his very life and nature! Self-existence choosing to share his life and nature with us, to have us as his children.

God is our Creator. That is the first status we have before God as humans: creatures, with him as our Creator. The second status we had before God was our status under sin. This is often spoken of from a judicial or juridical standpoint. God, as Creator, is also Judge, and under sin he sees us as guilty, as wrong. We are in a state or condition contrary to his intention.

## Redemption, Regeneration, and Fellowship

After redemption, something happens. God saves us. His salvation does not just deal with the consequence of sin and death. He regenerates us. If we start with the fact that he deals with the consequence of sin and death, we might think about it only from a judicial standpoint: sin, death, and darkness have been dealt with, so we do not need to experience the consequence. In that sense, we say we are justified.

But that is not all he did. In that redemptive work, bearing the consequence of sin and death, he gives us his Spirit and regenerates us. That act of giving us his Spirit brings us into fellowship with him.

“Fellowship with him” here does not mean that we are relating with him. It means we are now fellows with him, fellows in a particular context. We are not fellows with God in the most fundamental and substantial context. Substantially, he is self-existent. We are not fellow self-existent people. We are fellows in the fact that he gives us his nature and life. We are fellows of that nature and life. We have his Spirit.

That is the idea of fellowship, what we can call ontological fellowship, used in 1 John 1:1–3. That is why we know 1 John 1 is not written to believers, because you cannot be inviting believers into the fellowship of having God's life and nature. That would be very strange.

“That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life.” This issue of speaking to unbelievers began to arise in some of their letters because they were concerned that some people in their midst were not believers. You see it in Revelation too: “If anyone overcomes,” he has this. They were throwing questions at how some people were acting. They saw people acting like heretics, like idolaters, in ways that concerned them. You see them throwing those posers at them, to examine themselves.

“The life was manifested, and we have seen and bear witness, and declare to you that eternal life which was with the Father and was manifested to us.” He is saying that what is self-existent, we have now witnessed in the person of the Lord Jesus. Eternal life, the self-existent, we have witnessed. He says he was with the Father, so he begins to show this dynamic within self-existence of three persons.

Self-existence has been manifested to us. There is a dynamic within them of three persons, the Trinity, which is a revelatory truth. It is not a truth known by observation and reason. We can know there is God by observation and reason. We cannot know God is three in one by natural observation and reason.

## Revelation and the Fellowship of the Son

“That which we have seen and heard we declare to you,” which is God become man. We are declaring the truth of the incarnation to you, “that you also may have fellowship with us.” That means there were people who were not believing that truth. “And our fellowship is with the Father and his Son.” He is talking about our having that shared nature with the Father and his Son, Jesus Christ. He is saying that the person of the self-existent I have been talking about, who was manifested, is the Son who was with the Father within the reality of self-existence, that life eternal.

Some of what I am saying is logical, and some is not logical, just revelatory. It is Bible truth. Let me repeat: the truth of the Trinity is not a logical truth; it is a revelatory one that we accept upon the fact that the Bible has already submitted rational, logical truth. Therefore we can trust its revelatory details. There are certain revelatory truths that are not known by observation and reason. For example, that there is a self-existent God is known by observation and reason. That there are angels is not known by observation and reason.

There may be a way observation and reason help you to see that there must be a cosmic intelligence aiding what is happening in our world. You can notice, “How could that thing have happened exactly that way? That level of luck or coincidence is too much. It is too to the T. There is something going on behind the scenes.” By revelation you now know there are angels involved a lot in orchestrations, and you say, “Now it makes sense.” But honestly, without the revelatory truth, that rational, observational truth is not enough. It is not enough. Certain things are known by revelation; certain things are known by observation and reason.

## Sharing the Divine Nature: Ontological Fellowship

He says we want you to have fellowship with us: to share what we have, to have in common with us. “Fellowship” means to have in common, and there are many ways that word can be used. When you say you are having fellowship, you have something in common. It can be that people are sharing what they have together among themselves. In that sense, they are having in common what belongs to another. He sees it as belonging to others as well, so he shares what he has with them.

You must not read those things foolishly. It does not take away from the personal agency you have to manage your resources or finances personally, but you see, “What I have, I am a steward.” God sees all of us as sharing together, so I bring what I own for the benefit of others. It is one of the mentalities we have been given.

He says, “Truly our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ.” This fellowship here is not relational fellowship. There is a way you say, “I just went to fellowship with someone.” That one is relational. An example of relational fellowship is Acts 2:42: “They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers.” They were not just learning; they were in fellowship, in having things in common. Some argue that it is giving or the act of breaking bread, but even relationally the word “fellowship” there qualifies. They continued in relational fellowship. “We just had a good time of fellowship together.” We related.

But the fellowship here is ontological: we share the same thing. For example, you have fellowship with every other human being in that you share humanity with them. It is not a divided humanity; all of you have one hundred percent humanity.

That idea is also used in 2 Peter 1:4, when Peter says we have been made partakers of the divine nature. The word “partakers” [Greek term unclear] means we have in common the divine nature. We have been made those who have in common the divine nature. We have in common divine nature.

I like the way Paul says it in 1 Corinthians 1:9: “God is faithful, by whom you were called into the fellowship of his Son, Jesus Christ.” He is saying that the calling of salvation is that we should come into the fellowship of his Son, to have in common with his Son Jesus Christ. Again, this is ontological fellowship.

Another text showing this ontological fellowship is 2 Corinthians 13:14: “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Spirit is with you.” Let us read it this way: the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, which is the love of God and the fellowship of the Spirit, is with you. What God has given in Christ, which is the expression of his love, is the fellowship of the Spirit that is with you.

When you say, “The love of God is with you,” that love of God with you is that he gave you to have his nature. That is the highest expression of God's love: to give all of himself to you, to give his life and nature to you.

You can say, “The grace of the Lord Jesus is with me,” and you mean what God has given via Jesus is with me. What is that? His Spirit. “The love of God is with me”: what God has given as the act of his love is with me, his Spirit. The grace of our Lord Jesus, the love of God, the fellowship of the Spirit is with me.

Whenever you read Paul's complimentary close and he says, “Grace with you,” he is talking about the Spirit of God in you. It is a reminder in the complimentary close. It is another way of saying what people sometimes say, “In Christ,” “Yours in Christ,” “Yours as having all things in Christ,” “Yours as being a new creation in Christ.” It is a reminder of who you are.

Let me show you examples of where he uses it at the end of his letters. When he says “Grace with you,” it is the divine nature in you, the Spirit with you. In Galatians 6:18: “Brethren, the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.” What is that grace of our Lord Jesus Christ? The fellowship of the Spirit. The love of God is the fellowship of the Spirit. God's love in giving Jesus, the grace of Jesus to do redemption, is that we should have his Spirit.

Sometimes even in the introduction he says, “Grace, mercy and peace with you from God the Father.” His nature is now with you via the work of redemption accomplished—the status, the forgiveness, everything. By the Spirit in you, it is with you: a reminder of who you are at the beginning of his letter and at the close of his letter. It is in almost every letter. You see, “Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ,” in Galatians 1, and at the ending in Galatians 6 as well. We see, “Grace with all those who love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity,” in Ephesians 6:24.

Grace is with me. I have the fellowship of the Spirit. When you say, “Grace with you,” you are affirming who you are in Christ. That is why we can call it a benediction. The benediction is an affirmation of who you are in Christ.

Different churches can have different benedictions expressing that today. It does not mean you must say, “You guys did not share the grace.” You can have a different benediction expressing the same thought, confessing God's word. For example: “We are Fullness of Christ Church, redeemed by his blood, indwelt by his Spirit.” What is that? The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: redeemed by the blood, indwelt by his Spirit. Is that the fellowship of the Spirit? Indwelt by the Spirit. But you add, “Called to his purpose, mandated with his mission.” You are seeing what you have the Spirit for. It is an affirmation, a benediction of the truth of the indwelling Spirit, of our fellowship with the Father and with the Son.

## From Shared Nature to Relationship with the Father

There is such a thing as ontological fellowship, but ontological fellowship ought to lead to relational fellowship. For example, there is a way we relate with each other because we are human beings and understand we are fellows. It is different from the way we relate with a dog. No matter how much you love a dog, you are not a fellow with a dog. There is no dog anybody has discussed metaphysical issues with. I have even gone too far: there is no dog anybody has discussed scientific issues with. “Hey, dog, do you understand the quadratic equation I was looking at earlier?” You cannot have that kind of fellowship with a dog, because there is a limitation by ontological fellowship on the relational fellowship you can share with a dog.

That is also why the kind of limitation a human being can have with God is different from a child of God having fellowship with God. Your ontological fellowship creates a kind of relational fellowship with God. The status you have before someone affects the kind of relationship, the kind of relational fellowship you can share.

Think of politicians, or a president of a nation. There is a kind of fellowship their staff and party members can have with them that will be different from the fellowship their child or wife can have with them. Ontological realities, or sometimes judicial realities, create relational fellowship.

There is a relational fellowship we have with God as our Father that is different from the relational fellowship we have with him as our Creator. If we were to relate with God as our Creator, it would just be awe, no warmth. It would be awe: “Oh my God.” But when it is your child, there is now warmth. “Abba, Father.” There is now familiarity, in a positive sense—not familiarity like “see finish.” There is warmth. God is my Father. There is warmth in how we relate, love in how we relate, boldness. Our relationship is not just awe.

Some of you know people who relate with your parents just in awe, for whatever reason, based on their status with them, and you respect that. But when you go there, you cannot start pretending that same awe when you are relating with your parents. If your parents function in a leadership position, whether politically or family-wise, there will be awe with which some people relate to them. It is not that you do not relate with them in awe or regard them. You regard them, but there is more to your relationship than the regard and awe you have for them. There is also warmth, a familiarity that comes with it—a positive one.

We have been called to fellowship with God. The question we asked when teaching “Fellowship: Our Status before God” is that when God justified us and made it possible for us to be reconciled to him, we are told he gave us access. When he justified us and brought peace between us and him, he gave us access. What we are going to realize will take us to our teaching today: he gave us access to him within the heavenly world. The teaching is the heavenly world.

## God's Self-Existence and Our Access to Him

I want to explain the concept of the heavenly world to us, and I think I will explain it better than I have before. I am going to take some things further than we did earlier: concepts like heaven upon the earth, what happens when people die, where they are. There are some basic things I want to establish further in that teaching about heaven.

We have corrected some ideas about heaven. “What is heaven?” “Heaven is where God lives.” Really? Where was God living before that heaven? That heaven has always been, and God found it? Then that heaven is now God. If heaven was first and God found heaven and started living there, then heaven is God. You are struggling with the concept of explaining where God always dwells, because there is no way to talk about where God always dwells. At best you can say God dwelt in himself. A self-existent being is just self-existent.

The concept of self-existence is a very hard concept. If you think about it, you will have a bit of a headache. What we experience now in time is already all experienced by him, because he is one eternal now. He is just an “I am.” If you go deeper into the understanding of God as self-existent and examine his aseity, simplicity, and what some people call [term unclear], particularly Thomas Aquinas—pure actuality, that God is pure act—it means you cannot even think of a process of him wanting. We can only talk about it as a matter of order.

You are saying his good pleasure, his will and his purpose were all at the same time. Everything you are saying he did one by one, you are saying he just did once. There will not be two instances with him. There is a reason it must be like that as a matter of philosophical necessity, but we cannot go into all those stressful details today.

Truly, do not stress yourself either, because God does not intend to relate with us that way. He wants to relate with us in our own way, the way he has made us, in the motion of time, in the moving nature of time. It is like asking God, “Will I fulfil my purpose?” He knows, but that is not a question he will ever answer to you. “Will that person go to hell?” He knows, but that is not a question he will ever answer to you. If God is going to replace someone, he already knows, but he is not going to relate with you from there. Even though he has known in his one pure now, his one pure “I am,” his one pure state of actuality, as a matter of his nature, as a matter of his way of interaction and fellowship, it will not be like that.

When God gave us access to him, we need to ask: you gave us access to you, but access to where? The Lord Jesus brought up the question of “where” in our having access to him. We are not the ones who brought it up. He brought it up a number of times. When that woman asked him whether it was in Jerusalem that we were supposed to worship God or on the mountain, he said, “You will neither in Jerusalem nor on this mountain worship the Father.” There is a place he is seeking people to worship him. There is a “where.” The access we have is to a particular “where” that we must understand.

## Justification, Peace and Access by the Spirit

Romans 5:1: “Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.” We are seeing two things: a judicial status and a relational status. Judicially we are justified; relationally we are at peace. A judge can say you are justified and may still be beefing you. There may not be peace between the two of you. Maybe he has issues with you, but he does his duty as a judge. Normally, if he has issues with you, he would have to recuse himself from the case. If you have personal issues with the judge, they are not supposed to judge the case. In this case, God is the judge who is still faithful, still just.

But we must be clear: whenever we talk about peace with God, the Bible is consistent that the enmity was not from God's side. The enmity was from man's side. That is why the Bible says we were reconciled to him. You will not see it say he was reconciled to us, as though he was beefing us. He was not reconciled to us. Our state of being in sin is a state of enmity toward him. We fixed that issue and became reconciled to him.
