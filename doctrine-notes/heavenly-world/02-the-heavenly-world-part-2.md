# The Heavenly World — Part 2

## Worship and the Nearness of the Heart

Praise the Lord, brothers and sisters. We are looking at the heavenly world. We started looking at that on Sunday in view of GCC. It is important that we always examine this subject because it is an important part of the Christian life. Whenever we study anything along these lines, we are looking at the way the Christian life is in practice.

There can be a kind of Christian life that is not exactly what it is meant to be in practice in the mind of the Lord. There can be a kind of worship that is not exactly what it is meant to be in the mind of God. Last time, when we did True Temple Worship, we had more time to go into doctrinal details. This year, I want to put my attention on the practical details I want us to see.

In Matthew 15:7, the Lord said, “Hypocrites, well did Isaiah prophesy about you.” There are people like these, and we must not assume that their pattern no longer exists. It could be that we fall in that category and have to repent. He says, “These people draw near to me with their mouth, they honor me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.”

There is a way in your worship of God that you can be far from God. That statement ought to provoke this question: Are you close to God, or are you far from God? A lot of things people do in worship keep them far from God while they are very active in what they are doing.

One of the things I bother about is what people often call praise. As I have said, I do not see anything wrong with it ordinarily. I think it has a testimonial use: the celebration of testimonies of what God has done. It is useful. Sometimes they even say, “High praise.” They have taken it to another level. There are known ministers whom, when I hear they are being invited, I say, “So, they will be hot”—literally hot, because you will be sweating.

There is nothing wrong with it ordinarily. But if it becomes all that people do in what they call the worship of God, then there is something wrong, because usually those activities are not interactions with God. You are not interacting with him. It is a one-sided activity: you are excited, celebrating and thankful about what God has done. It has its place. It can become wrong when it becomes all that you do and all that you like. People say, “I find what people call worship boring.”

It is a strange thing to want to engage with God but only want to do the things that are about you—your own joy and celebration of what he has done. You do not want to talk to him as a person, honor him or worship him.

He says, “Their heart is far from me.” There is the argument that their heart being far is about their doctrinal belief, and that is true. He is saying these people are active in everything they are doing, but what they believe at the core is not even right. He was saying that because they were concerned about the washing of hands. They are so concerned about external things, but internally they are not even close to him.

The principle in that text holds true: someone can be doing everything that looks like they are relating with God, but the part that matters most in their relationship with God—their heart—is far. Someone can turn up for every prayer-watch session, morning, afternoon and evening, but according to this text it is possible for that individual still to be far from him.

Are you close to him, or are you far from him? Being close to him or far from him is a question of your heart, where your heart or your mind is. Are you mindful of him? Is your mind present with him?

## The Different Realities Called Heaven

When we look at the subject of the heavenly world, we do so understanding that God, in the beginning, created a heavenly world. God did not just create an earthly world; he created a heavenly world. When it says, “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth,” we must understand what heaven it is saying God created and what heaven God did not create.

For example, God did not create heaven, his throne. There is no separate throne from him. Heaven, his throne, is [unclear wording] himself. That is why it is not wrong when some people say, “Heaven is where you are,” even though there is a context in which it is not true, because there are wicked spirits in the heavenly. Heaven can be a spiritual world overlapping a material world, but at the height of what we call heaven, you can even say it is who he is. The highest thing in existence is God, the self-existent.

Then there is the heaven God created. We can see arguably three or four heavens God created. In original creation, we see three: the atmospheric heavens where the birds fly, the stellar heavens where the stars are, and a spiritual world that overlaps a material or natural world.

There is the atmospheric heaven, the stellar heaven, the spiritual heaven where spiritual beings are and function, and God's person or authority called heaven. In Isaiah 66:1, he says, “Heaven is my throne, the earth is my footstool.” In that context, we ought to think of the earth as the entirety of creation, the entirety of created existence.

When he says, “Heaven is my throne,” are we assuming he is saying, “Atmospheric heaven is my throne”? I bet my bottom dollar he is not. Are we assuming he is saying, “Stellar heaven is my throne”? I am sure he is not saying that. We could also ask whether he is saying, “The spiritual heaven is my throne.” Very likely that is not what he is saying either, because all kinds of activities can happen in the spiritual heaven.

His throne, which means his authority, was functional before he created anything at all. Or did he only become enthroned after creation? When he says, “Heaven is my throne,” we have reasons to understand this as his self-existent authority. It is from that point, from that place, from that seat, that he has done everything else he has done. His self-existent state, his self-existent authority, is called heaven.

We were saying on Sunday that there are different kinds of experiences in the heavenly world. When we speak of the heavenly world God made, we are talking about the spiritual heaven. The different experiences in that world are sometimes compartmentalized, bounded. There can be real boundaries between different kinds of experiences in the spiritual world. There are experiences that pertain to human beings who are no longer in the body, bounded from experiences that pertain to those who are in the body.

Matthew 5:34 says, “But I say to you, do not swear at all: neither by heaven, for it is God's throne.” “I swear to God in heaven”—[unclear phrase about swearing by self-existent authority]. “Nor by the earth, for it is his footstool; nor by Jerusalem, for it is the city of the great King.” The Lord again uses that term, heaven being God's throne. Stephen also quoted it in Acts 7.

It is very important that we appreciate what Scripture calls heaven when we speak about the heavenly world: things of heaven, of the high, of the beyond. “Wicked spirits in heavenly places” means places of the beyond. “Heavenly Jerusalem” is a Jerusalem that is not of here, but of the beyond. “Heavenly Father” is not of here, but of the high or the beyond.

“So will your heavenly Father do to those who do not forgive their brother from their heart”—you see that in Matthew 18. In Matthew 6, you see, “Our Father in heaven.” We would be wise to think of that as the One who is highest: our Father who is of the highest, who is self-existent. Hallowed is your name.

## Heaven as Height, Beyondness and God's Presence

There are at least five descriptions, five separate realities, called heaven in Scripture. When I speak about the heavenly world, which one are we talking about? We are talking about the spiritual world that overlaps the material or natural world.

There are the atmospheric heavens where the birds fly and the stellar heavens where the stars are. These are natural, but they develop the concept of the term heaven for spiritual things. Because they are high above, anything regarded as above, beyond or high in comparison with another can be called heaven.

Today, when people use “heaven,” they use it more for anything pleasurable. But the word itself was not defined by pleasure originally. I think in the Greek the word heaven was defined by mountain as well, which I think is [Greek terms unclear], meaning to lift—something high, above, beyond.

Today, someone says, “Heaven, baby,” as one particular song says. The person is not saying, “You are my baby that is high,” but that you are pleasurable or sweet to me. I think the song actually says, “You could be my heaven,” and then there is supposed to be a comma. He is probably not saying, “You could be what is highest to me,” but, “You could be what is pleasurable to me.”

This is the way language develops; it keeps mutating. Originally, heaven is about something being high or above. Then it is used for God and the world God wants us to abide in, which people understand to be blissful, without sin and death—the future reality of the new heavens and new earth, often called heaven. That idea of blissful, pleasurable, without sin, death and darkness is then taken to other realities.

Someone eats food and says, “That food was heavenly.” Or you go from a place in Nigeria to another country and say, “That place was like heaven.” That is how language mutates. But heaven on its own was about something high, above, lifted.

We see its use taken into God's self-existent state; into a spiritual world above the natural but overlapping it—above in the sense that it influences the natural; and into the church, which is heavenly Jerusalem. The church has a specific reputation for being called heaven because God's own heaven, self-existent, is now taken as immanent in the church. The Father and the Son have chosen to have their dwelling there. God, who is transcendent, who is beyond, has chosen to be immanent, present in the church.

## Access to the Father by Birth

When we speak about the heavenly world, where are we found in that world? Ephesians 2:18 says, “For through him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father.” When we talk about having access by the Spirit, we are not saying Jesus gave us a card called the Spirit. He is saying that by the regenerative work of the Spirit, the regeneration of the Spirit, we have access to the Father.

The idea is the way a child who is born has access to their parents. Even a child who is conceived has a sense of access to their parent right from the womb. That kind of access is on a different level. The parents are, as it were, stuck with the person: this is their child.

That is why the Lord said, “I will not leave you orphans,” or, “I will not leave you comfortless. I will come to you.” He says the Father and himself will manifest themselves to us. “How will you do that and not to the world?” If you love him, believe him and keep his commandment, his Father will love you, and they will make their home with you. We are not a people left as orphans. We are not without an ever-present parenting.

We have an ever-present parent, with ever-present parenting. Having access to God by the Spirit means we have access by birth. Our access by the Spirit is access by birth, so our access to God is direct by birth.

Ephesians 2:19 shows us where we have access: “Now therefore, you are no longer strangers nor foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God.” You are part of God's family. He is showing your status generally, something like a tribal or racial status. You are no longer strangers or foreigners. You are fellow citizens with the saints, members of the household of God.

After showing our status, he shows what we are locationally. He starts speaking with respect to location: “Having been built on...” This location is not just where you are; it is also who you are. You are part of a building, and that location is where God dwells. That access to God is access to his dwelling place. We are his home, his house. He makes his home with us, and that is where we are present. When the believer does not know this, he might not function as he is supposed to.

## Understanding Supernatural Experience

I was reading something someone wrote. There has been a trend of attacks on a particular man of God because he is known to talk about visions, revelations and supernatural experiences—heavenly or spiritual experiences. Someone wrote a post, not from the standpoint of faith but from a natural standpoint, which could be considered a bit disparaging. Let us grant that perhaps he did not intend to be disparaging.

He said, “This person may not be lying.” People were attacking him for his experiences and saying he was lying. I have no concern about whether he is lying; I do not even know the details. But the writer said, “This person may not be lying. It may be that he is sick.” That was where I had an issue with him.

He went on about how people have all these experiences and how it is not an uncommon phenomenon, calling them religious psychoses. My problem was the labeling: people having these experiences and going as far as calling it psychosis. He said some of these people ought to belong in a psychiatric ward. That is not true.

It is like someone taking recreational drugs and having certain experiences. There are people headed toward a psychiatric ward, indeed. But there are certain recreational drugs or intoxicants that people take which create certain experiences, and nobody believes they belong in a psychiatric ward. You cannot fault the experience they had, the phenomenon they went through, but it does not go all the way to saying psychosis.

He was right on one hand: these experiences are not strange to the world. You can argue that they are purely natural phenomena with no spiritual value; that is a separate thing. But I do not think it shows that you have studied the matter enough to immediately give people having these experiences the verdict of religious psychosis. When you want to say someone has psychosis, there is instability. This person would very likely not be as stable; otherwise, you could not be functioning effectively like that. You would have seen a breakdown somewhere.

Even from the standpoint of someone who believes in supernatural experience, [unclear wording]. I think God will use your mind, as he always does with everything. I cannot fault the involvement of his mind. I cannot fault the details of what he is receiving. I cannot fault many things, but I am not going to label it as someone going through religious psychosis.

This is why we must be able to explain supernatural phenomena, particularly revelation. Otherwise, those kinds of naturalistic positions may start shaking people, or they will just be dogmatic about it. Many experiences we read in Scripture, judged from a natural standpoint today, would be considered those kinds of things—not psychosis again, because that fellow is probably showing a little of his ignorance on that matter. That is not how quickly you judge.

Do you know what it means to be psychotic? It is not that you had an experience, you are still composed, and you have integrated the experience into your life in a very natural way. There is going to be a breakdown of how you function in the natural world when what you have received, or the experiences you have had or are having, set in. It will break down the natural flow of your world; it will not be integrated. We need clear explanations about these things.

## Worship as Engagement Rather Than Performance

The believer's walk with God is in the heavenly world. You cannot say, “I am praying,” without being heavenly. You are automatically being heavenly, because prayer is in the heavenly world. That is why we should be concerned about things people do when they say they are interacting with God which do not get them engaging in the heavenly world.

These are not thoughts just to be critical; I think they are important for us. Something bothers me about worship ministers. I have seen this tendency while someone is leading people in worship: they are singing, demonstrative, with their eyes open. I am not even saying that having their eyes open is a problem. They are moving their hands, following the rhythm, doing all these descriptions. I think, “This is not the most effective way.”

If you want to interact with God, interact with God and forget about what you are doing. It is God you are talking to. You are in a conversation with God, who has made his home with you, whose house you make up. Sometimes, when you say something like this, someone says, “[Unclear name] is being mystical.” He is not being mystical; he is being scriptural. Are you actually talking to God?

If you think you are singing and praying, can you be praying? Imagine I am praying, “Our Father who is in heaven, hallowed be your name,” while looking at the audience. Anybody who sees you behaving like that can never believe you are talking to God in prayer. How, then, do we think someone is actually talking to God in worship when they are doing that?

I am just giving you an example. This has nothing to do with the song, certainly not the minister; he is very likely the opposite. [Minister's name unclear] is probably the opposite. “I could go on and on, on and on,” and he is looking at you. I am thinking, “You are supposed to be talking to God.” “But my words are not enough”—are you talking to me or talking to God? You are looking at me. You are focused on me, not engaged with God.

Perhaps you are enjoying how I am moved, which probably means I am talking to God. You are enjoying how I am moved by talking to God, but you are not talking to God. You are supposed to be the worship leader, leading me in my worship of God, which means you are doing the same. If I happen to open my eyes, or, as people do these days as a bad habit, hold my phone, and I see the way you are talking to God, I should be concerned.

This is a real issue. It is concerning because it is taking worship away from what it is and into a performance. A performer is not going to be closing his eyes. He is fueled by how people are moved. There can be an atmosphere in those places that feels like idolatry.

When we worship the Lord, you are not fueled by how people are moved. You are fueled by that engagement with him. You have gone there, engaging with him, and that is what people join you in. This is the reality of the worship of God.

That is why it is not a matter of, “Is there praise? Is there worship?” That mode people like to call praise has what it does, and it is okay. You are celebrating; it has testimonial use. You are giving glory to God for the good things he has done, the excitement and joy of who he is. Fine. But if you want to interact with him, that is a different matter. You are interacting with him, unconcerned about people. It is him you are dealing with.

If we are not careful, the same mindset will seep into prayer. People can be praying and it is performative. When I am praying, nothing else concerns me. I pray where I am, taking the prayer forward: “God, I am here now.” If my mind is going off, I cannot force the prayer. I bring it back: “Father, here. Here.” Then we go from there.

## Spiritual Fellowship Integrated with Natural Life

When we keep teaching about Navigating the Supernatural in the Natural World, True Temple Worship, and now The Heavenly World, it is because something is wrong with the practice of the faith if our attention is always in the natural when we say we are engaging with our Father. That engagement is the fundamental part of the faith.

God's purpose is sonship: children of God and heirs of God. The fundamental thing we are called to do is fellowship with God. That means engaging the supernatural world, the heavenly, the Spirit, the spiritual world, while still actively participating in this natural world.

We particularly started with Navigating the Supernatural in the Natural World so that you would understand the integrated nature. If it is not integrated, knowing that this natural world was created by God, that is truly why people call it psychotic: you are going to become a problem to people. There are cases where people assume they are having an experience, and there is a breakdown in their engagement with the natural world. That is a problem even scripturally.

You are fellowshipping with God in the morning, then continue your day in the natural world while carrying the air and atmosphere of that fellowship. Sometimes you pause and engage in that world again. If it is not that way, something is wrong. I know there may be experiences that are immersive, internally and externally immersive, and experiences that overlap, but your groundedness in this natural world must remain.

When we spoke about the mechanics of the spirit as background teaching to this, we spoke about, I think, five important mechanisms that must be at play in our participation in the heavenly world.

I would not regard that man's experience as someone being psychotic, no matter how he describes it. Someone says, “If you check their description, it describes something they already had in their mind.” That is not supposed to be surprising, because compression and conversion are meant to be normalized in our participation in the supernatural world.

Where I often would not trust someone's experience is the foundation of their belief system. If you do not have the word of truth, it is very likely that your engagement in the heavenly may not be by the Spirit of truth. You may have limited participation by the Spirit of truth because of the limitation of the message of truth you have. There are mechanisms that ought to be in place in our participation in the heavenly world.

## A Dwelling Place for God in the Spirit

Notice Ephesians 2:20: “Having been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets.” We who have access to God have been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief cornerstone. “In whom the whole building, fitly joined together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord.” Then it says, “In whom you are being built together a dwelling place for God in the Spirit.”

Could they have used “in the heavenly” here? Yes. I do not think “in the Spirit” here necessarily means the Spirit God has sent. Imagine it said, “You are built a dwelling place of God in the Spirit God sent.” You could mean it in a sense like “in Christ,” being the people who are in Christ. But he is likely saying a dwelling place of God in the spiritual world.

It can also mean a dwelling place of God in Christ, a dwelling place that is by the Spirit. Everyone forming that dwelling place is a person who has the Spirit. You could have used “a dwelling place of God in light.” God could not dwell in the people in darkness, because they were not in the Spirit; they were in darkness. He now forms his dwelling place in the Spirit, in light, in life, in Christ.

But I think it leans more toward a dwelling place of God in the heavenly, “in the spirit” as location, not as our ontological reality or nature, even though both are fine.

It is the same when the Lord Jesus says you will neither in Jerusalem nor in this place worship the Father: those who worship him worship him in spirit and in truth. Which is that—“in spirit” as location, or “in spirit” as ontological state? I believe it is “in spirit” as location, although “in spirit” as ontological state would not be wrong. That also means that when I engage the Father, my attention cannot be more in the natural. John 4—these are facts of our worship of God and our engagement in the heavenly world.

## Heaven Overlapping the Earth and Our Location in It

We have established that there is a heavenly world God created. It is a spiritual world overlapping a natural world. One of the core texts for that is Genesis 1, which is a redemptive account. God said, “Let there be a firmament between the waters, dividing the waters above from the waters beneath,” and he called the firmament heaven. This very space we are in, he called heaven. He said, “Let the waters be gathered together, and let the dry land appear,” and he called the dry land earth.

We see heaven on the earth. That space upon the dry land is heaven on earth, an idea of the spiritual world overlapping the natural. Does that mean only the space overlapping this one is heaven? No. In their time, they would never pass this space. Today, if someone goes into space, is heaven still overlapping the material world? It is. But that was their way of explaining that within this natural world there is a spiritual world overlapping it.

They always still had a sense of distance: that world is higher, even though it overlaps. In Jacob's dream there was a ladder leading up to heaven. God was above, and angels were ascending and descending. They are overlapping the world, and God was above them all.

The Lord Jesus said, “After this you will see the Son of Man raised up and angels ascending and descending on him.” It is through humanity that we find the ministry of angels happening, and that is supposed to be on the increase from the resurrection. Through humanity, we are supposed to see an increase of activities from the heavenly world in the natural world—in favor, preservation, orchestrations, in pursuit of God's purpose, natural assignment and spiritual assignment.

Our concern is this: if God created a heavenly world, and man is the means by which the heavenly and earthly worlds interact, what is our location in the heavenly world? That is what we are looking at in Ephesians 2:18. Our location is God's house. We make up God's dwelling place. We can say our location is the Father's house, as Jesus our Lord called it.

## The Father and the Son Make Their Home with Us

When I find myself a new creation, born of God by the Spirit, I have access to the Father. We have access to him in his dwelling place, which is all of us as believers. All of us make up his dwelling place, the Father's house, the temple of God. I realize that individually the Father and the Son make their home with us.

Look at John 14. In verse 18, he says, “I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.” Despite the fact that he is going to send the Spirit, when he has sent the Spirit he will come to us.

I have seen an unfortunate interpretation: “I will send the Spirit; I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you” means that sending the Spirit is him coming. Jesus says in John 14:16, “I will pray the Father, and he will give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you forever, even the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, for it neither sees him nor knows him; but you know him, for he is with you and shall be in you.”

He was saying that he needed to go so he would pray the Father to give them another Comforter. Verse 18 then says, “I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.” The interpretation goes that the Spirit he sends is him coming to us, not leaving us orphans. I think the interpretation goes further to say that the Father and the Son making their home with us is the Spirit being sent to dwell in us. I am not sure about that last one—let us say I am not sure—but I am sure about the first one.

That is bad interpretation. It is: “I will send the Spirit to be in you, number one. But even upon that, I will still not leave you orphans. I know I am leaving you now, but do not worry; I am coming back to you again.” The conversation continues when he says, “A little while longer, the world will see me no more, but you will see me.”

Now everybody is seeing him, both natural and spiritual people, people of faith and those not of faith. But soon they will not see him anymore, and yet you will see him. That word “see” matters. He says, “You will see me.” We are supposed to see Jesus. “Because I live, you will live also.” At that day, you will know [unclear wording of the quotation concerning being in the Father and in one another]. We will share the same nature. There will be fellowship with the Father and the Son, as 1 John 1:1–3 says.

Verse 21 says, “He who has my commandments and keeps them is he who loves me. He who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and I will manifest myself to him.” It was clear. Judas, not Iscariot, understood that Jesus was saying, “I am going, but when I am gone, I will still be around.” He got what he was saying: “When I go, I will send the Spirit, but I will still come back myself and be with you.”

Judas, not Iscariot, said, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us?” You will be perceptible to us. “Manifest yourself”—emphanizo. Emphanizo means something is in the light, contactable by your senses.

Imagine we are all in complete darkness, and someone brings in something new, perhaps a table. None of us will know that the table is there because it is not manifested to us. Our sense of sight cannot pick it up. If we touch it, it becomes manifested to us; we can feel it. When he says, “I will be manifested to you,” he is saying, “I will be perceivable to your senses.”

He said, “You will see me,” which is already the sense of sight. But he also says, “I will manifest myself to you”—I will be perceivable to your senses. Is sensing the presence of God a thing? That is just one part, the sense of feeling. The idea of seeing God is a scriptural reality, and it is even spoken about as though it should be common.

“How will you manifest yourself to us and not to the world?” I want you to think of “manifest,” although often used for what is visible to our sight, as overall perceptibility to our senses. How will you be perceptible to our senses and not to the world's? That fellow who wrote the post would call this religious psychosis, because he is saying, “I am going to bring you into that kind of state.”

The Lord answered, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word; my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.” He is saying there is a personal experience of the person who has believed, where the Father and Son make their home with him, and it is an experience perceptible to his senses and sight.

## The Reality of the Heavenly World and Our Perception of It

I love this because there are two things here. There is an ontological reality: the Father and Son make their home with you. But there is also the fact that your senses are working. Your senses can engage it. On Sunday, I spoke about tangibility: a sense of tangibility and perceptibility ought to accompany our engagement in the heavenly world. Is that the point the Lord is making here? Yes.

There is tangibility—that it is an ontological reality—and the perceptibility of what is tangible. This lectern, this pulpit, is here. I can touch it. It is one thing that it is here, and another thing that I can perceive it. Someone might be dead: the table is there, but the person has no perception of it. That is the idea of “Because I live, you will live also.” Those who are dead in that state will not be able to perceive.

That person's condition does not change the fact that the table is here, but their perception of it is gone. He says two things will be the case: there will be that reality ontologically—you make up the Father's house, and the Father and Son make their home with you—but there will also be your perceptibility of that reality.

In talking about our participation in the heavenly world, we must establish both things. There is a real heavenly world we are in, and there is a real, tangible and perceptible engagement with that world.

When we say “clouds,” that is a description of something high above, because we know the clouds are above. We are saying heaven upon the earth, clouds of glory: a tangible, perceptible, visible and audible heavenly world that is here. I know “cloud” can also have another use for God himself, the manifestation of his glory. But “clouds of glory” is heavenly reality spoken of positively. You can speak of dark clouds as well, sometimes used for God in judgment, which can be seen as God's withdrawal or the withdrawal of light.

Are you aware of this reality, that you are in the heavenly world? And are you aware that your experience of that reality ought to be tangible? That is what I want to get to.

## Natural and Discretionary Impulses

This can sound strange: “Are you saying it must be tangible?” It ought to be tangible. It should not be strange that your experience of that world is tangible, to whatever degree. We might speak of natural degrees and discretionary degrees in this case, following the way the gifts of the Spirit work, which you could call the way our spiritual senses work.

I remember giving someone an example of natural and discretionary impulse. I was talking about [unclear name or phrase]. At first, I thought he told me he was not [unclear phrase concerning the Holy Ghost]. Later, after I told him my experience and different things, he said he actually had done it. The issue was that he did not do it like that, so he thought he did not have it. He should just do it like that.

I explained the difference between natural and discretionary impulse. The impulse to urinate is not there all the time. If it is there all the time, you have a problem. The impulse to the other bodily function cannot be there all the time either; how would you attend church? Even the impulse to pass wind cannot be there all the time, although for some people it is at a higher level. If you eat a lot of beans, then you have that impulse more.

In the same way, if by the grace of God you pray a lot, the impulse for certain supernatural things that are discretionary will be higher. You have put yourself in a position where those things are more available to you.

But there are things for which the impulse is natural. The impulse to look at anything is available; you can just decide to look. Even in our natural body there are natural impulses, and there are discretionary impulses, impulses that come at particular times, by occasion or situation.

You could say the impulse for money is natural: people want money. But if something suddenly happens—a sound, “boom”—the impulse to look there is occasion-determined. Something happens, giving the impulse, “What is that?” You react, a reflex. That impulse is occasion-determined.

## Engaging the Father's House through Persistent Faith

Let us establish something. We are in the heavenly world; that is one reality we must be aware of. But our participation in that world ought to be tangible. There should be a real perceptibility to our engagement in that world. Can you hear a sound from that world, as though you hear a sound? Yes. Our level of participation in that world should be tangible and perceptible.

Even when we minister to the sick, and when we pray, our participation in that world should be tangible and perceptible.

“We will come to him and make our home with him. We will manifest ourselves to him. The world will not see me, but you will see me. Because I live, you will live also.” There is a way you will be alive that will make you see him, perceive him, that will make him manifest to you when they make their home with you.

We can now ask: How do we get to see, perceive or have these experiences in the Father's house become tangible to us? We already know the answer. How we engage with the Father, how we fellowship with him in this house, how we see and perceive, is by faith standing in prayer. Any result that happens there is by continually asking, seeking and knocking.

Luke 11:2 says, “When you pray, say: Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. Forgive us our sins as we forgive those who are indebted to us. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.”

We know the way we engage in this house is by prayer. If I am going to perceive and see, it will be because I am engaging in the house—that is, praying. But how do I engage that prayer itself? With the persistence of faith. Prayer is what it means to engage God, to engage the Lord in this home they have made with us. In what manner do I go about the business of prayer? With persistence of faith.

If we find ourselves in this heavenly world but are not walking in all its realities, it points to something in our persistence of faith. That is why we must be careful about a lot of teaching as opposed to a lot of praying. A lot of issues are for the place of prayer, to be addressed in prayer.

Honestly, I feel we should be able to have meetings where we can just pray without having to bother about time. Just be there, pray and enjoy these things. There are times I do not even feel like talking. What am I preaching again? We already know that the end of all the preaching is faith standing. Is it not better to spend some more time in prayer, some more time standing, some more time in faith, so we can really change?

As you are praying, you take the things that really matter from what has already been passed to you. You know the ones to address, and you work on them. It is even better when you can hear God more precisely within the shorter time and address the areas that need to be addressed.

If God's design for the heavenly world is for us to make up his house and have tangible, perceptible experiences in it, we must stay in the persistent engagement of faith to have these tangible, perceptible, visible and audible experiences, whether they happen by natural or discretionary impulse.

There is the heavenly world, but engaging it is by the persistence of faith. We must stay there until we have the tangible, perceptible, visible and audible experiences he desires us to have in the glory cloud, in the heavenly world. Again, “cloud” is about heaven here, using the idea of atmospheric heaven to describe the spiritual world overlapping the natural. We must engage in persistence of faith until we have those experiences there.

## Tangibility, Perceptibility, Visibility and Audibility

Let us go to Luke 9. We would argue that this experience, which would likely come out as a discretionary experience, is born out of that persistence of faith in engaging the heavenly world.

We have a heavenly world as a matter of ontological reality, and engagement in that world is persistence of faith, causing tangible, perceptible, visible and audible experiences in it. Even in a time of morning prayer watch there should be a level of tangibility, perceptibility, visibility and audibility of the heavenly world. “I will manifest myself to you”—I will make myself perceptible to you. It was expected that this was how things would be.

In times of prayer, whether you expect it or not, it should be a reality. There should be a degree of tangibility, perceptibility, visibility and audibility of the heavenly world toward us. Luke 9 is one example of that happening; you might say it is a discretionary one.

There are natural experiences where we feel the heavenly world, where it is tangible and perceptible to us. There are discretionary ones where more visible and audible things are happening. I would argue that tangibility and perceptibility, at least at the lowest degree, should be a matter of natural experience. We may argue that visibility and audibility land more on the discretionary side, made possible by our being disposed to the natural experience of tangibility and perceptibility.

You will see experiences where they say the glory filled the house, which lies more on the tangibility and perceptibility side. At times they say they saw the cloud; that is more on the visibility side. Sometimes a sound came: audibility. But just the experiences in glory are more on the tangibility and perceptibility side. People being as though in a cloud or fog should be more on the natural side of our experience. As we embrace more of these, discretionary experiences serving as signs, wonders and even comforts to us will become more commonplace.

## As He Prayed: The Glory on the Mountain

In Luke 9, it came to pass about eight days after these things that he took Peter, James and John and went up on the mountain to pray. “And as he prayed...” If he had not prayed, would we likely have seen this? Was it still a discretionary experience? Yes, but it was still as he prayed.

As Peter prayed, he fell into a trance. If he had not prayed, would he likely have fallen into it? No. Was the trance natural or discretionary? Discretionary, but it was still as he prayed.

As Paul prayed, he saw a man named Ananias coming to lay hands on him. If he had not prayed, would it likely have happened? Likely not. Could it still happen? Maybe yes, but likely not. That seeing is discretionary. It is not necessary that, because you are praying, you should see someone coming to lay hands on you and that his name is Ananias. But you set yourself up for discretionary experiences by giving yourself to the natural experiences with their tangibility and perceptibility.

“As he prayed, the appearance of his face was altered, and his robe became white and glistening. And behold, two men spoke with him,” who appeared in glory. We are seeing visibility and audibility here. I am telling you that glory is often a matter of tangibility and perceptibility. You should be able to feel glory, to be clothed with glory. You will see that they were overshadowed in something.

Peter and those with him were heavy with sleep, and when they were fully awake, they saw his glory and the two men who stood with him. They saw: visibility. As the men were parting, Peter said, “Master, it is good that we should be here. We should make three tabernacles, one for you, Moses and Elijah,” not knowing what he said. He likely spoke out of impulse.

While he was saying this, a cloud came and overshadowed them. This is something tangible and perceptible. They were fearful as they entered the cloud—not that they were walking inside it, but as the cloud overshadowed them, it says they were entering it.

Does that experience happen in a Christian meeting, where a glory clothes you, wears you? Yes. A natural observer will call it an altered state of consciousness, something psychotic-looking. But they are meant to be tangible and perceptible experiences in the Father's house, in the heavenly world, which should then come with discretionary visible and audible experiences in that world. I hope you get hold of this order.

The cloud overshadowed them, and a voice came out of the cloud—audibility—saying, “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” Many times, the reason we have limited discretionary experiences of the cloud of glory, of the heavenly world, is that we have not even engaged the natural experience: being in that cloud in a tangible and perceptible way.

Sometimes people call it soaking or ascension. Those words describe the experience of the heavenly world, which should be a matter of natural experience. Are we in the heavenly world or not? Are we in the place of this cloud or not? Those are questions we should ask.

## Peter's Trance and the Place of Natural Engagement

In Acts 10:9, “The next day, as they went on their journey and drew near the city, Peter went up to the housetop to pray about the ninth hour. He became very hungry and wanted to eat, but while they made ready, he fell into a trance.”

Falling into a trance is discretionary. But something must have been present naturally, which we would argue is the tangibility and perceptibility of the heavenly world, of the glory cloud, which then accommodates the discretionary visibility or audibility that can happen.

Internally immersive experiences, externally immersive experiences, internal overlap experiences and external overlap experiences are very likely more on the discretionary side. The basic thing we are talking about is tangible and perceptible experiences that then accommodate those things as God wills.

We should be a people of the heavenly world, engaging it tangibly and perceptibly. People can be living their lives and not know that they are so far from him. Being far from him can sometimes be felt. I know sometimes people say, “I do not feel anything, but I am sure I am fine.” That is true. But sometimes it can be felt. You know by feeling, by tangibility and perceptibility, that it has been a while since you tasted his glory.

It has been a while since you were just there, engaging in the holiest, in his presence, in his home that he has made with you, in his house that you make up with all the saints. You can tell it has been a while, and you have been far. “Draw nearer to me, and I will draw nearer to you.” You need to draw nearer.

Let us pray for ourselves, just talking to our Father as we ought to, as it should be.
