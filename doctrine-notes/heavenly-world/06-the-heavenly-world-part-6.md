# Five Uses of Heaven and Paul’s Experience of Paradise

We have seen the five uses of heaven in Scripture. Number one, atmospheric heaven. Number two, stellar heavens. Number three, self-existent heaven, God’s throne. Number four, spiritual heaven, the spiritual world overlapping the natural. We made a huge part of that explanation from Genesis 1:6 downwards.

Number five is the church as heaven, because this is where the self-existent heaven desires to reside. For that case, where God dwells, or “hear from heaven Your dwelling place,” it wouldn’t just be the self-existent heaven today. It would now be Your intended dwelling place as well, what they were trying to depict as temple that has now become a reality, which can then be called heaven: the church. We can speak of those five views of heaven.

When Paul says in 2 Corinthians 12:1–3 about his experience of being caught up into paradise, we are likely looking at his experience in the heavenly world of light, or we can call it the church. # The Resurrection Appearances Were Physical Experiences

It’s important to know that the encounters people had with the Lord at His resurrection were not visions; they were physical experiences. That’s exactly the point they were trying to make: “We thought we were seeing a vision.” We had been with this person on the mountain before; we knew that was a vision.

When we saw Him, we said, “We believe in the supernatural. We see a vision, fine. Maybe He has gone; He wants to talk to us. Okay, thank You. But You are giving physical vibes. Are You really physical?” “Come and touch Me. A spirit has not flesh and bone.” This is not a vision. This is not a heavenly experience. This is a natural experience.

Even though He did supernatural things like walking through the walls and disappearing, it was a natural, physical experience. It’s like saying you were caught up, you teleported physically. That is a necessary testimony in the message of the gospel. That’s why they didn’t see His body, then they now saw Him bodily.

It’s not a spiritual resurrection or a spiritual experience. I get that it is still an encounter, but it’s a natural experience. They saw Him physically, touched Him. They asked Him to eat at one instance; at another, He just ate with them by the sea. It was a physical experience, a natural experience, for the resurrection.

“Of such a one I will boast; yet of myself I will not boast, except in my infirmities.” He considered the experiences as something worth boasting about: someone who has that kind of fellowship with God. # Jeremiah: Standing in the Lord’s Council and Receiving His Message

A very major text for how functioning in the heavenly world, or as a prophet, works is Jeremiah 23\. You’re supposed to stand in that place. Verse 18: “Who has stood in the council of the Lord, and has perceived and heard His word? Who has marked His word and heard it?” There’s that understanding of how the heavenly world, the supernatural, works.

“Behold, a whirlwind of the Lord has gone forth in fury, a violent whirlwind. It will fall violently on the head of the wicked. The anger of the Lord will not turn back until He has executed and performed the thoughts of His heart. In the latter days you will understand it perfectly. I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran. I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied.”

They didn’t stand there, yet they ran. The word of God had not come to them, yet they ran, prophesied. They didn’t stand in the council of the Lord to perceive and hear His word. “But if they had stood in My council, and had caused My people to hear My words, they would have turned them away from their evil way and from the evil of their doings.”

Now look at “burden” in verse 33\. You needed that background to appreciate what he’s talking about. “When these people, or the prophet or the priest, ask you, saying, ‘What is the oracle of the Lord?’” That’s the word “burden,” the same word massa. “What is the burden of the Lord?”

When we say the oracles of God, you have the burden of the Lord, the message He has sent that you are unfolding in words, converted and compressed to words, to vision. But there’s the burden; the message is sent. The message of the Lord came to you.

“You shall then say to them, ‘What oracle?’” Let me use “burden” so we’ll be consistent. “‘What burden? I will even forsake you,’ says the Lord. As for the prophet and the priest and the people who say, ‘The burden of the Lord,’ I will even punish that man and his house.” They’re saying this is the burden of the Lord, and He says they are lying.

“Thus every one of you shall say to his neighbour and everyone to his brother, ‘What has the Lord answered?’ and ‘What has the Lord spoken?’ The burden of the Lord you shall mention no more, for every man’s word shall be his burden, his oracle; for you have perverted the words of the living God.” You are claiming as the burden what is just your own words. That’s what he’s trying to say.

“Thus you shall say to the prophet, ‘What has the Lord answered you?’ and ‘What has the Lord spoken?’ Since you say, ‘The burden of the Lord,’ therefore thus says the Lord: ‘Because you say this word, “The burden of the Lord,” and I have sent to you, saying, “Do not say, ‘The burden of the Lord,’” therefore behold, I will utterly forget you and forsake you.’”

Stop prophesying. I’m not giving you any revelation; don’t say “burden of the Lord.” Because they still say it, He’s going to judge them.

The way the prophet receives when he stands in the holy place is a burden that he now unravels. There’s a burden of the Lord, the burden of the message of the Lord that you receive and unravel. You perceive and hear or see. Who has stood in the council of the Lord to receive His burden, and perceived and heard His word? That message, you are now going to perceive, hear or see it.

# From the Spirit’s Impulse to Knowing, Hearing or Seeing

We can call that burden an impulse, because we see the operation of the hand of God in how we speak with tongues, how they got filled in Acts 2\. They were all filled with the Holy Ghost. The hand of the Lord is upon them, and they began to speak with tongues. They were filled with the Holy Ghost; the Spirit gave them utterance, which is burden, or impulse, and then they spoke with tongues. Filled with the Holy Ghost, the Spirit gave utterance, spoke with tongues. The Spirit gave a burden.

When we are praying or worshipping, we are generating the cloud of His glory, which we call the cloud of incense. We have explained this before. Leviticus 16: there will be a cloud of incense as the physical demonstration of how the cloud of glory comes to be via prayer and worship. The incense was explained as prayer and worship: Psalm 141:2, Revelation 8:3–5, then Luke 1:9–11 or 12\. The incense is prayer or worship.

When we are generating the glory cloud, bringing about intoxication, the hand of the Lord to be upon us, there will then be a burden. There will be a burden, and that burden is what will now be known, perceived, heard or seen. This is what this is about when the word of the Lord comes to us.

This is the general process, you might say, of how we function in the supernatural. We can go and inquire of the Lord. The hand of the Lord is upon us. Then the burden of His answer, His message, comes to us, and it is unravelled before us as a perception, a knowing, or a hearing. We’re hearing a voice; it could be a still small voice. That’s one thing we can learn from Elijah: it can simply be a still small voice. Or it could be a sight, a vision.

We can inquire of the Lord. We can ask Him: “Whatever you ask, I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.” In this house we can ask, and we can have those experiences. We can ask. We can ask. We can ask. We can ask.
