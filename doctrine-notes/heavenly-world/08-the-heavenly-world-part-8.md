# Fellowship and the Reality of the Heavenly World

We have been looking at the heavenly world. This is our final teaching on it before GCC, which is the major reason we have been looking at this teaching.

The reality of the heavenly world is not one the believer can ignore. It is at the core of our faith and the reality of God's purpose. In Ephesians 1, Paul tells us that God's purpose is sonship. We have studied Romans 8:15–17: sonship is to be God's child as well as his heir. We are called to fellowship with him, to walk in his nature and character, and to carry out the assignment he has allotted to us.

Fellowship with God is most fundamental in our walk in his purpose. Fellowship with him is most fundamental in and to our walk in his purpose.

Ephesians 2:18 shows us how we fellowship with God: “Through him”—the Lord Jesus—“we both have access by one Spirit to the Father.” Via the work of redemption Christ has done, he has brought us back to God's desire and intention from the start: to be with him, to fellowship with him. Through him we have access by one Spirit to the Father.

We know who we have access to. But there is a question to answer: where do we have access to? Where is the Father for us to have access to him?

# The Father's House and the Question of Where

This discussion came up earlier with the Lord's disciples in John 14\. He told them, “In my Father's house there are many mansions. If it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you.” He said that when he returned he would bring them to himself, that where he was they might be also.

They got the idea of the “where,” but Thomas asked in verse 5, “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?” He had just told them he was going to the Father's house, and they were asking where exactly that was.

This issue of the “where,” the heavenly world, is so core to our practice of the Christian faith. There will be a degree of ineffectiveness in our practice of the faith without an understanding of the heavenly world.

They asked, “How can we know the way to the place that you are going?” In verse 6 he says, “I am the way, the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” Then he says, “If you had known me, you would have known my Father also. From now on you know him and have seen him.”

I do not think this text, in saying they know him and have seen him, is saying that Jesus is the Father. You start to have modalist beliefs that way. We see a separation of the personalities. He says that he and his Father will come and make their home with you. You cannot say “I,” then “my Father,” as separate entities, and say he means exactly himself.

There are things they would have seen and known about God in seeing him. Because of his active relationship with God, they would have had a witness of him as well. When he went to raise Lazarus from the dead, he said, “Father, I know you always hear me. I say this for the people that are here.” Then he said, “Lazarus, come forth.” He had been revealing the Father all the while they had been with him. They had seen the Father's works and character: “My Father works; I also work.”

That means, to a degree, this is a statement you can say. I know that in another way we say it is a statement you cannot say, because he is self-existence become man. But someone walking with you can see God at work: “I am his son; I am his child. You have seen him at work in my life.” There must be clarity on that.

They say, “Lord, show us the Father, and it is sufficient for us.” But let me not derail our conversation, because our conversation is on the “where,” the heavenly world.

Appreciating the heavenly world is a fundamental part of the Christian faith, unfortunately much ignored today. It is not an extra or a by-the-way matter. Prayer is so core to our faith, and the engagement we are meant to have in prayer should be with an understanding of the heavenly world.

If God's purpose is sonship, to be his child and his heir, and as his child we are primarily called to fellowship with him, and where we fellowship is in the heavenly world, then we need to understand the heavenly world.
