## God's Revealed Purpose and Good Pleasure

I want you to see Ephesians 1:9. We're looking at what this purpose of God is. Then it's going to help us circle back to the relevance of the heavenly world to us. It says, “Having made known to us.” God has made known to us the mystery of his will. The reason he had to mention this is because you cannot know someone's will except by revelation.

If I say, “What are you planning to do in life?” you have to reveal it to me. Sometimes you can take a guess by observation and reason. For example, if I see you studying law, I could suspect that you want to become a lawyer. But it could be a decoy. It might not be what you want to do. It could be something you are forced to do, but you have another plan in mind. Observation and reason may not suffice to establish with certainty someone's will: what someone is going for, what they want, what their ambition is.

Revelation is the gold standard for knowing someone's purpose, will, plan, goals. Revelation is the way to know. The second way to know is when it has been fully done, which might be late. But the way to know ahead of time is revelation.

If someone by necessity is of truth and of life, then his revelation becomes sure and certain. If someone should reveal to you as a human being, “This is what I'm trying to do,” you might be unsure. But it would be different if God, who by necessity is good, life, light, and of truth, reveals his purpose. Then we have utter certainty about what he has revealed to be the case.

In Ephesians 1:9, it says he has made known to us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he purposed in himself. The word “good pleasure” means what you like. I often use the example of your favorite food. Typically, nobody has a logical reason for having a favorite food. It's very emotional: the way you feel, the way it works with you, the way your taste buds have aligned with it. We can maybe logically explain materially how it came to be your best food. But to you, it is your favorite food just because you vibe with it.

Someone can say it's because of your taste buds. Maybe this is what you first tasted and what you were exposed to first, or it has this uniqueness to you on the scientific level. But to you, the reason why it's your best food is that it is just your thing. You have a connection to it that you cannot explain.

For example, I think one of the reasons for my love for puff-puff is that we didn't used to eat puff-puff that often. There was this aunt of ours that, every time she came around—maybe every holiday, every three months—would buy it. It was cheap, but it was so special to us. Every week, we used to buy what we called goodies: snacks, meat pie, things like that, every Saturday. But when she came around, she was going to buy puff-puff, hot, wrapped inside newspaper, inside black nylon. There was just a connection I had with that thing. I said, “Something is special about this thing.” In fact, meat pie was not as special as that puff-puff.

One day I realized, “She's buying it at [junction unclear]. This must be the place she's buying this thing.” At that time my love for puff-puff had already been established as my good pleasure. I can't explain; there was just something special about it. Honestly, I think that's probably where it started from, because later on, even when we started to do it at home, I started getting tired. But that affinity had already been fully formed.

I do think that's how most times people's favorite food is: an affinity that someone can probably explain scientifically, but the person himself at that moment doesn't really care about what established it. “I like this particular thing. I vibe with it. It is my thing.”

In this case, we are seeing something called God's good pleasure: what he just likes. Then he says what he likes became his will. I use the example of having this favorite food, what you just like, but there are days you wake up and have a craving for it. You could ignore the craving. Maybe you are trying to stay on budget, and you say, “I'm not going to buy it.” But other times you say, “I have some extra. I can indulge.” It moves from your good pleasure to what you want—a craving—and all the way to what you have determined to get.

In this case, this is not like a food issue that is repeated over and again. This is fundamental to God. There is something he desires, called his good pleasure, that then is also called his will, as a matter of order. It's difficult to picture this as a matter of time in a self-existent being. He has this good pleasure that is also his will, and then it is what he has determined to have. He purposed it in himself, so it's called his purpose. That's Ephesians 1:9.

If we are going to find out what God's purpose is, the question we need to ask is: What is this good pleasure that he has, and what is his will?

In Ephesians 1:5, you will see Paul talk about this. He says, “Having predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ unto himself, according to the good pleasure of his will.” The best way to read verse 5 is like this: According to the good pleasure of his will, he has predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ unto himself.

Because of the good pleasure of his will, because of this thing that he likes, that he wants, he predestined us. He did a redemption to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ unto himself, showing us that what he always liked was adoption as sons. He now predestined us to the same via redemption because sin had happened. Prior to sin, this is his good pleasure. Upon sin, he predestined us back to that good pleasure, to that purpose of his which he calls adoption as sons.

If we ask the question, “What is God's purpose?” the answer will be adoption as sons. But it's actually one word in the Greek: the word “sonship.” God's purpose, God's will, God's good pleasure is sonship.

Don't forget, we're looking at the heavenly world. We are trying to find our way to that conclusion about the heavenly world. God's good pleasure is sonship. Why are you on earth? Sonship. Why did God even make the world? Sonship.

I want you to note something in Ephesians 1 before I leave. Verse 4 says, “Just as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world.” When we say “foundation of the world,” we are saying the establishment, the making, the building, the creation of the world—when he put the world down, when he brought the world to be. He chose us in him before there was a natural world, a material world, and before there was a heavenly world.

He chose us before there was a material world. He chose us before there was a heavenly world. Before there was any world at all, before there was the heavenly world or the material world, God already chose us in him. The world, whether heavenly or not, is created by God. The world in which angels dwell and function, and man dwells and functions, was created by God.

Where was God before he created the world? That question is a weird one because we are talking about the self-existent. The “where” is himself. There is no other “where.” The moment you identify a “where” that precedes him, you have already created a problem. That “where” that precedes him becomes the person that is actually God.

We can say God was in his self-existent reality. God's heaven, the most primary heaven, the most primary lifted place, the most primary high place, is God himself in his dwelling in himself. Then he created the world, both a material world and a heavenly world.

## Sonship, Fellowship, and the Necessity of a Spiritual World

We have established what God's purpose is. We have said God had his purpose in mind before he made any world, whether material or heavenly. Now let's look at what that purpose is all about. In Romans 8, you find Paul explain in detail what sonship is all about.

Romans 8:15 says, “For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but the Spirit of sonship”—the word “adoption,” the same word here. “You received the Spirit of sonship whereby we cry, Abba, Father.”

For God to realize his desire of sonship, he needed to make a world, a location, a place in which that reality would be fulfilled. The required world for that reality to be fulfilled was not just a material world. If they are going to interact with him, he also needs to make a world in which it is going to be possible for them to interact with him.

Why do we have a heavenly world? Because of God's purpose. Let me say it properly: the heavenly world is a necessity in God's purpose. You're going to see why. Remember it says here that we have received the Spirit of sonship whereby we cry, “Abba, Father.” We are interacting with God. We are relating with him. God is spirit. For us to be able to relate with him, we have to be able to do it in a spiritual world. That's what we're calling a heavenly world—a spiritual world whereby we cry, “Abba, Father.”

Look at verse 16: “The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God.” The first reality of sonship is that we are children of God. Then he says, “If children, then heirs; heirs of God, joint heirs with Christ.” Two realities make up sonship, which is God's purpose. First, we are children of God, and as children of God he has called us to fellowship with him. That's what we're looking at in the teaching *Fellowship: Our Status Before God*.

A lot of times when fellowship is used for us relative to God in the Scriptures, it is ontological fellowship. Ontological fellowship means sharing the same nature as him. But it's different from the fellowship that we know he also called us to by giving us access, which is relational fellowship. The teaching was really to teach relational fellowship. But to be faithful with what the Scripture says about the word “fellowship,” you have to first establish ontological fellowship, because it is even that that leads to relational fellowship.

Let me give you an example that we used in the teaching. There's a way you can have fellowship with a dog. You and the dog are relating. You are even playing. But your fellowship with the dog does not include any scientific discussion, let alone any metaphysical discussion. There's a limited fellowship you have with a dog.

You are having relational fellowship—that means relating, a time of being together—with a dog, but it is limited. You can't discuss certain things with a dog because the dog does not have your language and therefore does not have your level of reasoning. Don't forget, we have said that even though all other animals can be proven to have natural reasoning, there is zero proof, and it is also very unlikely, that they have metaphysical reasoning.

You try to kill an ant, a very small one, and you realize that it can think enough to know something is about to happen and is trying to escape. If a dog sees someone it's familiar with, it will run to the person. That means it's thinking naturally enough: “I know this person,” and naturally enough to know someone it doesn't know and probably should take as danger. Natural reasoning is going on. But there is zero proof of metaphysical reasoning.

That's because metaphysical reasoning requires the complexity that we find in human language. For you to think beyond the natural, you have to have this abstract form of communication that we have in human language. It is one of the core arguments for the metaphysical nature of human beings. The fact that we reason beyond the natural by virtue of the kind of language that we have is proof that we are something beyond the natural as well.

In God's purpose there are two realities. The first is that we are children of God. The second is that we are heirs of God. As children of God, we are called to fellowship with him. Also, we are called to walk in his character and nature. As heirs of God, we are called to carry out his assignments: the inheritance he gives to us, what he allots to us, the allotments he gives to us. Inheritance is what is allotted to you—the allotments or the assignments that he gives to us.

I'm a child of God; I'm to fellowship with God. In sonship, the fundamental part is that you are a child of God, which means you are to fellowship with him, then walk in his character and nature. The core question you need to ask is: Where do I fellowship with God? There's “Who do I fellowship with?” and that's clear: God, your Father, our Father. Then there is “Where do I fellowship with him?” That will help you appreciate the role of the heavenly world in God's purpose.

The heavenly world is a necessity in God's purpose. We are unable to have an effective walk in God's purpose without the reality of the heavenly world. The heavenly world is a necessity for our walk in God's purpose. Why is it a necessity? Because in God's purpose we are called to fellowship, whereby we cry, “Abba, Father.”

If you ask, “Who do I fellowship with?” you say God. If you now ask, “Where do I fellowship with him?” you are asking a question that someone has asked before in John 4. When we were looking into this study last year, we called it *True Temple Worship*. When we were looking into the study two years before, we called it *Navigating the Supernatural in a Natural World*. But this time around, I just call it *The Heavenly World*.

## “I Go to Prepare a Place for You”

Look at John 14. You cannot avoid the subject of the heavenly world in your walk with God. See how often the Lord has spoken about the heavenly world with different terms. In John 2, when he says, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I raise it up,” was he talking about the heavenly world? Yes. He's going to raise up something that is not in the natural in three days. In John 4, when he says worship God in spirit and in truth, was he talking about the heavenly world? Yes. Now in John 14, we see another mention of the heavenly world.

John 14:1 says, “Let not your heart be troubled. You believe in God, believe also in me.” He's saying faith in God as Creator is not enough. There needs to be faith in redemption, which is believing in him. He is God become man for the sake of redemption. Believing in him is a specific kind of faith: redemptive faith, salvific faith. “You believe in God, believe also in me.”

Verse 2: “In my Father's house are many mansions.” A very unfortunate translation. In fact, my own margin puts “many dwellings.” The word [Greek term unclear] there is not the word for mansion. I don't even know what caused all this. I suspect it was first a theological submission on the mind of the translator before the translation, because why would you say, “In my Father's house are many mansions”?

It's so sad that this text has robbed people of a vital, effective relationship with God. The use of this text majorly in most circles is for mansions in heaven—heaven at the end, or when you die, then there are many mansions there. That's why I've seen people who said they had a vision, went to heaven, and Jesus showed them their mansion in heaven that was still being constructed, that God is building it phase by phase.

A particular person, from an account that I heard, got there and found out that her mansion was not painted. I don't even think it was plastered. They had just laid the blocks. I'm not doing righteousness enough to build my house, my mansion in heaven.”

That text has robbed many people of the point the Lord was trying to make. Sometimes when you misread a text like that, you can lose not just the meaning, but the effect of it on your walk—not just the meaning of the text, but the effect it is meant to have on your walk.

At this point you have some questions to ask. What is the Father's house? “In my Father's house are many mansions. If it were not so, I would have told you.” Full stop. “I go to prepare a place for you.” He's saying in God's desired dwelling place, in the temple where God wants to dwell as a matter of desire, there are many spaces or room for everybody.

That was his intention. Now sin has happened, so I need to do a redemption that makes it possible, that cleanses them to be able to form part of God's house, and then take their place there as a part of God's house, as a stone, as a block in God's house. In my Father's house, the desired house where he wants to dwell, there are many spaces: a space for every person, for every man. If you ask the question, “Who did Jesus die for?” every person. No. I think there is enough in God's word to say that's not true. Even the idea that some sins lose salvation, I think there is enough in God's word to say that's not true.

But that there may be one sin that loses salvation, that one you cannot easily dismiss. There is this very strong case that one sin, unrepentant unbelief, might lose salvation, or if it is present, means the person was never saved—whichever position you want to take. ## The Father and Son Make Their Home with Us

“The spirits of just men made perfect.” There is this spiritual temple that the Lord Jesus has built, that we are, that God dwells in. We have already seen the personal implication of our making up this temple collectively: as individuals, the Father and the Son now make their home with us. They are dwelling in us collectively, but as individuals they make their home with us. The Lord Jesus taught that in John 14.

The emphasis of this teaching, which we do every year, is this year on the tangibility of the heavenly world—the tangibility and therefore the perceptibility of the heavenly world.

We make up the Father's house as the church. Every believer, every saint, collectively in the Spirit, in the heavenly world, we make up the Father's house. We make up the city of God. We are that heavenly Jerusalem. We are Mount Zion.

In that same John 14, after he told them about the “where” and all of that, by verse 19 he said, “A little while longer and the world will see me no more, but you will see me.” Can you imagine that? When he says, “The world will see me no more,” it means they will not be able to physically see me, hold me or perceive me. All their senses will not be able to perceive me. They are seeing me now physically, maybe smelling me, touching me, hearing me. But there will come a time they will stop hearing, seeing, perceiving and feeling me. At that time, you will still be seeing me.

They were weirded out a bit, as anybody who hears that should be. But this issue is very much in the Bible, and almost all the apostles talk about it as a way we relate with God today. There is an ongoing, real house that we are. He makes his home with us, and we are actually relating with him. If you do not know it, you have a very limited view of God. You will be doing a conference call, a video call, with someone who is beside you, thinking the person is distant. You will have a very limited engagement with him.

He says, “A little while longer and the world will see me no more, but you will see me. Because I live, you will live also. At that day you will know that I am in the Father, you in me, and I in you.” That means we all have the same nature.

“He who has my commandments and keeps them”—that is the person who is of faith—“it is he who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and manifest myself to him.” He is saying, “The reason you will see me is that, even though I would truly have gone physically, I would decide to manifest myself to those who are of faith.” The reason the world will not see him is that he will truly have gone physically. But for those who are of faith, who have his commandments and keep them, he will choose to manifest himself to them.

At that point Judas, not Iscariot, asked, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us and not to the world?” What kind of situation would that be, where we are seeing, hearing, feeling and perceiving you, but nobody else is doing that?

“We will make our home with him.” Please, not “in him”—with him. You as an individual are not the dwelling place of the Father and the Son. No, absolutely not. We are collectively the dwelling place of the Father and Son; individually, they make their home with us.

## Solomon's Question about God's Dwelling Place

The question we should answer is, what is heaven? The Father and Son say they make their home with us; the Father also dwells in the church. But what the Lord told us when we pray is, “Our Father who art in heaven.” Where is he when you say that? You said he is in heaven: what do you mean? What is heaven? Where is heaven? What is happening in heaven? We can have a very limited scope, view and understanding of heaven, and we need to fix that.

First Kings 8. Let us see how Solomon himself struggled with his understanding of where God is dwelling. He knew, “It is clear by his revelation that God wants to dwell somewhere, but I am not sure whether where we are making is where he is dwelling. Maybe there is another place he is dwelling for now. He is claiming he wants to dwell here, but we are going to figure it out later.”

In 1 Kings 8:12 Solomon spoke: “The Lord said he would dwell in a dark cloud.” That is heaven; do not forget the way they understand heaven. “I have surely built you an exalted house, a place for you to dwell in forever.” He built a physical temple, the temple that Jesus said he would destroy and in three days [raise up]. They knew the idea: God says he wants to dwell in a particular temple. But Solomon was struggling with it. How does it make sense?

Notice what he said: “I have built you an exalted house, a place for you to dwell in forever.” Now see what he says later, in the same breath. After verses 12 and 13, go to verse 27: “But will God indeed dwell on earth?” He is doubting what he has been told, that he is supposed to build God a dwelling place. That is what he was told by his father. But he is also aware of the truth of God as self-existent. How does it make sense that his spiritual self is dwelling on earth?

“Will God indeed dwell on earth? Behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain you. How much less this temple which I have built?” Did you not just call it an exalted house for him to dwell in forever?

“Yet regard the prayer of your servant and his supplication, O Lord my God, and listen to the cry and the prayer which your servant is praying before you today: that your eyes may be open toward this temple night and day.” Can you see that he does not think God is in the temple? He says his eyes should be open toward the temple: use it as a representative place. From where you actually are, take notice of whatever people are doing toward it or around it. “I am not really sure you are here.”

If he was in the temple, Solomon would not say, “Your eyes may be open toward the temple.” In his mind he believed God was staying in the self-existent place, which in that sense we say is above or beyond all, and from there he should pay attention to this place.

“Toward the place of which you said, ‘My name shall be there,’ that you may hear the prayer which your servant makes toward this place.” He is making prayer toward that place—not that God is in it hearing it, but that God should pay attention from where he is and notice it. “And may you hear the supplication of your servant and of your people Israel when they pray toward this place. Hear in heaven your dwelling place; and when you hear, forgive.”

In his mind, God's heavenly dwelling place was not that place yet. He is struggling to accept that this is God's dwelling place, even though he knows that is what he has been told. Of course, he has been taught that God's dwelling place, his self-existent state, is heaven as well. How can the self-existent be fully contained here? Of course he cannot. Even in this, he is self-existent beyond, but this is his chosen place of immanence: to be present and be immanent in the church.

## Atmospheric, Stellar and Self-Existent Heaven

What does the Bible say heaven is? We have talked about the five uses of heaven. The very word “heaven” in the Hebrew and Greek has the idea of something that is high, above. I am sure of the Greek; I am not sure about the Hebrew. The word is related to the word for mountain. In the Greek, the word for heaven [Greek term unclear], the word for mountain, “oros,” and I think it is even rooted in the word “to lift up” [Greek term unclear]. Something that is high. “Shamayim” is also the same idea: something that is high, above, beyond.

That is what they understood as heaven naturally, and it was their description for where the birds fly and where the stars are. When you look up and see the birds fly and say, “The birds of the sky,” the word “sky” was the same word for heaven for them. They would have said, “The birds of heaven.” They would say, “The stars of heaven.” They would use the same word: the stars of the place that is up, and that other place that is also up.

In our own parlance, our understanding of what they saw as heaven is the atmospheric space above—what people call atmospheric heaven—or the stellar heaven, the place that is above.

When they understood that God is the self-existent source of everything, that means he is most above and most beyond. He is Most High. They would regard his heaven as the highest heaven. The self-existent state is called heaven. That is the sense in which you say, “Heaven is my throne.” My throne, my authority—it is not a physical throne. If you say heaven is his throne, are you saying the throne was first, or God constructed the throne? Neither. His authority is called heaven.

“Heaven is my throne. The earth is my footstool.” In that sense, earth would be everything created. “My authority is most above, most beyond, Most High. The earth, what is created, is my footstool.” It is what I am exercising authority over; it is what I made, everything I created.

How many heavens have we seen now? Three. We have seen atmospheric heaven, stellar heaven, and now we are saying self-existent heaven, God's own heaven. Only God is there. That is why it can also be called “unapproachable light, which no man has seen or can see.” No person can taste self-existence. We can partake of that nature, but you cannot taste what it means to be in that real condition, that person as self-existent.

That is First [Timothy] 6. Before, when I used to read it, I would think, “What do you mean? Is Paul making a mistake here?” Even as a child of God, with God's nature, you have to understand the limits and boundaries of what you are saying.

## The Angels as the Host of Heaven

That is the third heaven we have seen: atmospheric heaven, stellar heaven and self-existent heaven. The fourth heaven we see in Scripture is very key. One of the first ways to see it is that angels are described as stars and as the host of heaven. There are beings who function in a particular heaven. Are we saying they function where the stars are or where the birds are? What heaven are we saying the angels are in?

Let me show you where they speak about the angels as the host of heaven. Start with Job's account, Job 38:7: “When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy.” We know that in the book of Job, “sons of God” talks about angels, angelic beings. You saw that from Job 1 and 2, where it says that when the sons of God came to present themselves before Yahweh, Satan came in among them.

There are arguments about how to see Satan at that period: whether we are seeing Satan as completely negative or as a lawyer, one of the angels serving in that role. If we read their history, the development of the understanding of supernatural, spiritual beings that are evil is not a very straightforward explanation. Satan can be seen as an accusing lawyer, not necessarily as this person that you [usually understand him to be]; one of them genuinely coming to make an accusatory case. That is the role; it is not a bad thing in that context. But let us stick with our normal use now.

The person is the plaintiff, the person making the case—the prosecuting lawyer, the prosecution. They are prosecuting: “Are you sure this person…?” Many times they come because they have looked at something and found the person wanting in something.

We talk about Job being blameless, but the honest truth, when we read the material, is that Job was guilty of something: fear, anxiety, not being in faith about some things. In character, he was doing quite well. But the funny part is that the other one was more important. He was doing well in character, but even with imperfect character, he needed that other one more. The prosecuting lawyer is supposed to come and find a fault, a case to try to test. Let us leave that for some other day.

“When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God…” He is calling the morning stars the sons of God. He is describing them as stars. Our understanding would be stars that dwell in the stellar heaven, but he is not talking about things in the physical heaven.

Which heaven is this that they function in? Nehemiah 9:5–6: “Stand up and bless the Lord your God forever and ever! Blessed be your glorious name, which is exalted above all blessing and praise! You alone are the Lord; you have made heaven, the heaven of heavens, with all their host, the earth and everything on it, the seas and all that is in them, and you preserve them all.” Then it specifies: “The host of heaven worships you.”

You made them. You made those entities and the space they are in. You made the space called heaven, the heavenly world, and then you made its host. The host you made to be in that space worship you. What beings is he talking about?

Psalm 148:1 onward: “Praise the Lord! Praise the Lord from the heavens; praise him in the heights! Praise him, all his angels; praise him, all his hosts!” He is saying the angels are his host, the host of heaven. There are hosts whose location of activity is something called heaven.

Which heaven? We have seen three heavens. Are they populating the atmospheric heaven or stellar heavens? Are they active in the stellar heavens? The stellar is most used to describe them: they are called morning stars. Are we saying that is where they are, where the stars are? They use the stars to describe them a lot. They often use that picture to explain their activity, even though it is not the intention of how we should see them.

For example, when Jacob had that vision and saw a ladder reaching up to heaven, his idea was of stellar heavens, angels there and God above them. That is seeing God's authority as higher than where the angels are, with the angels active on the earth.

You can say they saw the stellar heavens, even though they are physical stars, as a space of spiritual activity that influences the earth. It is common not just in biblical theology but in other forms of philosophy and mythology: the gods are above, dwelling up there in the stars, and God is above them. But you will see in the Bible's teaching that, although the description is the stellar heavens, where it places that heaven is right here on earth. In many of the images it looks like it is up there, with God higher than that.

## Jacob's Ladder and the Angels' Activity on Earth

Genesis 28, from verse 10: “Now Jacob went out from Beersheba and went toward Haran. So he came to a certain place and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. And he took one of the stones of that place and put it at his head, and he lay down in that place to sleep.”

Verse 12: “Then he dreamed, and behold, a ladder was set up on the earth, and its top reached to heaven.” Which heaven is he describing the top as reaching? The stellar heavens. “And there the angels of God were ascending and descending on it.” He is noticing that they are affecting what is going on on earth. They are busy with the activities of earth, working up and down the activities of earth.

There is an access that they have, a ladder. You will find that this access is not actually a ladder. Some people call it a portal. You know what a portal is like in some of these Marvel movies: one big hole, and people can move from there to the earth. I think it is part of Avengers. He is saying there is an access between heaven and earth. They are ascending and descending through that access.

“And behold, the Lord stood above it”—above that ladder. It reached where they were, and he was above. “I am the Lord God of Abraham … and the God of Isaac; the land on which you lie I will give to you and your descendants.”

Where is this particular heaven? Are the angels where the stars are? Are they in the stellar heavens? We can start to suspect, probably from Paul, that this is not how they see them. Paul, for example, in Ephesians 6:12 would say that we do not wrestle against flesh and blood—they thought their enemies were physical human enemies—but against principalities, powers, rulers of the darkness of this age, spiritual wickedness in the heavenlies. It is starting to give us a sense that they are actually here and present, affecting things.

We see other texts, like Ephesians 2: “You were dead in trespasses and sins, in which you once walked according to the course of this world”—cosmos—“according to the prince of the power of the air.” That means the atmosphere you are in. “The spirit who now works in the sons of disobedience, among whom also we all once conducted ourselves … fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind, and were by nature children of wrath.”

In Paul's explanation, the activity is here: the prince of the power of the air. They are present here in the atmosphere—not just the bad angels in this sense, but the good ones. This understanding of the heavens as present here with the earth is not just Paul developing something. Even the Old Testament people sometimes had that picture. The very first clear place we see it is Genesis 1.

## The Expanse Called Heaven in Genesis

Genesis 1:6. Do not forget the picture in Genesis: he is showing the whole earth that God created as fully flooded. The earth, including the atmospheric heaven, is like a box, fully flooded. Where the sun, stars and lights are is another box. That one was dark, not flooded. Light had gone out of there.

You can see one big box and one very small box: perhaps a cube as the earth, then a small, flat rectangular box as the stellar heavens. The stellar heavens had gone dark; the earth was fully flooded. “The earth was without form and void, and darkness was upon the face of the deep”—upon the fully flooded state of the earth. It was without form and void, chaotic, and there was darkness on it. From the heavens themselves light had gone out, and darkness was now there on a fully flooded earth.

Then God said, “Let there be a firmament between the waters.” That is verse 6. “Firmament” means space or expanse [Hebrew term unclear]. “Let there be a space or expanse between the waters,” dividing the waters above from the waters beneath. The waters above are what we today call the clouds; the waters beneath will be called the sea.

That is not even the interesting point. The point is what he called the space. “God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament; and it was so. And God called the firmament Heaven.” Do not forget, the firmament is the space, the expanse, dividing the clouds and the sea. Where is that? Here. He called that space heaven.

This space that we are in is also called heaven, because he is saying we are in a spiritual world, a heavenly world, that overlaps a natural one. He calls this space, this expanse he put in between, heaven. There is another expanse up in space where the stars are. But this space he made between the clouds and the sea, the space we are in, he called heaven.

Some translations call it sky. I have seen the argument that the space is the upper part where the birds fly, and I do not think it is a correct argument. I have mentioned that argument before. As far as we know, he called the entire space heaven. Then, when he dealt with the water, he called the dry land earth, meaning the space was on the earth.

Those making that argument say the birds of the sky are flying in the upper part of that space. But from what we can see, it was a fully flooded earth, and he created a space between. The space between, reaching up and down, is heaven. He put the waters together, and the dry land is called earth.

Look at verse 9: “Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.” God called the dry land earth, and the gathering together of the waters he called seas. The waters are gathered together; there is dry land. The dry land is called earth, and the expanse between both places is heaven.

You see another firmament, another expanse, in Genesis 1:14: “Let there be lights in the firmament of the heavens to divide the day from the night.” That one is in the space in heaven, the stellar heavens. “Let them be for signs…” It is talking about the stars in their own space, a space beyond the air. “Let them be for lights in the firmament of the heavens to give light on the earth.”

When we see day or night on our own earth—the whole earth in this case, where there are clouds, the space and expanse he called heaven, dry land and sea—the light is coming from the stellar heavens. The stellar heavens are often used to describe the spiritual heavens where the angels are. But where the angels actually are, when he talks about the rulers of the darkness of this age and the prince of the power of the air, is this atmosphere we are in. That is why the atmosphere itself is called heaven. We are in a spiritual world overlapping a natural world.

## A Spiritual World Overlapping the Material World

We are in the spiritual world and the natural world. The idea of calling the dry land earth and the space above it heaven is to say there is a spiritual world overlapping the natural. Today, if you leave the earth and go to the moon, you are still in heaven upon the earth. If you leave the earth and go to the moon, is God still making his home with you?

I do not know Neil Armstrong's persuasion, but there are believers who have gone to the moon. There are believers who have gone to the International Space Station. Was God still making his home with them? Were the Father and Son still making their home with them? Or did he leave them for that time: “It is only the earth I like. As you are on the moon now, you are on vacation spiritually too”?

People have been on the moon over a week, I think, or several weeks. But I know that for the International Space Station it has been months, three months, sometimes worse, like the ones who almost got lost or abandoned there. Are they still in heaven upon the earth? Yes. They are still in a spiritual world overlapping a material world.

“God created the heavens and the earth” is “God created a spiritual world and a material world.” You can say, “God created the heavens and the earth” is “God created atmospheric heaven, stellar heavens and the earth, the ground level.” You can also say it is “God created the spiritual world and a natural world.” We see that we are in a world where there is an overlap between the spiritual and the natural.

The word “air” in Ephesians 2, “aer,” talks about the heavenly as well. Ephesians 2:1 onward: “And you he made alive, who were dead in trespasses and sins, in which you once walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit who now works…” According to the prince, the ruler of the power of the air.

If you ask where the principalities in Ephesians 6:12 are ruling, they are ruling in a particular atmosphere. I want to show you that the word “air” is the word for the normal air we have here.

Ephesians 6:12: “We do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers.” Can we even see wrestling with fleshly things outside us, the world, as part of such a wrestle? Yes. The nature of sin and death is in the air from man's sin. There is that word, “Do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts.” There is that pressure, now managed and ruled by these forces.

“Against principalities, powers, rulers of the darkness of this age, spiritual wickedness in heavenly places.” This can also read as a persecution issue going on there. As I think about it, we often use Ephesians 6 mostly for persecution, but it might not just be persecution; it can even be warfare for character.

When you see “prince,” “principalities and powers,” and “the prince of the power of the air,” you are seeing the same word, just that one is in the plural: rulers. Spiritual wickedness in the heavenly. Which heavenly are they in? The atmosphere around us, the air. I did not use Ephesians 2 in “Fellowship: Our Status before God,” and I should have; it is a very important part.

Look at that word “air” so you can see that the air it is talking about is the air we are talking about. You find it used seven times in seven verses. In Acts [reference unclear; transcript reads “21:23”], where they were persecuting Paul, “As they cried out and cast off their clothes and threw dust into the air.” They did not throw dust to where the birds are.

It is the expanse we are in, from the clouds to the sea and the dry land below. There was such an expanse in a fully flooded world that covered both the upper part where the birds are, atmospheric heaven, and the lower atmospheric part we are in. That expanse was the whole space, and it was called heaven. He caused the waters to be gathered together, perhaps dried it up to a degree, and the dry land appeared. He called the dry land earth and the water sea.

They threw dust into the air. In 1 Corinthians 9:26 he said, “I fight, not as one who beats the air.” That air is right here, the atmosphere. You see it also in 1 Corinthians 14:9: if what I speak is not known to people, I shall be as one who speaks into the air. When you are talking and somebody does not understand you, it is as though you are speaking into the air, into the atmosphere. That is the word “air.”

You find it in Ephesians 2, where we are, and 1 Thessalonians 4:17: we shall meet the Lord in the air. Are you saying there is an air above now? This one would be like atmospheric heaven, one you could have called the sky. We meet the Lord in the air. Then you find it in Revelation 9:2, “The air was darkened,” and Revelation 16:17, where the angel poured out a vial into the air.

Please do not misunderstand me. What Moses was describing is not literally the case that the heaven created is just this expanse. It was God's way of explaining that where you are functioning is also where spiritual realities are. You are functioning in this place, on the dry land where there is sea, but where you are functioning there is a heavenly space. You are functioning in heaven upon the earth. You are functioning in a spiritual world and a natural world at the same time.

Even if you leave this particular earth and are on the moon, you are still functioning in heaven upon the earth. If you can stay on Mars successfully, you are still in heaven upon the earth. The planets we have not had any access to are still heaven upon the earth. Heaven upon the earth spans wherever there is a material world. Wherever there is a material world, there is simultaneously a spiritual world overlapping it. It is seen as on top because its influence is exerted upon what you are seeing naturally.

We are in a spiritual world overlapping a natural world. That is the fourth heaven, spiritual heaven. How many heavens have we seen? Number one, atmospheric heaven—perhaps you say sky heaven. Number two, stellar heavens. Number three, self-existent heaven, God's heaven. Number four, spiritual heaven: a spiritual world overlapping a natural or material world.

## Different Experiences and Compartments in the Heavenly World

Where is the church built? In the spiritual heaven. Where is Hades? In the spiritual heaven. Where do people go when they die? Somewhere in the spiritual heaven. Where are we now? Somewhere too in the spiritual heaven.

Where exactly in the spiritual heaven do people go when they die? I do not know. There are some things we do not know, because they have no value to our walk with God and his purpose here on earth. We know what gives us the relevant comfort we need for what is to follow.

Prior to the Lord Jesus, there was always a debate about the afterlife. Some people are still debating it, but for us as believers we do not have that debate. People of faith, of redemptive faith, genuinely could have had that debate prior to the Lord: whether, when people die, they are conscious at all or are just in a permanent state of sleep.

There were all kinds of debates, because you see texts saying they are in a state of sleep, to be awakened later into the resurrection. I think Daniel has some texts people like to use that way. Then some would say, “Jacob said that when he dies he will go and meet his son.” They would just be in the same place, but both still asleep, and so forth. There was an argument about whether they were conscious in what we call the afterlife.

The Lord made it pretty clear in Luke 16 that they were conscious afterward, so that is not our debate to have. It is also an important detail because it shows that there are different demarcated boundaries in the heavenly world. There is one thing happening in one, and a great gulf is fixed so that you cannot access the other, as a matter of revelatory truth.

There is one compartment of torment, of judgment, and another compartment of bliss, life, ease or comfort. That is why today, when we say the Father and Son have made their home with us, it is described in Scripture as a completely different experience from when you die and go to be with Christ. The Father and Son are with you, yes; and yet there is such a thing as dying and going to be with Christ, a separate experience within heaven upon the earth.

When it is described that way, it is saying that you are without Christ in that state of experience. After he has told us that the Father and Son have made their home with us, now he says that at this point, while you are still in this body, you are without him in that sense. We will see 2 Corinthians 5, then Philippians 1.

As far as we know, in the heavenly world there is a Hades compartment which used to house righteous and unrighteous people. But the righteous compartment of Hades has been emptied since the resurrection. There is still a Hades compartment that had two demarcated sections, but the righteous section has been emptied because those people are now part of the church. Remember Hebrews 12:22–24.

Even for the ones who are in the church in this heaven upon the earth, there is an afterlife compartment of the church different from an in-this-life compartment of the church. Where is it?
