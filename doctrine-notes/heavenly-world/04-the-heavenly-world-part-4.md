# The Heavenly World — Part 4

## We Are God's Dwelling Place in the Spirit

We are God's dwelling place. He said that you are the dwelling place of God in the Spirit. The “you” out there is plural, so we are the dwelling place of God in the Spirit. You cannot read that and say, “I am the dwelling place of God in the Spirit.” That would be a mistake in understanding the text. We are the dwelling place of God in the Spirit; I am part of the dwelling place of God in the Spirit.

He has made us a spiritual [unclear], where we are to fellowship with him, which is part of his purpose for us. Peter is going to express the same thought in 1 Peter 2. This was not a Pauline thing alone; it was an apostolic thing. You find that it is the position Peter holds.

We could start from verse 1: “Therefore, laying aside all malice, all deceit, hypocrisy, envy, and all evil speaking, as newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that you may grow thereby, if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is gracious.”

He is saying, “If indeed you are saved, now it is time for you to move further and grow.” On that thought—if you have tasted that the Lord is gracious—he says, “Coming to him as to a living stone.” You have had that experience where you came to him as to a living stone, as one who is the foundation of a building. You came to him as part of a building: “A living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God and precious.”

Verse 5: “You also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.” You also are living stones, and you are being built up. This is a house that is still being built. But there is a way you can come to “finished work” that you cannot finish, and you are reading texts using a continuous tense as though they were speaking in the perfect tense.

I remember one meeting in 2016 where the theme was “Build Up.” I struggled with some explanations I heard about how “built up” here and in Ephesians was already done, and that it was a matter of how we were reading it or how it was translated. This cannot be correct. This house is still being built. People become part of the building, and they were not before. They had the potential to be, but they were not. If they did not receive the gospel, would they be part of the building?

“You are being built up a spiritual house, a holy priesthood.” Is that house already existing? Yes. Is it still ongoing? Yes. Is God's dwelling place an already existing place? Yes. Is it still an ongoing building, a continually expanded building? Yes. It is like saying there is Redemption Camp, but they are still building, still extending things. They want to expand further and do beautiful things.

## Spiritual Sacrifices in a Spiritual House

We are built up a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ. I like the part that says “to offer spiritual sacrifices,” because there is something we are supposed to do in that house. We are built up a spiritual house, but we are also the priesthood of the house. We are everything: the building, the priesthood, the sacrifices. [Unclear passage concerning Jesus as the sacrifice.]

The sacrifices we offer here are spiritual sacrifices, just as the house itself is spiritual. People can choose to symbolize spiritual realities. We can gather in a house or even build a church building, but that does not make that the real house. In the same way, the sacrifice itself is not anything physical; it is spiritual. We offer spiritual sacrifices in this house, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

In church, we offer spiritual sacrifices. Today, a lot of our sacrifices are acknowledgments of the sacrifice of Jesus and of ourselves as the eventual product of that sacrifice.

Hebrews 13:15 says, “Therefore by him”—by Jesus—“let us continually offer the sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of our lips, giving thanks to his name.” Giving thanks there is affirming, confessing his name, if I remember correctly. In this house we offer spiritual sacrifices, one of which is the sacrifice of praise to God. We acknowledge his name and praise God for what he has done: “Thank you for redemption.”

It is interesting that they would have one Day of Atonement, but every other day they repeated very similar sacrifices. They still did the burnt offering and offered incense every morning. For us, we do not have a repeated Day of Atonement. We have one day of redemption, and yet there is every day of acknowledging redemption. Thank God for redemption. By the altar of redemption, by the washing of the Holy Ghost, we are now the dwelling place of God.

Verse 16: “But do not forget to do good and to share, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.” He is talking about giving in church: doing good and sharing.

Today, people can argue that when they gave back then, it was only for saints in need. I do not think that is true, because there was also giving for ministers. Galatians 6 says, “Let him who is taught in the word share in all good things with him who teaches.” I suspect what must have evolved over time in church is one centralized giving for all the purposes for which they give: saints' welfare, ministers' honor, and church operations.

You could already see a form of centralized giving when they put it at the apostles' feet, although initially it was majorly for saints' welfare. It seems there was also an adoption, perhaps early on, of giving for ministers. Paul spoke to the Corinthians about whether he alone had no right to be paid. It seems there was one centralized giving for all the purposes: saints' welfare, church operations, ministers' honor. We can add missionary work. These are different reasons to give.

“Do not forget to do good and to share, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.” This includes saints' welfare and ministers' honor, but we could also include the needs of operations, because that is helping the work altogether. Church operations then were low compared to now. For many churches, perhaps the biggest part of the use of resources is gathering in a place, using equipment, and all the things that must go on. Sometimes you have to build buildings—things that were not the case before.

## Jesus and the Final Spiritual Temple

When we speak about God's purpose and the reality of sonship, our fellowship with God, we cannot divorce it from where God dwells, which is the temple that we are. This temple, this spiritual house, is in heaven. Ephesians already tells us that we are a holy temple in the Lord. We can say we are the third and final temple.

I find it very interesting that the conception of the temple as spiritual is especially found in the person of Jesus. At the time, many people's thinking was very physical. They thought that rebuilding the temple, even after it was destroyed, would be the role of the Christ. I find the spiritual temple more compelling than any physical temple as the fulfillment of the prophecies. It seems more existentially necessary than a physical one. If the temple is about God's dwelling, I have very big doubts that God's dwelling was ever meant to be a physically built temple.

Therefore, I would lean toward a Christ who did not lay claim to building a physical temple, but a spiritual one, as the fulfillment of the prophecy. That seems to be the Christ. This is a case for the faith as well, particularly for Jews who would want to argue otherwise.

John 2:13: “Now the Passover of the Jews was at hand, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem. And he found in the temple those who sold oxen and sheep and doves, and the money changers doing business.” Verse 15: “When he had made a whip of cords, he drove them all out of the temple, with the sheep and the oxen, and poured out the changers' money and overturned the tables. And he said to those who sold doves, ‘Take these things away! Do not make my Father's house a house of merchandise!’”

He called the temple the Father's house. Then his disciples remembered that it was written, “The zeal for your house has eaten me up,” or “has consumed me.” The Jews answered him, “What sign do you show to us, since you do these things?” In other words, “You are acting like the Christ. What sign are you going to show us that you actually are the Christ?”

Then he says something expected of the Christ: to build a third temple. “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” In their minds, this supposed Christ is not okay. “Fine, perhaps this temple is not the real, final temple. But even if you want to build the real temple and start your physical kingdom, how can it take you three days to build it?”

This is one huge claim in which the Christ is telling you, “I am not building a physical temple; I am building a spiritual temple.” He is in a class of his own. He knows precisely what he is talking about: things that are a matter of existential truth.

The Jews said, “It has taken forty-six years to build this temple, and will you raise it up in three days?” Even if he was the Christ and wanted to build the perfect temple, three days seemed impossible. “But he was speaking of the temple of his body. Therefore, when he had risen from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this to them; and they believed the Scripture and the word which Jesus had said.” They believed the Scriptures that talked about the building of the temple.

The final temple that will last forever is a spiritual temple. They now understood what the Scriptures meant and the words of the Lord Jesus. We are God's final temple. God dwells in us.

But there are still questions to ask. The Father's house, God's temple—where does this exist? We call it a spiritual house. If we are a spiritual house, there is a spiritual domain here that makes up that spiritual house. We are here; how then do we have that spiritual house?

## Worshiping the Father in Spirit and in Truth

In John 4:20, the woman at the well asks a question that relates exactly to this: “Our fathers worshiped on this mountain, and you Jews say that in Jerusalem is the place where one ought to worship.” By Jerusalem, she meant the temple in Jerusalem. She was asking, “Where is the right place to worship?”

Jesus said to her, “Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when you will neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, worship the Father.” The word “worship” there is to show affection or to adore. As I was saying on Thursday, praise and worship is not fast pace or slow pace. To praise someone is to extol, to speak highly of, to hail someone. To worship someone is to adore or to be affectionate toward them.

The word for worship [Greek term unclear] is connected with the word for a dog, used as a verb: to be dog-like toward someone, the kind of affection a dog shows someone to whom it is loyal. The point is the affection you show toward someone.

He says, “Neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, worship the Father.” That mountain was a place of worship. Abraham worshiped, Jacob worshiped; people had worshiped there. They took it for granted that this was God's desired place of worship. But as things unfolded, Jerusalem became where the tabernacle was and where the temple was built. Jerusalem became the noted place of worship.

Jesus said the hour was coming when they would worship the Father neither on the mountain nor in Jerusalem. You might think, “Is there another country where we are going to worship the Father?”

Verse 22: “You worship what you do not know.” He had just revealed God as Father to her, and that revelation was especially present with the Jews, to whom God had spoken about sonship. In Romans 9, speaking about the Jews, Paul says, “To whom pertain the adoption”—to whom pertains sonship.

“You worship what you do not know.” You know [unclear], but you do not know him as he wants to be known and worshiped. “We know what we worship, for salvation is of the Jews.” God had committed a redemptive plan through them, so that revelation was with them. This does not mean other people were not of faith. This specific revelation of God was found with them. Someone else could also have known God, but they were the ones elected for that revelation, to depict it.

“But the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshipers”—those who are really worshiping, worshiping in reality—“will worship the Father in spirit.” The place of their worship would not be a physical place. “In truth” means it is the real place of God's worship. There is nothing complex about “spirit and truth.” He is simply saying that it is a nonphysical place, and it is the real place of worshiping God. It will be for real, not figurative, not merely depicting worship in spirit and in truth.

I think we need to devote more time to understanding our worship of God. We also need to be very intentional about being effective in our worship. It looks like an untapped scope of our walk as believers. We have not really become as effective as we should be.

## Praise, Worship, and the Mood of a Song

Praise is extolling and hailing; worship is adoration and affection. It is not about fast pace or slow pace. You can be fast-paced and be showing affection toward someone. There are rappers who have sung songs of affection and adoration for a woman, and the songs are fast. “I love you” is affection, not praise. They could also sing a fast-paced song of praise, hailing the person: “You are so this; you are so that. When I see you, I feel like this.” It can be fast-paced and praise, or fast-paced and worship.

It can also be slow-paced and praise. One of the songs sung that day was about “You are great, greatly to be praised.” That is praise. If you want to classify it, do not call it worship; call it praise. “You are so great that you ought to be praised.” You are praising and hailing the person. It is genuine: “How great you are.” You are praising him, and it is slow-paced.

You could also sing songs of worship: “I love you more than anything.” It is affection, like the kind of affection a dog expresses toward the one to whom it is loyal: “You are the only one I love. You are the one I am committed to. I pledge my allegiance to you.” You are not praising; you are worshiping, adoring, being affectionate toward him.

Praise and worship cannot be known by pace, because at any pace you can praise and worship. But the pace of a song can determine the mood of the praise or worship. There can be celebratory praise and worship, and there can be more solemn, usually conversational, praise and worship.

In celebration, you are celebrating on account of the person. Have you seen how people celebrate birthdays? They invite someone who can hail the person, calling the person's name, and everybody is dancing. They are celebrating on account of that person. The person is happy that they are celebrating on his account, even if they are not talking directly to him.

God does not mind celebratory praise and worship. Sometimes I see some of it and wonder whether it is too much. I think the joy is enough to get the point. Some things seem to be stepping outside the reverential boundary. Some are so carnal and distracting, which is even worse. There needs to be a way to contain some of it.

But it would be dishonest to say that the Bible does not endorse celebratory praise and worship. That is not true. They did it: people danced and used all kinds of instruments.

I always want to point out that in the earliest days of the church, they were very wary of celebratory things. They debated using instruments. In fact, they did not use instruments; those opposed to them were winning for a while before people decided to accommodate them more and more. I do not know why, since the Old Testament texts accepted them so much. Of course, very early on they were wary of things like entertainment.

That is the same issue today in how celebratory praise and worship is done: entertainment takes the place of a genuine atmosphere of reverence. Sometimes the spirit of reverence is completely gone. It is a big issue. I think there are ways to work around it; it does not have to be either/or. There can be contexts.

Personally, I think toward the end of meetings, where people are giving thanksgiving for one thing or another—perhaps they have just got married or are dedicating children—it does not have to be so solemn. They are celebrating on account of God, genuinely grateful to the Lord: “See what you have done.” I do not think there is anything wrong with that.

But we have to check the spirit of reverence. This comes with more fun and celebration, but when someone deliberately does something irreverent, outside the bounds of what is acceptable within the atmosphere of the church, I think the person should be cautioned. It affects other people as well. Caution is required in that regard.

## Direct Engagement With God

The worship of God is in spirit. If you genuinely want to interact with God himself, your mind will be in the Spirit. If you want to celebrate on his account, your mind might not be there. This was one of the strongest points of those who advocated strongly against the use of instruments and those sorts of things. [Unclear passage.] What we call conversational praise and worship is the point.

When we read this text, it makes one point clear: the focus in our praise and worship of God is that we are supposed to be engaging with him directly. Does he accommodate things we do on his account while not directly engaging with him? I think so, from his word. But if we really want to engage with him directly, we must be aware that it will be conversational, solemn praise and worship.

Verse 23: “But the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth.” He is pointing to himself as an example. His mind was not concerned with the temple or a place. He was already correcting that in the light of his role as the Christ.

“For the Father is seeking such to worship him.” This is what he desires. “God is Spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.” If you are actually going to worship him, it must be in spirit and in truth, the way it really is.

The worship of God is primarily conversational, meditative praise and worship. We can then say we want to celebrate on his account. We know that is not engaging with him directly, and we know he accepts that, so we can do it. But it is like activity outside the temple, outside the holiest. It would be strange if we were inside the holiest to converse with him and were doing that. People did it a lot outside the temple: they rejoiced, ate, feasted, and drank. We must be able to make that distinction.

## Reading All the Biblical Evidence Honestly

When I say this, it is not because I am trying to “balance” or be on both sides. My major reason is that, as an honest Bible reader, when you say there was nothing like this or that, will you not see it when you read your Bible? Will you not realize that what you are saying is dishonest?

When you read that Miriam and the women took their timbrels, danced, and sang, will you not realize that they were celebrating? You cannot have an honest Bible reading if you deny that people, in their love for God, had times when they were simply celebrating. It was not conversational.

I have no business trying to be on both sides. The issue is whether you can sustain your Bible reading like that, knowing you are not being honest with all the texts. You can have good intentions. I have realized that the good intention is that people are causing distraction, being irreverent, taking away the weight that comes with worship.

[Name unclear] lamented this in *Greater Glory* and spoke about how the Lord had spoken to him. I agree that we are not seeing as much of God's glory as we should because of irreverence in the church and in our worship. Those corrections are important. But taking that to the degree that you throw out celebratory praise and worship becomes an error of its own. You will then have to defend a position you were not obligated to defend, because you have taken that position by force. You start saying, “That dancing is rejoicing,” and so forth. Really?

That is my only issue here. Do you want to be biblically wholesome, or simply hold your own position? You must accept both. You must accept the primary role of conversational, solemn praise and worship in the worship of God. You cannot spend the bulk of your worship time outside this if it is genuinely the worship of God.

After Jesus had taught all this, the woman said, “I know that Messiah is coming, who is called Christ. When he comes, he will tell us all things.” It was as though it was becoming too difficult: “Worship in spirit—what do you mean? I know the mountain. This is complex. The Messiah will tell us.” He had to tell her, “I who speak to you am he.” It is very rare that he does that; I think there were only two or three occasions.

One of the things Jesus came to establish clearly—because one role of the Christ is to make things clearer, to tell us all these things—is that worshiping God is in the Spirit, in the heavenly world.

## The Vastness of Creation and the Heavenly World

We have had to explain the concept of heaven and make it clearer. What is heaven? What is the heavenly world? We have done this over and again, but I think the teaching has become extremely clear, if I may say so, especially as we talked about the compartments in heaven.

I was saying on Thursday that there are more planets in the world than grains of sand on earth. Geologists have approximated how many grains of sand there are on earth. It is mind-bending. How can it just be us human beings? For me, I still suspect it is just us human beings. I know the odds are not stacked in our favor in terms of probability. You have a galaxy like ours with billions of stars and planets. I think I should have said stars rather than planets earlier.

Almost all of them do not allow life forms, except earth. Earth is this perfectly conducive place for life forms. The argument is that we have billions of such galaxies. Are you saying that each does not have at least one? Let us say a million of them do not have one, or a billion do not have one. Could there not be another with one just like ours, also existing there, believing redemptive truth, with whatever is going on there? What are the odds?

The challenge with knowing if there are other life forms is that when we view them through telescopes, we are viewing them perhaps a million years behind, or something like that. How do we know that what we are viewing is not so old that those places have since developed to a degree where life forms have evolved there?

For whatever reason, my personal intuition is that we are the ones. This is not a biblical truth being submitted; Scripture does not submit anything about it, so it is difficult to say. I also have no philosophical basis for it. Something personally makes me feel that we are the ones. Many people feel the other way. I know Christians whose intuition is the other way, and that is fine; it does not affect anything.

My intuition is that, even with all the billions, we are still the only ones out there. What is the point, then? I feel it reflects the new heavens and new earth, the eternity ahead of us, and our exploration in that world. That is my personal opinion.

It makes me feel that eternity will not be boring. Even this world is not that boring. Look at how much there is to explore. There is so much that it seems impossible to explore fully in God knows how many lifetimes. We have been exploring earth for so long within civilization—let us say seven to ten thousand years—and still have not finished. Imagine so many worlds like that. I believe it is a reflection of what is ahead.

When we speak of heaven as a spiritual world, that spiritual world exists in all these galaxies, planets, and stars. If you go to the sun, it would still be heaven upon the earth. When people are on the moon for seven days, it is still heaven overlapping the earth. When people are in the International Space Station for months, it is still heaven overlapping the earth.

We have explained what heaven is from Scripture. The term itself was taken from natural things called heaven, such as “the birds of heaven.” I should take you to the texts so that you do not simply repeat it and, when asked to show it, not know where it is. I often assume that what is in my head is in other people's heads, which is not good.

In Genesis 1, when God was reordering the firmament that he called heaven—[unclear passage]. We are in the spiritual world overlapping the natural world. In that heavenly world, I must be careful of the influence of different spirits.

## Testing the Spirits Behind Beliefs

Let us look at 1 John 4. When we see belief systems trying to persuade us, we are told that these beliefs are powered by spirits. John says, “Do not believe every spirit.” You might wonder what spirits have to do with beliefs, but beliefs are powered by spirits. Truth is powered by God. God is Spirit, and the Spirit of God is called the Spirit of truth. In the same way, there is the spirit of error, of falsehood, with spirits involved in powering that. A permissive belief about character is powered by spirits.

First John 4:1: “Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.” Examine the beliefs coming for you. That is why he says to test the spirits.

“By this you know the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God.” Essentially, we can say that every spirit confessing redemptive truth is of God. “And every spirit that does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not of God. And this is the spirit of the Antichrist, which you have heard was coming, and is now already in the world.”

“You are of God, little children, and have overcome them, because he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world. They are of the world. Therefore they speak as of the world, and the world hears them.” That is according to the course of this world. “We are of God. He who knows God hears us; he who is not of God does not hear us. By this we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error.”

There are spirits powering beliefs. Therefore discernment is key in a world where spirits power beliefs and try to get us to hold one view or another, one worldview or another.

“Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God.” Is there a spirit of atheism? Yes. Atheism is powered by spiritual beings as well. Is there a spirit of redemptive truth? Yes. It is powered by the Spirit of God and, in its propagation, by angels as well. Would there be spiritual forces acting around atheism? Yes. Do you have discernment to work on? Yes.

Sometimes when people are about to enter error, it begins with frustration. They are already in a problem, and then they see something else: “I went out and saw this book.” It will seem as if the universe is conspiring, as people like to say, to ensure that you settle in that error. You meet a friend, and the conversation unexpectedly goes there. You receive validation you were not expecting. Spirits sponsor things.

We are in a spiritual world. People often see it wrongly. They readily talk about someone shaking something, or doing [unclear]. There is all of that too, and we are looking at it in the podcast about authority. But the way those spiritual things work is also the way things by the Spirit of God work.

Someone is called a false prophet, which means he receives revelation and gives utterances. He will have to be intoxicated in the spirit—not the Spirit of God—and then perhaps make proclamations. There is fellowship with demons just as there is fellowship with God. There is being strong in darkness just as there is being strong in light. It is the same process, but the belief system causes the actions to stay in darkness or in light. Both are like prayer. The underlying philosophy differs: truth or falsehood, and therefore the spirits you deal with.

Even when someone is fellowshipping with demons, he will likely tell you he is fellowshipping in light. That is why Scripture says Satan himself transforms himself into an angel of light. Think of these pantheistic types of people fellowshipping with demons. We should not exaggerate these things, but we should be aware of them.

## Reverence and the Compartments of the Heavenly World

People sometimes dance to “I will enter his gates with thanksgiving.” Even if you enter the gate, you are probably not yet in the building itself. The reverence people have once they enter the holy place—not even the holiest—is very serious. But when entering the gate, of course they rejoice. There is nothing wrong with that. Perhaps that is why people sometimes try to do praise early, then return to worship for the meeting. Either can work. It can also be very distracting, with people's minds gone afterward.

There is a heavenly world overlapping a natural one. We have looked at the compartments, the different parts of that world. There is wickedness, darkness, and there is light. We said there are two axes, two divisions we can consider. One division is based on nature: light and darkness. Another is based on whether you are alive or in the afterlife.

There are people on the side of darkness who have died, and people on the side of darkness who are alive. There are people on the side of light who are dead, or sleeping, and people on the side of light who are alive. Thus we can see four compartments in the heavenly world.

Some people ask where those who have died are. We do not know. That is the answer. There is no point saying, “I will tell you later.” There is nothing to tell later. We do not know. If you insist that they are under the earth, you are preaching your own theology. There is no basis for that. If you had a basis, you would have said it. You do not know.

Are they on Mars, in the heaven upon the earth there, or somewhere else? Whatever it is, we know they are in the heaven upon the earth. We also know there is a possibility of interaction, but not a possibility of translocation. There was a great gulf fixed even in Hades, when those on the side of light were in Hades before the resurrection, so that they could not move from here to there or from there to here.

In the same way, we can say there is a great gulf fixed—not as literally—in crossing from darkness to light or light to darkness, even in this age, while alive, not only in the afterlife. In heaven there is a compartment of light and a compartment of darkness, and there is a compartment of the living and a compartment of the sleeping.

For us, we are presently in the compartment of light, in this present life, not in the afterlife. We have the right to certain experiences in that compartment. Let us go to John 14. We are supposed to have experiences in the heavenly world overlapping the natural world.

## Stillness and Spiritual Perception

One of my concerns is that our mind is the major issue in engaging in the heavenly world, and our mind can be so distracted. Many of us are not used to having our mind still. We are used to being stimulated in the natural. Even when we say, “Let us be still,” we still want natural stimulation. Some people do not know how to be quiet: just be still, genuinely quiet.

If you are always stimulated in the natural, you cannot be stimulated—or sensitive—in the spiritual. You should be able to maintain a fine balance between your spiritual sensitivity and your natural sensitivity.

John 14:18: “I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.” Verse 19: “A little while longer and the world will see me no more, but you will see me. Because I live, you will live also.” The woman had said, “When the Christ comes, he will tell us all things.” This is the Christ telling us that worship has changed. Peter repeats the same thing; Paul repeats it.

We can imagine a use of “heaven” and “earth” that means the afterlife and this present life, both of which are heaven. In that case, we must understand the context in which the words are used.

Have you been seeing the Lord? Someone might say yes figuratively, as though he has been “kind of” seeing him, but does not want to be too sure.

One of the experiences we are going to have in this age, not merely in the age to come, is “You will see me.” Seeing is one perception used for all perception: a part for the whole, or the whole for a part—I am not sure which term applies. You can take “You will see me” as “You will perceive me.” “Because I live, you will live also.” You will be able to perceive.

Two things must happen: I have to be present, but you also have to be alive to perceive. “I will be present with you, and you will be alive to perceive.” There is a real work to do on our perception as believers. That is why I was talking about sensitivity to the natural and the spiritual.

They say that if you have to be alone for three days in a place with nothing, you begin to understand. There was something [name unclear] did in a completely white box, with nobody supposed to talk to him. I do not know how many hours it lasted. At a point you cannot take it; you must find something to stimulate yourself, something to do, or you will go crazy. He was counting steps, doing different things, looking for something to do.

But it might also be proof that people cannot even sit still for a while. Sometimes you can stay still for six hours. Let me tell you from personal experience: when you actually have to learn to be still, you may sleep off. It is that bad. You have to learn to be spiritually sensitive.

Sometimes during a prayer watch you have to find something to do: “Let me use my phone. Let me walk.” Your body is looking for something; you feel you must do something. You are too much “in the mountain” or “in Jerusalem.” You need to be in the Spirit.

## “I Will Manifest Myself to Him”

He is saying, “I will be present with you, and you will be alive.” There are experiences you should have: you should see him. “At that day you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you.” That one is nature: we have ontological fellowship.

Verse 21: “He who has my commandments and keeps them, it is he who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and manifest myself to him.” Manifest means to be visible, or perceptible. When you manifest yourself, you are not only visible to the person; the person can now touch you. You were outside the person's senses before, and now you are within them.

If this place were dark and I did not know certain things were there, I would not know because I could not see them. I could not engage with or touch them. If I already knew where they were, I could engage with them, but in this case it is the idea of being completely unaware, in my senses and perception, that something is there. He says, “I will manifest myself to you. I will be visible, perceptible, to you.”

When we think of the heavenly world, it is not just theoretical. He presents it as very experiential: “You will see me.” What are the options of interpretation? One is, “Mentally you will simply know that I am there.” But look at the choice of words: “I will manifest myself to him.” Are you sure that is the idea? It does not look like a mental recognition that he is with us, which other people do not have. He is pointing to a tangible, perceptible reality, before we even speak of experience.

Verse 22: “Judas, not Iscariot, said to him, ‘Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us, and not to the world?’” He did not answer, “You will have a mental recognition of it.” Verse 23: “Jesus answered and said to him, ‘If anyone loves me, he will keep my word; and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.’”

Does that look like a mental recognition? No. There is going to be a reality: they will make their home with you, because you will make up their house. The Father and the Son make their home with me. That is our reality. It is not merely a mental reality; it is perceptible and visible.

Let me show an example of a related word in Romans 1:19. It is not exactly the word [Greek term unclear], but it is related. One of the ways that word is used concerns how God has made himself known by the created world. Imagine the tangibility of the created world. Of course, the spiritual world does not have the same tangibility, but there is a reality as present as natural reality.

## A Real Spiritual World and a Present Heart

People say the spiritual world is as real as the natural world. There is a reality of a spiritual world just as there is a reality of a natural world. Romans 1:19 says, “Because what may be known of God is manifest in them, for God has shown it to them. For since the creation of the world his invisible attributes are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made.”

We can understand the idea of something being manifest from the natural world we see around us. There is a spiritual world as well, a real spiritual world. We are supposed to have experiences in the world we are in. Sometimes we rob ourselves of these experiences because we are not engaging with God in the Spirit. We are more focused on the natural.

Matthew 15:8–9: “These people draw near to me with their mouth, and honor me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. And in vain they worship me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men.”

You must be concerned that, even when you actively pray, it may not mean that you are actively engaging in the Spirit. When they went to Jerusalem and to the mountain, were they praying? They were. It is possible to have all the external trappings of praising, worshiping, or engaging with God, yet lack the internal thing that matters most.

“Their heart is far from me.” You can say their heart is not on him. This can be about beliefs, but I believe by principle it can also be about attention. It is like, “Draw near to God and he will draw near to you.” Is it possible that you have been very busy relating with God with your mouth, lips, and hands, but the key part—your heart, either in beliefs or in attention—is far from him? That can affect your worship.

Is our attention on him? Sometimes our attention can be on what we are doing: our praying itself, our speaking, our singing. Our attention can be on the people.

One thing I encourage music ministers to do is sing for yourself. Do not sing for the audience. If you sing for the audience, you are not worshiping. When you sing for yourself, you know how to come in with the ad-libs that carry them along. But if you are not first singing for yourself with God alone, and you are trying to do that, you will not be worshiping, because your mind will not be on God.

I was telling them yesterday that you are bringing people into your experience. Even if someone is far off, when he sees the way you are going, he will enter into it. That is the role of the leader. They expect you to be ahead, and you are drawing them in. That is the role of a priest: bringing people along into the experience.

You have to put your focus and attention on the Father. If your beliefs about him are right, your focus and attention ought to be on him.

## Incense, Prayer, and the Cloud

Our prayer and worship are called our incense in Scripture. Offering incense is the final activity in the worship of God in the temple. After the sacrifice, washing, setting the lamps, and all those things, you offer incense on the altar. Some argue that it was inside the holiest; some say just outside it.

Many times we talk about the cloud of glory. I totally believe there is a supernatural cloud that shows up in those experiences, but the incense itself was sometimes called a cloud.

We were talking about phases of glory last time. There was glory before the tabernacle, but the Lord told them that when they made the tabernacle, he would sanctify it with his glory. Then we saw that glory upon the tabernacle when they finished it.

Exodus 29:42: “This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of meeting before the Lord, where I will meet you to speak with you.” There would always be gratitude for redemption. “And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory. So I will consecrate the tabernacle of meeting and the altar.”

Exodus 40:22: “He put the table in the tabernacle of meeting, on the north side of the tabernacle, outside the veil; and he set the bread in order upon it before the Lord, as the Lord had commanded Moses.” Verse 24: “He put the lampstand in the tabernacle of meeting, across from the table, on the south side of the tabernacle; and he lit the lamps before the Lord, as the Lord had commanded Moses.”

The person is setting up the whole structure, one thing after another. “He put the gold altar in the tabernacle of meeting in front of the veil.” There are arguments about which side of the veil: inside, just in front, or outside in front. “And he burned sweet incense on it, as the Lord had commanded Moses.” He actively did something. He set the bread, put up and lit the lamps, took the golden altar of incense, and burned sweet incense on it.

“He hung up the screen at the door of the tabernacle. And he put the altar of burnt offering before the door of the tabernacle of the tent of meeting, and offered upon it the burnt offering and the grain offering, as the Lord had commanded Moses. He set the laver between the tabernacle of meeting and the altar, and put water there for washing; and Moses, Aaron, and his sons would wash their hands and their feet with water from it. Whenever they went into the tabernacle of meeting, and when they came near the altar, they washed, as the Lord had commanded Moses. And he raised up the court all around the tabernacle and the altar, and hung up the screen of the court gate. So Moses finished the work.”

“Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle.” Can this be the incense? Yes. Can it be a supernatural cloud? Yes. Can incense itself be called a cloud, using the exact same word? Yes.

Leviticus 16:11: “Aaron shall bring the bull of the sin offering, which is for himself, and make atonement for himself and for his house, and shall kill the bull as the sin offering which is for himself. Then he shall take a censer full of burning coals of fire from the altar before the Lord, with his hands full of sweet incense beaten fine, and bring it inside the veil.” You can see why people argue that the altar was inside the veil.

“And he shall put the incense on the fire before the Lord, that the cloud of incense may cover the mercy seat that is on the Testimony, lest he die.” There is a cloud of incense. The incense, which is prayer, is itself described as a cloud. Your worship and prayer can be what causes the cloud to be there.

“When you do this, the glory of God will appear.” When you pray or worship, the glory of God will appear. The degree to which you pray or worship is the degree to which there will be a cloud of incense. We have to be careful here: the degree to which you are mindfully praying toward God is the degree to which there will be perceptibility and tangibility of his glory, his presence.

That thinking was built into the theology of the tabernacle in the Old Testament. The degree to which you effectively engage in prayer and worship is the degree to which you perceive his tangible presence and glory, experience the tangibility of his glory. You are perceiving his glory tangibly to the degree that you offer incense, which is mindfully praying and worshiping. That was depicted with physical incense, but it can be a matter of your spiritual engagement.

There is an argument to be made that, although there is a supernatural cloud and supernatural fire—sometimes the fire consumes the offering—the incense itself can be described as a cloud. There is a lesson: you are responsible for the fire and the cloud in the temple, in the worship of God.

## Incense Offered to Other Gods

Let us see Ezekiel 8 as well. I believe these are the two places where we see “cloud” used for incense. Offering incense was also done to other gods. One lamentation of Scripture is that they were offering incense to other gods, which means they were fellowshipping with demons, fellowshipping on the basis of other belief systems.

Not every fellowship that did not use the name Yahweh was necessarily another system. Think of the wise men. The word for them, “magi,” is the same word used for a magician. “Magic” there is not the way we use the word today for illusion. For them it could be scientific, like someone who knew what to mix and give you. You will hear more in *Authority: Our Status Over Darkness*.

There is a history of [practitioners unclear] in Africa knowing how to make incisions to make people immune to certain communicable diseases, perhaps chickenpox. They discovered something and performed it as though it were with the gods, but there was actual science to it. Many such practitioners were spiritual, but also scientific. There would be an underlying philosophy in their belief system.

Clearly, the people referenced in Matthew must have had a positive, redemptive belief system for them to recognize the Christ and come to worship him. In the same Scriptures we see another person called a magician opposing redemptive truth. When people say, “All our fathers were like this,” are you sure? Some were positive, some negative; some redemptive, some not.

People from Chaldea, or practicing Chaldean beliefs, could be redemptive, as in Matthew 2. But others with the same origin and similar-looking practices were not of redemptive faith and even opposed it, opposing all righteousness, as in Acts 13. We have to be careful how we appraise these issues. Listen to *Authority: Our Status Over Darkness* for more details.

Ezekiel 8:9: “He said to me, ‘Go in, and see the wicked abominations which they are doing there.’ So I went in and saw, and there—every sort of creeping thing, abominable beasts, and all the idols of the house of Israel, portrayed all around on the walls. And there stood before them seventy men of the elders of the house of Israel, and in their midst stood [name unclear], the son of Shaphan. Each man had a censer in his hand, and a thick cloud of incense went up.”

This was not toward God. There was fellowship with other gods, with demons, and in that fellowship there was a cloud, symbolic of a manifest presence. We must be aware of these possibilities.

The word “cloud” can be used with incense, and the cloud is the making of the worshiper bringing in the incense. You are the one doing the praying and worshiping. Your prayer is called incense. Consider Revelation 8:3–5, and Luke 1, where incense was being offered while the people prayed outside. Psalm 141:2 says, “Let my prayer be set before you as incense, the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

If incense can be referred to as the cloud, then how we worship and pray brings the manifest presence of God. Before saying “how,” let us first say that our prayer and worship bring the cloud.

In their time, could there also be an independent cloud? Yes. Their incense could be like a cloud, and there could also be a separate manifest cloud. In our time, if prayer brings the manifest presence of God, there can be further experiences where it is actually visible or audible. These are important details concerning our worship of God today in the heavenly world—true temple worship happening in the heavenly world.

## Mindfulness in Prayer and Worship

Now I can talk about how we pray and worship, because mindfulness is a core part of how we engage. Our spirit is spirit with a mind, just as our body is body with a brain. If our minds are not involved in the spiritual world, we will not have the experiences we are supposed to have in the Spirit.

If someone's brain is dead, even though everything else is working, the person cannot receive the signals he should receive and does not function as he should. In the same way, when you are not active with your mind but are active with your spirit—“I pray, my spirit prays”—you can be doing something in the spiritual world without perceiving what you are doing. You are not feeling it or fully noticing anything you are doing there. Our mind must be present.

“These people draw near to me with their mouth, and honor me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. And in vain they worship me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men.” Their beliefs cause them to lose out on reality. If they knew he was in the Spirit and put their minds to it, it would be true worship. But what they are doing is vain; they are not even looking to him. Their attention and mind are not on him.

Their belief is the first issue. If your belief about the worship of God is not that it happens in the Spirit, that is already a problem. Your belief about it is not spiritual. If your attention is not in the Spirit, that is another problem.

We must learn to be in the Spirit in our prayer and worship: really spend time in the Spirit, be present there, and perceptively experience what is available to us. There are all kinds of possibilities. In the heavenly world is God's surgery room, God's strategy room, where he can give direction for our lives: “This is the next thing to do. Pay attention to this. Look at how to do this. Try that. Take this step; take that step.”

There are some things I believe are set experiences: perceiving in the spiritual world, being with the Father and Son, perceiving God's presence and glory, being conscious of it. We can argue that visibilities and audibilities may not be set experiences; perhaps they are in the discretionary realm. But perception of God's tangible presence, I believe, is in the realm of what should naturally occur.

When we are intoxicated, there are further experiences outside our control. But we can set ourselves up for them when we are in the Spirit, to receive all God is saying to us.

## Observation and Reason in the Spiritual World

What do we mean by paying attention to God? Our observation and reason are exercised on him. It is not only what we are not doing, but also what we are doing. It is not merely, “I am choosing not to think in the natural.” I am actively thinking in the Spirit. The Father and Son are here, and I am actually observing and listening.

Paying attention is observation and reason. The Father and Son are here. In worshiping them, I am asking questions, thinking: “I will set myself upon the watch.” I am observing and reasoning: “What are you going to say about this matter before you? This is what I am in faith for. I am standing on it, Lord; this should be done.” Our observation and reason are exercised in the supernatural, in the spiritual.

It can be like receiving a word of knowledge. You are observing and reasoning in the spiritual: “Lord, is there any case here? Is there anything you want to mention?” Something stands out by salience: “Is there something like this? Am I really sure this is what you are saying?” You are looking at it. You are not doing nothing; you are doing something.

“Father, Son, you are here. Is there something you want to talk about?” You are observing and reasoning about it. It is not emptying yourself. It is not Buddhism. They are present, and you are engaging and asking. It is observation and reason. That revelation comes from a domain that is not empirical, but spiritual and supernatural. It is still observation and reason.

We say “observation, reason, revelation.” Sometimes we mean revelation in a general sense. I am talking about supernatural revelation: observation and reason in the spiritual world.

## A Settled Foundation and the Reception of Revelation

Do not forget what we said earlier. When engaging the spiritual world, the first thing is a strong and settled doctrinal foundation. Your metaphysical truth, natural truth, human-person truth, human-society truth, historical truth, and biblical-revelatory truth must be settled. A strong doctrinal foundation is necessary in engaging the supernatural.

Second, normalize conversion and compression. One of the great ignorances these days is people mocking the supernatural. I laugh because the devil has sponsored people to mock supernatural and revelatory experiences. I am speaking about believers, not unbelievers; we understand the unbelievers' position.

Even if you have doctrinal issues with someone, you can say, “I think this person's experience is corrupted by his wrong doctrine. His reception of that experience is corrupted.” But do not start faulting the very kinds of experiences through which we have historically received veritable revelation as believers. People's conversion and compression can still be faulty.

I told you that Micaiah's conversion and compression was faulty. He described the Lord asking who would do something against [name unclear], and one spirit saying he would put a lying [unclear]. Nobody thinking within the view of biblical doctrine now would see that experience in precisely that way. The conversion and compression would be different.

Yet that does not take away from God's sovereignty. Because of his doctrinal position, the conversion and compression of the revelation he received—that something would happen against this person—showed God's authority and everyone submitted to him. Was it not true that there was a lie? Was it not true that the man was going to die, as he saw and said, describing the people without a shepherd? Yet there was conversion and compression.

If we will walk in the heavenly world, settle and normalize conversion and compression. There will be experiences and a message to be converted. When you receive a song, the content is converted into your language. The content might be, “We are offering for the things of God,” or “The glory of God is here.” Those are realities without a language. But the reality is converted to your language, to the kinds of words used in your Christian circle.

Conversion makes it relatable to you. Compression means that things so high are stepped down to what you can handle, so that they do not overpower you. For some people, compression means seeing internally rather than externally. Normalize that a real revelation from God can be compressed into an internal vision or a knowing. It could have been expressed in a bigger way, but it is compressed to that, and converted to what they can relate to in their language.

Normalize conversion and compression in heavenly experiences. I think it is one of the biggest things people need to do. Then you can understand that perhaps God was trying to tell someone something, yet there is an error in the reception. A lack of appreciation of conversion and compression causes problems.

## Internal, External, Immersive, and Overlapping Experiences

The third thing we discussed in the mechanics of the Spirit is recognizing different kinds of experiences. We gave different axes to work with, especially internal versus external and immersive versus overlap.

Immersive experiences mean you are only seeing the spiritual experience. Overlap means you are still conscious of both. You can have an immersive experience internally, where you are not conscious of anything else, like sleeping and dreaming. Sometimes, as you are about to wake up, you know you are there; at other times you do not. Sometimes you know, “This is not the only place I am. I am here, but I know I am seeing something else as well.”

There can be internal overlap, internal immersive, external overlap—seeing something externally alongside what you are already seeing externally—and external immersive experiences. You need to be aware of these kinds of experiences and their general progression.

Since we are both natural and spiritual beings, we fundamentally start with internal experiences, with the likelihood of internal immersive experiences. That is the normal progression, and you should be familiar with it. Sometimes compression meets you at your degree of familiarity.

Internal overlap, then internal immersive. After that, my thought is perhaps external immersive before external overlap. It may be easier to have an external immersive experience, because you might not even know it is external. Later you may say, “My eyes were wide open, it was all cloud, and an experience was happening.” People may ask whether your eyes were really open, and there can be a debate. I would put external immersive as the third level and external overlap as the fourth, a different level.

Some things can happen as external overlap, such as seeing something like a cloud. You may wonder whether you are the only one seeing it or whether others are seeing it too. With external immersive experiences, there are often debates over whether the experience was really external immersive or internal immersive.

We should know the different kinds of experiences that can happen as we are intoxicated, filled, or [term unclear] in the heavenly world. Our familiarity with different degrees of experience can determine how a message from God is compressed.

Remember that engagement is by observation and reason, but the observation is directed to the spiritual, not the natural. You are not doing nothing. Someone says, “But I was still thinking; my mind was still active.” Yes, it should be active. Do not let that stop you from receiving what is coming.

Someone receiving a word of knowledge may ask, “Lord, do you have anything? Is there anything?” Then something can hit you, stand out, become salient, touch you. Something feels delivered to you: “This is what is going on.” You ask, “Is this really the case? Is something like this happening?” Depending on how certain you are, based on experience and how strongly it hits you, you may say, “I could be wrong, but is something like this going on?” This is the pure mechanics of the things of the Spirit.

## Maintaining Executive Control

The fourth thing we said concerning the mechanics of the Spirit is the need to maintain a faith stand. Sometimes, while I am preaching something, I am also getting the revelation. When I discussed our nature in the life podcast, I never used John 14, and I should have used it when I made the connection with Luke 11.

I wrote two faith stands here. In discussing the taxonomy of heavenly experiences—taxonomy simply means categorization—I was careful to distinguish where executive control is present and absent. There were four axes: internal versus external, overlap versus immersive, executive control present or absent, and faith imagination versus autonomous generation.

You must maintain a faith stand for executive control. Do not enter spiritual experiences in which executive control is absent. Executive control means you can end the experience. It does not mean you are generating it. There can be autonomous generation, with executive control still very much present.

“The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.” “If anything is revealed”—that is autonomous. They are observing and reasoning within the spiritual world: “Father, you are here. What do you have?” While listening and observing, something can hit them autonomously and they begin to see it, but they can end it at any time. Executive control must be present.

Have you ever had words of knowledge that you did not give? You can receive serious things and still dismiss them as though it were all in your head. Later you realize the person really had that issue, and it pains you deeply. Yet executive control was present. It can be very serious and still remain under your executive control.

The content is autonomous; it is not self-generated. You may wonder why your mind is wandering like that, but control is present. You could say, “No, rubbish,” and end it. There must be a faith stand for executive control maintained in heavenly experiences.

This is important because some of these things, particularly this fourth point, are why people have gotten into a mess. “I am trying to yield.” Does yielding mean not having control? No. You are observing and listening in that world to allow autonomous content: what will hit you, what you will begin to see. But the overriding factor is that, even while having the experience, you can end it. It is your right.

## Faith, Asking, and Autonomous Experiences

The last thing we said was: faith-stand imagination, or self-generated content, leading to autonomous experiences under executive control. I thought I might tweak that, but it is still true.

First, you have a faith stand on the reality of the Father and Son making their home with you and being available to give what you ask. In John 14, he says that in that day, when he sends the Spirit to be in us and the Father and Son make their home with us, we can ask things and they will answer us.

You stand in faith: “Father and Son, you are here. You said that if I ask, I will receive.” That includes supernatural things: “The works I do, you will do also.” I can receive a Mount-of-Transfiguration-type experience or whatever it may be. I am asking, “What do you have?”

That is faith-stand confession or imagination of an already existing reality: the Father and Son are here, and I can ask and receive. That leads to autonomous experiences.

John 14:12: “Most assuredly, I say to you, he who believes in me, the works that I do he will do also; and greater works than these he will do, because I go to my Father.”

One thing that robbed us of the beauty of John 14:13–14—and for a long time I struggled with this—is that it was first explained to me as the believer's authority in the name of Jesus. I never thought to see it as asking and receiving in an open-ended Luke 11 context.

After speaking of greater works, he says, “And whatever you ask in my name, that I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.” Who said it was only [unclear]? Who said it was not a word of knowledge, a word of wisdom, or interpretation of tongues?

Verse 14: “If you ask anything in my name, I will do it.” “In the name” and “by the Spirit” are parallels. In that situation, when the Spirit is in you, ask in his name and he will do it.

Can this mean that, relative to the gifts of the Spirit, when we ask we receive, as in Luke 11? Yes. “Ask, and it shall be given; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you.” Does this show our initiative concerning these gifts and yet God's discretion? Yes. It shows the interaction between our initiative, God's impulses, and our initiative to act on them.

Can I be in the heaven upon the earth, the home of the Father and Son, asking for direction concerning an issue? “Father, in this meeting there are people who need me to minister to them. What are the things I should minister? You are already here making your home with me.” That is faith-stand confession and imagination. “You are here, ready to answer. What are you giving me?”

This leads to autonomous experiences while the faith stand for executive control is never lost. Conversion and compression are fully normalized: “I know I will receive it in my own language, in my own way, in the way I receive. Give it to me; I will receive.”

Do not let people's criticism stop you. You have received from God as you are, in your state. If you find that you were not completely accurate, understand that your conversion or compression may have affected it. Do not let that stop you from receiving from God.

It is straightforward when we read John 14:12–14 alongside Luke 11. John 14 is about the workings of the supernatural, and it fits hand in glove with Luke 11: ask and receive, seek and find, knock and the door will be opened.

“Whatever you ask in my name, that I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.” “Lord, I need revelation concerning this person I am talking to.” Whatever you ask in his name, by his Spirit, as you are with him in this strategy room: “Lord, I need supernatural direction concerning my spiritual assignment. Am I supposed to do this or that?”

It is unfortunate that this was limited to authority, because that could have robbed us of much concerning the other gifts of the Spirit. I encourage you: ask.

## Our Right to Ask and Receive

When functioning in the heavenly world, if it is not simply fellowship, then ask. For fellowship and edification, you already know what to do. When we speak about experiences of the glory of God, answers for ministry, and those sorts of things, ask. For spiritual growth, you stand on God's record. For fellowship, you get to fellowship. When we talk about the gifts, ask, knowing you have a right to be answered.

“Whatever you ask, I will give to you.” You already know this is within the context of what is righteous and according to God's word. You are ready for the application of natural and discretionary impulses. But what do you have to do when it comes to greater works? Ask. That is what we have to do in this room.

I wish I had made this connection when I was fitting Luke 11 with the supernatural. I always knew Luke 11 could concern supernatural faith stands. But faith as a means of engagement for greater works, supernatural works, and our supernatural nature becomes clear when you place it in John 14.

What do we want to ask? Something for ourselves, or for someone else? We can gather in a Christian meeting and ask. “Let him who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.” Let him ask. It is the same thought: “Whatever you ask, I will give.”

“I want to have this to bless somebody.” If he does not have it, fine; keep quiet and speak to yourself and to God. But he can ask, assured that he has a right to receive. If for some reason he is not receiving—whether on his end or for a discretionary reason—then let him leave it. There is no big deal. He had the right to ask.

I can ask for supernatural experiences and happenings within the dynamics of natural and discretionary impulses. I can simply ask. That is the beauty of it: I can always ask and receive, seek and find, knock and have the door opened. What a blank check to supernatural experiences and results.

Before asking, we often say, “Can it really happen? What about God's discretion?” Just ask. Leave the discretion to him. There is a faith-stand confession or imagination of where we are and the fact that we ask and receive. As we observe and listen in that realm, standing in faith, something can hit us.

We do this with strong conviction, normalizing conversion and compression and knowing the kinds of experiences that can happen: internal overlap, internal immersive, external immersive, or external overlap. Any of them may happen, but with a faith stand for executive control and a faith stand to ask and receive. That is how we function. There is no mystery; it is that simple and clear in God's word.

I ask and I receive. If you ask, stand on your watch, and have not received, ask again later. It happens. I talk to God about many things. If I do not receive then, I know he will answer me; I will still get it. I postpone it to a later time, unless the matter is really bothering me at that moment.

In many prayer-watch sessions, sometimes while praying for the nation, I say, “God, today I want to pray about this issue.” I ask: “What is the best thing? What is the next thing? How should we go about this?”

I want us to ask something for ourselves, then ask for someone else and minister to them. We can always ask. It may concern your spiritual assignment, your natural assignment, experiences, or anything you want to ask about: “I want to talk to you about this issue. What do you have to say about it? Concerning this person, what can bless him most? What should I minister?”

First, minister to yourself and receive. “Whatever you ask in my name,” by his Spirit. Thank you, Lord. What a privilege.

## Taking Initiative as a Member of the Body

I almost feel that the other thing is a sermon to be preached, so I will preach it generally rather than trying to speak it forth. It is time for you to take initiative concerning how things ought to be.

Let me use a natural example. When you see something and think, “This is not how church should be in this area; this is how it should be,” do not wait for someone to give direction. Take initiative. It is time to make that change on the inside, seeing yourself as someone who can lead the cause and take charge, even as someone called by God to do it: “God has given me this idea, direction, or wisdom about how we can do things, so I can take initiative.”

Some say, “I see this problem, but I will wait until I am given the responsibility. I do not want to be too forward.” That is a wrong spirit. It is better to be too forward and be told it is not your time for that, than to have something laid on you and never say, “I think this is the direction we should take. I think I can execute it with this initiative. We can do it this way.”

Be the kind of person who, when something is laid on your heart, can say what to do and present it. “I do not want to be too forward” is the wrong spirit. We have tensions in many things in life: between love and discipline, between order and submission on one hand and pursuit and ambition on the other. But it is not right simply to say, “I do not want to be too forward.”

I saw the Lord laying different things on different people, but some were holding back. He may not be laying it on my heart, and for the sake of effectiveness it may not be my business. It is on someone else's heart. Instead of saying, “I see this; I feel God's Spirit moving on me,” they hold back. It could be something natural or spiritual: “I think we should do this this way.” They say, “I do not want to bring that suggestion.” That is not of God's Spirit.

Be careful of that inhibiting spirit that does not allow God to work fully through you, that does not allow you a sense of ownership in a mission. You are not seeing your part. You are abandoning too many things that are your part because you are not being forward. You need to say, “I can do this and make things work better.” It could be natural or spiritual. That is how the body works, as in 1 Corinthians 12.

God may show something to you, not to me. How many things can one person do? Imagine an idea comes to someone's mind: “Every day, church can smell nice. I will come and do something about it; that will be my contribution.” How would I know? Someone else may feel that he is supposed to do it, but say, “I do not want to be too forward.” That is not the Spirit of God. It is the devil trying to ensure people do nothing. People are giving spiritual reasons for doing nothing.

Honestly, I had not thought about this throughout the past week. It came by God's Spirit. I think I need to tell other people too, because people may be walking in that wrong spirit, perhaps one we have caused in some way.

Whenever you have something, present the initiative. Push for it. That is the Spirit of God. It shows that you understand the body of Christ and what church is: “I am a part. This thing I am seeing—could it be that others are not seeing it? Perhaps they are seeing it, but other things overwhelm their minds more. Perhaps this is my part. There is something I am supposed to push, and I will suggest my part.”

Whatever it is, do not say, “We need to be bigger before I do that.” That is not true. We need to be disciplined so that we always do it. If we do not do it small, we may not do it big. Start doing it so you always do it.

If you have that spirit of not wanting to be too forward, I cast it out of you. It is not the Spirit of God. When God lays something on your heart, it is because that is your part. Imagine how that would stir you to take doctrine more seriously, and how useful you would be in the world.

Whatever he lays on your heart, do not repress or inhibit it. I saw something concerning that, and I think it is important. Any system where people have this kind of inhibiting spirit hinders the extent of what God can do. It limits many things, including excellence and the working of the Spirit.

It is better to make a mistake and be corrected than to hold back and lose much good. “I want to do this.” Do it, and we can correct what needs correction. How many good things do we lose because we are trying to avoid bad things? We can correct and fix the bad things. As someone said, “I would rather a little excess than a whole house with no fire. We have enough damp cloths to put out the fire.”

Taking initiative is better than doing nothing. It applies to the supernatural, the natural, leadership, and many other things. I pray you get hold of it, because it really hit me in the room, just as we were discussing.

We will talk more about our engagement. Today we have shared important things about functioning in the heavenly world. If you practice them, I think you are in a good place. We have put down a good picture. Let us bless the Lord and thank him as we close this session.
