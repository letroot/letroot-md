# The Heavenly World — Part 5

## Incense and the Manifest Glory of God

What we saw on Sunday, while looking at the subject of incense, is that there is a correlation between incense and glory. When we offer incense—which we would ordinarily say is our worship of God, our praise of him—we cause the glory of God that is latently present to be manifestly present. There is something we can do to bring about the glory.

We are talking about that correlation between the cloud of incense and glory. Go to Leviticus 16:13. What I find interesting about Leviticus 16 is what was said in Leviticus 9. There is that which brings the glory, that which causes the appearance of the Lord, which can now be tangibly perceived and visibly and audibly received. I want to teach us something simple and important today that we have to get.

In Leviticus 9:1, reading down to verses 4 and 6, it says, “It came to pass on the eighth day that Moses called Aaron and his sons and the elders of Israel.” If you read everything before this point, Moses had set up the tabernacle at the end of Exodus and was unable to enter. By the beginning of Leviticus, he was able to enter. God spoke to him from Leviticus 1 through Leviticus 8 about how temple worship was meant to be done.

At the end of Exodus 40, verse 35 says, “Moses was not able to enter the tabernacle of meeting, because the cloud rested above it, and the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle.” We can even argue for the incense having been put in place. In Exodus 40:24, “He put the lampstand in the tabernacle of meeting, across from the table, on the south side of the tabernacle; and he lit the lamps before the Lord, as the Lord had commanded Moses.” This was something actively going on. Verse 26 says, “He put the golden altar in the tabernacle of meeting in front of the veil; and he burned sweet incense on it, as the Lord had commanded Moses.”

We can argue that there is the manifest cloud, which is spiritual and supernatural, and there was a physical cloud, the cloud of the incense. Moses was not able to enter the tabernacle of meeting because the cloud rested above it and the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle. Whenever the cloud was taken up, the children of Israel would go onward.

Verses 36–38 are like a general conclusion. The actual conclusion of that event is Exodus 40:35. Verses 36–38 describe what started to happen from that point: they now had the tabernacle, the glory of God was there, and they started to move based on the glory. But the actual end of the event of building the tabernacle was that Moses could not enter.

In Leviticus 1:1, “Now the Lord called to Moses, and spoke to him from the tabernacle of meeting, saying.” He called him and spoke to him: “Speak to the children of Israel, and say to them: When any one of you brings an offering to the Lord, you shall bring your offering of the livestock—of the herd and of the flock.” He was instructing him from Leviticus 1 through chapters 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. It was now about the consecration of the priests, I believe.

We get to the major point in chapter 9: “It came to pass on the eighth day.” From that incident of setting up the tabernacle, a week later Moses called Aaron, his sons and the elders of Israel. He said to Aaron, “Take for yourself a young bull as a sin offering and a ram as a burnt offering, without blemish, and offer them before the Lord.” This is based on the instruction he had received in chapter 8 about the consecration of the priests. It is for atonement. Today, you might say it is for deliverance from the reign of sin and death.

“To the children of Israel you shall speak, saying, ‘Take a kid of the goats as a sin offering, and a calf and a lamb, both of the first year, without blemish, as a burnt offering.’” This was atonement for the children of Israel, and again it involved a sin offering and burnt offering. “Also a bull and a ram as peace offerings, to sacrifice before the Lord, and a grain offering mixed with oil; for today the Lord will appear to you.”

Verse 5: “So they brought what Moses commanded before the tabernacle of meeting. And all the congregation drew near and stood before the Lord.” When you say, “Therefore, having boldness to enter into the Holiest,” that means you already have your sacrifice and everything ready through the blood of the Lamb. For us, that is the work Christ has done for us to be able to enter. We are now in a state where the Lord can appear to us. In fact, he has told us, “I will manifest myself to you.” John 14 is the same thing as this: “I will appear to you. When redemption has been done, I will manifest myself to you.”

They drew near and stood before the Lord. Then Moses said in Leviticus 9:6, “This is the thing which the Lord commanded you to do, and the glory of the Lord will appear to you.” He is saying with almost all certainty that when you do this, the glory of the Lord will appear to you.

I know some people are not comfortable when we talk about the reality of the glory as a matter of receiving it by faith. But it takes receiving it by faith to then see it in the degrees of its manifest presence, its manifest state or reality. “The glory of the Lord will appear to you.”

## The Cloud of Incense and the Altar

In Leviticus 16, when they were to make the offering on the Day of Atonement, the one that was to apply for the entire year, notice what it says about incense. Leviticus 16:11: “Aaron shall bring the bull of the sin offering, which is for himself, and make atonement for himself and for his house, and shall kill the bull as the sin offering which is for himself. Then he shall take a censer full of burning coals of fire from the altar before the Lord, with his hands full of sweet incense beaten fine, and bring it inside the veil.”

This is where you find some arguing that the altar of incense is before the veil, and some saying it is inside the veil, after the veil. There are all kinds of arguments. Historically, there was a fight between two camps about whether it should be before or inside the veil. Where do you think I stand on that? I think the altar is inside the veil. That is where our fellowship with God is, where the glory of God is supposed to be manifest.

Some say it should be before you enter into the Holiest, just outside the veil, just before you pass it. Others say it should be inside the veil, when you enter into the Holiest. When either camp had the high priest, they would move the altar of incense. The other would say that was why things were not going well during that period. You can imagine the superstitious things that followed.

It says, “Bring it inside the veil.” Then verse 13 adds, “And he shall put the incense on the fire before the Lord, that the cloud of incense may cover the mercy seat.” That is the kind of argument some use to say it must be inside the veil. Others go to what we just read in Exodus: it was before the veil. Some can argue that perhaps it is just for the Day of Atonement that it should move inside. You can read about it; there were arguments between different camps on that.

“The cloud of incense” is what I wanted you to notice. The very same word for cloud was applied to the incense. We know there is a manifest cloud that is a spiritual reality, but we are seeing that the incense was also described as a cloud. In this sense, permit me to say, they were almost manufacturing the cloud. They had a physical means, a physical technology, a physical pattern of bringing about the cloud.

I think it was done—even though there would still be the manifest cloud—to show that there is something you do that brings about the cloud that is spiritually seen. What you do with regard to the incense, bringing that cloud of incense, is what brings this manifest, spiritual, supernatural cloud that you see. We talked about that on Sunday.

We have studied what that incense is. It is also called sweet incense. The fellowship we have with him, our prayer, our fellowship, our worship of him, is sweet incense.

Look at Psalm 141. Our incense is our prayer and worship, our communication with God. The good pleasure of our communication with God, the thanksgiving, the sweetness of it, comes before him as a sweet-smelling aroma in the light of redemption.

The act of making incense, which brings about a cloud, is supposed to represent the act of praying. People were meant to be praying outside as they went to offer the incense; they were bringing the prayer toward God. Via redemption, we now have a means by which our prayer comes to God righteously, sweetly, not negatively. It is positive; he is receptive to it because there is no hindrance of the contradiction of sin and darkness.

Remember, the cloud itself is representative of glory. There is an explanation being made: there is a way to bring about the cloud, to bring about the glory. It is like saying there is a way that surely generates a cloud. When you are making incense, you are certainly generating a cloud, or generating glory. Is there a way we can generate a cloud of incense or the glory of incense? Yes.

On Sunday I told you there is a negative use of the cloud of incense in Ezekiel 8:11, but I am not going back there now. In Luke 1, as he was doing that, an angel of the Lord appeared to him. In the midst of an activity of worship and fellowship, the ministry of angels happens at such times.

The last place we will see something that clearly explains this concept of incense is Revelation 8. In verse 3, “Then another angel, having a golden censer”—that is where they put the incense—“came and stood at the altar. He was given much incense, that he should offer it with the prayers of all the saints upon the golden altar.” Much incense. Imagine you had very little incense in your censer. There would be a cloud, but it would be very light. If you add a lot of incense, it is going to be very thick, very foggy.

There is an explanation here that our worship and prayers generate the cloud of glory. The cloud of incense was the natural manifestation of what was supposed to be spiritually manifest. It is being used to teach something about the cloud. He was given much incense that he should offer it with the prayers of the saints. Here “it” is italicized: “That he should offer with the prayers of all the saints upon the golden altar which was before the throne.”

“The smoke of the incense”—you are seeing the concept of cloud and smoke—“with the prayers of the saints, ascended before God from the angel's hand. Then the angel took the censer, filled it with fire from the altar, and threw it to the earth.” He is bringing about the answer to that prayer. The effects of the prayer are now going to be seen. There were noises, thunderings, lightnings and an earthquake.

This is supposed to show the cumulative fulfillment of our prayers—that is why it says “much incense”—leading to the end of the age. You could give it a Romans 8 kind of idea: we do not know what we should pray for; the Spirit helps our infirmities, according to the will of God, all to the close and end of this age.

What the Lord gave Moses in the tabernacle is a technology about how to bring about the cloud of glory. If the incense was spoken of as a cloud, he is using the cloud of incense to represent what will be spiritually manifest: the cloud of glory. If we understand that incense itself is praying, then praying or offering the incense brings about the smoke or cloud of incense, which represents the cloud of glory. Prayer brings about a cloud of glory. It definitively generates a cloud of glory.

Little incense will mean little cloud, little smoke. Much incense will mean much cloud, much smoke. We can directly account for how the glory is generated. He said, “Do this, and the glory of the Lord will appear; the Lord will appear to you.” That which is latently present will become manifestly present when you engage him in prayer, in persistent faith in prayer. There is enough in God's word to make that submission.

The word of the Lord came to Abraham in a vision, and Abraham started to engage in that vision. The fact that it is mentioned as a vision makes it seem likely to be an internal overlap situation. Look at verse 4: “And behold, the word of the Lord came to him.” It came the first time, and he responded. He knew it was the word of the Lord. After he responded, it says the word of the Lord came again.

I have seen people explain “the word of the Lord” using the Hebrew word [unclear] or the Greek word *logos*, connecting it with John 1:1, to say it was a person who came to them. I am not blaming them; that explanation may be allowed. But if it were a person, it would not say the word of the Lord came again in verse 4. The word came, communicated something, he responded, and then the word of the Lord came again.

“This one shall not be your heir.” He had just made a case that this one should be his heir, and he got a response: “This one shall not be your heir, but one who will come from your own body shall be your heir.” Then God brought him outside and said, “Look now toward heaven, and count the stars.”

Is this an internal overlap vision? He is hearing the voice of the Lord, probably still awake and able to see what is going on around him while God engages him. He is still in that mood, that atmosphere, but God says, “Go out.” He led him outside and told him to count the stars. “I cannot count them.” “So shall your seed be.” We do not have any idea that there is a manifest appearance of a being here. We just know that the word of the Lord came to him.

He believed, and it was accounted for righteousness. God continued talking: “I am the Lord who brought you.” Abraham said, “Lord, how shall I know that I will inherit this?” God told him to make a sacrifice. This is still internal overlap. He gathered the things for the sacrifice. Verse 11 says that when the vultures came down, Abraham drove them away.

Now verse 12: “When the sun was going down, a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and behold, horror and great darkness fell upon him.” He started to see a redemptive vision in a trance. This time it was no longer internal overlap; it was internally immersive. Someone could argue external immersive, but it says deep sleep, so we say internally immersive. He saw great horror. God said, “Know certainly that your descendants will be strangers in a land.”

Imagine him praying, perhaps saying, “Yahweh is God, Yahweh alone,” and communicating in his heart, “Lord, how will this happen?” As he is praying, he sleeps off. Brothers and sisters, sometimes sleeping off in prayer can become a revelation. Please do not twist this. For some of you, sleeping off was not revelation; it was just pure sleep. But there are times it can be like that and lead to something.
Are you aware there were times a physical being appeared to Abraham as well? This is important. He had this vision, and the Lord spoke to him. Many people are missing God's interaction and engagement with us. They find it difficult to relate because they are complicating things. The word of the Lord came to Abraham.

If you do not know how to relate to the word of the Lord, you do not know how to function in revelation. The major text on this issue is 1 Samuel. Samuel was the prototype prophet who would teach the sons of the prophets. You then start to see this phrase become common, though we first saw it with Abraham.

As I was undertaking a study on this, I felt led while resting this week to play a teaching by Kenneth Hagin. I was tired and scrolling, and saw a teaching. It had been a while since I had listened from that quarter, so I downloaded it. I also saw a teaching by [name unclear] about heavenly materiality. I was teaching the heavenly world and trying to talk about tangibility, so I thought I should listen to it.

I was excited because it resonated with things I knew: words of knowledge and discerning of spirits, perhaps seeing a vision before going to a meeting. He told someone, “This person will be seated exactly here.” One of them came and said, “I saw the person.” The person sat just where he had said. He described those things very well.

I have had something similar. I remember seeing something while on a bike going to a meeting. There was conversion and compression. I saw what the person was wearing: green and white. When I got there, I was looking and did not see the person. I do not know whether any of you were there. It was in the days of [place unclear]. I thought the person wore a green top and white. Then I saw a lady wearing white in that same corner. I was wondering, until she stood and I saw she was wearing a green skirt. That is why I knew about conversion and compression. He was talking about these different experiences and mentioned how the prophets said the word of the Lord came to them. He said he was sure not everybody heard it; if they had all heard it, the prophet would not have needed to say it to them. It was not heard by them, but by the prophet.

He was making a point about how experiences begin, which resonated with me: they start out small. There was a woman he wanted to minister to who was about to tell him something. It got me interested: the word of the Lord. ## Samuel Learns the Word of the Lord

In Jeremiah and Ezekiel, you can hardly count the times it says the word of the Lord came to them. It is the most common way. We also see the burden of the Lord coming, because that can be an emotion.

1 Samuel 3:1: “The boy Samuel ministered to the Lord before Eli. And the word of the Lord was rare in those days.” When you read Ezekiel and Jeremiah, you see the word of the Lord coming so freely. Here, it was rare at that time for people to say the word of the Lord came to communicate to them.

Something came to me this evening while meditating on God's word. We often say that when someone prophesies, instead of saying, “The Lord is saying,” they should recognize it is their reception. While it is true that you can mess things up, if you say, “The Lord is saying,” we still judge it. But the prophets said it freely. Today someone can barely say, “The word of the Lord came to me, and he says this,” without, “We have to be careful.”

I know there are times you must be careful, but not every time. Even in the Bible, sometimes someone says, “I now knew indeed that the word of the Lord came to me.” Sometimes, if the word of the Lord came to you, say, “The word of the Lord came to me.”

Some of you have been there when I knew something, but then used, “Is there anybody here?” when I had seen a vision in which I was pointing at the person and telling them. Why be disobedient? Just speak the word. Where you are supposed to be careful, be careful.

1 Samuel 3:1 says, “The word of the Lord was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation.” It came to pass when Eli was lying down in his place, his eyes having grown dim so that he could not see, and before the lamp of God went out in the tabernacle where the ark of God was, while Samuel was lying down, that the Lord called Samuel. He answered, “Here I am,” ran to Eli and said, “Here I am, for you called me.” Eli said, “I did not call; lie down again.” Samuel went and lay down.

The Lord called Samuel again. Samuel arose and went to Eli: “Here I am, for you called me.” Eli answered, “I did not call, my son; lie down again.” Now verse 7: “Samuel did not yet know the Lord, nor was the word of the Lord yet revealed to him.”

It was almost taken for granted that the modus operandi of God's interaction is the word of the Lord. Knowing the Lord means becoming familiar with the word of the Lord, not becoming familiar with a particular figure. This is the same thing he told them when speaking through Moses: “Did you see any form?” No, but they heard a voice.

In Genesis 3, they heard the voice of the Lord in the garden and hid themselves from his presence. God's coming is typically first a sound. There can be times when there is a form, what people often call the visible Yahweh, like Abraham had when the Lord told him what was about to happen. But the more common way, because God is self-existent, is the word of the Lord, with glory accompanying it.

Get familiar with the Lord. Samuel did not yet understand the workings of Yahweh, nor had the word of the Lord been revealed to him. The Lord called him a third time, and he went to Eli. Eli perceived that it was the Lord calling. It can be an internal voice that looks like an external voice.

Eli said, “Go, lie down; and it shall be, if he calls you, that you must say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.’” Samuel lay down in his place. The Lord came, stood and called as at other times, “Samuel! Samuel!” Samuel answered, “Speak, for your servant hears.” The Lord said, “Behold, I will do something in Israel at which both ears of everyone who hears it will tingle.”

If you have started to miss the voice of the Lord, you must do something. If the voice has started to grow dim, go and fix it. Or perhaps you have been ignoring the voice of the Lord, when in that place of prayer the word of the Lord comes to you. It is supposed to be commonplace.

You say, “I do not receive anything.” Really? The word of the Lord did not come to you? How do you receive words of knowledge? Something came to you. You are there in the heaven upon the earth, seeking and asking, “Lord, is there anything?” As you ask and seek, the primary way it happens is that the word of the Lord comes to you. Something comes to you.

Verse 19: “Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that Samuel had been established as a prophet of the Lord. Then the Lord appeared again in Shiloh. For the Lord revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the Lord.”

The appearance of the Lord was not that Samuel saw a form; it was by the word of the Lord. “The Lord appeared to me” can mean “The word of the Lord came to me.” The Lord made himself known by the word of the Lord. I think we complicate it when we start talking about the visible Yahweh, the Word who was with God and is also God. First, it is simply the modus operandi of this issue: the word of the Lord came.

## The Word of the Lord Concerning Leaders and Assignments

1 Samuel 15:10 shows that this became Samuel's mode of engagement. He knew that if the Lord was going to come to speak to him, it could be this way. “The word of the Lord came to Samuel, saying, ‘I greatly regret that I have set up Saul as king.’” Can the word come to someone like that? Yes. Can God say he regrets someone being a leader? Yes.

“I greatly regret that I have set up Saul as king, for he has turned back from following me, and has not performed my commandments.” We should be careful. The word of the Lord.

1 Kings 6:11: “Then the word of the Lord came to Solomon, saying, ‘Concerning this temple which you are building, if you walk in my statutes.’” Can God come to you concerning this app you are building? You had better wake up. Build it according to the pattern. Build it like this, like that. God will work with the technology of the day to do what is supposed to be done. He will speak the pattern according to which you should build, and you had better listen every step of the way.

“If you walk in my statutes, execute my judgments, keep all my commandments, and walk in them, then I will” fulfill what was promised. “I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will not forsake my people Israel.” If you do what he says, they will see his glory manifest among you. What he promised will be fulfilled if you do according to his word. The word of the Lord will come to you to build what you are supposed to build and do what you are supposed to do.

## Acting by the Word of the Lord

1 Kings 13:1: “Behold, a man of God went from Judah to Bethel by the word of the Lord, and Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense.” Can you imagine—not the priest. “Then he cried out against the altar by the word of the Lord, and said, ‘O altar, altar! Thus says the Lord.’” This is the young prophet. He went by the word of the Lord.

Verse 5: “The altar also was split apart, and the ashes poured out from the altar, according to the sign which the man of God had given by the word of the Lord.” He was acting by the impulse of God's word, what God spoke to him.

It is not normal for you to be walking with God and the word of God not to be coming to you. If you knew how it is supposed to come—as Samuel did not know, then later did—you would be able to receive it. If you do not know how, even when it comes you will ignore it or mistake it for something else. You will receive it when you know how it operates, when you know that relating with the Lord is by the word of the Lord coming to you.

I wish I could show you more in Ezekiel and Jeremiah. Sometimes what you might call flimsy, even like a joke, is part of what he communicated: “The word came to me and told me.”

In 1 Kings 13:9, the prophet says, “It was commanded me by the word of the Lord, saying, ‘You shall not eat bread, nor drink water.’” He left. The king wanted him to stay, but the old prophet came and lied to him that he had also been told to host him. In verse 17, the young prophet said he had been told by the word of the Lord not to eat bread or drink water. Verse 18: “I too am a prophet as you are, and an angel spoke to me by the word of the Lord, saying.”

Verse 20: “It happened, as they sat at the table, that the word of the Lord came to the prophet who had brought him back.” This time it was true; the other time he had lied. 1 Kings 16:1: “The word of the Lord came to Jehu the son of Hanani, against Baasha, saying, ‘Inasmuch as I lifted you out of the dust and made you ruler over my people Israel, and you have walked in the way of Jeroboam.’” You find it came via a prophet. ## Elijah: Provision, Direction and the Still Small Voice

1 Kings 17:1: “Elijah the Tishbite, of the inhabitants of Gilead, said to Ahab, ‘As the Lord God of Israel lives, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, except at my word.’ Then the word of the Lord came to him, saying, ‘Get away from here and turn eastward, and hide by the Brook Cherith.’”

Many people have missed walking with God because they are expecting a vision. In a vision, what can come to you is the word of the Lord. It is the primary modus operandi of God's relationship, from Genesis 2 into Genesis 3, Genesis 6, Genesis 12 and Genesis 15: the word of the Lord came, with the glory of the Lord.

In verse 8, “The word of the Lord came to him, saying, ‘Arise, go to Zarephath.’” You can miss God's provision if you miss the word of the Lord. Verse 16: “The bin of flour shall not run dry, according to the word of the Lord through Elijah.” Verse 24, the woman said, “Now by this I know that you are a man of God, and that the word of the Lord in your mouth is the truth.”

1 Kings 18:1: “It came to pass after many days that the word of the Lord came to Elijah, in the third year, saying, ‘Go, present yourself to Ahab, and I will send rain on the earth.’”

1 Kings 19:9: “There he went into a cave, and spent the night in that place; and behold, the word of the Lord came to him, and he said to him, ‘What are you doing here, Elijah?’” He said, “I have been very zealous for the Lord God of hosts; for the children of Israel have forsaken your covenant, torn down your altars, and killed your prophets with the sword. I alone am left.”

God said, “Go out, and stand on the mountain before the Lord.” The Lord passed by, and a great and strong wind tore into the mountains and broke the rocks in pieces before the Lord, but the Lord was not in the wind. After the wind was an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake was a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire. After the fire, a still small voice.

When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his mantle and went out and stood in the entrance of the cave. Suddenly a voice came to him, saying, “What are you doing here, Elijah?” Familiarity with the Lord is familiarity with the word of the Lord, or the voice of the Lord.

Elijah repeated the same thing. The Lord said, “Go, return,” and told him to anoint this person and that person. Afterwards he told him he had seven thousand who remained. That was one of the sassiest replies. Elijah had said he was the only one. God first gave him what to do, then told him there were seven thousand more. Be careful. It is the word of the Lord, the voice of the Lord.

1 Kings 21:17: “The word of the Lord came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, ‘Arise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel.’” Verse 28: “The word of the Lord came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, ‘See how Ahab has humbled himself.’”

Do you understand what I mean by ordinary? It was not for one grand occasion. It was very regular. When 1 Samuel 3 says the word of the Lord was rare in those days, people did not know God's mode of operation, so they could not become familiar with the word coming to them about this issue and that issue. It can be a daily thing.

Sometimes we are always trying to play humble. You know the word of the Lord came to you on a matter, but you say, “It just struck me while I was thinking through this.” The word of the Lord came to you. You can hear in conversion and compression, limited to your understanding, and later say, “The word of the Lord on this matter became clearer to me.” But it is supposed to be coming to you regularly.

We are talking about revelation. In one sense we can speak about the word of the Lord as what he has already spoken, through observation and reason: “Their voice has gone out into all the earth,” Psalm 19. But here we are talking about the word of the Lord coming by revelation and inspiration.
## Ezekiel: The Hand of the Lord and the Word of the Lord

This is what counts in walking with God. Many times it is simply glory being manifested: a tangible presence and a voice. Then Ezekiel says, “I looked, and behold, a whirlwind.” Sometimes you might not see anything. This time he saw a whirlwind and other things, then heard things.

Ezekiel 3:16: “It came to pass at the end of seven days that the word of the Lord came to me, saying, ‘Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel.’” The word of the Lord can come to you: “You are supposed to man this territory. Ezekiel 6:1: “The word of the Lord came to me, saying: ‘Son of man, set your face toward the mountains of Israel, and prophesy against them, and say, “O mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord.”’” The word came to him to tell them to hear the word of the Lord.

Inquire at the word of the Lord. Be there in that place of waiting. What is he saying? Listen: “Lord, what are you saying? What are you doing at this moment?” Inquire at, or inquire for, the word of the Lord today.
