## Affection Between Parents and Children

The next word is `philostorgos`, from `phileo` and `storge`. `Storge` concerns one's kindred, especially parents and children. So the bond expected between parents and children is a bond of friendship. We said we will discuss parenting under marital matters in the faith of Christ. The high point of the parent-child bond is friendship, but the responsibility of parenting is not simply the responsibility of becoming a friend. Friendship is the effect produced when you have done your responsibility as a parent well. Parenting is a job God gave you.

The job itself is not friendship; friendship is what develops as you do the job well. People sometimes make the mistake of saying, "All I want is to be my child's friend." But the child does not yet know what he is doing. Scripture says foolishness is in the heart of a child and the rod will drive it out. That is not merely about physical caning; it is about teaching through persuasion, instruction, and correction. We start with persuasion, but the work also includes instruction and correction.

That work will drive the foolishness out. Foolishness in a child is not a surprising moral defect; it means the child does not know. How can someone who has just entered the world know anything? All of us were foolish when we were born because we did not know. Wisdom means clarity because you now understand; foolishness means you are unclear and therefore act without understanding. A child begins knowing nothing. You have to drive out that state of ignorance by the rod of teaching.

The rod is teaching. You have to persuade, instruct, and correct, and you need a plan for doing that. The child will not automatically know how to treat people well. He will not know what to do if you do not tell him. You have to drive out ignorance of those things, or someone else will fill him with something else. When parents do their responsibility and children receive it, the effect is friendship. But people who try to be friends with their children first often run into problems.

If you do the responsibility, you can end up as friends. The responsibility is not shouting and screaming. It includes instruction and correction, as even God our Father does. You show the truth and persuade the child of the truth. When a child in naivety does something wrong, there are times you must instruct and correct him: "No, that is not right." These are the formative years, and that particular job will expire. It is not an eternal work. After a point, you move on.

You are no longer trying to run the person's life. Let the person be. What remains is counsel: "I have taught you this, and I trust you will do it. In this situation, I think you should take note of this." You continue to pray and maintain the person's trust. I heard an account of a parent telling a child, "I have trained and taught you. You now have your choices and can decide what to do. No matter what happens, I will always love you, but it is your life now. I will always have your back." Some of the children initially turned the wrong way, but the parent remained present even when they faced serious consequences.

You should declare that they will live right, walk rightly, fulfill God's purpose, and walk in God's path. But you have done your formative job; it has expired. What remains is counsel, prayer, best wishes, and words of faith. Parenting ought to happen within marriage, although for many reasons there are situations of single parenting. Romans 12:10 says, "Be kindly affectionate to one another."

Have the kind of family affection that relatives have, and have that family love as brothers. We also have the word `philoteknos`, to be fond of one's children. Strong's adds "maternal," probably because Titus applies it to women, but the word itself means to be fond of one's children. God wants us to have a bond of friendship with our children, a bond in which they can come and tell us what has happened.

## A Friendly Mind

The word `philophronos` means to have a friendly mind: to be kindly disposed, courteous, and affectionate. Your disposition itself is affectionate. It comes from `philophron`, to be friendly of mind. Acts 28:7 says, "In that region there was an estate of the leading citizen of the island, whose name was Publius, who received us and entertained us courteously three days." Next session we will put all of this together in the actual practice of friendship and friendliness in the faith of Christ.

This describes the way Publius did everything; it was his frame of mind. He had a friendly mind. He received them and entertained them courteously for three days. There is a similar cultural expectation that a stranger may be hosted for up to three days without interrogation: you feed him and care for him. After that, you may ask why he is there and what arrangement should be made. Similar instructions are discussed in Jewish sources and early Christian writings concerning traveling preachers. If a preacher stays beyond the expected period, it is reasonable to ask what he is doing.

Once the stay becomes longer, the person is more than a guest and has effectively become a family member, so contribution and arrangement become appropriate questions. The related word `philophron` appears in 1 Peter 3:8: "Finally, all of you be of one mind, having compassion for one another; love as brothers, be tenderhearted, be courteous." Be friendly of mind. Do things in a friendly, affectionate way.

## Seven Expressions of Friendliness and Friendship

As we conclude this session, we have seen seven expressions of friendship which we will build on seriously in the next session. The first is greeting and salutation. The second is conversation. The third is revelation, or disclosure of exclusive information. The fourth is training: teaching, rebuking, and chastening.

If someone is your friend, you want the person to become better and perhaps more skillful at something. You try to help the person become what he should be. That is one reason close relationships are costly: when I keep talking to someone, I am investing time and attention, and I expect that investment to produce change. It is an investment in both directions.

There are days when you see issues, pray about them, and want them resolved. Sometimes God speaks to you again about the person. It is work, even full-time work. Words of knowledge may function by the Holy Ghost because of a person you are helping. It is not an easy investment. The fifth expression is shared activity in shared purpose and pleasure. We saw Jesus eating with people. Where there is common purpose or wholesome pleasure, a friend becomes involved.

The sixth expression is support and help for the other person's vision and purpose. The seventh is honor through material giving and the supply of resources. We are going to begin seeing the deliberate steps involved in engaging all these expressions in our relationships.
