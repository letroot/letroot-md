# Relationships in the Faith of Christ (3)

Ephesians 4. We said the church is meant to bring us to a complete knowledge of the word. But we answered the question: why is the church doing that? Why does the Lord want the church to do that, to bring us to a complete knowledge of the word? What we saw is that there is a purpose behind what leads up to the church. The work of redemption, what leads up to the Lord Jesus instituting and establishing the church, has a purpose behind it. There is a reason why that work was done, and it is God's eternal purpose.

Ephesians 3 tells us that what God accomplished in Christ is his eternal purpose. Ephesians 1 explains that purpose to us in detail. God's purpose is the reason for redemption. When redemption is accomplished and we are now brought into God's purpose, or we are now taking that first step by redemption into God's purpose, what is to follow naturally is the perfecting, the equipping, the training, to make us fulfill that purpose.

We have seen that that perfecting is about knowledge, and that knowledge is supposed to produce growth. Knowledge must be regarded as an end in itself and then a means to an end. It may sound strange, but it must first be seen as an end in itself. The fact that you know is a goal accomplished of its own. The fact that you have grown and now know God is itself a work accomplished. Then following that, that knowledge is meant to produce your walk.

## Knowledge, Stability, and Growing Into Christ

Ephesians 4:14 says that we should no longer be children tossed to and fro by every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men and cunning craftiness. Relative to this matter of relationships in the faith of Christ, being tossed to and fro includes the different kinds of opinions about our personality that are out there.

One of the things we said is that for believers, character is the same. It is a baseline that must not change. But when it comes to personality, personality can differ for many reasons. Sometimes people conflate the issues. A person may be talking about personality, but then the danger is that when they start using language like temperament, they are now touching character.

Disposition can be part of what distinguishes people. Some people are more mildly disposed. Jacob was called a mild man. Esau was not a mild man. He was a hunter, more adventurous. Jacob wanted to be inside; Esau wanted to be out in the field. Those are dispositions. But the moment you say temperament in a way that excuses anger or other works of the flesh, you are no longer dealing with personality; you are dealing with character.

That is why we must be careful about the things we say about ourselves from our background: "Me, I am just like this. Me, I am just like that." Sometimes we are touching character issues when we say those things. Some of them can be personality issues. You may say, "I like to think," or, "I can be quiet." Fine. But when it is a character issue, what you should be saying about yourself is what the word of God says about you.

If you say, "I am always very suspicious," that is not a good thing to be saying. Why are you suspicious? That is not good character in Christ. The same with being judgmental. You can slip into acting as though suspicion and harsh judgment are just your style, when really they are things that need correction.

We must be able to make the necessary distinctions so that we are not tossed to and fro.

Ephesians 4:15 says that, speaking the truth in love, we should grow up into him, Christ, in all things. All things includes the various aspects of our lives where we are supposed to model Christ. Without question, relationships are a major part of that, particularly because of what follows right after Ephesians 4:11-16. Relationship forms the bulkiest part of that instruction. From Ephesians 5:22 to 6:9 Paul is speaking about relationships. He speaks about other issues too: material matters, different character issues, the things of the Spirit, the preaching of the gospel, ministry, spiritual purpose. But of all of them, the bulkiest part was about relationships.

Therefore, the perfecting of the saints is going to include the perfecting of the saints in their relationships, to fulfill God's purpose.

## Relationships Are Critical to Fulfilling Purpose

Why does God want us perfected in relationships? To fulfill his purpose. Look at Paul. Imagine Paul was not friendly and Paul did not have friends. Look how Paul had friends. If he did not have friends, I seriously question whether things would have happened the way they did. If he was obnoxious, just doing his own thing, that would have worked against purpose.

I do not think it is a good trait in life to be obnoxious. A person may have a tendency to privacy, but being obnoxious is contrary to scripture's instruction to be hospitable.

What about the Lord Jesus? He also needed relationships. Where would he have been buried if Joseph of Arimathea did not make his tomb available? If he had not been properly buried, what would have been the testimony of the empty tomb? Relationships mattered. You need friends, people that will empower purpose.

Jesus did have friends because he was friendly. Scripture could say about him that he was a friend of publicans and sinners. For them to say that, he was clearly friendly. This is important for us to see. Relationships are part of fulfilling purpose, and so God is perfecting us in relationships.

## God Must Train Us in Relationships, Not the World

There is a danger in not being trained by God in any area. If you are not perfected as a saint in relationship, and you are perfected by the world in relationship, you will not know how to discern. Philippians 1 is key here.

Paul says in Philippians 1:9, "This I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all discernment." The issue is that it is knowledge that gives discernment. Discernment means that you are able to perceive what applies here and what applies there. Your love abounds more and more in knowledge and in all discernment.

That means knowledge brings the expression of our love, but it does so with discernment. You approve the things that are excellent, what is right, what should be. You separate issues.

Philippians is a good place to see this because Philippians has serious relationship concerns. Paul loved those people and they loved him. Yet there were issues among them. Philippians 4 says, "I implore Euodia and I implore Syntyche to be of the same mind in the Lord." There were divisions, and people were now forming camps around it. Paul says, in effect, stop camping around this or that side. Help these women, who labored with me in the gospel.

Conflict is part of the faith. It is part of the conversation of friendship. You cannot avoid conflict in close relationships. But you have to know what kind of conflict you are dealing with.

## Friction and Collusion

There are two kinds of conflict. There is friction, and there is collusion.

Conflict is unavoidable in any relationship with real proximity. There is no way you can have relationship with proximity without having some friction. But friction is not the same as collusion.

If it is friction, all you need to do is grease it, and then it becomes smooth. But collusion is when there is a jam, and now you need repair. That is what many people experience and then call normal in relationships. They say, "You cannot escape conflict in relationships," but what they are often calling normal is collusion.

That is when terrible words have been spoken, things have been said that cannot be taken back, and serious damage has been done. Even when there is forgiveness, and as believers we must forgive, the mark of that thing having happened is still there. That is why collusion is best avoided.

This is one more reason relationship was a big issue for Paul. Talking to his disciples, teaching them, and training them to be perfected in relationships was key for him. He was talking to husbands and wives because he had several of them in the churches, and he did not want situations that would affect God's purpose for their lives and for them as a whole.

So there is a critical fact here: the purpose of God is affected by how we handle relationships. God's purpose is at stake. Therefore, it is important that we are perfected as saints in relationship.

## Our Role Is Friendship

In the first part of this teaching we saw the relationships we have in the faith of Christ. Then we saw our role in those relationships. Our role in the relationships of the faith of Christ is that of friendship.

We further divided that into friendliness with all and friendship with some, the some being committed friendships by agreement.

In this second part of the teaching, we are explaining the practice of that friendliness and that friendship. We are looking at the practice of being a friend, which means someone who is friendly and who is a committed friend to some.

The reason we have to do that is because Ephesians 4 points out that the implication of the perfecting in knowledge is our walk. The word walk, `peripateo`, means to take definite, deliberate steps in a path. In this teaching we added that we saw it used alongside the idea of pattern and imitation. That means it carries exactness. You are doing the exact same thing. You are following the type. You are copying the pattern.

There is no room for, "This is how I want to do my own." No. This is exactly how it is to be done. These are the exact steps. This is what to do in this situation, and this is what to do in that situation.

## Walking as Exact Imitation

When scripture says walk, it is as though you are seeing where Christ walked and placing your feet exactly there. That is how the Spirit works. This is how Jesus walked. That is what you are to do.

It is not a bare "What would Jesus do?" kind of Christianity in the sense of external copying without life. Even though it is imitation, it is born of our nature. It is a faith-walk. It is what he would do as that which I now do because of who I am in him.

If we affirm our nature without the steps, it will lead to frustration. It is my nature, yes, but these are also the exact steps.

So we have to go back and look at the words friendship and friendliness and see the expressions that come from them, where they were used, so that we can start to discern what the steps look like.

## Affection and Cultivated Bond

In the Old Testament we saw two major words. One has to do with affection, and the other has to do with cultivating, pasturing, shepherding, associating. In the New Testament, those two streams come together in the language around `phileo` and related words.

The point is that friendship is about affection and cultivated affection. It is about developing affection.

This is where we must distinguish `phileo` from `agape`. `Agape` is love by principle, by duty, by properness. It is what ought to be. I ought to love people. I ought to be kind to people. I ought to love good and hate evil. That is right, and that is necessary.

But `phileo` is not about that. `Phileo` is about fondness. It is about affection. It is about process and experience. It is about being experientially affectionate toward people.

The character of our `agape` will show up there, yes, but what is really going on in `phileo` is cultivated affection. So when we say our role in relationship is friendship, it is not only that we should dutifully love people, though we should. It is also that we are to create a bond of affection with people.

You can be kind to a brother, not speak ill of him, think the best of him, and still not have developed any bond of affection with him. But God expects more than mere principled non-offense. In relationships he wants you to develop a bond of affection.

## Jesus as a Friend of Men

When the word `philos` is used of Jesus, notice what the text shows us. Matthew 11:19 says, "The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'Look, a glutton and a winebibber, a friend of tax collectors and sinners.'"

Notice that he was eating and drinking with people. That is shared activity in shared pleasure. It was an expression of friendship. They called him to the house, and when they started serving food he did not reject it. He engaged in shared pleasure with them. People will know that kind of thing. It is a place of bonding. It is a place of showing affection.

Luke 7 says one of the Pharisees asked him to eat with him, and he went to the Pharisee's house and sat down to eat. Jesus was being friendly. He was not obnoxious at all. He was very friendly.

That is important. Some believers have cultivated a picture of spirituality that is stiff and inaccessible. But Jesus was friendly.

## Rejoicing With Others and Supporting Their Joy

Luke 15 shows another expression. The man calls together his friends and neighbors and says, "Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep which was lost." Friends rejoice with people. That is part of friendship. There is support in celebration. There is moral support. You rejoice with the person.

John 3 gives another beautiful example. John the Baptist says, "He who has the bride is the bridegroom, but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom's voice. Therefore this joy of mine is fulfilled."

John uses friendship to describe his attitude toward Christ's increase. His point is that in the other person's accomplishment is your own joy. If it is your friend, it cannot be competition. You are there to support and help the person's purpose.

That is friendship. "What do you want to do? This is what you want to do? I have your back. I am going to do everything to ensure that it works out." That is a friend.

Jonathan is a strong example of this. He understood purpose. He knew God had called David. He said, in effect, "You will be king, and I will be next to you." He accepted the purpose of God in David's life.

This is why jealousy and competition in relationships are so destructive. Friendship does not seek to circumvent the other person's place. If I am for someone, I know the person's goals and I want the person to achieve them. If I know the role God wants the person to play, I want the person to shine in that role. I do not want to take the person's place.

## Agreement, Trust, and Shared Purpose

This is part of why agreement matters in relationships. Many people have problems in life because they do not have agreement. If you are going to walk in friendship, there must be agreement, clarity, and commitment.

That applies even more strongly in marriage. There should be agreement about money, agreement about responsibilities, agreement about the way life is going to be handled. When people do not have agreement, they are setting themselves up for trouble.

But the deeper point here is the heart of friendship. If I am for someone, I am truly for that person. I know the person's goals. If this is what the person wants to do in God, I want it to happen. That is not hypocrisy. That is friendship.

## Laying Down Your Life and Revealing Your Heart

John 15:13 says, "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends." There is self-giving for the benefit of the friend.

Then Jesus says, "You are my friends if you do whatever I command you. No longer do I call you servants, for a servant does not know what his master is doing." This brings out another expression of friendship: revelation. Disclosure. Sharing what is hidden.

In friendship there is disclosure of exclusive information. Depending on the relationship and the level of trust, there are things you reveal because friendship includes openness. It is not friendship to say, "That is just how I am. I mind my business. We are just here to do our duty." No. In the relationships where friendship is the role, there will be disclosure.

This does not mean there is no discernment. There is discernment. But the point stands: friendship includes revelation.

Third John 14 says, "I hope to see you shortly, and we shall speak face to face. Peace to you. Our friends greet you. Greet the friends by name." There again you see another expression: greeting.

## Greeting as a Real Expression of Friendship

Greeting is basic, but it is not trivial. In friendship there will be greetings. And not just greeting as people who met once and are done. There is regular checking up. Catching up. Maintaining cordiality.

Some people say, "I do not really like texting or checking on people." But this is not about what you feel like. There is your nature, and there is the step. This is one of the steps. You act on it.

That is why scripture is very loud about greeting. It keeps saying, greet one another, greet the brethren, greet the saints by name. Greeting is part of the practice of friendliness and friendship.

## Affectionate Love for God

John 5 says, "The Father loves the Son, and shows him all things that he himself does." That word there is one of the `phileo` words. Notice the revelation again: the Father loves the Son and shows him all things. Part of friendship is revelation.

John 21:15 also shows shared purpose. Jesus says to Peter, "Do you love me? Feed my lambs." It starts as Christ's purpose, but now Peter is being drawn into shared purpose. "If you love me, help my purpose. Feed them."

This is important too: there is such a thing as affectionate love for God. Sometimes scripture uses `agape`, but there are places where the language of `phileo` is used for the Father, the Son, and our relationship with them. That means there is a cultivated affection for God.

There is such a thing as not only believing the right things about God, but liking him, loving him affectionately, knowing him and finding him altogether right and good. With all the hearing of the word and studying of God's word, should you not love God? There is a way you start to know him and you like him. He is utterly likable.

## Rebuke, Chastening, and the Work of Friendship

Revelation 3:19 says, "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten. Therefore be zealous and repent." This is another expression of friendship.

Part of `phileo`, part of cultivated affection, is that you want to train, to help, to mold. You want the person to become better. You notice something and say, "This is not good. This is something you need to look at and correct." Why? Because there is a bond of affection.

Of course there must be balance here. People have free will. You cannot live the person's life for the person. But where there is friendship, there will be genuine desire for the person's growth and correction.

## Brotherly Love in the Church

Now let us come to the direct words used for these things. One of the key words is `philadelphia`, brotherly love. It is fondness for brothers, those of the same womb. In the church that means those of the same spiritual birth.

Romans 12:10 says, "Be kindly affectionate to one another with brotherly love." First Thessalonians 4:9 says, "Concerning brotherly love you have no need that I should write to you, for you yourselves are taught by God to love one another." Hebrews 13:1 says, "Let brotherly love continue." First Peter 1:22 speaks of sincere brotherly love. Second Peter 1:7 speaks of adding brotherly kindness and then love.

God does not just want us to relate with agape. He wants us to have `phileo`. He wants us to have affectionate bond. He wants us to have brotherly love.

That means if I come to church, finish the meeting, and just go home without cultivating any bond of affection, that is not `philadelphia`. The believer is supposed to cultivate affection in the local church.

You should not have anybody in church with whom you have no affectionate bond at all. You are supposed to cultivate affection with people. You are not looking for random things to say. There is what the Bible expects of you. There is discernment in what to discuss and how to know people, but the point is that you are supposed to develop a brotherly bond.

How do you not know what your brother is doing? Is that normal? No. Brothers are supposed to know each other. Believers from different places are to relate like people from the same womb, people brought up together.

This is why Paul could say they were taught by God about brotherly love. The Spirit of God in us bears witness that these are our people. This is our family. So we are invested in knowing them, asking how things are going, and relating accordingly.

## Affection in Marriage and Family

Another word is `philandros`, used in Titus 2:4. It means to be fond of one's husband, to be affectionate as a wife. Titus says the older women should admonish the younger women to love their husbands. The word there is not merely "dutifully do right by your husband." It is the language of cultivated affection.

So in marriage, our role is not merely to be functional. It is not baby-making, seed-contributing, or duty-performing. Your role is to be a friend. That is how purpose is going to be fulfilled in marriage.

That is also how you deal with conflict as friction rather than allowing it become collusion. And it is not only marriage. In all friendships, do not let things get to the point of collusion. There will be friction, but friction is different from collusion. Friction you grease. Collusion you now need repair.

That means you should have discussed beforehand how you handle conflict and how you resolve it.

Titus 2:4 also uses another word for loving children. God wants bond and affection there too.

When we talk about parent and child, the high point of that relationship is friendship, but parenting itself is a responsibility first. Your job is not to start by trying to be your child's friend in the sentimental sense. Your job is to do the responsibility of parenting. When you do that responsibility well, the effect should be friendship.

The child comes into the world not knowing. Foolishness is in the heart of a child, and scripture says the rod drives it out. That is not about brutality; it is about teaching, instruction, correction, persuasion. You are driving out ignorance. The child is not going to know if you do not teach him. You have to instruct, persuade, and correct.

If you do your responsibility well, then you end up being friends. But if you refuse responsibility because you just want to be your child's friend, you may lose both responsibility and friendship.

There is a time the direct formative role expires. Then what remains is counsel, prayer, words of faith, trust, and presence. But in the early years you must do the job.

## Kindness, Hospitality, and Material Honor

Another word is `philanthropia`, fondness toward mankind. Acts 28:2 says the natives showed Paul and the others unusual kindness. They kindled a fire, welcomed them because of the rain and the cold, and gave material help.

This teaches that friendship and friendliness include honor by material resources. If this person is going through something, how can I make it easier? How can I make it better? That is part of friendship.

Titus 3:4 says, "When the kindness and the love of God our Savior toward man appeared..." God's saving action was an expression of his `philanthropia`. He washed, he saved, he gave his Spirit. He gave. That is part of how bonds of affection are expressed.

It is not only for people who have much. Even little things count. Thoughtfulness counts. Giving something small counts. The point is the heart of honoring the person.

Acts 27:3 says Julius treated Paul kindly. That is friendliness toward a human being. This includes strangers and ordinary human interactions. It means you treat people kindly, not coldly.

## Hospitality to Strangers

Then we have the words for hospitality to strangers. Romans 12:13 speaks of giving to hospitality. Hebrews 13:2 says, "Do not forget to entertain strangers, for by so doing some have unwittingly entertained angels." First Timothy 3:2 says a bishop must be hospitable. Titus 1:8 says the same. First Peter 4:9 says, "Be hospitable to one another without grumbling."

Christians are supposed to be fond of strangers. We are supposed to cultivate that posture. A leader especially cannot say, "I do not do well with people." That is not something you excuse. If the call of God on our lives includes leadership in any sense, then we do not have the right to refuse people.

Be hospitable without grumbling. Host people without burdened resentment. If something longer-term needs arrangement, make arrangement. But do not let hospitality become bitterness. Do it purely.

This also shows again that relationships affect God's purpose. In entertaining strangers, some entertained angels. Ministry can come through hospitality.

## Affectionate Greeting and the Holy Kiss

Another cluster of words has to do with the kiss, an affectionate greeting. Luke 7:45 shows Jesus saying, "You gave me no kiss." In that context the issue is not romance but affectionate welcome.

Then scripture explicitly says believers should greet one another with a holy kiss. Romans 16:16. First Corinthians 16:20. Second Corinthians 13:12. First Thessalonians 5:26 says, "Greet all the brethren with a holy kiss." First Peter 5:14 says, "Greet one another with a kiss of love."

The point for us is not the exact cultural form of the gesture but the substance: affectionate greeting. There should be warmth, open affection, and holy expression of welcome among believers.

And notice First Thessalonians 5:26: greet all the brethren. Not some. All. This is not a small matter. It means you must not have people in church toward whom you are cold, absent, and disconnected.

## Love for God, Not Attachment to Pleasure or Status

There is also the word for being fond of God, and it is contrasted with being lovers of pleasure. Second Timothy 3 says men will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, and lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.

The problem is not that pleasure exists. Food can be pleasurable. Rest can be pleasurable. The issue is attachment. What are you attached to? What are you fundamentally committed to? The lover of God is attached to God and therefore to purpose. Life is about God, and God's purpose gives shape to everything else.

There is also a warning against love of strife and love of being first. Luke 22 shows the disciples disputing which of them should be considered the greatest. That tendency can still show up among believers. We must be careful.

If you frame life around being the best, the greatest, the first, you can slip into rivalry. Better to believe that all of us are supposed to grow into the maturity of Christ. If God gives someone leadership, let that person think as first among equals, not as inherently special in a way that others can never share in.

Otherwise, comparison and rivalry will destroy peace.

## A Friendly Mind

Another expression is being friendly of mind. Acts 28:7 says Publius received them and entertained them courteously for three days. First Peter 3:8 says, "Finally, all of you be of one mind, having compassion for one another; love as brothers, be tenderhearted, be courteous."

That means the mind itself is friendly. The disposition is courteous, kind, affectionate. It is not enough that you do a thing outwardly; the heart and frame of mind must be friendly.

## Seven Expressions of Friendliness and Friendship

As we conclude, we have seen several expressions of friendliness and friendship that we are going to build on.

The first is greeting and salutation.

The second is conversation.

The third is revelation or disclosure of exclusive information.

The fourth is training: teaching, rebuking, chastening, helping the person grow.

The fifth is shared activity in shared purpose and pleasure.

The sixth is support and help for the person's vision and purpose.

The seventh is honor in material giving and supply of resources.

These are not accidental things. These are deliberate steps involved in the practice of relationship in the faith of Christ, in the practice of friendliness and friendship.

That is what we are going to keep seeing: the deliberate steps involved in engaging all these things in our relationships, so that in all regards of our relationships we can walk in a way that fulfills God's purpose.
