# Relationships in the Faith of Christ (5)

We are looking at relationships in the faith of Christ. We need to move forward fast and effectively, but we also need to get the point very clearly.

In Ephesians 4, Jesus gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ. Then Paul says that we should no longer be children, tossed to and fro, but that speaking the truth in love we may grow up into Christ in all things.

What we have said from this teaching is that Paul is showing us the reason for the church. The church is for the saints to be perfected. That is because of God’s purpose on our lives. The purpose of God on our lives requires this process, this undertaking, this training, this building, across all areas of our lives, so that we can walk in that which God has called us to.

Knowledge is not the end game. Many believers are satisfied with knowing: “I know the word. I know what the word says.” But that is not the end. You can know the word and still not be doing the word, not acting on the word, not taking steps. The training of the church makes me able to fulfill purpose, but it is my walk in that training, my walk in the knowledge I receive, that makes me fulfill God’s purpose for my life.

## Walking Means Taking Definite Steps

When Paul goes on in Ephesians 4:17 and begins to talk about walking, he is very deliberate. That word “walk” means to take steps, definite steps. So if I say I am walking in relationships the way God wants me to, it means I am taking definite steps in relationships the way I am supposed to.

You can judge whether a believer is doing God’s word in any area of life by the steps that person is taking. You can judge whether the person is walking as the Lord desires from the steps that person is taking. Your steps show whether you are really walking in God’s purpose and plan for your life.

The knowledge we are taught should produce conviction, and that conviction should produce steps. The Bible is very loud, especially in Ephesians, about relationships. It is loud about husband and wife, about secular relationships, about parental relationships. So this is not an area in which the believer can afford to be ignorant. We are supposed to know the steps we are to take.

The first thing we saw in this series is the reality of relationships in the faith of Christ. We have relationships in Christ. But we then zoomed in on our role in those relationships.

## Do Not Define Yourself by Worldly Labels

Sometimes people carry baggage into relationships. They carry all sorts of ideas and ideologies about themselves into how they live and how they relate. We must be careful with that. There can be tendencies, yes, but our character is not supposed to be defined by the world, by our background, or by personality labels. Our character is supposed to be defined by God’s word.

For every believer, our character is relational. Can a believer have introverted tendencies? Yes. Can a believer have extroverted tendencies? Yes. But that is not the issue. The issue is not whether you are introverted or extroverted. The issue is whether you are Christian. Your work is not to be introverted or extroverted. Your work is to be Christian. Your character is to be Christian.

So stop rehearsing in your heart, “That is just how I am. That is just me. That is what I do.” Why not repeat God’s word to yourself? Why not say, “This is who I am in Christ”? That is what you are supposed to be expressing.

Many times we limit the impact of what God wants to do in us and through us by failing to walk properly in relationships. How much influence you can be for the Lord in your class, your residence, your family, your workplace, or any environment at all can be hindered by failing to walk properly in relationships.

If the Bible is so loud about the walk, and the largest part of the walk is the walk in relationships, then if you are failing in relationships you are surely failing in a major part of the Christian life.

## Our Role Is Friendliness and Friendship

Our role in relationships is friendliness and friendship. Friend to all, friendship with some. That is very important.

We took time in the last sessions to look at what friendliness and friendship mean. In the Old Testament usage we saw ideas like cultivating, shepherding, and affection. We saw fondness, attachment, warm kindness. That means the believer is one who cultivates affection. The believer is affectionate toward people.

There is a way you feel toward your natural family: “These are my people.” In Christ there is supposed to be that same sense of warmth and fondness toward the saints, not necessarily first in the content of your conversation, but in your attitude. Good morning. How are you doing? There is a sense of affection, warmth, and fondness that should mark the believer’s character.

If you struggle with holding back, you have to break that. You cannot hide behind personality language. Again, your assignment is not to be introverted or extroverted. Your assignment is to be Christian. You enter a car, you greet people. You are in a place, you show warmth. You are not trying to be strange. You are expressing the character of Christ.

So let me ask plainly: are you friendly? Are you an affectionate person? Are people afraid to greet you, or are you easy to approach? Do not say, “That is how nobody understands me.” No. You go to people, you talk to people, you share conversation with people. This is family. There is a motive for it.

And much of the problem is pride. Some people do not initiate conversations because they are proud. They say, “I just do not know how to do it.” Often that is pride hiding. Friendliness and friendship require initiation. You go and say, “How are you? How is everything?” You learn to begin.

## Expressions of Friendliness

We looked at the expressions of friendliness and friendship. The first is greetings and salutations. You greet people. You send a message just to greet them. You call just to check on them. “I hope your health is okay. I just wanted to say hello.” That is friendliness.

The second is conversations of familiarity, what we called observatory information. That is the kind of general conversation by which people begin to know one another.

The third is disclosure, revelatory information. That is when the relationship has moved further and there is now the sharing of more personal matters.

Then there is building one another, even through teaching, training, support, recommendation, and standing for one another. There is also shared activity in shared purpose or even shared pleasure. You can say, “Let us go and eat somewhere.” You can pray together. You can spend time together. There is also honor, where you speak well of the person, express respect, admiration, and value.

These are all real expressions of relationship. They are not fake. They are not superficial performance. They are how affection is expressed.

## The Kind of Information You Share Matters

One major thing we looked at was the kind of information we share in our conversations. This is very important because it helps us know what we can discuss with different people and how relationships are formed properly.

We said there are categories of information: spiritual information, general or personal information, and the distinction between observatory and revelatory information. So there are things you can discuss with almost anybody. There is natural general information. There is also spiritual observatory information, like preaching the gospel: Jesus died and rose again.

But there are things you do not tell just anybody. You do not make disclosures to strangers. You do not start revealing personal things to people you have not known. Relationships take time. You have to be patient and you have to know people.

So we spoke about a kind of progression in relationship: strangers, acquaintances, secular relationships, siblings and parents, saints, local church members, core friends, spouse, leaders. Different relationships permit different kinds of information and different kinds of access.

You must be able to distinguish what kind of information can be shared at what level. If I am talking with a stranger, I can be affectionate and friendly, but I am not going to begin disclosing personal revelatory information. That is foolishness. There is no pathway for that yet.

Even when we spoke about spouse, the point was that people should be careful not to manipulate with sentimental statements. Your spouse is your spouse, and what you owe each other as spouse is real and sacred. But friendship still has its own nature. There can be people with whom there are particular areas of counsel and purpose-sharing that are different. So we must be responsible about relationship categories and about confidential information.

## Practical Steps in the Walk of Relationships

We wrapped up that section by asking: what are the definite steps we take in this walk?

The first is daily greetings and salutations toward all. Every day, greet people. Create opportunities for affection.

The second is friendliness at every church meeting toward local church members. Every church meeting, express friendliness. Strengthen bonds of affection. We are supposed to have bonds of affection in church so that we can be a blessing to one another and also receive blessing from one another.

The third is friendliness every workday toward secular relationships. Be affectionate toward people at work, in class, in your environment, so that you can influence them. Let people meet the believer’s character in you.

The fourth is weekly building of core friendships. You must intentionally build friendship every week. Not just general interaction, but actual friendship. There must also be refreshing of general relationships like family and siblings.

The fifth is regular aligning and realigning with leadership. Let leadership know what is going on with you, where you are, how you are doing, what you are looking to do, so they can better help you in the purpose of God.

If you do well in relationships, you will do well in many things. If you do not play your role in the relationships God has brought into your life, you can hinder purpose both for yourself and for others.

## Friendship Must Be Chosen Carefully

Now we began to focus on friendship itself, because many believers say they have friends, but they do not actually know what friendship is.

One of the major places where Scripture addresses friendship is Proverbs. There we see the wisdom of God concerning how friendship is formed, how it is engaged, and how it is restored.

We said we can speak about friendship in three broad ways: forming friendship, engaging friendship, and restoring friendship.

The first text we considered was Proverbs 12:26: the righteous carefully chooses his friend. Friendship is not a careless matter. “Friend” is not a loose word in the Bible. You do not just call anybody your friend. The righteous chooses his friend carefully.

That means friendship is serious business. There is careful selection. There is careful evaluation. It is not pressure. It is not sentiment. It is not exploitation. It is not manipulation.

That also means that wicked people do not form friendship the right way. They seduce people into relationship. They use gifts, wealth, flattery, advantage, or other means to draw people in. But the righteous does not do that. The righteous goes through the proper process.

We also saw that a man of many friends can come to destruction, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother. Not everybody around you is your friend. A friend loves at all times. Friendship is not merely having plenty of people around you.

## The Sweetness of a Friend Is the Counsel of the Soul

Proverbs 27:9 says that oil and incense rejoice the heart, and the sweetness of one’s friend comes from the counsel of the soul.

That is very important. What is sweet about friendship is not gifts, not presence alone, not superficial words. It is the counsel of the soul. It is the advice, the plans, the direction, the inward contribution of the person.

And counsel is for purpose. Proverbs 24 connects wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and counsel with building the house and waging war. So counsel is connected to fulfilling purpose.

That means the reason for friendship in God’s word is purpose. There is friendliness for fellowship and communion among ourselves, but friendship is deeper. Friendship is for purpose.

## Friendship Is Formed by Shared Purpose

So how is friendship formed? Friendship is formed by revealing purpose to one another and committing to the same. You can say it is formed through shared interests, shared values, shared purpose, and then commitment to one another’s fulfillment.

There are people who are friendly with you. That is good. But friendship goes further. Friendship reaches the point where this person knows what you are trying to do and is committed to it, and you know what that person is trying to do and are committed to it.

That is why you must initiate friendship. Do not sit down expecting friendship to somehow happen to you. Jesus formed friendship with people. He took initiative. If you want strong friendships in church, you will have to form them.

John 15 helps here. Jesus said, “You are my friends if you do whatever I command you. No longer do I call you servants, for the servant does not know what his master is doing; but I have called you friends, for all things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you.” Then he said, “You did not choose me, but I chose you.”

So friendship involves revelation and purpose. Jesus revealed the Father’s things to them. He brought them into what he was doing. In that same way, friendship in the faith of Christ is not just smiling together, dancing together, or hanging around together. It is shared values, shared purpose, and mutual commitment.

Abraham is called the friend of God because there was a specific purpose, a real alignment. Friendship is not vague sentiment. It is specific.

The journey of friendship is from showing affection, to sharing interests, to sharing values, to sharing purpose, to committing to fulfill one another’s purpose.

## Friendship Is Engaged by Truthfulness

How do we engage friendship? By truthfulness.

Friendship is not flattery. Friendship is not telling people what they want to hear while they go on destroying themselves. If I am your friend and something is wrong, I must be able to tell you the truth. Truthfulness is how friendship is engaged and maintained.

So when we say truthful, we mean truthful in word, truthful in attitude, truthful indeed. The highest form of loyalty in friendship is truthfulness. Sometimes truth can be soft, sometimes it can be mild, sometimes it can be sharp, but it is still truth.

If someone is going off in values, if someone is becoming ineffective in purpose, friendship tells the truth about it. Friendship is committed to the person’s fulfillment, not to mere emotional comfort.

That is why false comfort is not friendship. Consoling people away from truth is not friendship. Pretending all is well when all is not well is not friendship. Betrayal often begins with a lack of truthful character.

## Friendship Must Share the Same Values

You cannot build friendship where the values are absent. Humility matters. Honesty matters. Integrity matters. Loyalty matters. If those things are not there, then what are you building on?

Someone who is proud will become competitive and envious. Someone who is manipulative or exploitative is not choosing friendship righteously. That is why the righteous chooses carefully.

Many believers are merely friendly with people and then call that friendship. No. Being affectionate is good, but friendship is not just smiling together. Friendship is not just convenience. Friendship is not just closeness of atmosphere. Friendship is shared values and shared purpose.

If you have purpose, you will desire friends who can drive you in that purpose. In class, in your workplace, in church, you should have friends in this sense. People who know what you are trying to do and are committed to seeing it done.

## Restoring Friendship and Resolving Friction

Then there is the matter of restoring friendship. In friendship there will sometimes be friction, conflict, words, attitudes, actions, and misunderstandings. Real friends will want to resolve that friction and bring about harmony.

Here it helps to notice the difference between Peter and Judas. Judas had become selfish and dishonest. The values had broken down. The purpose was betrayed. But with Peter, there was failure under pressure, yet the values and purpose still remained. Jesus reached out to restore Peter.

So when shared values and shared purpose remain, friendship can be restored. There should be a reaching out, a checking, a resolving, a bringing back of harmony. That is part of friendship too.

But if what is operating is selfish ambition, fleshly wisdom, or demonic wisdom, then that is another matter entirely. Friendship in Christ is not built on self-seeking. It is built on shared values, shared purpose, and truthful commitment.

## Build Real Friendships in the Faith of Christ

So let us be clear. Every Christian is to be friendly toward all. But every Christian should also move on to build friendships properly.

At your workplace, be friendly every day. In church, be friendly every meeting. Every week, build your core friendships. Every month, align with leadership. But in all of this, know what friendship really is.

Do you have friends in the secular who know what you are trying to do and are there for it? Do you have friends in the church who know your purpose, who know what you are trying to do in God, and who are committed to it? Are you that kind of friend to others?

We must not settle for superficiality. We must not confuse friendliness with friendship. Friendliness is real and necessary, but friendship is deeper. Friendship is the counsel of the soul. Friendship is shared values. Friendship is shared purpose. Friendship is commitment to each other’s fulfillment. Friendship is truthfulness.

The righteous carefully chooses his friend. So be friendly to all, but choose friendship carefully. Build it intentionally. Engage it truthfully. Restore it when needed. And let all of it serve the purpose of God in your life.

Jesus, you are my Master and my Lord. I submit unto your word. Because I am part of your body, everything I do must be in accordance with your word. May God cause us to walk in relationships as he desires, to be friendly toward all, to carefully choose friendships, and to be strengthened by them for the fulfillment of his purpose in our lives, in the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.
