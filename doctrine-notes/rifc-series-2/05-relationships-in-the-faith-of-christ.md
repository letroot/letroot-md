I'm so glad to have the church together to receive God's word, light, truth, and the revelation of Christ and his work in the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.

## The Church Perfects the Saints to Walk

Ephesians 4:11 says Jesus gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, and the pastors and teachers for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ; that we should no longer be children, tossed to and fro and carried about, but speaking the truth in love, may grow up in all things into Christ.

From this text in this teaching, we have said that Paul is telling us that the reason for the church is for the saints to be perfected. That is because of God's purpose on our lives. That implies that the purpose of God on our lives requires this process. The purpose of God on our lives requires this undertaking, where we must be perfected. The word is *katartismos*.

That perfecting, which is via knowledge, is to bring us to a perfect knowledge. It is to bring us to a maturity and growth that will cause our walk. That is what we are focused on in this particular part of Relationships in the Faith of Christ. We are very particular about the walk, as we said from the things we saw in the first part of the teaching.

When I'm in church, there is that recognition I must have: I'm in church because of God's purpose on my life. All of my life requires this training, this building across all areas of my life, to cause me to walk in that which God has called me to.

So we see that with that knowledge of the word, there is a call to walk. Sometimes people are just satisfied with knowing. We as believers can be satisfied with knowing: "I know the word. I know the word. I know what the word says." But you are not doing the word. You are not acting on the word. You are not taking steps.

In Ephesians 4:17, right after he speaks about this work of the church, he talks about the walk we are supposed to have, the steps we are supposed to take. Those are the steps that now make us fulfill that purpose. The training of the church makes me able to fulfill purpose, but it is my walk by that training, my walk in the knowledge that I receive in that training, that makes me fulfill God's purpose for my life.

Verse 17 says much about that walk: "This I say, therefore, and testify in the Lord, that you should no longer walk as the rest of the Gentiles walk." Paul is quite loud about walking. That word "walk" means to take steps. When you say, "I'm walking," you are saying, "I'm taking steps." It means to take definite steps.

So if I say I'm walking in relationships the way God wants me to, I'm taking definite steps in relationships the way I'm supposed to. We can judge a believer who is doing God's word relative to relationships, or relative to any other area of life, by the steps that the person is taking. Is this person walking as the Lord desires? You can judge from the steps the person is taking. Your steps will show if you are really walking in God's purpose and plan for your life.

When Paul says the work of the church is to perfect us to walk, to take steps, it means that the knowledge taught should make our conviction. We are taught the knowledge about the steps we have to take. There are different areas of our lives, and one of them is relationships.

The Bible is quite loud in Ephesians particularly on relationships and different kinds of relationships. It is loud about the relationship of husband and wife. It is loud about secular relationships. It is loud about parental relationships. So these are not different kinds of relationships that the believer can be ignorant about. I need to know the steps I am supposed to take. As believers, we are supposed to know and see that.

The first thing we saw in Relationships in the Faith of Christ was the reality of relationships that we have in Christ. But we zoomed in closer on our role. This teaching is to be acted on, because some of us just hear the word but don't act on it. If we are hearing God's word and we are not seeing steps that reflect what God's word is teaching, then we are failing and falling short of what the church is supposed to be doing in our lives. There are steps you are supposed to take. There is a way you are now going to be found doing things, a way you interact with people.

## Christian Character, Not Personality Labels

Sometimes people like to carry baggage, all sorts of ideologies about themselves, into the different things they do and into relationships. We warned about that in the earlier sessions. Of course, there can be things we prefer, but we made a difference between tendencies and definitions of our character. Our character is based on God's word.

For every believer, our character is relational. That means we relate well. Can we be introverted relational people? Yes. That is not the issue. You must be careful not to take your identity from the world, from things that are just your background and your own personal tendencies and have no bearing on God's word.

People have tendencies, but the question is: what do you embody? Is it the character of the Spirit? We must be careful as believers that we are not caught up in the world. We should have personality expressions that differ one from another, but we should be more concerned about this: what is my character, and am I expressing it?

Like we said also about faith, do not keep rehearsing to yourself in your heart, "I am like this. I am like that. This is who I am. This is what I am. That's just me. That's just what I do." Why not be repeating God's word to yourself? "This is who I am in Christ." That is what you are supposed to be expressing and supposed to be doing: who you are in Christ.

That is significant in God because many times we limit the impact of what God wants to do through us by failing to walk in his word as we are supposed to. What he wants to do through us in the lives of others, and what others are supposed to do to us, can be hindered by failing to walk properly in relationships in the faith of Christ.

How much of an influence can you be for the Lord in his purpose in your secular environment, in class, where you live, in your residence, within your family? Rather than being that influence, you can become a believer defined after whatever personality type you are claiming. That is a challenge we face today. We are faced with believers who are not particular about relationships based on purpose as they are supposed to be.

We cannot be light about relationships. If the Bible is loud about the walk, and the largest part of the walk is the walk in relationships, there is no way relationships do not affect much of our lives. You see that relationships are spoken about throughout the Christian life. If you are failing in relationships, you are surely failing in a major part of the Christian life.

That is something you need to check properly: how am I taking these steps? Who are the people I am now friendly with? Or should we even start with: am I a friendly person now, based on what I have learned in God's word? Am I being friendly with people? Am I affectionate towards people? Am I an affectionate person?

Are people afraid to greet you? When they greet you, do they meet affection? Am I a friend? We saw that there are relationships in the faith of Christ, but there is a role we play. Our role in relationships is that of friendliness and friendship. We saw friendliness and friendship. The question was: am I friendly? Am I practicing friendliness and friendship? Friend to all, friendship with some. Our role in relationships is friendliness and friendship. That is quite important.

Many people say, "This person is my friend," but they cannot tell you what that means according to the word of God. Who are your friends? Many don't even have friends. They don't know what they are doing, what it means that someone is their friend, or how to engage in friendship as they are supposed to.

Have you heard God's word and said, "How have I been able to know more people in church? How have I been able to be more friendly with people in church, to know them, understand what they are going through, help them, relate with them, and give them that warmth?"

Much of the hindrance is pride. Many of these things are pride. You cannot start a conversation because everything about friendship and friendliness requires initiation. You have to initiate it. You go in and say, "How are you?" Some say, "I just don't know how to do it." Often, you are proud. You are hiding under all sorts of things. No. You go to people, you talk to people, and you share conversation with people.

There is a heart and a motive for it. This is family. There is a way you are with your family. There is a way you relate. We are supposed to be changing our hearts and our attitudes. Your role in the relationship is friendliness and friendship.

Some people say they are shy in public, but the fact that a tendency has been present for a long time does not mean it is to define the believer's response. You change your heart and attitude by the word. The question is not whether a certain behavior feels natural to you. The question is whether it expresses the Christian character you have received.

You may enter a place and think, "Nobody knows me here." That does not remove the assignment. Greet people. Speak warmly. You are not making personal disclosures or forcing closeness. You are simply refusing to withhold the affection that should characterize you. There is a difference between affection and inappropriate familiarity, and the teaching is not asking you to confuse them.

When people greet you, they should not be afraid of what they will meet. They should meet kindness. You should not have a persona that makes everybody wonder whether they are permitted to speak to you. If all you say is, "People don't understand me," you have not yet asked whether you are making yourself available to relate in the character of Christ.

This matters for purpose. Through ordinary greetings, conversations, and warmth, doors can open for influence. In a class, workplace, residence, or family, the person who has experienced your Christian affection can take your words seriously when an opportunity comes to speak of Christ or to help. But if you have consistently held people at a distance and called that your personality, you may have closed a door that your walk was meant to open.

## Cultivating Affection

We took time in the last couple of sessions to look at what friendliness and friendship mean. We saw those words in the Old Testament text. One is to cultivate, to shepherd; the other is affection, to have affection for someone or another, that sense of affinity.

There is a way you feel toward your natural family: "These are my people." We are expected to have that toward people who were once strangers, not first in the content of our conversation but in our attitude. "Good morning. How are you?" There is that sense of affection, warmth, and fondness. That is the believer's character. He is one who cultivates affection. He is affectionate towards people.

If you struggle with that kind of thing, you have to break that habit of holding back. In this case it may seem as though we are speaking mostly to people with introverted tendencies, but when we speak about the excesses of extroverted tendencies, we will speak to that too. You have to be careful. You can have introverted or extroverted tendencies, but your work is to be Christian. Your assignment is not to be introverted or extroverted. Your assignment is to be Christian. Your character is to be Christian.

I see a stranger. I enter a car while traveling and say, "Good afternoon, everyone. I hope your day is going well." There is a sense of affection in the greeting. "How are you doing? How is everything?" That sense of affection starts with salutation and greeting.

We said friendliness is to cultivate affection, fondness, attachment, and warm kindness with people. Sometimes people have only certain places where they do those things. They do it here but not there; sometimes in church but not in class. No, that is how you are. You are friendly with all.

What does that do? It often opens doors in the purpose of God in your interactions with people in different ways. We are to be friendly. That is our role, and we are seeing what that friendliness means.

Your general mood and attitude should be affectionate. Your persona is affectionate. You radiate it. Your general mood and persona are affectionate. It isn't only something you switch on in one place and switch off elsewhere. It is a Christian character.

## Seven Expressions of Friendliness and Friendship

The next thing we looked at was the expressions of friendliness and friendship. We saw seven things, and I am going to recap them.

The first is greetings and salutations. You text people just to greet them. "Hi, I just wanted to check up on you. I hope your health is okay." You call just to greet them. You do it like a Christian across the board. That is our affection.

You may have just finished a Christian meeting and you greet people: "Is everything okay? Don't forget us in the kingdom of God." There may be a joke or two here and there. That is the beginning of the conversation. That is friendliness before we get to friendship.

The second is conversations of familiarity. You can put in brackets, "observatory information." These are the conversations by which familiarity develops. They begin with things you can both observe and discuss without demanding personal access.

The third thing is disclosure. You can put in brackets, "revelatory information." We will get to that because we are going on to friendship. We focused on friendliness a lot in the first sessions; now we want to ask, what is friendship? What does it mean that someone is my friend? Who is your friend?

Some people are friendly with everybody but cannot say who their core friends are. They cannot develop friendship. Sometimes they have to be convinced to form friendship. Why don't you have friendships that you have formed in the church? Don't only expect people to form friendship with you. You should be the one forming friendship.

Jesus formed friendship with people. He formed friendship with them for their own good. It was not only that people were looking for him; he formed friendship with people so that his influence came to them. They followed him, but they did not first initiate friendship with him.

This is how, in the body of Christ, leaders and ministers can enter into friendship at a level with others. You should not think that influence means people must only come looking for you. That can be an effect, but you should be the one initiating friendship. You go to the right people and say, "I just want to share something with you." Your tone is an affectionate tone. "Good day. Sorry to bother you, but I just want to show you something I'm sure you want to hear." It is an affectionate attitude. That is the beginning.

In one case you initiate with the gospel. In another place you begin with conversations about familiar things. You begin talking about different things, and from there you are influencing the person. That is why your general mood and attitude must be affectionate.

Don't wait for every friendship or every opportunity for influence to come around to you. Jesus did not merely stand at a distance until people created the relationship for him. He went to people. He called them. He made known what he had received. In the same way, a believer may walk up to someone and begin with an affectionate tone: "I want to share something with you." The initiation itself is part of the walk.

Sometimes the first day is only a conversation about familiar things. You talk about what both of you can see and understand. Another time the conversation goes further. From there you begin to know the person, and there can be an opportunity to influence the person with the gospel. You do not have to force all the stages into one meeting. Relationship takes time, and friendliness gives it a proper beginning.

If you are waiting for people to search for you before you relate to them, you are making their initiative the condition for your obedience. That is not what Jesus showed. You can be the one who forms the relationship for the other person's good. You are not exploiting the person or looking for what you can take. You are creating a path by which affection, truth, and help can come to the person.

This is also why the affectionate tone matters. You can have the right words and still carry a cold or superior attitude. But the believer's words, attitude, and purpose should agree. "Good afternoon. How are you doing? How is everything?" It may appear small, but it is one of the definite steps by which Christian character becomes visible.

The fourth expression is building one another. You can put, "via teaching, training, and so on." That is building one another. It can include supporting a person, helping them, or writing a recommendation. "I wrote a recommendation for you. I can vouch for you." That is what a friend can do.

The fifth is shared activity in shared purpose or pleasure. There are examples in scripture. The woman called her friends, "Come and rejoice with me." That is shared activity in shared pleasure. You can also have shared activity in prayer. Or you can say, "Let's go and have fun together. Let's go and eat somewhere." Take your friend out to eat. Spiritual friends can go and eat somewhere. You can pray together too.

You should already have a personal, private spiritual life, and that should be fine before you come together. You should not behave as though a person completes you in that sense. There should be confidence that this person genuinely loves the Lord and the things of God and can stand alone. When you come together there is support and help for the other person's vision, and there is also shared purpose.

The sixth is support and help for the other person's vision. This goes beyond merely sharing an activity. It means you are committed to helping that person carry out what they are called and trying to do.

Then there is honor in all relationships, and especially honor generally. Why not speak about the person? "I see you like this. I admire this about you. I respect this about you." You can write those things. You can look at them, reflect, and say, "This is what I see in this person." There is respect, admiration, and value.

So the expressions include greetings and salutations; conversations of familiarity; disclosures; building through teaching, training, recommendation, and support; shared activity in shared purpose or pleasure; support for the other person's vision; and honor. These expressions cultivate affection and take relationships forward appropriately.

## Categories of Information in Conversation

We also saw the kind of information we have in our conversations. This was a very major point. There are three classes or categories: spiritual information; general or personal information; and observatory versus revelatory information. Within those categories there are different kinds of information.

This is key because it helps answer, "How do I know what to discuss with different people?" Based on the kind of information I share in conversations, I can distinguish a stranger, an acquaintance, a secular relationship, siblings and parents, saints, local church members, core friends, a spouse, and leaders.

We divided relationships based on the kind of information that we can share with people. There is natural information, perhaps about the economy, politics, or things around us. That is natural, general information that you can discuss with anybody. There is also spiritual, general, observatory information. You can discuss that with someone while preaching the gospel: Jesus died and rose again. That is the gospel.

There are things about a local church body that are personal and some that are not. Some are observatory, such as the fact that you attend a local church. But there are some things that are personal and are not shared with just anybody. You must be able to distinguish what information you can share with whom.

For example, if I am talking with a stranger, I will not tell the person personal information unless I want to move toward acquaintance and there is a proper pathway. We can talk about Nigeria, the economy, a car, a business, or something in the place. That is general natural conversation, and there will still be some reservation.

But I won't tell the person natural, personal, revelatory information: "I'm planning to do this. I want to do that. This is what is happening to me." That is foolishness. Is general conversation part of being affectionate? Yes. But you don't make disclosures to a stranger. There is no relationship for that information yet.

That is life, and that is how all relationships begin. That is why relationships take time. You have to be patient. You usually try to know people first. You must be able to make those distinctions.

People get into trouble when they think affection requires disclosure. Affection does not mean telling everybody everything. You can be warm with a stranger and still keep personal revelatory information. You can preach the gospel to a stranger because the gospel is spiritual, general, observatory information: Christ died and rose. You can discuss what is happening in the country or around you because those matters are generally available. But plans, private struggles, confidences, and personal direction belong to another category.

If a stranger asks about something ordinary, you can answer and continue the conversation. Perhaps you speak about the place, work, a business, transportation, or the economy. That conversation can move the person from stranger to acquaintance over time. Yet you do not begin saying, "This is what I am planning. This is what is happening inside my home. This is the private matter I am facing." There is no basis for that disclosure.

The distinction protects both affection and wisdom. Without it, some people choose coldness because they fear oversharing, while others overshare because they think it proves openness. God's wisdom permits you to be affectionate without being careless. It permits relationships to grow through a proper process.

As relationships progress, the information can progress. An acquaintance has more familiarity than a stranger. A secular relationship may know aspects of your work and shared environment. Family relationships carry natural access and responsibility. Saints share spiritual identity. Local church members share a definite church life. Core friends have reached shared values, purpose, and confidence. Leaders need information by which they can help lead you. Each level has a real nature.

At the lowest level are strangers, then acquaintances, then secular relationships, then siblings and parents, then saints. Sometimes someone might not tell a sibling something because of the dynamics of that family, but in the way you esteem relationships, siblings and parents have a special place. If they are also saints, you take that into account. Saints are esteemed as saints.

Then there are local church members, core friends, spouse, and leaders. Sometimes you might share more with core friends than with a spouse because your friendship with a spouse may run in a natural way. Perhaps the person got married before being born again. He or she will never disrespect the spouse, but may not share as much information in some conversations with the spouse as with another person.

Even if you married a saint, there may be people with whom you already have friendships that run deeper in particular areas than the friendship with your spouse. People must understand that the slogan, "Your spouse must be your best friend," is not a Bible statement. Your spouse is your spouse. There is a friendship in being spouses, and what you have as spouses must be cherished and honored.

But it becomes manipulation to say, "We are spouses, so you must have no area of friendship with anyone deeper than with me." No. What we owe each other as spouses is real and sacred, but there might be people who are friends in some particular things more than your spouse. They may be your friends in one area of life, and you may be able to come back and discuss those things together with your spouse. Some other things you may not be able to disclose because they were given in confidence.

There are things you cannot say to your spouse even in the natural, not just the spiritual. A lawyer may have confidential information shared by a client and cannot go home to tell anybody else. A doctor has a fiduciary responsibility. You are irresponsible if you take confidential information and say, "But my spouse and I are one, so I can disclose it." That is not Christian. This is a matter of our lives.

I put spouse at that level because it may happen that your spouse is the person with whom you share most, and that is not bad. But it does not have to be forced. If you are spouses, be spouses in the sacred way that you are spouses. Don't corrupt it and don't manipulate it into something else. It does not hamper the relationship. The relationship is what it is.

## Definite Relational Steps

We wrapped up by asking, what are the steps we take in our walk? The first step is daily greetings and salutations toward all men. Every day you greet and salute people. Create an opportunity for affection. Greet the person. That is your heart: daily greetings and salutations toward all.

The second is friendliness at every church meeting toward local church members. I am putting the regularity first: daily greetings toward all; every church meeting, friendliness toward local church members. At least express it to different people. Perhaps it has been a while since you spoke with someone. You are trying to create and strengthen the bonds we are supposed to have.

We are supposed to have bonds of affection among everybody in church so that I can be a blessing to them and they can be a blessing to me. Sometimes you simply relate with someone, then the next day something happens and the solution comes through that person. The bond of affection allows you to be a blessing to the person and allows the person to be a blessing to you.

It is very bad when God brings relationships into your life and you don't play the role you are supposed to play there. You hinder his purpose there. If you are not friendly and you do not develop friendship in the relationships he brings into your life, you are going to miss out on roles those people are supposed to fulfill.

The third is friendliness every workday toward secular relationships. Every workday, express affection toward those secular relationships so that you are influencing them. Let them take you seriously, not because you are merely a clown or because they say vaguely, "This person is a good person," but because they learn the believer's character from you. Be affectionate toward people in church and in your secular relationships.

Do you know that if you do well in relationships, you will do well in many areas? Some people simply do not do well because of how poorly they handle relationships. You have to learn it from the believer's character.

The fourth is weekly building of core friendships. This is very important. Every week you must build your friendships. Look at your week in terms of building someone. It is not merely about a spouse or a child, where you already have clear responsibilities. What about core friendships? You have to build your friendships.

Within weekly friendship, include weekly refreshing of general relationships such as family and siblings. Call your parents. Refresh those relationships. We often accept that they are just there, but those general relationships should be refreshed.

The fifth is aligning and realigning with leadership. Regularly say, "I want you to know this is where I am now. This is the progress. This is what is going on with me. This is what I'm looking to do. I know you are praying for me." As you are being led and as you are following in God's purpose and plan, let leadership know what is going on. That way it is easier for them to receive from God for you.

Some people keep pulling away from the relationship. Don't be flighty. Don't keep skipping. Align and realign with leadership.

These steps must be definite enough for you to know whether you are taking them. "I am friendly" cannot remain only an idea in your mind. Whom did you greet? At the church meeting, with whom did you deliberately strengthen affection? In the workplace, how did your attitude express Christ? Which core friendship did you build this week? When did you refresh a family relationship? Have you allowed leadership to know where you are?

At every church meeting, do not speak only with the same person and leave. Look around for someone with whom the bond needs to be strengthened. Perhaps you have not spoken for a while. Go and greet the person. Ask how the person is doing. This is not artificial networking. We are members of one body, and bonds of affection create pathways through which members bless one another.

At work or in class, every workday is an opportunity. You are not trying to entertain everyone. You are expressing the believer's character. The people around you should be able to say that they encounter warmth, integrity, and care in you. Then your influence is not an isolated speech; it is supported by a walk they have observed.

Weekly building of friendship is more deliberate than general greetings. It asks, "How am I contributing to this person's fulfillment? What counsel, help, recommendation, prayer, or shared activity is appropriate this week?" Friendship is built because somebody takes such steps. If no one initiates, the relationship remains at friendliness.

The same applies to leadership. Aligning is not a vague statement that you are following. Give truthful information about progress, direction, and difficulty. "This is where I am. This is what I am trying to do. This is what has changed." That information helps the relationship serve God's purpose.

So the steps are daily greetings and salutations toward all; every church meeting, friendliness toward local church members; every workday, friendliness toward secular relationships; weekly building of core friendships and refreshing general relationships; and regular aligning or realigning with leadership.

## Friendship in the Wisdom of Proverbs

We have spoken about friendliness. What about friendship? One of the things I am concerned about is that some people talk about friends but don't actually know what friendship is.

The major book where we can see a focus on friendship is Proverbs. Interestingly, its submissions come from the law and from the experiences and instructions that preceded it. The Lord spoke about friendship in the law, and Proverbs develops that teaching and instructs a son in how to approach friendship. We are seeing what the law of God and the truth of the word have to say about what friendship means in our world.

In the New Testament texts, the word may be used in experience without the subject being taught in the same detailed form. You can see the principles from Proverbs operating in New Testament experience, but if you want the teaching itself, you go to Proverbs. If you don't pay attention to the book's material on friendship, you might miss it. Proverbs takes the narratives, the law's submissions, and the law's instructions, puts them together, and gives us a proper presentation on this matter of friendship.

We need to know what friendship is. It is not healthy to look for one person on whom to lay down your entire life and everything about you while you have no real relationships. Even in the secular world people are friends. They work together for a while, build together, and meet a definition of friendship. Believers ought to know how to enter into friendship.

Proverbs identifies and instructs us on friendship. We can speak about it in three points: forming friendship, engaging friendship, and restoring friendship. Those are the three broad ways we can speak about friendship.

The first text we need to see is Proverbs 12:26 because one challenge is knowing who a friend is. You will see that the word "friend" is not a careless word. It is not a word that you use for anybody in the Bible. Some texts even talk about the problems that can come from having a multitude of loosely called friends.

Proverbs 12:26 says, "The righteous should choose his friends carefully, for the way of the wicked leads them astray." Another rendering carries the idea that the righteous searches out or carefully selects his friend, while the way of the wicked seduces them.

The righteous carefully chooses his friend. He is careful. He is friendly, but friendship is serious business. It involves a careful plan and process. It is not using people for exploitation. Don't manipulate friendship. Whoever does that is playing an exploitative or manipulative role.

The righteous pursues friendship with a disposition of sincerity. We cannot use superficial things to arrive at friendship. I must be careful about friendship. The wicked do not go through the proper process; they entice or seduce people into relationship.

Proverbs 18:24 speaks about a man of many friends coming to ruin, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother. A person who scatters himself among companions can be spoiled or destroyed. Be friendly, but know who a friend is. Not everybody around you is your friend.

Proverbs 17:17 says, "A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity." A friend loves at all times. That tells you something about the constancy involved.

Proverbs 19:4 says, "Wealth makes many friends, but the poor is separated from his friend." Wealth attracts many people. That text also helps us ask whether what is being called friendship is actually friendship. Some people draw people by wealth or advantage, but the righteous carefully chooses.

All these texts bring us to the point that friends are not merely everybody who comes around. Friendship has a path, and Proverbs will show us what leads to friendship itself.

When scripture warns about a man of many companions coming to ruin, it is not forbidding friendliness toward many people. We have already said, friend to all in the sense of affection, but friendship with some. The warning is against scattering the access, commitment, and claim of friendship without careful choice. A person can have many people around and still have no friend who sticks closer than a brother.

Likewise, wealth making many friends shows that attraction is not proof of friendship. People may come because of what is available, because of an advantage, or because they hope to receive something. When the advantage disappears, they separate. The righteous must therefore look beyond the number of people and examine the values, sincerity, and purpose operating in the relationship.

"A friend loves at all times" does not mean a friend approves everything at all times. Love is not flattery. The same Proverbs that speaks of constant love speaks of wounds from a friend being faithful. Constancy is a commitment to the person's true good and fulfillment. That is why truthfulness will later become central to engaging the friendship.

These scriptures also show why the word "friend" should not be used carelessly. It carries love, counsel, constancy, sharpening, and commitment. You can have companions, acquaintances, church members, colleagues, and people toward whom you are affectionate without pretending that every one of those relationships has reached friendship. Calling the relationship by its true name lets you honor what it is and build it properly toward what it may become.

## The Sweetness Is the Counsel of the Soul

Proverbs 27:9 says, "Ointment and perfume delight the heart, and the sweetness of a man's friend gives delight by hearty counsel." Another rendering says, "Oil and incense rejoice the heart; the sweetness of one's associate stems from the counsel of his soul."

The sweetness of one's friend comes from the counsel of the soul, the counsel that issues from his soul or his person. What is sweet about the friend? Is it the friend's gifts? No. Is it merely the person's presence? No. Is it great words? No. What is called sweet in friendship is counsel.

What do you need counsel for? Counsel means advice, deliberation, a plan, and direction. What is sweet about the friend is the advice, plan, and direction that the person's soul brings to you. That is the sweet thing in friendship.

Many people say they have friends, but they don't have friends in this sense. They don't even know what friendship is supposed to be. Someone becomes their friend without the counsel of the soul ever entering the relationship.

See what Proverbs 24 says about counsel. Verse 3 says, "Through wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established; by knowledge the rooms are filled with all precious and pleasant riches." Then: "A wise man is strong, yes, a man of knowledge increases strength; for by wise counsel you will wage your own war, and in a multitude of counselors there is safety."

Wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and counsel are for waging war or building the house. Counsel is for fulfilling purpose. That is why the counsel of the soul is central to friendship. It helps a person build what he is supposed to build and wage the war he is supposed to wage.

Who then is a friend? How do you form friendship? Friendship is formed by revealing purpose to one another and committing to the same. You can speak of sharing interests, values, and purpose, but it progresses to revealing your purpose and committing to fulfillment.

In a secular setting someone can be your secular friend, not just a person you greet. You have reached the point in conversation where you have shared your goals, what you want to do, and the person is committed to helping you get it done. My friend is committed to what I want to do.

Shared interests can happen at the level of being friendly. You progress from that point until you know this person is trustworthy to be a friend. You do not tell what you want to do to everybody. Some might discourage you. Some might confuse you. You don't reveal purpose carelessly. Remember, the righteous carefully chooses his friend.

Proverbs 27:17 says, "As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend." He gives his friend an edge for what he is supposed to be doing. That connects again to the counsel of his soul. It may be by the person's words, example, and entire contribution that he gets the other person to do what he is supposed to do.

Life is for purpose, and the reason for friendship in God's word and by God's truth is purpose. There is friendliness for fellowship or communion among ourselves. Friendship goes deeper. Friendship is for purpose.

You don't merely say, "I know this person. We have known each other for a long time. That's my friend." A friend knows the things that you are to do and is committed to them. Not everybody reaches that point.

Jonathan could say concerning David, in effect, "You will be king, and I'm committed to it. It must happen." That is friendship. He was committed to God's purpose for David. Imagine revealing your purpose to people who are competing with you. That is a problem. A friend is committed to you and to the purpose.

Why don't people in church have friends like that? They are waiting for the other people to initiate it. You must initiate. Jesus formed friendship with people. You need people with whom there is shared value and shared purpose.

At your workplace you should move from general talk and observatory conversation into personal friendship where shared values make it safe to reveal goals. The person becomes committed to seeing your purpose fulfilled. They will sit down and think with you. "How do we ensure this purpose is fulfilled?"

The people around Paul laid down their lives because Paul's purpose must be fulfilled. That is friendship. Someone with whom I share purpose, and I am committed to that person's fulfillment, is my friend.

## Friendship Is Formed Through Shared Purpose

Friendship is not formed simply because two people have danced together, smiled together, or stayed around one another. Those things can be friendliness, affection, and shared pleasure. Friendship is formed through a progression.

It begins with showing affection. From affection there is sharing of interests. As conversation develops, you discover values. From shared values there can be revelation of purpose. From shared purpose there is commitment to fulfilling each other's purpose.

If we don't have shared values, we cannot build this. In the workplace, you can have personal friendships with people whose values make it possible for you to reveal your goals. When that is a true friendship, the person is committed to seeing your purpose fulfilled. The person will think with you, counsel you, and stand with you until what must be done is done.

This is why friendship must be chosen carefully. You don't reveal your purpose to everyone. Some people are competitive. Some are envious. Some will discourage or confuse you. But once you know that someone shares the right values and is trustworthy, you can move further.

Friendship does not happen automatically because people are in the same church. If you want that kind of friendship in church, you have to form it and initiate it. There are people with whom you have had conversations and seen shared interests and shared values. Now reveal what you are trying to do in God. "This is what we must get done." Then commit to one another's work.

Jesus illustrates this in John 15. He said, "You are my friends if you do whatever I command you. No longer do I call you servants, for a servant does not know what his master is doing; but I have called you friends, for all things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you."

Why did he call them friends? He revealed to them what he was doing. "All things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you." He brought them into the revelation and purpose. Then he said, "You did not choose me, but I chose you." That takes us back to Proverbs 12:26. You choose your friends.

You can be grateful to a person who has done something for you. You can say, "Thank you. I'm really grateful, very grateful." But to be friends, do I know your interests? Do I know your values? Do I know your purpose? Am I committed to its fulfillment? Those are key questions.

Abraham is called God's friend because the relationship was specific. God revealed a specific purpose and Abraham aligned with it. Friendship was not vague sentiment. There was purpose.

We must know what to call friendship. Do you have friendships in the secular: people who know your purpose in your secular work and are helping you do what you are supposed to do, people who are out for your fulfillment? Do you have friendship in the church: people who know how you want to grow, how you want to be strong, how you want to serve in ministry, and who are committed to helping you get it done?

Who is that person? If the answer is nobody, it may be because you did not form it. You did not initiate it. From your conversations you may have seen shared interests and shared values. Move forward carefully. Reveal what must be done and say, "We must get this job done."

These are true friends. Friendship is not superficial convenience. It is not merely that somebody makes you feel good or gives you something. Get your values right and your focus right, and then there can be friendship.

## Engaging Friendship Through Truthfulness

Proverbs 29:5 says, "A man who flatters his neighbor spreads a net for his feet." A friend does not flatter. This takes us to engaging friendship. Friendship is engaged by one thing: truthfulness.

If you keep telling a friend, "Don't worry," when the person is taking a wrong path, that is not friendship. Telling someone what the person wants to hear is not friendship. Evil consolation is not friendship. The relationship is engaged by truth.

What is friendship? This person must fulfill purpose, and you are committed to that. You don't merely say, "You can do it. You are good. You are fine," while the person is destroying his or her life. You must be able to say the truth.

Friendship in this regard is being truthful. The big issue with Job's friends was that they were not truthful about what was actually happening. They had an opportunity to say truth, but they mixed their assumptions with what they knew about God. God addressed them. The point is not that a friend never challenges; the point is that friendship must be governed by truth.

Truthfulness is how we engage in friendship. Period. It is not flattery and it is not false comfort. The question is: what is the truth? What does the Bible say? Sometimes you may say, "I think this," but you should know the difference between your thought and the truth of the word.

Truth can be respectful. It can be kind. Betrayal often comes from not having a truthful character. People pretend as if they are all good and nice, but they are not. So when we say truthful, it means truthful in word, truthful in attitude, and truthful in deed.

Psalm 35 describes people who rewarded evil for good, to the sorrow of the psalmist's soul. He says that when they were sick, his clothing was sackcloth; he humbled himself with fasting. He behaved as though the person had been his friend or brother. Yet in adversity they rejoiced and gathered together; ungodly mockers gnashed at him with their teeth. That is the opposite of truthful friendship. Their outward posture and inward attitude did not agree.

Being truthful is how we engage friendship. Friendship means I am sharing purpose and I am committed to your purpose. Friendliness involves conversations for affection and the development of shared interests and values. As it progresses, I see, "These values are shared. I can trust this person." Then purpose is revealed.

You begin by showing affection. You may discover shared interests: "That is beautiful. That is nice." Then you discover values: "These are values by which I can walk with this person." You discover the person's purpose, and you commit to it. You are a friend.

Truthfulness is the highest form of loyalty in this friendship. Love can sometimes sound sweet. It can be mild and soft. Sometimes it can be harsh or sharp. But it must be truth. It doesn't have to be one tone all the time. The question is whether you are truly committed to the other person's fulfillment.

Again, Jesus said, "You are my friends if you do whatever I command you. No longer do I call you servants, for the servant does not know what his master is doing." He had revealed purpose to them. "All things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you." Then, "You did not choose me; I chose you." Proverbs says the righteous chooses friends carefully.

You can appreciate a person without pretending the person is yet your friend. To be friends, do I know your interests, values, and purpose? Am I committed to your fulfillment? You do not go to people who have no shared values and try to force friendship. It is not possible.

Abraham was God's friend because there was something specific. We must know what to call friendship. There should be people in the secular who know your purpose in the secular and are committed to helping you do what you are supposed to do. There should be people in the church who know what you are seeking to do in God and in ministry and are committed to it.

From conversation you see shared interest and shared values. Then you reveal purpose: "This is what we have to do. We must get this job done." True friends then review and pursue it together.

Friendship is maintained by being truthful. Be truthful to your purpose and truthful to your values. Be true to it. When someone is going off in values or becoming ineffective and going down in purpose, friendship says the truth about it. That is friendship.

Purposeful people often recognize others who are purposeful. When they see the same kind of purpose in a person, they stick together. But don't gather many people around you merely for entertainment and call all of them friends. Get real friends. Not so many, but real ones. This is serious business because it concerns your life's business.

Sometimes pursuing one direction means you will not be pleasing to many people. You have to say no. You cannot keep giving yourself in every direction until you have nothing left. Focus in the direction of purpose.

In class you should have friends. In the workplace you should have friends. How will you get better at your work? There should be friends who sharpen you. In church, don't merely make companions who dance together and smile together and then think you are friends. You may only be friendly and affectionate. What is the friendship for? Where is the counsel of the soul?

If you have purpose, you will desire friendship with people who will drive you toward it. You want to share with people who will help drive the work home. That is why being truthful is how friendship is maintained and engaged. You are truthful. You are true to your shared values and purpose.

## Shared Values and the Character of Friends

It is difficult to build friendship where there are no shared values. The character matters. Humility is at the core. Honesty, integrity, and loyalty matter. If those things are not there, what are you sharing with the person?

Someone who is proud will be competitive and envious. A proud person may appear close but turn purpose into a rivalry. That is why the righteous carefully chooses his friend. The shared values are what make it safe to reveal purpose and commit to one another.

Friendship is not built by flattery. It is not built by gifts, wealth, advantage, or pressure. It is not built through exploitation or manipulation. Those are the ways of the wicked described in Proverbs: they seduce people into relationship rather than going through the path of affection, shared interests, shared values, shared purpose, and commitment.

The righteous carefully picks out his friend. The way of the wicked seduces people. Here "wicked" does not only mean people visibly violent or killing others. It includes people who are manipulative and exploitative. They do not choose friendship through the proper process. They seduce people by gifts, wealth, flattery, or some other advantage.

The righteous takes people through the process of shared interest, shared values, and shared purpose. There is sincerity. That is how friendship is formed rightly.

## Restoring Friendship and Resolving Friction

Then we come to resolving conflict in friendship. When friends share values and purpose, they will want to resolve friction and conflict and bring about harmony in their friendship.

This takes us back to the first things we taught. Consider a man like Judas. He was stealing. He was not financially honest. He was betraying the Lord and going after selfish ends. At that point there were no longer shared values and shared purpose on which to maintain friendship.

But with Peter there were shared values and shared purpose. Peter failed under pressure. We must be very clear on the difference. The Bible does not change the standard. Peter had the same values and the same purpose, but he was under pressure and got carried away. When you look at him, the shared purpose remained. He was still a friend. Jesus reached out to him to bring about harmony and restoration.

In friendship there will be times when we need to restore harmony and resolve friction. Say something. Check it: "What happened?" Friction may arise through attitudes, words, or actions. With Peter, it was in the things he said and did under pressure. Friendship deals truthfully with those things.

If selfish ambition is driving the person, however, that is a different matter. Scripture warns us not to be self-seeking. James 3 calls bitter envy and self-seeking an earthly, sensual, demonic wisdom. Friendship in Christ cannot be built on worldly, demonic, fleshly wisdom. It is not built on self.

So the journey of friendship is the journey from showing affection, to sharing interests, to sharing values, to sharing purpose, and then committing to fulfilling each other's purpose. You move along that way because you believe in each other and your values are the same.

It begins with showing affection and sharing interests. Through conversation you see the person's ideology and values. There is a pathway. But you cannot skip the pathway. You cannot simply meet someone and immediately say, "I've got your back. We are friends," when you have not shared interests, values, or purpose. There is no pathway yet.

We must develop friendship with the saints. Jesus said, "You did not choose me, but I chose you." Initiate friendship as Jesus did. Don't settle for superficial convenience or simply what someone can give you. Get your values and focus right. On that foundation, frictions involving attitudes, words, and actions can be resolved.

Restoration is based on the values and purpose remaining. If those remain, you can reach out, speak truth, address what happened, and restore harmony. If they have been abandoned for selfish ambition, manipulation, or betrayal, then you must recognize that the basis of friendship itself has been attacked.

## Carefully Choose and Intentionally Build Friendship

The righteous carefully chooses his friend. The righteous carefully picks out his friend. But the way of the wicked seduces people. Wicked people entice others into relationship. They may do it by gifts, by wealth, or by any other means. They do not take people through the righteous process of shared interests, shared values, and shared purpose.

So do you have friends in the secular? Do you have friends in the spiritual? Do you know each other's purpose? Is there someone who knows your purpose, what you are trying to do, and the work through which you want to reach the world? What are you trying to do now, and who is there for that purpose?

A friend sees the purpose and says, "How can we do it?" A friend begins to encourage you in that purpose. A friend is not merely someone you talk with about general things. A friend gives the counsel of the soul. Many times, things move forward because you are walking in God's purpose of friendliness and friendship.

Every Christian is to be friendly toward all, but don't stop there. Move on to build friendship rightly. Every day, be friendly at your workplace. Every church meeting, be friendly toward local church members. Every week, build your core friendships. Every week, review what you are doing together and how you are progressing. Regularly align with your leadership.

Be friendly toward all, but do not call everyone a friend. Carefully choose friendship. Know the values. Know the purpose. Reveal purpose at the proper time. Commit to one another's fulfillment. Engage that friendship with truthfulness. When friction comes and the values and purpose remain, pursue restoration and harmony.

Jesus, you are my Master and my Lord. Lord Jesus, I submit unto your word. Because I am part of your body, everything I do must be in accordance with your word.

May God cause us to walk in affection toward all, guide us to carefully choose friendships, and cause us to be strengthened by them to fulfill his purpose for our lives, in the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.
