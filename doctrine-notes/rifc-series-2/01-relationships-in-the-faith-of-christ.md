# Relationships in the Faith of Christ (1)

Father, I am so glad that I am your child and a part of your family. In this season, gather with us in your mercy and let us receive of your word light, truth, and revelation of Christ and his work in all its fullness, and of his fullness. In the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.

## The Church Exists for the Perfecting of the Saints

Let's look back to Ephesians 4:11. We are looking at teaching relationships in the faith of Christ. The first part of this teaching was about twelve or thirteen sessions, and this second part should be about three or four thereabout. We are calling this the practice, the walk, of friendliness and friendship.

Let me say from the start that the intention of this teaching is not simply marital matters, though it may be useful in such endeavors. The inspiration of the teaching, the reason behind it, is something much more important and much clearer than that.

Paul says in Ephesians 4 that the Lord Jesus gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, and the pastor-teachers. He gave them for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ. Very importantly, there is just one reason why we have the leadership of the church, and that is the perfecting of the saints. The other two things he mentions, the work of the ministry and the edifying of the body of Christ, are applications, one leading to the other. The perfecting of the saints leads to the work of the ministry, and the work of the ministry leads to the edifying of the body of Christ.

So if someone asks, "What is the reason for the church?" there is that singular reason: the perfecting of the saints. That is where the bulk of our attention should be. Paul himself puts the bulk of his attention there in the verses that follow. When I am in church, my whole attention should be on that. I am there to be perfected as a saint.

## Perfecting Is First About Knowledge

From verse 13 Paul starts to expand on the issue of the perfecting of the saints. He says, "Till we all come to the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ." So the point we are being brought to is about knowledge. The point God is leading us up to is a point in knowledge. He calls it the unity of the faith. He calls it the knowledge of the Son of God. He calls it the perfect man and the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.

Very clearly, then, the level of growth expected of us in God's family is defined first by knowledge. The text is really about conduct too, but it is defined first by knowledge, because it is knowledge that is going to lead to the conduct. God is very big on the knowledge that we have. Do you have the knowledge of the word that he wants you to have? If I say that a brother or sister has developed to the measure of the stature of Christ, I am saying that the person has that knowledge of the faith.

And when it comes to Christian faith, knowledge is an end in itself. Of course, it is also a means to another end, to the works and expressions we are supposed to have, but knowledge is first an end in itself. You need to know. He wants you to know. There is no bypassing the knowing. There is no circumventing the knowing. There is no way around it. You have to know, and you are not to know partially, mostly, or almost completely. You are to know entirely. God is big on knowledge.

Then verse 14 says, "That we be no longer children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine." Notice that he has still not first said conduct. He has said knowledge. Knowledge defines your level of growth. When you know, you have a certain level of growth, and that makes you a child no longer tossed to and fro. The goal at that point is knowledge that brings stability.

That is why precision matters. Sometimes people think being careful with details is unnecessary, but you have to be precise. You have to be precise in the outline of the message, in the content of the message, and in the explanation, such that you are very sure you know what you know well. You are able to define things the way you are supposed to, and you are seeing things the way you are supposed to.

If we are not seeing things the way we are supposed to, and we do not know the way we are supposed to, then we will be influenced by other people on how to see and do things.

## Truth Over Against the World's Categories

We live in a funny world today where, rather than believers looking into God's word to find their identity, you see them looking at the world and at different things the world is telling them. There are all these personality labels and temperaments, and people begin to define themselves by them. Of course, there is room for individual personality, but we are talking about character. Character in the scripture, for the believer and by the Spirit, is the same. Character is the same.

Personality may differ. A person may have a passion for soccer, or some preference picked up from different influences. That can become part of what people call personality. But character for the believer in the scripture and by the Spirit is the same. We must separate issues. One psychologist or philosopher puts a title behind his name and tells you that you are this or that, and you start confusing yourself.

It is so bad today that believers sometimes begin to speak about certain issues of mental health and mental state in ways that are shaped more by what they have read than by truth. There is no question about the reality of mental unhealthiness, but many times people expose their minds to things they read, and then they begin to act them out. Nothing is without spiritual influence in this world. Demons get involved. Information is not neutral. So we must be careful.

Paul says we should be no longer children tossed to and fro by every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men and crafty deceit. Then he says, "Speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ." That means our concern is: what is the truth about this issue? And there is truth about all things. There is truth about different areas of our lives. We are to grow up into him in all things by hearing what the truth has to say about everything.

There is a way Christ is in the demonstration of the Spirit. There is a way Christ is in the walk of the Spirit. There is a way Christ is in his relationships. There is a way Christ is in his material pursuit. There is a way Christ is in the pursuit of his spiritual purpose. We are to grow into him in all things.

## Christ Died and Rose to Fulfill God's Eternal Purpose

Before we come to the issue of relationships directly, we should ask why the Lord Jesus is so particular about the perfecting of the saints. Ephesians 4:7-10 gives us something important. It says that grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift, and that when he ascended on high he led captivity captive and gave gifts to men. Then it explains that the one who ascended is the same one who first descended into the lower parts of the earth. The ascension it is talking about follows his descent. So it is speaking about Jesus' death and resurrection.

The Lord gave the apostles for the saints after he died and rose again. That means whatever it is that he is interested in must be related to why he died. Often when people ask, "Why did Jesus die?" the answer given is, "He died because of our sins." That is true, but it is incomplete. He died because of our sins in order to fulfill God's purpose. Sin is man's rejection of God's purpose, and Jesus died to bring us back to God's purpose.

Ephesians 3:10-11 is very important here. It says that what was accomplished in Christ was according to the eternal purpose. Notice that phrase: the eternal purpose accomplished in Christ Jesus. That means redemption fulfilled God's purpose. There is an eternal purpose that God has always had. He has not always had sin, so his purpose is not something created by sin. Before sin became a reality, God already had purpose.

So what was accomplished in Christ, and what has now brought forth the reality of the church, is God's eternal purpose. Therefore, the perfecting of the saints is for the saints to fulfill God's purpose. When the saints are perfected relative to their spiritual purpose, that serves God's purpose. When the saints are perfected in their material life, in diligence, thoroughness, order, and character, that also serves God's purpose.

The Bible may not teach you technical details of business, marketing, or whatever field you are in, but it teaches you the character that will make you learn, function, and be effective as a believer there. The same is true of the gifts of the Spirit. It is all for God's purpose.

## Relationships Must Serve God's Purpose

So when the word says that, speaking the truth in love, we will grow into him in all things, that also includes relationships. One area of life where God's word is going to teach and train us is our relationships, so that we can fulfill God's purpose.

We are not teaching marital relationships in this session, though the Bible teaches on that too. But if the Bible is teaching you about relationships, it is teaching you for the sake of God's purpose. There is nothing that will be taught in scripture in the perfecting of the saints that is outside that purpose.

Sometimes people get lost in different things. If they ask you, as a believer, why you want a marital relationship, the honest answer at one level may be the desire for sexual and emotional companionship. That is part of human wiring. But the reason cannot stop there. The reason for a marital relationship must be to fulfill God's purpose. You are going to be in that relationship to serve God's purpose there.

That principle applies generally. The moment you understand that all relationships are for God's purpose, it should affect your attitude around and about them.

## The Bible Is Loud About Relationships

One thing we definitely cannot miss as we look at God's word is that the Bible is loud about relationships. By the time you start looking through scripture and asking what areas of life it addresses, you will find character, material life, things of the Spirit, and many other areas. But you cannot miss the fact that relationship is a huge part of human existence and of God's design in his purpose and plan.

And the interesting fact is this: how much you understand about relationship in Christ affects how much of God's purpose and plan can flow through your life. Look through the scriptures and see how much God's purpose depended on people relating rightly. Think of the wide reach of the gospel across different locations, and then look at what kinds of relationships were needed across those places for that to happen.

The extent and reach of God's purpose through our lives can be limited by the fact that we are not engaging in relationships the way he wants us to. God may desire that something should happen, and it may not happen that way because people fail to do what they ought to do. Of course, because he is God, he will find another way and another person to get it done, but that does not remove our responsibility.

Think of Saul. God wanted the kingdom established in a certain way, and Saul did not continue in that path. The kingdom was taken from him. Think also of Jonathan and David. Jonathan was a good man, sincere and well aligned with David in many ways, but there came a point at which he should have seen that his father was in rebellion against Yahweh and should have left. Choices in relationship matter. God will not force anything on anybody. Everything has a lot to do with you.

That is why we must be careful and discerning. What is the Lord saying in his word about relationships in Christ? There are things that must be decisively corrected and changed.

## The Different Relationships in the Faith of Christ

One major area of the perfecting of the saints is the perfecting of the saints as touching relationships. Again, this is not about marital relationship in particular, though that is included in the broader subject. Let your mind be thinking fully. I am talking about relationships that you are supposed to develop, relationships with disciples, classmates, fellow believers, leaders, family members, and others, relationships that have far-reaching implications for God's purpose.

If you proceed through Ephesians 4 into Ephesians 5 and 6, you see that relationships become a major part of what Paul says we have to be perfected in. From Ephesians 5:22 all the way to 6:9 he is talking about relationships. He speaks about wives and husbands. He speaks about children and parents. He speaks about servants and masters. So relationships are a major area of life in which the believer is supposed to be perfected.

We have already looked at the different kinds of relationships the believer is supposed to have in the faith of Christ. There is the relationship with God. There is marital or spousal relationship. There is parental or parent-children relationship. There is sibling relationship. There is relationship with saints generally. There is local church relationship. There is relationship with leaders. There are secular relationships. There are acquaintances. There are strangers.

That distinction between saints generally and local church relationship is important. There is a bond you have with all saints, and there is a closer bond you have with those in your own local church. Those are the people God is working with you among in that local context. You should know your church and be rightly joined there.

In secular relationships, of course, you may have colleagues and leaders. You may be a citizen under civil leadership. You may be a student with teachers. You may be an employee with an employer, or an employer with employees. There is instruction in scripture about those kinds of relationships too. The believer must know how to show up there.

For example, as a citizen you have responsibility. Carry out your responsibility as a citizen. That includes participating rightly where you find yourself, making your contribution, and reflecting the order that should be. There is a way you act as a believer even in secular places.

## Friendliness Toward All and Friendship With Some

The second thing we saw in the previous teaching is what our role ought to be in these relationships. Our role is friendliness toward all and friendship with some. The believer is supposed to be friendly toward all, and the believer is supposed to be friends with some.

You need to listen carefully to that distinction. To be friendly toward all does not mean you have to be friends with all. In the Bible a distinction is made between being friendly and being a friend, even though in ordinary speech the words may overlap. I can call somebody my friend in the loose sense of someone with whom I am friendly. You might say, for example, "my Muslim friends" or "my Muslim family members," and what you mean is that there is friendliness there. But when we are talking about the practice of friendliness and friendship, we have to understand what it means more carefully.

As a Christian you must be friendly. You cannot simply be cold, harsh, and withdrawn as though that were acceptable. That is walking in the flesh. We are to be friendly toward all.

At the same time, there is a more committed friendship with some. Friendship in that stronger sense is an arrival by agreement and commitment. There comes a point where two people know, even if subtly, that they agree on certain values and purpose and are committed to one another on that basis. "I have your back. You have my back. We have developed a bond. We agree, and we are committed."

Agree on what? Agree on values. Agree on purpose. Students may become friends because they both care about doing well in class. They know what they agree on, and they are friends around that. But for believers, friendship in life has to be around what life is actually about. It has to be around purpose.

## The High Point of Relationship Is Friendship

We also said that the high point of all relationship is friendship. Look at how God wants to be with us. God wants to be our friend. Abraham is called the friend of God. God wanted a family, yes, but not merely a family in some cold structural sense. He wanted a family he is friends with.

In God's word, the ideal point of relationship is friendship. Even in relationships where there is structure and authority, the high point is friendship. Parent and child ought to be friends. Leaders and followers ought to be friends. The Lord Jesus said to his disciples, "No longer do I call you servants; I call you friends." That is the direction.

Now, in some relationships that high point expresses itself mainly as friendliness. With strangers, for example, the duty is friendliness. In secular relationships too, much of the practical expression is friendliness. But when it comes to saints, local church members, and leaders, there are places where real friendship ought to be formed.

And this is why the believer must learn not to be phobic toward people. Scripture commends friendliness to strangers. There is a Christian posture toward strangers that is open, warm, and hospitable. Some people excuse a bad response to people by calling it personality, but often it is simply bad character. Personality may differ. Character is supposed to be the same. If you meet people and are habitually obnoxious, harsh, phobic, or closed off, that is not Christianity. That is not merely personality; that is something that must be corrected.

## Knowing the Words Is Not Enough

Now we come to an important issue. We have seen what the relationships are. We have seen our role in them. But knowing those details and knowing the words does not resolve the heart. Saying, "Relationship in Christ is friendliness and friendship," does not mean a person is actually doing that.

Every Christian teaching is for practice. The aim, the goal, the essence, the end point of Christian teaching is practice, what Paul would call walk. Knowledge is good, and thank God for knowledge, but we want knowledge unto walk. So the teaching of the walk must also happen.

That is why, after Ephesians 4:11-16, Paul moves into the language of walk. Verse 17 says, "This I say therefore, and testify in the Lord, that you should no longer walk as the rest of the Gentiles walk." Notice the "therefore." What follows from the perfecting of the saints is a walk. A walk changes things. A former way of living stops. Certain things cease.

He says you should no longer walk as the Gentiles walk, in the vanity of their minds, with their understanding darkened, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them and the hardness of their heart. Their walk comes from their mind. Do not walk like them as people who, because of unbelief, do not have the knowledge that should produce the right walk.

Then in Ephesians 5:2 he says, "Walk in love." In the book of Ephesians you cannot miss the emphasis on walk. The aim and goal of our nature and our understanding of God's word is that we walk.

So the real issue is not just whether we can define something, but whether we have the walk of what we have been taught. You can say the right words about character and Christ and still not have the walk. That is the problem. There must be a walk.

## Walk Means Taking Exact Steps

See how emphatic scripture is about this. Ephesians 2:2 says that in times past you walked according to this world. Your former nature was seen in your walk. Ephesians 2:10 says we are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them. Ephesians 4:1 says we should walk worthy of the calling with which we were called. Ephesians 5:8 says we were once darkness, but now we are light in the Lord, so we should walk as children of light. Ephesians 5:15 says we should walk carefully, not as unwise but as wise.

So there are exact steps that pertain to your calling, exact steps that pertain to light, exact steps that pertain to love, exact steps that pertain to wisdom. When scripture says walk, it is not talking vaguely. It means there are definite steps to take.

The way you spoke, the way you looked, the way you handled something, the way you responded to a person, these are steps. You can take a step in the Spirit or a step in the flesh. A believer who is warm, bright, cheerful, and open is taking a step of the Spirit. A believer who sinks into fleshly gloom, malice, spite, and harshness is taking the wrong step.

When we say walk in the Spirit, walk in the light, walk in Christ, or walk in love, we mean: take the steps that belong to that nature. There are definite steps. There is a path. You should know what to do from one point to another in a given situation.

That is why I keep stressing exactness. Walk exactly. Walk carefully. Understand what the will of the Lord is. Wise steps are steps in the will of God.

If, for example, a believer wants to form a godly marital relationship, there are steps. You do not just panic, wander into foolishness, or say, "My clock is running out, so anything goes." No. There are exact steps. You wake up and get serious with God's way. In every area there are steps.

The same applies to strangers, acquaintances, local church members, leaders, and saints generally. There is what believers do in each case.

## Christianity Is Imitation

Another key thought is that walking is imitation. Ephesians 5:1 says, "Be imitators of God," and then verse 2 says, "Walk in love, as Christ also has loved us and given himself for us." So walking in love means replicating exact steps. It means imitation.

How did Christ walk when he was here? We are to walk like that. Walking is replication. It is imitating. It is having exactness. You are not innovating morally. You are doing the same kind of thing.

That is why the believer is often instructed to imitate. Paul says, "Be imitators of me." Again he says, "Imitate me just as I also imitate Christ." The idea of replicating people in this sense is utterly scriptural. Christianity is imitation, of Christ and of those who faithfully follow him.

First Thessalonians says, "You became followers of us and of the Lord." Later in that same line of thought believers are said to imitate the churches of God. Hebrews says, "Do not become sluggish, but imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises." Hebrews 13 says, concerning those who have spoken the word of God to you, consider the outcome of their conduct and follow their faith. Scripture even says, "Beloved, do not imitate what is evil, but what is good."

So know whose steps to follow. When it comes to handling life, look at those who are following the Lord. Do not confuse yourself by setting worldly figures up as your pattern. When it comes to spiritual things, material things, conduct, and relationships, find those in Christ who are walking rightly and imitate them.

Sometimes people are struggling to be different when they should be striving to be the same. Personality may differ, and that is part of the beauty of life. People have different preferences, tastes, and modes of expression. But character must be the same. When we see bad character, we must not baptize it as different personality.

## Exact Steps in Relationships

So the Christian faith is about taking exact steps after your nature. There is an exact nature, and there are exact steps. In our relationships, then, if we know our nature but do not know our steps, we will be frustrated. We will say, "This is who I am in Christ; why am I not walking like that?" The answer may be that we have not learned the exact steps.

What does Christ do in relationships? How does he think? How does he handle people? What steps does he take? That is what we must see.

There are exact steps with strangers. There are exact steps with acquaintances. There are exact steps with local church members. There are exact steps with leaders. There are exact steps by which friendliness develops into friendship, and even exact steps that may lead from friendship, where appropriate, into spousal relationship. There are steps.

When scripture says to walk properly, to walk in love, to walk in wisdom, to walk by faith, to walk in the truth, or not to walk in craftiness, all of that means there are specific responses and specific patterns that accord with our nature in Christ.

For example, Romans says that if because of your food your brother is grieved, you are no longer walking in love. That means you took the wrong step. You may be right about the thing itself, but the way you acted toward your brother was wrong. The issue becomes the step.

Likewise, when scripture warns against craftiness, it is teaching that there are ways of maneuvering, manipulating, and handling matters that are not the steps of the Spirit. Believers are not to move that way.

And when scripture speaks of walking in the truth, it means that the truth in you is not only a message you know but a path you tread. The truth has steps.

That is why the call to walk is so loud across the New Testament. The believer is not only called to possess a nature but to take the steps of that nature. Scripture will show us those exact steps for different areas of life, and that includes our relationships.

## Toward the Practice of Friendliness and Friendship

So this is what we are doing in this teaching. We are looking at the exact steps of the practice of friendliness and friendship. We are asking: what are the exact steps a believer takes in relationships in the faith of Christ? What are the conversations we have? What do we do with strangers? What do we do with acquaintances? What do we do with local church members? What does friendship look like in practice? How do we move from mere acquaintance to purposeful bond in Christ?

The Christian faith is about exact steps. The truth is not vague. The Spirit is not vague. Light is not vague. Love is not vague. Christ is not vague. So our relationships are not supposed to be vague.

There is a loud call in scripture for the believer to walk, and that means there is a loud call for the believer to take exact steps. We have exact steps to take in our lives, and we have exact steps to take in our relationships. We shall begin to see what these exact steps are when it comes to our relationships as we consider what our relationships ought to be and how Christ himself handles them.

Say this: I take exact steps after my nature in Christ in all my relationships, and by so doing I fulfill God's purpose and plan for my life.

Lord, we pray for what you are working in us by the truth of your word. Let the truth that already abides in us be brought to our hearts and minds so that we walk as you desire we should. Reveal to us our steps to walk, that we may fulfill purpose. In the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.
