# Relationships in the Faith of Christ (4)

Praise God. We continue our teaching on relationships in the faith of Christ.

Our Father, I am so glad that I am your child and a part of your family. In this meeting, gathered with my siblings, I receive of your word light, truth, and revelation of Christ and his work in all its fullness, and of his fullness that is in me, in the name of our Lord Jesus. Amen.

Let's begin from Ephesians 4:11-16. Paul says that the Lord Jesus gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ; that we should no longer be children, tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine, but speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, who is the head, even Christ; from whom the whole body, joined and knit together by what every joint supplies, according to the effective working by which every part does its share, causes growth of the body for the edifying of itself in love.

## The Perfecting of the Saints in Relationships

From that text we have established certain facts in this teaching. First, the reason for the church is to be perfected in the knowledge of the Son of God. And God's desire for us to be perfected in the knowledge of the Son of God is because that is how we are going to fulfill the purpose for which Christ died and rose and established the church.

We also noticed that there are different areas of our lives that this perfecting pertains to. Verse 15 says that we are to grow up into Christ in all things. That means there are different areas of our lives where we need to grow, where we need to be perfected, where we need to become effective.

It is not impossible in a believer's life that he can be effective in one area and lacking in another. First Thessalonians 3 shows that. Paul says he is praying to see their face and perfect what is lacking in their faith. So there can be things lacking. There can be areas where a believer is not yet effective.

One major area of the believer's life where he cannot afford to be ineffective is in relationships. The opportunity to relate with people is often an orchestration of God for a man of purpose. If you do not make maximal use of the opportunities to relate with people, the relationships that cross your path, from strangers to acquaintances to relatives to siblings to parents and so on, you may miss out on the benefit and blessing they are supposed to be to you down the line.

When Paul talked about the growth that comes by knowledge in Ephesians 4, a major part of how he expressed that growth was in relationships. From Ephesians 5:22 through 6:9, he spoke about husbands and wives, parents and children, masters and servants. He spoke about other things too, but relationships took up most of his time. So the purpose of this teaching is to perfect the saints in relationships so as to fulfill purpose.

That means there must be that mindset in you: the reason I have to understand relationships the way God wants me to and engage them the way I ought to is to fulfill purpose. That grand sense of God's purpose that we are engaged in must affect the way we treat the relationships that we have.

## Friendship as the High Point of Every Relationship

Earlier in this teaching we saw the relationships we have in the faith of Christ. God expects us to have relationships. We also saw our role in those relationships. We can sum it up as friendliness and friendship, or the role of a friend.

The high point of every relationship is being a friend. There is what the relationship is in itself and there is the high point of it. You are a parent: there is the responsibility of parenting, but the high point of that relationship is being a friend. You are a child: there is the responsibility of being parented, but the high point is being a friend. Siblings: the point of relationship is being friends. In marriage too, the high point is friendship.

People can enter a relationship without the intention of friendship. Even in First Corinthians 7, Paul speaks of marriage in a way that shows someone can go and marry for reasons that are not friendship. But the high point expected by God is that they should be friends, that they should be friendly toward one another.

Now, in the Christian faith, knowledge is first an end in itself. You need to know. But then there is the second point: you need to walk. The word for walk is to take definite steps in a particular path. It is not enough to say, "What would Jesus do?" in a vague way. It is, "What would Jesus do? That is what I do." That means it is your nature, and you are replicating his steps by faith.

So the believer must always want to find out: what are my steps in every situation and circumstance toward different people? What are the steps I take? What are my definite steps in relationships?

## Strangers and the Christian Duty to Receive Them

Let us look at some of the relationships Scripture speaks about more closely, especially strangers and acquaintances.

The word for strangers is `xenos`. It means foreign, alien, novel, and by implication a guest. It can also be used with reference to a host, as the one entertaining the guest. Christians are not to be obnoxious toward strangers. Some believers are effectively xenophobic in attitude. They say, "I don't really do well with strangers." That is not a Christian attitude. In Christ, we are not obnoxious toward strangers.

Matthew 25 is very important here. Jesus says, "I was hungry and you gave me food. I was thirsty and you gave me drink. I was a stranger and you took me in." And again, "When did we see you a stranger and take you in?" Then he speaks to others and says, "I was a stranger and you did not take me in." That means people of faith are meant to have this expression, and those outside the faith typically will not have it.

It is of the man of faith in Christ, of the one with the Spirit of God, to receive strangers. We receive strangers. We relate well with them.

Third John 5 says, "Beloved, you do faithfully whatever you do for the brethren and for strangers, who have borne witness of your love before the church." That is Christian testimony: what you do for brethren and for strangers.

So when we speak of strangers, we are speaking of people who are new to you, alien to you. The instruction is not that every stranger instantly becomes your committed friend, but that you receive them. You are affectionate toward them. You do well to them. You treat them warmly. You cultivate affection toward them.

## Acquaintances, Relatives, and What Relationships Know

Now let us consider acquaintances. The idea there is of knowing by observation. Luke 2:44 says that Joseph and Mary sought Jesus among their relatives and acquaintances.

That text helps us see a distinction. There are acquaintances, and there are relatives. Relatives are biologically defined; acquaintances are socially defined. Someone can be your relative and still function mainly at an acquaintance level socially. A stranger is not biologically defined at all; it is socially defined. The person is simply new to you.

The word for relative, `suggeneis`, means a relative by blood or by kinship. Sometimes it can refer to countrymen or people of common stock, and sometimes to close family.

So we should not confuse biological nearness with actual relational depth. Most relatives, in ordinary practice, are simply acquaintances socially. Some relatives become close friends, but not all do. Scripture does not hide these natural distinctions. Paul could speak of his countrymen, his relatives, his kinsmen according to the flesh, and there was nothing wrong with that.

Luke 23:49 also says that all Christ's acquaintances stood at a distance watching these things. So Jesus had acquaintances. Jesus was huge on relationships. John 18 shows that one disciple was known to the high priest, and that relationship became useful in the purpose of God. Relationships matter in fulfilling purpose.

What distinguishes these relational levels is knowledge and information: what they know about you and what you know about them. Strangers do not know you and you do not know them. Acquaintances know some things about you and you know some things about them. Knowledge and information distinguish relationships in their levels.

## The Order of Relationships

If we are going to list relationships in terms of what they ought to know and what you ought to know about them, we can put them in an order.

First, strangers.

Second, acquaintances, with relatives often fitting here unless there is closer friendship.

Third, secular relationships.

Fourth, siblings.

Fifth, parents.

Sixth, saints, meaning fellow believers generally.

Seventh, local church members.

Eighth, core friends.

Ninth, spouse.

Tenth, leaders.

Then, of course, God above all.

As a rule, the closer the relationship, the more appropriate knowledge and information are involved. But even then there must be discernment. Not every kind of information belongs everywhere.

## The Expressions of Friendliness and Friendship

We identified seven major expressions of friendliness and friendship.

The first is greetings or salutations.

The second is conversations.

The third is revelation or disclosure.

The fourth is building or edifying one another.

The fifth is shared activity in shared purpose or pleasure.

The sixth is support and help in the other person's vision and pursuits.

The seventh is honor and material giving.

We want to work through these practically, beginning with the first two in more detail, because the life of friendship is built very much around them.

## Greeting as Christian Culture

Greetings and salutations are major. They apply to all relationships, from stranger upward. Do not enter a car, a bus, a house, a place, a room, any space, without greeting people. Greeting is major in the Bible.

The Greek verb is `aspazomai`. It carries the sense of drawing toward, welcoming, embracing. Greeting is not merely uttering a word. It is drawing the person close. It is welcoming. It is cordial. It is warm.

From the first use of the word, we see how important greeting is to the Lord. In Matthew 5:47 Jesus says, "If you greet your brethren only, what do you do more than others? Do not even the tax collectors do so?" That means you should be able to greet even the person who is against you. The person that spoke ill of you, the person that did evil to you, you should still be able to say affectionately, "How are you?"

Matthew 10 and Luke 10 show the same culture. When you enter a house, greet it. Speak peace upon that house. The point is not the exact formula but the affectionate spirit. Learn to greet warmly: "How are you? Good to hear from you. I hope everything is well." Learn to greet affectionately.

Luke 1 says Mary entered the house of Zacharias and greeted Elizabeth. Mark 9 says people ran to greet Jesus. Acts repeatedly shows Paul greeting believers wherever he went. He landed somewhere and greeted the church. He called disciples to himself and embraced them. He arrived elsewhere and greeted the brethren. He greeted the leaders in Jerusalem before he began to tell them things in detail.

That is why Paul had friends. He had a genuinely welcoming spirit. He always initiated relationship by greeting people warmly, by drawing them close.

Romans 16 is the home of greetings. Paul greets person after person by name: Prisca and Aquila, Mary, Andronicus and Junia, Amplias, Urbanus, Apelles, those of Aristobulus' household, Herodion, Tryphena, Tryphosa, Persis, Rufus, Philologus, Julia, Nereus and his sister, and many others. He recalls records. He remembers labor. He expresses affection. He calls one beloved. He mentions another's service. That is friendship. He knew them. He had connection with them. He wasn't just preaching at people and running off.

First Corinthians, Second Corinthians, Philippians, Colossians, First Thessalonians, Second Timothy, Titus, Philemon, Hebrews, First Peter, Second John, and Third John all show greeting as part of Christian life. "Greet one another with a holy kiss." "All the saints greet you." "Our friends greet you. Greet the friends by name." Greeting people is Christian culture.

So at first instance, greet people. After first instance, continue greeting them. Maintain it. Reach out. Say hello. Check on them. Greet with warmth, not cold routine. Sometimes that greeting itself becomes a channel of encouragement and care.

In the local church especially, embrace this culture. Greet people. Do not form cliques. Everybody is your brother and sister. You can have closer friends, but do not have camps. Do not say, "I prefer to greet this brother; I don't like to greet that sister." No. That is not Christian brotherliness.

## Conversation Makes Relationships Consistent

The second expression of friendship is conversation. Greetings are conversations themselves, but we separate this one because now we are talking about information.

All relationships are really about conversation. Starting with the first relationship, God and man, God spoke to man. He said, in effect, "I want you to be in my family. I want you to have my life." Fellowship itself begins with speech. Relationship becomes consistent by conversation.

Sometimes what makes friendship obvious is that one person feels, "How can I do this without telling him?" That is friendship. A friend greets you. A friend converses with you. It is not mere title or sentiment; it is expressed in actions.

Now, because conversations carry information, believers need discernment. You must know what to say to whom and when to say it.

## Three Classes of Information in Conversation

There are three classes by which information can be considered.

First: natural versus spiritual.

Second: general versus personal.

Third: observatory versus revelatory.

Natural information includes things like material pursuits, science, arts, commerce, natural philosophy, worldview, governance, economics, and social issues.

Spiritual information includes salvation, spiritual growth, doctrine, ministry, matters of the local church, leadings of the Spirit, visions, and revelations.

General information is information that is not personal to the individual.

Personal information is information that pertains directly to the individual.

Observatory information is what can be known by observation, what is manifest publicly or ordinarily knowable.

Revelatory information is disclosure. It is what has been entrusted, unveiled, or shared, not merely observed.

When you put those classes together, you get eight possible kinds of information:

1. Natural general observatory.
2. Natural personal observatory.
3. Spiritual general observatory.
4. Spiritual personal observatory.
5. Natural general revelatory.
6. Natural personal revelatory.
7. Spiritual general revelatory.
8. Spiritual personal revelatory.

You must be able to distinguish these by discernment.

## The Need for Discernment

Philippians 1:9 says, "And this I pray, that your love may abound still more and more in knowledge and in all discernment." The word there is `aisthesis`, perception, discernment, judgment. It is the ability to apprehend properly by the senses. It is grasping what a thing is.

Believers must not talk anyhow. Do not say, "Me, I just say anything to anybody." No. That is not wisdom. Even in your own home there are things not everybody needs to know. Life is not for gossiping. Life is for purpose. Everybody has his own purpose and should face it.

This applies in both natural and spiritual matters. If you mishandle revelatory information, people will avoid sharing things with you. That is because you do not understand friendship. You are filtering away trust.

Think of professions. A doctor learns things about people that are not to be spread around. A lawyer hears things entrusted in confidence. Someone working in an office or organization may be privy to information that is not for indiscriminate discussion. If you take such information and use it for gist, you are not acting as a believer and certainly not as a friend.

Not even leaders have business with certain kinds of natural confidential information that do not concern them. You must know where information belongs.

So discernment matters. Know what to say to who, who to say what to, and when to say it.

## What Strangers Should Hear

What do we discuss with strangers?

With strangers, the normal range is number 1 and number 3: natural general observatory information and spiritual general observatory information.

That means with strangers you can discuss things happening in the world, science, arts, governance, and ordinary public matters. You can also discuss salvation, spiritual growth, doctrine in general, the need for a local church, and the gospel. Those are public truths. They are not private disclosures.

If you want to move a stranger into acquaintance, then number 2 can begin to come in: natural personal observatory information. You begin to ask, "What do you do? Where do you stay?" That is how you begin to know one another naturally.

In some cases, number 4 may also come in based on spiritual general conversation: spiritual personal observatory information. For example, you may mention your own church in an ordinary, observable way, or explain how you practice something in order to help the person. But you are still not going into private revelations, leadings, visions, and deeply personal disclosures.

You do not enter a bus and begin to narrate your personal revelations and God's leading on your life to a stranger. That is spiritual personal revelatory information, and there are only a few categories of relationships where that belongs.

## What Acquaintances and Relatives Should Hear

With acquaintances and relatives, what applied to strangers still applies, but now number 2 is already in play because you know one another to some extent.

So you can discuss natural general observatory, natural personal observatory, spiritual general observatory, and at times spiritual personal observatory.

If an acquaintance is moving forward into friendship, then number 6 may begin to appear: natural personal revelatory information. You begin to trust the person with more detail. You might say, "I am actually planning this. What do you think about it?" It is no longer just observatory knowledge; now there is disclosure based on growing trust.

But you cannot go and tell your plans to just any acquaintance. It has to progress. There must be some alignment, some demonstrated trust, some basis for sharing.

## What Secular Relationships Should Hear

In secular relationships, what normally applies is still the rule for strangers and acquaintances, unless the relationship has developed into real friendship.

You may talk along natural and public lines. You may know each other personally in ordinary observable ways. And if, through that relationship, you find that your values align spiritually, it may move into friendship.

However, secular relationships also involve number 5: natural general revelatory information. That is information shared among people who are privy to it because of work, office, profession, or responsibility. That information stays within the circle that is meant to know it.

If your boss tells you something in confidence, you do not go home and narrate it as gist. If a client tells you something by privilege, you do not spread it. That is not godliness. That is not friendship. That is irresponsibility.

## Siblings, Parents, and the Role of Friendship

With siblings, the rule of acquaintances applies, and then the extent of friendship determines how much farther things go. If your sibling is also your core friend, then number 6 can come in strongly: natural personal revelatory information. You know, "This person has my back. We are committed." Then some disclosures become appropriate.

With parents, the same basic rule applies, but with an important difference. When you are not yet independent, certain natural personal revelatory matters are not optional. There are things your parents need to know because they are responsible for you. You cannot be under their care, facing serious issues, and act as though it is none of their business.

Once you are independent, that category may function more by the level of friendship and wisdom. You may say, "Dad, Mom, I think you should know this. Something is going on." Then counsel can come.

## What Saints Generally Should Hear

With saints generally, the range is mainly numbers 1 to 4. That means natural general observatory, natural personal observatory, spiritual general observatory, and spiritual personal observatory.

What does not normally belong there is revelatory information, whether natural or spiritual. With saints generally, you do not begin discussing revelatory matters about your local church, your leader's disclosures, or deeply personal private things.

Doctrine, general gospel truths, broad matters of the faith, and ordinary observable matters are fine. But a believer should not become the sort of person that is always trying to hear inside information, always looking for gist, always spreading things about people and ministries.

That is not confidence in what God has given you. That is not healthy spirituality.

If saints become friends, then natural personal revelatory information may be shared to some degree depending on how core the friendship is. And spiritual personal revelatory information can also enter there to some extent, but still with discretion.

## What Local Church Members Should Hear

With local church members, numbers 1 to 4 apply, and number 7 also applies: spiritual general revelatory information.

That means there are things belonging to the shared life and instruction of the local church that local church members can discuss together. You can encourage one another with instruction the church has received. You can talk about acting on what was taught. You can stir one another up.

Conversation happens by questions and comments. You ask, "How are you doing?" "What did you learn?" "What do you do?" "What course do you study?" You comment, you compliment, you explain, you share positions and conclusions. There is nothing wrong with getting to know people.

The caution is this: spiritual personal revelatory information is not for local church members generally. That belongs mainly with leadership and close, appropriate friendship. For example, the details of someone's discipleship, leadership dealings, or private ministry matters are not for general church conversation.

Likewise, natural personal revelatory information does not have to be scattered among church members. If there are serious issues in your life, leaders may need to know, but that does not mean everybody in church needs to know.

So relate with people. Learn what they do. Learn how to encourage them. Talk about doctrine. Talk about life in ways that are appropriate. But do not create problems by carrying information that should not be carried.

## What Core Friends and Spouses Should Hear

Core friends are different. With core friends, you can discuss everything from 1 to 4 and from 6 to 8, according to the level of trust and the nature of the friendship.

Core friendship means you trust them, you know them, and you are committed. You can talk about plans, burdens, personal goals, spiritual concerns, private disclosures, and the things that genuinely belong in committed friendship.

The spouse is similar in that sense, but even here there must be discernment. Not every piece of information about other people is now yours to tell because you are married. If your spouse is not the person's leader in church, you do not go and discuss somebody's discipleship matter, private struggle, or leadership dealing with your spouse. That is irresponsible.

There are too many other things to talk about. Why go and pick what will create problems? Do not involve another person in a matter he or she has no business with. Keep people's matters where they belong.

## What Leaders Ought to Know

When it comes to leaders, the same broad rule as core friendship applies, but with a special obligation. What leaders ought to know, you should make sure they know. Not everything in existence, but what they ought to know.

If something is going on in your life that affects your walk, your purpose, your church life, or your spiritual progress, and your leaders ought to know, then let them know. That is part of being aligned with God's leadership in your life.

Not everyone gets every detail. But what they should know, they should know.

## Conflict Handling as Spiritual Conversation

Conflict handling and resolution belong under spiritual information. Believers should not act as if conflict is merely a natural annoyance. If something is wrong, say it. Do not hold it and say, "I didn't want to say it." That is how people create time bombs.

At the stage of friction, matters can still be resolved easily. You say, "I might be wrong, but I am concerned. What is going on?" That is healthy. That is better than keeping quiet until there is collision.

If you do not resolve friction, it will eventually become collision. And then what could have been handled by simple conversation becomes a major repair job.

Jesus said if your brother offends you, go and tell him between you and him alone. If he does not hear, then take one or two more. Then it can go further appropriately. That means we must know how to call ourselves to order in friendship.

Among genuine friends, you do not wish differences away. You resolve them. You agree on how to move forward. Otherwise you are only postponing a bigger problem.

## Building, Shared Purpose, Support, and Honor

The other expressions of friendship also need to be practiced.

There is revelation. Relationships move forward by appropriate disclosure. You reveal things that should be revealed at the right level. You do not reveal what should not be revealed.

There is building. Friends should edify one another. Sometimes that includes instruction, correction, sharpening, rebuke, or encouragement. Building means that when I know the person's vision and purpose, I want to give tools, resources, help, prayer, and guidance so that the person becomes what God wants him to be.

You must believe in the person's purpose. You must believe that there is something God wants to do in that life, and you are invested in that happening.

There is shared activity in shared purpose or pleasure. Some of that can be planned. Friends spend time together. They do things together. In marriage, of course, that has its own dimensions, but even outside marriage, shared activities matter in friendship.

There is support and help for the other person's vision. You cannot be found competing in friendship. You should be thinking, "What can I do to help this person succeed? What resources can I lend? What time? What partnership? What presence?" Building is about the person's growth; support is about helping the person's cause and pursuits.

Then there is honor and material giving. These things are to be planned too. Some things in friendship must be deliberate. You do not drift into strong friendship. You cultivate it.

## Commitments for Practicing Friendship

Now let us come to the direct instruction.

First, there must be a commitment to greeting. As touching all humanity, I will warmly greet people. Anywhere. In church, after meetings, in the natural, I will greet people affectionately.

Second, there must be a commitment to cultivating friendships by conversation, especially in the local church. I will ensure that I know people in the local church in natural and spiritual observatory ways. I will know what they do. I will know something of their worldview. I will know how to encourage them.

Third, carry that into your secular relationships too. Know people in your class. Know people at work. Be affectionate toward people. Talk with them. Learn what they do. In some cases, a spiritual conversation may open up as well.

Fourth, be committed to cultivating and maintaining core friendships. That one is work. If communication dies, the relationship is gone. Strong relationships do not happen by magic. People make them that way. You must work at them.

Two people can be co-workers or co-laborers in church and build a relationship that genuinely pushes the course of the gospel because they chose to. They inspire each other. They sharpen each other. They set goals together. They stir one another up. That does not happen accidentally.

When you say you do not have strong relationships, often it is because you are not working at them. Core friendships must be developed and maintained. Even simple things matter: calling, checking in, honoring birthdays, reaching out, keeping touch.

Fifth, be fully aligned with the leadership of the church. Maintain strong relationship with God, and be aligned with the leadership God has placed over you. In that relationship, there should be honesty, openness, and alignment. If something is not right, ask. If there is a concern, speak. If there is something leaders ought to know, tell them. No games, no tricks, no hidden agenda.

When it comes to leadership, there must be alignment, not competition. If I am holding back what I ought to say, or competing secretly, then something is wrong. This is not a place for gimmicks.

## Local Church Focus and the Fulfillment of Purpose

Take your local church seriously. That is where God has called you. Your local church does not become better because you are focusing on other churches all the time. Your local church fulfills its purpose by focusing on what God is doing there.

It is what you focus on that grows. Thank God for what others are doing, but your attention should be where God has placed you. That is how you will fulfill God's purpose.

So cultivate friendships in your local church. Be friendly in the natural. Maintain core friendships. Be aligned with leadership. Always know what information belongs where. Learn discretion. Treat what is meant to be secret as sacred. Do not use friendships for clout, drama, or validation. Our lives are not a show; they are a pursuit of purpose.

There is Scripture that says to learn to mind your own business. Believers should know how to live their own lives. Do not turn what should be reality into drama.

## Work at Friendship for God's Purpose

Men and brethren, I have said what I need to say. Relationships in the faith of Christ are to be practiced. This matters ahead of marital teaching because marital matters are only a subset. This is about how to relate with people generally as friends.

Commit to affectionate greeting, salutation, and hospitality.

Commit to cultivating friendships in the local church.

Commit to friendliness in the natural.

Commit to maintaining core friendships. Work at them. Ask, "How can this become better? How can this friendship better serve God's purpose?"

How can I and this worker work together? How can I and this leader work together? Paul and Barnabas worked at it. Barnabas went to look for Paul. He brought him in. Relationships that serve God's purpose are cultivated.

You have to have faith, trust, and hope. Drop suspicion. Believe the best. When you see contradiction, still believe God for the person's future and speak accordingly. If your friend is becoming worldly, stand in prayer and talk to the person. Say, in effect, "I am concerned. God called us. We are supposed to do this together. We cannot be going this way." Be that friend.

Relationship in the faith of Christ is work. It is practice. It is taking definite steps. There must be definite commitment. Commit ahead of time and do not drop it.

Core friendships are long-term commitments: commitment to regular conversations, commitment to a result the person is supposed to have, commitment to pursuing that result, commitment to supporting the person to get there, commitment to building the person.

In core friendship, you must know the person's purpose and be committed to making it work. The person must do well naturally. The person must do well spiritually. The person must fulfill God's purpose and plan. You cannot call yourself a friend and be unconcerned while the person's life is defaulting. A friend says, "No, not this way. There is another way."

What will the church be like when we are all like that, when we are actually friends? The weak brother will be strengthened. The strong will become stronger. Our countenance will be sharpened one by another. We will all walk more effectively in what God wants us to do. Our interest will not be selfish and personal but the good of the other person. That is what Jesus wants. That is what the Spirit is working in us.

## Closing Prayer

Let us pray for ourselves concerning all our friendships.

Lord, concerning all the friendships that I have, help me to be a true friend. Help me to practice friendship. Help me to commit to friendliness toward all men and to be a genuine friend to the people you have given to me. I do not only have the nature of friendship; I take the steps of friendship.

I commit to all men in friendliness and affectionate hospitality. I commit to the members of my local church. I am committed to knowing them and helping them as much as I can. I take the steps of friendship toward the people I know in the natural and toward the saints I know generally. I take the steps to know them, to be affectionate toward them, and to help where I can.

Most importantly, for my core friendships, I commit to maintaining them, to knowing the results they are supposed to have, and to being dedicated to their fulfilling it. It is not on my watch that they will falter, fail, shake, or fall. Lord, I am standing for them. I am praying for them. I am trusting for them. I am believing for them. I trust your word and your power, your Spirit at work in them.

I will be involved in building them, in shared activities, not just shared activities for pleasure but shared activities for purpose. I will be there in aiding them, in guiding their steps, in helping them to get it right, in helping them to get done the things the Lord wants them to get done, in supporting their cause, in seeing their vision fulfilled, spiritually first and also naturally.

Lord, I am that true friend. Lord, I am like you. Lord, I am like Paul. Lord, I am like the men of Scripture. Lord, I refuse strife. I know what to do to maintain friendships. I ensure that conflicts remain friction and do not become collision. By the power of the Holy Ghost, by the nature and the steps of my new nature, I am a true friend in all that I do.

Lord, I am fully aligned with leadership, and I am a genuine friend to every leader you have made a leader to me. Fully aligned to the cause and vision that I share with them. No games. No tricks. I walk in the path you have set for me, working with partners in the work of the ministry, taking my place and fulfilling my role.

By the testimony of our friendships, let your purpose be fulfilled. Let the kingdom be expanded. Let the church grow. Let godly character be formed. Let your glory be seen in the testimony of our friendships.

Lord, we are grateful for what you are working in us by your word, the steps you have guided us to take. We commit to those steps, one after the other, to imitate you just as we find in your word, in the name of our Lord Jesus. Amen.

Men and brethren, God bless you. Love you. Behold the best to you, and do well to you.
