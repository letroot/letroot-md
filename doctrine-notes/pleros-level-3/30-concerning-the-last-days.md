# Concerning the Last Days

## Why the Last Days Matter for Spiritual Growth

We're looking at concerning the last days. One thing is clear from Scripture: we need the unity of the faith to have the kind of growth God wants us to have, so that we can walk in and fulfill his purpose. Any part of the knowledge of God's word that we lack is going to affect our spiritual growth to one degree or another.

With a subject like this, people ask, “What's the point? The last days will just happen. What does it have to do with my spiritual growth?” You will be surprised. It actually has a number of things to do with your spiritual growth. You will probably not have the right attitude to your walk in God's purpose, or to your pursuit of God's purpose, without the knowledge of the last days.

The kind of attitude the Lord was expecting, the instructions he gave about readiness and anticipation, the attitude they were supposed to have about stewardship, are all dependent on your recognition of the last days. The degree of the emotion you have, the disposition and attitude we should have, depend on this matter of the last days. You even see it in Romans 8: we are groaning; we're expecting. These things cannot be present without knowledge of the last days, or what we can call the eschaton, the last, or eschatos, the last days.

We cannot fulfill God's purpose without having the unity of the faith, if we don't know everything God wants us to know. One thing clearly part of the unity of the faith is the subject of eschatology. Paul told us he has given us that message we need for our growth. In Ephesians 3, he said he had already briefly written in a few words, and when you read you understand his knowledge in the mystery of Christ.

By the time you read the earlier portions of that material, Ephesians 1, you can see Paul's knowledge in these things. He starts telling us how we are blessed today, how it goes back to what God desired from the beginning. He wanted us in his purpose, which is sonship. One of the last things he emphasized is found in Ephesians 1:13–14. Part of the Pauline theology and submission is the subject of the eschatos, the eschaton, the last things, the last days.

## Sealed by the Spirit Until Redemption

Ephesians 1:13: “In him you trusted after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation. Having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise.” You're like, “It's not talking about anything last-day. It's just talking about having the Spirit today.” But hold on. You are sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, “who is the guarantee,” the arrabōn, the collateral, “of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of his glory.”

Paul is drawing a timeline. There's a timeline between when people start to have the Spirit of God in them, a reality he is saying happened in Christ after redemption was fulfilled, and what he calls the redemption of the purchased possession. Based on what Christ has done, people start to become sealed with the Spirit, and that timeline runs until the redemption of the purchased possession.

One thing we must not ignore, which you'll find even in our righteous-nature teaching on the podcast, is that there's a reality of having the Spirit that people had before Christ came. That is not exactly what God wanted. It was not regeneration, but there was a way they had the Spirit. I'm framing it as they had the Spirit of God with them.

What example can I use? Let's say you have a wand or a tool you can use with your hand to do different things, maybe a magnetic tool you can use to pull things. It's different from when you now do an operation where they make your hand have the qualities of that tool, but you could do the same things. There's a reality of having the Spirit prior to Christ that we must acknowledge. Several texts of Scripture point to that. We cannot afford to ignore it.

When righteousness was reckoned to people, because they were still not righteous by nature regeneratively, they would still call it a reckoning. But it came with having the reality of the Spirit with them. It's the same way the Lord was telling the apostles when he was with them.

John 14:16–17 says, “I'll pray the Father, and he will give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you forever, even the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, for it neither sees him nor knows him. But you know him, for he dwells with you and shall be in you.” Is the Lord saying there's such a reality of the Spirit being with? I know people say that because Jesus was with them, he's saying the Spirit is with them as Jesus is with them. I don't think so. I think it's showing us two realities of how the Spirit of God has engaged: the Spirit with people prior to Christ, and the Spirit in people.

It's like having a wand, tool, or magnetic gadget. You press the button and it pulls different things to you. But then your hand is operated on, reconstructed, infused with the same abilities. We are getting to such a time in our world where certain natural abilities that are with us might start to be put in us. I don't know what you feel, but I'm not too sure I'm overly comfortable with some of those dynamics. I think I like to have my body and have the tools. There's a bit of difference.

There is that distinction, but let's not focus on that. Paul is saying that from this point where the Spirit is now in people, not just with people, there's a timeline until what he calls the redemption of the purchased possession.

You see him talk about that timeline again in Ephesians 4:30: “Do not grieve the Holy Spirit, by which you are sealed until the day of redemption.” That day is a single day, a one-time thing. There's an activity going on when the Spirit is in someone, sealing the person until the day of redemption. You start to notice that they are talking about one day at the tail end of that timeline.

## Last Days, Latter Times, and Other Last Things

The first contact we have with the words last days in the New Testament text is Acts 2:17: “It shall come to pass in the last days.” Some people say, “The last days are coming,” or, “We have just entered the last days now.” Someone says, “John said it's the last hour. Imagine what it is now. We're in the last second. It has been the last hour since then. I think we're in the last microsecond!” I'm just joking. We are in the last days; we need to explain it.

Peter was quoting: “In the last days, says God, I'll pour out my Spirit.” Second Timothy 3:1 says, “But know this, that in the last days perilous times will come.” He was talking about his time. We shouldn't be surprised that people are going into error, teaching all kinds of falsehood, because perilous times will come.

Peter's last-days statement was talking about something good: pouring the Spirit on all flesh. We have seen Paul talking about the Spirit being in people until the redemption of the purchased possession, until the day of redemption. But here Paul is talking about things that are terrible: perilous times.

Hebrews 1:1–2: God, who at various times and in various manners spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken unto us by his Son. What is this last days all about? James 5:3, in its last sentence, says, “You have heaped up treasures in the last days.”

First Peter 1:5 says you are kept by the power of God through faith for salvation ready to be revealed in the last time. He's using the singular. That's similar to Paul saying the Holy Ghost seals you until the day of redemption. It's almost like he wants to say the last day itself: the very final moments. He uses the plural in verse 20: Christ was foreordained before the foundation of the world but was manifest in these last times for you.

These are forms and inflections of eschatos. Eschatos is essentially a contrast with prōtos, first. The issue is first and last. The Lord Jesus says several times about himself, “I'm the first and the last.” Eschatos means last, final, the concluding thing, whether the final period as plural or final time as singular.

Second Peter 3:3 says scoffers will come in the last days, saying, “Where is the promise of his coming?” So among the first things you will see in the last days are scoffers asking about the promise of his coming. First John 2:18 says, “Little children, it is the last hour,” or last time in the KJV. That shows how quickly they were expecting things to happen. Some people say they were disappointed prophets. No. There's supposed to be a continued attitude of anticipation, which I think we tend to lack.

Anybody of redemptive faith must in their lifetime have an anticipation that affects how they pursue what they are doing. They see themselves as sojourners, that it can end soon, so they are trying to get their own part done.

Jude 18 also says there will be mockers in the last time. Jude and 2 Peter are very similar. Peter says scoffers in the last days; Jude says scoffers in the last time. Whether they use last days or last time, it's still the same thought, the word eschatos.

There are other things called last, without days or time. I don't want to include Paul as the last of all the apostles in 1 Corinthians 15:8; that is not applicable here. But 1 Corinthians 15:26 says, “The last enemy that will be destroyed is death.” There is a last enemy pertaining to this last time.

Verse 45 says the first Adam became a living being, and the last Adam became a life-giving Spirit. That's the Lord Jesus Christ. There's a lot about the person of the Lord Jesus touching this issue of last. He was foreordained before the foundation of the world to be manifest in these last times. He is called the last Adam, by whom the last enemy will be defeated. He must reign until he has put all enemies under his feet; the last enemy destroyed is death.

In verse 52 there's the last trumpet. There are the last plagues in Revelation 15:1: seven angels having the seven last plagues. Revelation 21:9 also mentions one of the seven angels with the seven bowls filled with the last plagues. We will do a teaching on understanding the book of Revelation in level three. It's Revelation, not Revelations.

The Lord Jesus is called the first and the last. That doesn't mean he's the first Adam. It doesn't mean anything first is him. The first Adam now has a negative connotation, and he is the last Adam. He is called the first and last in Revelation 1:11, 1:17, 2:8, and 22:13. I've run through the biblical references to last days and last things. What do last things mean? That's the question we need to answer.

## What Marks the Beginning of the Last Days?

As far as Joel goes, and as far as Peter takes what Joel was teaching, after redemption there is the outpouring of the Spirit. Peter is therefore saying Christ's work of redemption, his death and resurrection, is the marker.

Joel did not give us a Christ figure in his own account, but across Scripture, whenever the eschatological order or pattern is shown, a Christ figure is present in view of the Christ himself. Patterns are always going on, even till today.

Peter is saying that from the person and work of the Christ, and his redemption being fulfilled, we are now in the last days. The last days are counted either from redemption being fulfilled by the Christ, or from the presence of the Christ to fulfill redemption. I don't want to use incarnation there, because that would be just the Lord Jesus, the true eschatological pattern. You will see eschatological patterns across Scripture.

That's why, in a text like 1 Peter 1:20, it says he was manifest in these last times for you. His presence is the marker of that last time, the period of redemption. The afterward of Joel, the last days of Peter, is after restoration and redemption from sin and its effects. We have not only identified that there are last days; we have identified what they are. If we are not careful, we will talk about last days vaguely without submitting the order of eschatology. What leads to the last days? What follows from them? Certain things are at play before there can be the issue of last days.

## The Ten-Part Order of Eschatology

What does that order or pattern look like? I'm going to give us a ten-part pattern. The whole idea of last days is that something went wrong, someone is fixing it, the person has fixed it, and this is now called the last days. The former days are the period where something is wrong and needs fixing. Without that issue, there would not be a need to distinguish the period of waiting from the period after fixing it toward its consummation.

The first thing is the offer of God's purpose. Whenever you see an eschatological pattern, there will first have been the offer of God's purpose. Second is the rejection of God's purpose.

In Genesis 1:1–3, God creating the world means he has offered his purpose. That there was darkness means a rejection of his purpose is presumed to have happened; chaos has happened. The moment you see “Let there be light,” you are seeing redemption in promise or at work.

Third is the reign of sin and death, what we may call the sevenfold reign of sin and death. I will explain it, because you will particularly need it when we look at understanding the book of Revelation.

Fourth is the promise of redemption. If there were no promise of redemption, there would not be two sets of days, first and last, former and latter. They would be stuck with one day, the day of death, darkness, and evil until its consummation.

Fifth is mission toward redemption, or activities toward redemption.

Sixth is the middle point, which we can call the beginning of the last days: the birth, life, ministry, and work of the Christ. The person does a redemptive work or role, having to defeat something, address something, win over something. It can be falsehood. There is a stage where people are in death or darkness and need a revival of something.

Seventh is the increased reign of sin and death. This is important. In the last days, after the work of the Christ, that sevenfold reign will increase. It does not mean there won't be a walk in redemption. There will be a walk in redemption.

Eighth is mission toward glorification. We can also speak of walking in it, but mission toward glorification, or mission toward consummation, the consummation of the work of the Christ.

Ninth is the day of the Lord. Tenth is the eternal state, or new heavens and new earth.

That is the ten-part order of eschatology. Can we see it in Genesis 1:1 to 2:3? Can we see it in Noah? Yes, there's life afterward, even though in all these smaller patterns it gets messed up again, showing us that this is not the real deal. We can see it in Abraham, in Moses, where there is an experience of freedom afterward. That does not mean there won't be an increase of sin and death or persecution following. We can see it in Joshua and the judges.

There is one true eschatological order, pattern, or timeline, the one with the Lord Jesus at the center. But there are many smaller patterns in Scripture, usually used to teach it. When Peter, Jude, or the Lord discuss the day of the Lord, they draw examples of days of the Lord from these smaller patterns. They draw on a Sodom and Gomorrah situation to explain what will happen eventually. Those are not the grand eschatology; they are smaller patterns of its happenings.

## Accounts of the Pattern Across Scripture

Let me enumerate the major accounts. There is the foreknowledge account of eschatology; the primal account, Noah; the patriarchal account, Abraham, including Isaac and Jacob if you want to put them together; the Exodus account; the conquest account, Joshua, which you might extend to the judges as they were supposed to conquer and subdue more places; and the kingdom account.

We could keep going and include the exile and post-exile accounts, but you can put kingdom and exile together. Then there is the Jesus account. You can see different patterns of eschatology in Scripture.

## The Sevenfold Reign of Sin and Death

What is the sevenfold reign of sin and death? Go to the original happening from which we have the eschatological account. God gave them the offer of his life. They rejected it. God promised redemption in Genesis 3 by a seed. You can argue that in immediate fulfillment it is Noah, because they were expecting a seed, but in actual existential fulfillment it is the Lord Jesus, God become man himself.

During the period after they rejected God's purpose, was there a reign of sin and death? Yes. They became mortal; the earth became corrupt. We can say they were left mortal, and the earth became or was left corrupt. Those are technical issues on another level that we don't want to get into.

The first thing is the reign of sin and death. Sin entered the world. Then there is mortality of human life. Third, corruption of matter. Fourth, what we can call spiritual or worship disorder.

What is the first example of spiritual or worship disorder in this primal account? Cain. God is saying there's a right way to do it; do it the right way. There are all kinds of arguments about exactly what was going on, an issue of historical context, but something is happening there. Someone is worshiping right and someone is worshiping wrong. There is spiritual order or disorder.

Then there is governance disorder. Those giants: exploitative and tyrannical leadership, taking advantage of people, claiming they were the sons of God. It is like a king seeing anybody and saying, “That's my wife.” Exploitative, tyrannical leadership. Some people will argue that it is actual angels executing it. I don't think this is the right time to go into that. Whichever way you stand, something was going on in governance order in Genesis 6.

Another is economic disorder. In this first primal account it is difficult to pick out a clear economic disorder, but you can probably find it in Genesis 5, Lamech's lamentation about their labors and toils. Verse 29, after Noah was born: “This one will comfort us concerning our work and the toil of our hands, because of the ground which the Lord has cursed.” The external conditions of the world are not necessarily the best. It is mild here, but as you go forward, economic disorder is a major issue when they talk about the reign of sin and death.

The last is social disorder, involving moral conduct, lewdness, and so forth. The social order has been disordered. There is the reign of sin and death, mortality of human life, corruption of matter, disorder in worship, disorder in governance, disorder in the economy, and social or moral disorder. You will always see this sevenfold reign of sin and death.

When the Christ is on the scene, or redemption is accomplished, there will be an increased reign of death. When Noah was on the scene, assuming the beginning of the last days in that pattern, Genesis 6 now mentions increased disorder, so much that it grieved God's heart. This is so bad.

## Noah, the Flood, and the Afterwards

Let me show you the word afterward that we saw in Joel 2:28. It is used in Genesis 6, showing an eschatological intention. After Noah is mentioned, Genesis 5:32 says Noah was five hundred years old, and begot Shem, Ham, and Japheth. Then, when men began to multiply on the earth and daughters were born to them, the sons of God saw that the daughters of men were beautiful and took wives of all whom they chose.

The Lord said his Spirit would not strive with man forever, for he is flesh, and his days would be one hundred and twenty years. “There were giants on the earth in those days, and also afterward.” That's the same word achar. You are seeing them depict two timelines, things going on before Noah came on the scene and afterward, when the sons of God came to the daughters of men and they bore children to them, mighty men of old, men of renown.

The Lord saw that man's wickedness was great in the earth. Is that great tribulation? We are already seeing it at the beginning. This is the order of eschatology embedded in Scripture. Sin has become increased, great, multiplied in the earth. If I'm correct, great should be ra. I'm not sure.

The Lord saw man's wickedness was great and every thought was evil continually. The Lord was sorry he had made man, for it grieved him. We are seeing an increased reign of sin and death. “I will destroy man whom I have made, both man and beast.” We are seeing a day-of-the-Lord kind of statement. But Noah found grace in the sight of the Lord.

This is a primal account of eschatology. There is an increased reign of sin and death afterward, and mission toward glorification. Was Noah supposed to preach? Yes. Was there mission toward glorification? Yes, until destruction. What would be the day of the Lord in this primal account? The flood. Then the subsequent state would be like the new heavens and new earth. It was not the new heavens and new earth, but you get the idea. There's an eschatological pattern.

When that is done they start telling another one. God offers his purpose to man again from Noah's time, and men reject it, act in the flesh, and so forth. Another cycle. There is one true eschatological pattern or timeline, but we see these smaller patterns. It is well acknowledged in the teaching of the apostles and the Lord Jesus, where they draw several kinds of day of the Lord to explain the true judgment at the end.

This sevenfold reign of sin and death is important when you get to Revelation and see the seals, trumpets, and bowls. You need to understand the increased reign of sin and death and the degrees of increase until what is described as the day of the Lord. The seals tell you the original state of sin and death, the trumpets reveal an increased reign, and the bowls depict the day of the Lord itself.

## The Olivet Discourse and the Coming of Christ

Let's go to the Olivet Discourse, about the most focused eschatological discussion, particularly pertaining to the true eschatology with the Lord at its center. If we're doing concerning the last days as a single teaching, it deserves our attention. Then we'll look at the day of the Lord and wrap up. There is so much we are not looking at: concepts like indignation and consumption, examined across all the texts. But we cannot afford to ignore this passage when studying the true last days.

Matthew 24:1: Jesus went out from the temple, and his disciples came to show him its buildings. He said, “Do you not see all these things? Assuredly I say to you, not one stone shall be left here upon another that shall not be thrown down.” As he sat on the Mount of Olives, the disciples came privately and asked, “Tell us, when will these things be? What is the sign of your coming, and of the end of the age?”

People sometimes approach this text as speaking strictly about what would happen in AD 70, the destruction of the temple, and that would be it. I don't think that is a proper reading. There's some truth to it. Some statements the Lord makes are strictly about that, but some extend beyond it.

I know people go to, “This generation will not pass till all these things are fulfilled.” Yes, everything he says there became fulfilled in anticipation of his coming from that point, but his coming itself is not fulfilled. Jesus has not come. Or they say he never left. That's another problem. Jesus actually left.

I know John 14:23 says the Father and Son are with us. Even at the end, he went up but was still with them, not even merely figuratively or positionally, actually. But there is still the ascension of the Christ, where he is not with us in that physical sense. There is his coming back in that sense as well, the consummation of his work, where we share in what he accomplished by having a glorified body.

Let's say what is as it is. I know some things border on mystery, but that does not mean we should jumble everything together. There's a sense in which Jesus said goodbye, and we must not say he never said goodbye in that regard. There's a sense in which he did not say goodbye. There's a sense in which the Lord left and a sense in which he did not leave.

“What will be the sign of your coming and the end of the age?” Jesus answered, “Take heed that no one deceives you.” The end of the age cannot be figurative. It's the end of the age, like when Jesus says he will raise them on the last day. That's not something figurative or something that happened in the past. It's something that will happen.

This is an issue Paul faced, where people were either saying the resurrection had happened or there is no resurrection. Those things are considered heresy. We are getting into substantial things in the gospel, not side issues or just an opinion on a text. They are at the core of the entire journey of God's purpose. It has not happened. You can't be talking as though it has happened.

The first resurrection, you might say, our spirit being raised together with Christ, has happened. The second resurrection has not happened.

## The Beginning of Sorrows and Increasing Disorder

“Take heed that no one deceives you. Many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am the Christ,’ and will deceive many. You will hear of wars and rumors of wars. See that you are not troubled, for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet.”

This discourse lays out an eschatological pattern starting from the last days, how the last days themselves will be. Remember, last days are when the Lord has come. I gave you the pattern before he comes, the whole picture. The Olivet Discourse lays out a journey going forward from when he is around.

Many will come claiming to be the Christ and deceive many. It was happening and is still happening. Wars and rumors of wars must come to pass, but the end is not yet. “Nation will rise against nation, kingdom against kingdom, and there will be famines, pestilences, and earthquakes in various places. All these are the beginning of sorrows.”

There are two ways to take this. One is that the Lord is teaching the typical order of eschatology. The beginning of sorrows is like the initial reign of sin and death, number three in the pattern, before number seven, the increased reign. Another is that he is teaching the order of events upon his resurrection, what will happen with the church. Both are true, particularly the second in this case. Even with the church there is an eschatological pattern that starts with a beginning of sorrows.

“Then they will deliver you up to tribulation and kill you, and you'll be hated by all nations for my name's sake. Then many will be offended, will betray one another, and will hate one another. Then many false prophets will rise up and deceive many. And because lawlessness will abound...” Notice the then, then, then.

Warfare can be governance disorder: people trying to rule by force, fighting one another. Did we see spiritual disorder? Many come in his name and say they are the Christ, deceiving many. Governance disorder? Nation against nation. Famines and pestilence, economic disorder; earthquakes, corruption of matter. He is showing the sevenfold reign of sin and death. There will be the reign of death already, people spiritually dead, mortality of human life, corruption of matter, spiritual disorder, governance disorder, and economic disorder.

He didn't mention social disorder clearly at first, but you'll soon see it. They deliver you up: persecution and opposition. At a point in the teaching, I think I removed reign of sin and death from the list, because it is already the title, and put persecution and opposition as a separate thing. You can put that as number eight, but we typically call it sevenfold and take out that first item, which is the heading. Mortality of human life must be there, because there will be increased mortality. You see it in Revelation, people dying more.

When you hear that, it's part of the pattern. It's not that we're supposed to sit down, accept it, and do nothing. But it is what it is. I think I separated persecution later, so you can put it as number eight, understanding that the first was probably the heading.

They deliver you to tribulation, kill you, and hate you. Many are offended, betray one another, and hate one another. What's that? Social or moral disorder. False prophets rise and deceive: spiritual disorder. These are after the beginning of sorrows. After the beginning there is an increased reign of sin and death.

Because lawlessness abounds, the love of many grows cold, but the one who endures to the end will be saved. “This gospel of the kingdom will be preached throughout the world as a witness to all nations, and then the end will come.” Mission toward glorification in verse 14. The end coming is the day of the Lord.

## The Abomination of Desolation and Great Tribulation

Let me give you an outline of the discourse. First is the beginning of sorrows, which you can call the initial reign of sin and death. Second is persecution and apostasy. Third is the worldwide preaching of the gospel. Fourth is great tribulation.

From Matthew 24:15, when he talks about the abomination of desolation, there is a true understanding that it applies to the destruction of the temple in Jerusalem. But again, it is a pattern of eschatology that is not only about that destruction, but a period of increased reign of sin and death. There is Daniel connected to this, which we explain in level five, but we cannot go into all of it now.

“Therefore when you see the abomination of desolation spoken of by Daniel the prophet standing in the holy place,” whoever reads, let him understand, “then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains.” This abomination has to do with someone calling themselves God. You see a similar reference in 2 Thessalonians 2, showing it can still happen independently of the destruction of Jerusalem.

It is spiritual disorder intertwined with governance disorder. In Revelation you see the union between governance disorder, called the beast, and spiritual disorder, called the false prophet. The false prophet tells people to worship the beast, and they work together.

When Jerusalem was destroyed there was already someone calling himself God or son of God, the Caesar. The Jews were having problems; they were being called to worship and forced to do things. Now it was time to flee. You can say it is the AD 70 destruction of Jerusalem and be right. You can say it is not just AD 70, that it can go on when spiritual, governance, and economic disorder work together, and also be right.

On one hand it is useful for the immediate destruction about to happen. But it is not a break away from the eschatological pattern of great tribulation, the increased reign of sin and death.

There is an argument we make from Revelation and Daniel that the tail end of the last days will witness a particular increase. It will be difficult to tell, because we are seeing a reign of sin and death normally, and sometimes we can see it has increased. We can say we are in an eschatological time, but cannot tell, “This is now the final one.” Yet there will be such an increase at such a time.

That is why you can say everything was already fulfilled in that generation. All the required conditions in the pattern for the day of the Lord were fulfilled in their generation. AD 70 itself is proof of increased death. In their time they could take for granted worldwide preaching of the gospel, like we can today. All the conditions were met. Anticipation has been a thing since their time and should be until now.

The abomination of desolation is a merger of sorts between governance and spiritual disorder, spoken of also in 2 Thessalonians as the man of sin, or humanity in sin. You may specify a man of sin, particularly where governance and spiritual orders are merged or working together somehow.

Those in Judea should flee to the mountains. Someone on the housetop should not go down to take anything from the house; someone in the field should not return for clothes. Woe to those pregnant or nursing babies in those days. It's difficult to say this is just the actual day of the Lord, because he's telling them to escape, but he uses descriptions pertaining to the day of the Lord.

“Pray that your flight will not be in winter nor on the Sabbath. Then there will be great tribulation.” The word thlipsis is for pressure, such as has not been since the beginning of the world until this time, nor shall ever be. You can point to many things that look like great tribulation. Even in Genesis 6 they point to something that looks great, but it is still not the great. The conditions have been fulfilled, but there will still be the final one.

“Unless those days were shortened, no flesh would be saved; but for the elect's sake those days will be shortened.” If anyone says, “Here is the Christ,” or, “There,” don't believe it. In that era of increased reign, false christs and false prophets will show signs and wonders to deceive, if possible, even the elect. Still spiritual disorder.

## Cosmic Signs and the Gathering of the Elect

“See, I have told you beforehand. If they say, ‘He is in the desert,’ don't go out. ‘He is in the inner rooms,’ do not believe it. For as lightning comes from the east and flashes to the west, so will the coming of the Son of Man be. Wherever the carcass is, there the eagles will be gathered.”

I don't want to explain some of those things here because of the many debates around them. There are positive interpretations for verse 28, such as all of us gathering to the Christ, and an immediate application to people being killed in Jerusalem. But none of this means the coming of the Christ has happened.

You will start having a Jehovah's Witness-like theology. I'm not just name-calling or labeling; it's not correct. You start giving a situation Paul was correcting, that it happened around nineteen-something. No, it has not happened. What Paul was saying will not be something we can describe like that.

If you say it was certain people, the 144,000, who went when that happened, that some people disappeared then, you are misreading the whole framework. There will be a general resurrection. There's no 144,000 resurrection before that one. The 144,000 is symbolic for the Jews who will be part of the multitudes saved, or the elect by faith.

Verse 29: “Immediately after the tribulation of those days, the sun will be darkened, the moon will not give its light, the stars will fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens will be shaken.” These are what we call cosmic signs, part of the day of the Lord. At this point he is pointing us to the day of the Lord.

The tribulation of those days in verse 29 is the one mentioned in verse 21: great tribulation, a particular increased reign of sin and death at the tail end of the last days. After that there will be cosmic signs, the introduction of the day of the Lord.

Could anyone have seen many events through history and said, “This is the great tribulation; we are expecting the day of the Lord soon”? Yes. Can you look at things now and say so? Probably because you are far from Ukraine you don't say so. If you are living in some places in Nigeria, the conditions have been fulfilled and it can happen.

“The sign of the Son of Man will appear in heaven, and all the tribes of the earth will mourn, and they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with great power and glory. He will send his angels with a great sound, and gather his elect together.” The day of the Lord will happen.

Returning to the outline: beginning of sorrows, persecution and apostasy, worldwide preaching, great tribulation, then the day of the Lord, number five. Great tribulation includes increased spiritual disorder: people saying the Christ is here and there, trying to deceive the elect. Sixth is the revelation and coming, the parousia, of the Christ. Seventh is the resurrection, what people like to call the rapture. We can put rapture in brackets for what people mean, but it is the resurrection of the saints, actually a general resurrection. It's not that we are going away. I'll explain when we look at the day of the Lord.

## Readiness, Faithfulness, Judgment, and the Eternal State

After this the Lord teaches readiness. Verse 32: learn the parable from the fig tree. When its branch becomes tender and puts out leaves, you know summer is near. When you see these things, know that it is near, at the doors. “This generation will by no means pass away till all these things take place.” All the conditions will be fulfilled. That's the implication, and therefore we must be in that state of anticipation.

“Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will by no means pass away. Of that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of heaven, but my Father only.” Then, “As the days of Noah were...” This teaches readiness because you don't know.

Before the flood they were eating and drinking. You see him borrowing earlier, smaller eschatological patterns. As the days of Noah, so will the coming of the Son of Man be. They were eating, drinking, marrying, giving in marriage, until Noah entered the ark. They did not know until the flood came and took them away. So will the coming of the Son of Man be.

He goes on teaching faithfulness and readiness, even with the wise and foolish virgins and the parable of the talents. Then he comes back to judgment. From Matthew 25:14 there are two teachings on judgment. You see judgment of the saints in the talents and judgment of all people in the sheep and goats. Within those teachings is teaching on the eternal state: some enter everlasting punishment, and the righteous enter everlasting life.

Let me make the outline clear. Beginning of sorrows; persecution and apostasy; worldwide preaching of the gospel; great tribulation, increased reign of sin and death; day of the Lord; revelation and coming of the Christ; resurrection; teaching on readiness and faithfulness; judgment of the saints and of all people; eternal state.

There are two arguments about how to read the great-tribulation section. I think when teaching level five I said that Matthew 24:15, “Therefore when you see the abomination of desolation,” refers back after verse 8, the beginning of sorrows, to the phase of being delivered up to persecution. It calls attention back to the period of increase. That's why the false-christ issue repeats, spiritual and governance orders working in opposition to the truth.

On that approach, what happens in verses 9–14 is being described as great tribulation. When you see these things, it is a time of great tribulation. The same signs appear there. That great tribulation will lead to the day of the Lord.

Seventh in the outline is resurrection. Let's just say resurrection, because it will be a general resurrection. Eighth, the teaching on readiness and faithfulness, runs from Matthew 24:36 to Matthew 25:13. Ninth, judgment of all, is Matthew 25:14–46. Tenth, the eternal state, can be put at Matthew 25:46: entering into damnation or eternal life. That's how we can summarize the Olivet Discourse.

## The Order of the Day of the Lord

What's the general outline of the day of the Lord as we have it in Scripture? This will be important for understanding Revelation. I have to go through this quickly, and we'll open it more when we get to that teaching.

First are cosmic signs. You find these in the Old Testament texts as well, not just the New Testament. Second is the revelation of the Christ. Third is general resurrection. John 5 talks about this: all who have died come back physically.

Within that general resurrection there is the resurrection of the saints to the Lord, which is fourth, the marriage of the Lamb as explained in Revelation. We might call it being joined with the Lord at that point physically. The saints are in the general resurrection, but our resurrection is unto the Lord. We go to the Lord within that event; it is a split-second type of issue.

Fifth is indignation on the unbelieving. There is judgment poured out on unbelieving people. Sixth is consumption of corrupt matter. Corrupt matter itself is destroyed. Both prophets and apostles teach this. There will be a judgment of the unbelieving; they will suffer that death within this world. There will be a judgment on the cosmos; it will suffer within this world.

Seventh is judgment of the world. This one is the judgment of the world before God, not indignation on the earth. General resurrection and the marriage of the Lamb happen on the day of the Lord, arguably within a split second depending on how you look at some texts. Then indignation, consumption, and judgment of the world before God. Then eighth, the second death. Ninth, judgment of the saints. Tenth, new heavens and new earth.

That is the day of the Lord. When we get to understanding Revelation, we will open these things better and they will become clearer, I trust.
