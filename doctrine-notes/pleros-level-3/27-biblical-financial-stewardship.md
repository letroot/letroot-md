He was like, “The master is saying, ‘You can no longer be a steward.’ I need to have a plan.” A plan for what? The future, retirement, the aftermath. That's what he decided to do. He called every one of his master's debtors and said, “How much do you owe my master?” He said, “A hundred measures of oil.” He said, “Take your bill, sit down and write fifty.” He was garnering favor with people, planning for the future.

I know some people say he's teaching us discounts. Forget all that. Those are not the issues. The guy is a bad guy as far as the story is going. First, he's a bad guy for wasting. Second, he's a bad guy for cooking the books. Look at verse 9: “And I say to you, make friends for yourselves by unrighteous mammon.” Notice he's saying, “I say to you,” to say, “Do what this guy did.” Imagine you put that Jesus said, which he did, “Make friends for yourselves by unrighteous mammon,” and that's what we are supposed to be doing: using money to buy people to be my friends. ## Diligence, Orderly Plans, and Natural Success

If walking in the Spirit, walking in truth, walking in the law is going to bring favor before God and men, and one of the things in walking in the law is walking in discipline or diligence, what would diligence do that can be a fruit of favor? Proverbs talks about things diligence will do that are fruits of favor. One of them is natural success. When you are walking in the favor of God, which is walking in the Spirit, walking in things in the Spirit including discipline and diligence, it is predicted in God's word to bring natural success in your natural assignment.

I want you to look at Proverbs 10:4, what Scripture says on diligence. That tells you the law of the Lord is not against abundance, wealth, and prosperity by productivity with the favor of God. But the law of the Lord does not put an undue burden on people for that.
