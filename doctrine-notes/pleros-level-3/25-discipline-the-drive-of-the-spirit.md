# Discipline: The Drive of the Spirit

## Knowing God's Purpose and Seeking Its Fulfilment

Our Father, I am so glad that I am your child and a part of your family. In this meeting, gathered with my siblings, I receive of your word light, truth, revelation and growth by the knowledge of Christ and his work in all its fullness, and by the knowledge of him who is in me, in the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.

We are looking at discipline, the drive of the Spirit. I have talked like this a couple of times, but I feel that my understanding of it is really better, and it is something I want to share with you.

One of the first things, or one of the earliest things, we ought to know in the Christian faith is what God's purpose for our life is. Maybe that comes after knowing about redemption. For some people, it might even start with first having to agree that there is God. Some of us went through that journey. So let us start from there: knowing the truth of God's existence and knowing the truth of redemption. Usually, those are the first two things you will know.

But following those two things, you believe in the reality of God and the redemption he has accomplished in Christ. The next thing of great importance that you must know is the reality of God's purpose. It is so important because, when you know the truth of God and redemption, you have answered the what question. What is this world I am in? What is this existence I have found myself in? You have answered it in two ways: what is it like, and how did it come about? Then why is it the way it is? What is it all about, this contradiction where there is good and evil, life and death, light and darkness?

At that point, the next question is, “But why am I here? Why did God make me in the first place? Now that he has redeemed me, what am I supposed to do?” Those would be the same: why he made us is what he has redeemed us to. When you know God's purpose, which we have studied a great deal, your greatest aim should be to fulfil it.

Let us go over that again. What is God's purpose? Paul clearly explains it in Ephesians 1:9. God has made known to us the mystery of his will, what he wants, according to what he likes, his good pleasure, which he purposed in himself. God's purpose is what he wants that he likes.

We have used several examples. If you like something and start to crave it, now you want it. You ordinarily like it; now you are craving it and want it. But you can ignore it. Maybe you are trying to budget and save, and you say, “I crave this thing. I want it, but I will not get it.” This thing I am teaching should even be a financial principle. It is not everything you like, and now even crave, that you must get. You are not God. You are man. It is a serious matter for all of us.

We must learn that some days we crave things and do not get them. There are also times when you crave something and can say, “I can splurge.” It is okay. Enjoy. First Timothy 6:17. What is God's purpose? That pleasure he had which became his will, which became his determination, is what we now call his purpose.

## Predestination Leads to Sonship

Ephesians 1:5 speaks of his having predestined us. I like the example I gave someone yesterday while talking about predestination. Let us assume you write JAMB to enter university. For someone who is not Nigerian, that is the examination you write to enter university. Let us say you write it to study law.

When you finish writing, you already know that what you have written is poor. “It is very likely that I will get maybe 230. This prospect of law is almost out of it.” But it is your good pleasure to study law. It is your will and even your determination: “I want to study law.” So you say, “I have already done badly. I will tell them to let me start A levels.”

What are you doing? You are predestining to study law ahead of a possibility. Now, this is not foreknowing; this is forecasting. You are projecting and forecasting that, with what you have done, you are likely not to get what you need. You do not foreknow. You are forecasting. “I am likely not to get this, so I am planning. Let me do A levels so that I can come in that way.”

I do not know whether that happens with law too. I think you can do A levels and come into the second year to study the course. I am not too sure. But you can see the point. Predestination is like predetermining something. It is not the normal flow of events; it is a response.

What God predetermined to do is not sonship. What he predetermined to do leads to sonship. He predestined redemption. Notice what he says: he predestined us to sonship by Jesus Christ. That means you need to find out what he predestined in Jesus Christ. It is redemption: that we should be holy and without blame before him in love. Verse 4. Bringing us back into that state, to be blameless through redemption, is what he predestined.

Having predestined redemption to sonship by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will. It is because of the good pleasure of his will that he predestined redemption by Jesus. Whatever that predestination leads to shows you what he wanted initially. Someone choosing to do A levels so as to enter university for law shows that what the person initially wanted was to be in university studying law.

If the predestination is to sonship, it means his good pleasure, will and purpose itself is sonship. We have examined this several times and established that God's purpose for our lives is sonship.

## Sonship Means Being God's Child and His Heir

We have explained what sonship entails. Romans 8:15 says we have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear, but the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, “Abba, Father.” We have received the Spirit of sonship. That is the word adoption there.

The reason we keep emphasizing sonship over adoption is that the mindset we have about adoption today is not what Paul had in mind. Paul had in mind the idea of placement, that someone who was an actual child was put in a place. In our minds, when we hear adoption, we think someone who is not a person's child has been taken as that person's child. They are two different things on our minds. That is why we say it is preferable to say sonship. It is even more wholesome to say sonship.

He has given us the Spirit of sonship by whom we cry, “Abba, Father.” What does sonship entail? Verse 16: the Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God. The first reality of sonship is that we are children of God. The word is teknon. We are born of God, which means we are born to fellowship with God. That is why we cry, “Abba, Father.” We are born to fellowship or interact with God.

The second reality of being a child of God is that we can walk in his nature. We can also relate with our siblings or family. Fellowship with God and fellowship with the brethren are part of being his child. God called us to be in his family, to walk in his nature and his character. You walk in his character and nature, including his power.

But go further to verse 17: if children, then heirs. The reason God's purpose is called sonship is that you are not just a child. If God's purpose were called a family, that would have been all there was to it. But it is called sonship to make clear that God did not just want you as his child. He wanted you as his heir. That is the idea you must have of what it means to be in the image of God: not just a child, but an heir.

An heir is someone who has been allotted what the person giving it has. Consider the example I like to use of Dangote as a very wealthy man. Let us assume he has just a son. I do not know whether he has a son; I know of three daughters. But suppose he says, “This child is not just my child; this child is my heir.”

There are wealthy people who do not make their children their heirs. Perhaps they plan to give them all start-up capital, which is a form of inheritance anyway, but they want to give away their wealth. If he wants to make his child his heir, that will not just mean giving the child his wealth. It means giving him ownership even of the things he owns, the companies. The person becomes responsible for directing them, unless he wants to sell them.

When we say we are heirs of God, it means that what God owns, he gives to us. Something must change in your mind about your role on earth when you understand that you are God's heir. The moment you understand that he has given what he owns to you, the world, you begin taking responsibility for the world and the way it is.

## Our Responsibility in the Cosmos

Some people who talk about national transformation have this in mind. I think there is a way to go about it without giving a wrong idea of what the gospel's mission is. Our mission is not so much a nation or national issue; it is a cosmos issue. Of course, there is no way to talk about that without including the nation.

We are supposed to manage the world after life, as much as it is up to us, knowing that we cannot exhaustively do it. Darkness will get worse even as light gets brighter until the end of the age. But we should have it in mind that we are supposed to manage the cosmos.

I want to write about this carefully, because there are problems on both sides. If I write it one way, some people will be very happy, while people on the other side may think I have sold out. I want to consider the two sides together. I want to address the individual side before the collective side: you are an heir of the world, and you are supposed to participate in the cosmos, even your own microcosm, which may be your community, nation or environment.

So you are supposed to vote, for example. The moment you accept that, as many people do, you cannot necessarily reject the other side, which is now on a higher scale. We are supposed to strive to manage this cosmos the way God wants. You are supposed to exemplify that.

You cannot do it perfectly, because no matter how you get involved in government, you are still the beast. The reason is that you have to govern as a representative of the entire people, including unrighteous people. That means you must still sustain unrighteousness. The system the Lord will meet when he comes is still a system of unrighteousness.

God's purpose is sonship, and there are two main realities: child of God and heir of God. As heirs, we understand our assignment as twofold: natural assignment and spiritual assignment. For natural assignment, I usually want to stay with your academics, career, business or social causes. But in this case, I would add civil engagement. It is not natural assignment in quite the same sense, but we can put it on that side.

Your civil engagement, engaging with the cosmos from that angle, is part of your place as a son of God. A son of God is an heir of the world and is to inherit the earth. We know our sonship will do its full work in bringing this cosmos out of corruption into incorruption. That will be the main thing. Nevertheless, we are still to be engaged in the cosmos today out of our philosophical persuasion, out of what some may call religious belief.

## Religion, Devotion and Human Design

I am bothered about something I discussed in the last session. People keep saying, “Religion has blinded people. Religion is this. Religion is not going to last.” Then someone says, “It is not religion; it is the kingdom.” I think many of these things are just semantics.

Before, when people said religion, I thought they meant self-righteous legalism. Sometimes I do not know what they are talking about any more. Which one do they mean? What religion do you do? Are they saying they want to go into spirituality? “I don't do religion; I do spirituality.” It depends on what you mean by your form of spirituality. Do you mean your methodical belief system and practice?

Everybody has a religion. Religion is about devotion, a devout way of doing things. Even people who do not believe in God will find that you cannot sustain humanity without a level of devotional practice, to different degrees. Functioning metaphysically, not just naturally, is part of God's design for human beings.

You find people with different devotional practices, different causes they fight for and preach. Some even have regular meditation practices. They say it is like a workout for the mind, something needed for regulation. They have an application for it. They even have their own person who leads the meditation, and their daily things to think about. I am joking about their having a pastor and devotion, but do you follow the point? Physically and metaphysically, you will be religious.

When James says pure religion involves looking after widows and orphans, have you not seen people who do not believe in God looking after the earth, animals and other concerns? You will find a way. You may be looking out for minorities, which is good, of course. It is the way you are. You will find an expression for it.

I do not think we need to speak against religion simply as religion. If we are speaking against religion as self-righteous legalism, then I understand. Any metaphysical system built like that is a problem. Even natural systems built that way can be problematic. They create undue pressure and try to force compliance. All sorts of problems eventually come out of it: hypocrisy, rebellion and frustration.

Let us return to what we were saying. God's purpose is sonship. You are his child and his heir. I have mentioned your natural assignment. The second assignment you have as an heir of God is your spiritual assignment. We have a natural assignment and a spiritual assignment.

## What We Maintain and What We Pursue

Whenever people hear about purpose, not even specifically God's purpose, the idea is, “What am I supposed to pursue and fulfil?” Man has a concern for pursuit and fulfilment by design. What am I meant to pursue? What am I meant to fulfil? What am I meant to accomplish?

It is almost as though that design, which is spiritual or metaphysical, is also physiological. People start to die when they have nothing to pursue. You see someone old and frail but living for something, finding a way to keep going until it is done. People were saying that one of our presidents would die, but he did not die until he had finished. I do not know why, and I do not mean that this happens all the time, but people tend to survive when they are pursuing something. It is almost physiological. That is part of human design.

When we talk about purpose in that sense of pursuit and fulfilment, it is our assignment, our place as heirs of God, which accounts for it. When I say, “I am a child of God; I am to fellowship with the Father,” there is nothing there that I am out to finish. There is something I maintain. I keep my relationship with my siblings, even with other people whom I want to become part of our family.

That family mindset is not about trying to fulfil an assignment; it is maintained. There are things we maintain in purpose, but there are things we pursue and fulfil in purpose. I am keeping relationships with people, relating well with my siblings and also with other people who are in God's likeness. I desire them to be saved and to be in God's image. All that is happening.

What accounts for our pursuits in purpose and our fulfilment in them is our place as heirs of God, the assignments he gives us. I have things to do in this cosmos. I have people to preach to so they can be saved, people to disciple so they can walk in God's purpose. I have a natural assignment to fulfil, to serve other people, make things better and move things forward, as should be the case with someone who has God's life.

There is that sense of pursuit and fulfilment in purpose. In pursuing our assignments to fulfil them, one character trait is at the heart and centre of it. That is what we are studying today. The text that helps us in this regard is 1 Corinthians 9.

We said in the teaching on the hidden man that our fulfilment of purpose rises and falls on the hidden man. We can be more specific about what fulfilling our assignment hinges on: it rises and falls on discipline. In this case I am saying, “Discipline, the drive of the Spirit,” because I am speaking from the standpoint of the new man.

As you will see, people can be disciplined without the Spirit. They can be disciplined by the flesh. There is discipline which is the drive of the flesh, and discipline which is the drive of the Spirit.

## Paul's Natural and Spiritual Assignments

Although we are here for 1 Corinthians 9:24–27, I want you first to see the preceding context. Paul was making a case for why he would not stop doing his natural assignment. Even though he could put it aside, he says he will keep doing it. Why did he say that?

Start from verse 1 so that you see the thought that leads to what he will say: “Am I not an apostle? Am I not free? Have I not seen Jesus Christ our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord? If I am not an apostle to others, yet doubtless I am to you. For you are the seal of my apostleship.” He is making a case for his apostleship.

“My defence to those who examine me is this: Do we have no right to eat and drink?” He was given to what people now call a fasted life, and you can learn that. It is not that he fasted always; we do not know that. But he said he fasted often.

“Do we have no right to take along a believing wife, as do also the other apostles, the brothers of the Lord, and Cephas?” Paul was so consumed with and committed to his mission that he set aside his right to marry. He mentions the other apostles, the brothers of the Lord and Cephas. Then, “Or is it only Barnabas and I who have no right to refrain from working?” Can you see the natural assignment? “Have we no right to refrain from working?”

“Who ever goes to war at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat of its fruit? Or who tends a flock and does not drink of the milk of the flock?” Normally, we should be paid in our spiritual assignment. He is saying that, in that warfare, we should be paid. If you plant a vineyard, you should eat its fruit. The people to whom they have sown should be people from whom they reap.

They were still receiving support, but they did not stop working. That is the point. He is saying he was not demanding it. Even this statement shows that those who choose to operate in that model are not necessarily wrong. That is very important.

We will have a way of balancing that model at the right time, when it is easier to do so. Sometimes people need to give themselves entirely to the spiritual assignment, because the nature of their commitment requires their full time. This is a matter of thinking on the truth.

Consider the scale of what pursuing the mission takes today, the kind of dedication required for its many aspects. If you want to be effective in discipling the nations, you have to write books. You need people who are editing. You need people doing many things which require full-time work. There are many things you can still do part-time, but, in my opinion, you need a group doing some things full-time if you want to be effective. There is a way to consider that which is righteous.

Paul speaks about the right to earn from spiritual assignment, not just an expression of honour. He has used natural examples: warfare, planting and animal husbandry. Now he asks whether he is only speaking as a mere man, because Scripture says the same thing.

## The Right to Receive Material Support

What Paul has just done is what I always talk about: he used natural truth, and now he is about to use revelatory truth. “Do I say these things as a mere man? Or does not the law say the same also?”

“For it is written in the law of Moses, ‘You shall not muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain.’ Is it oxen God is concerned about? Or does he say it altogether for our sakes?” You can see that he takes it as a principle. A natural-truth principle is there in revelatory truth and can lead to this conclusion.

“For our sakes, no doubt, this is written, that he who ploughs should plough in hope, and he who threshes in hope should be partaker of his hope. If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your material things? If others are partakers of this right over you, are we not even more?” The case is very clear.

I am showing you how he gets to discipline, and I want to make two points. “Nevertheless we have not used this right, but endure all things lest we hinder the gospel of Christ.” We do not want something to become friction in freely communicating the truth of God's word. This should be the natural disposition of a minister.

Nevertheless, if your eyes are set on the goal of fulfilling the mission, you must be careful about the degree to which your natural assignment takes your attention. This is particularly so when your choosing, election and calling require more of you spiritually. Everybody has something to do in spiritual assignment, but your own election and calling may require more.

Paul's election and calling are not the same as those of, perhaps Ananias, a certain disciple. They are not the same as those of just any person, even though those people should also grow towards becoming deacons and bishops. He says that he has not used this right, but endures all things lest he hinder the gospel.

“Do you not know that those who minister the holy things eat of the things of the temple, and those who serve at the altar partake of the offerings of the altar? Even so the Lord has commanded that those who preach the gospel should live from the gospel.”

I do not think you can escape the fact that it is a biblical truth that people can earn fully and exclusively from spiritual assignment, if you want to be honest with Scripture. There is good reason for it. The Lord has commanded that those who preach the gospel should live from the gospel.

## Holding Together Right, Honour and the Mission

“But I have used none of these things.” Rather than enforcing that right, Paul kept it as a matter of honour. We must find a way to balance this. I do not think a person who is just starting a church work should use it as a right. That is very suspect. But we must find a way to balance the truth of that right with its being a matter of honour.

It is important because, honestly, we take people's time. That is the truth. Time is work, life, result and reward. You can wake up one day as a pastor, already having planned and being driven: “Today is the day for this and that.” Then someone comes. It can be anything. Someone's parent may come to lament about something, not the kind of complaint you have heard before about someone not being serious, but a question about whether this person is doing the right thing in a particular regard.

You can spend an hour and twenty minutes on that one conversation. When you have spent that time, you need to go and spend another hour or more with the person concerned. These are not illustrations; they are realities. You really woke up saying, “Today is the day for this.” Your time is already being shared, and it is not just one person. “Let us talk at this time; let us talk at that time.” It is your work time.

It is wrong to say that people doing this work are not supposed to earn. It is not even sensible. It is a lie. Some people may make ungodly demands, but let us be biblical. They should earn. It should be a matter of honour, but there is also a place for right.

Paul says, “But I have used none of these things, nor have I written these things that it should be done so to me; for it would be better for me to die than that anyone should make my boasting void.”

Even though he says that, let us be honest. If you are a leader in Corinth and do not sit down to consider, “With what he has explained, it means we are not doing something well,” something is wrong. I am not going to say you should do it, but that is how I see it. Corinth was a very large church. He may have been pointing to selfishness. Simply sitting back because he says he is not making a demand misses what he has explained.

“For if I preach the gospel, I have nothing to boast of, for necessity is laid upon me; yes, woe is me if I do not preach the gospel!” That means he must still do the work, yet he is having to do his natural assignment alongside it.

Keep that thought in mind when we get to what he says later. He began with his natural assignment. The real conversation was not about marriage or fasting; it was about that natural assignment. Verse 17 says, “If I do this willingly, I have a reward; but if against my will, I have been entrusted with a stewardship.”

“What is my reward then? That when I preach the gospel, I may present the gospel of Christ without charge, that I may not abuse my authority in the gospel.” He does not have to enforce a right. He preaches without charge. That brings us to verse 19.

## Becoming a Servant for the Gospel

Verse 19 says, “Though I am free from all men, I have made myself a servant to all, that I might win the more.” Notice the movement. Paul has been saying, “I will do my natural assignment so that my spiritual assignment is not affected.” Now he describes his passion for that spiritual assignment: “I am free from all, but I have made myself a servant to all.”

Both points matter. When he speaks about discipline, we must realize that the discipline applies to his natural and spiritual assignments together. He is showing his engagement with both. There is another way we will see this, but that is the first.

“To the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might win Jews; to those who are under the law, as under the law, that I might win those who are under the law; to those who are without law, as without law,” although he is not without law towards God but under law towards Christ, “that I might win those who are without law.” To the weak he became weak, so that he could win the weak.

That is relevant even to writing books. If you know that people are weak in reading, and people in this generation are becoming weaker at it, you must give that serious consideration. “I have become all things to all men, that I might by all means save some.”

He does it for the gospel's sake, so that he may be a partaker of it with them. They can be fellow sharers in it, able to receive it without friction or something hindering their reception. That leads to verse 24.

## Running to Obtain the Prize

“Do you not know that those who run in a race all run, but one receives the prize?” Is this spiritual or natural? It is actually natural. It is an illustration. He is going to apply it to spiritual things, but it is a natural illustration: when people participate in competition, athletics and things like that, they are all running, but one receives the prize.

“Run in such a way that you may obtain it.” The reason he tells them to run in such a way is connected with verse 23, where he says he may be a partaker with them. What he is experiencing, they will start experiencing. Both will be pursuing together.

Of course, the level of their calling may differ, particularly in spiritual assignment, which he has in view. But he says that all who run in a race run, while one receives the prize. Run in such a way that you obtain it. How?

“Everyone who competes for the prize is temperate in all things.” If your translation says “strives for mastery,” that is agōnizomai, the expression for competing. Everyone who competes is temperate in all things. The word egkrateuomai is the word for exercising self-control or discipline. This is what makes them win.

“Now they do it to obtain a perishable crown, but we for an imperishable crown.” That is in both our natural and spiritual assignments. Paul is saying that the reason he wants to keep working is so that the spiritual assignment is not affected. Yet he must fulfil that spiritual assignment to receive the crown.

Your engagement in your natural and spiritual assignments is described as a race in which you are to win a prize. What is needed to win? You must be temperate, self-controlled or disciplined in all things. You are doing it to obtain the crown.

## Clear Goals and a Clear Plan

“Therefore I run thus: not with uncertainty. Thus I fight: not as one who beats the air.” What must be clear? Your goals. But there are two things here: a goal and a plan for fulfilling it.

If you are running towards a destination, you must know how to get there. If you are fighting to win, you must also know how you intend to win. A boxer does not train only with a goal, but also with a plan. “I need to land a punch, but the other person is defending himself. First I will do this to distract him; then I will do that.” There is a goal and a plan.

Once Paul speaks about acting without uncertainty, both things are involved. A plan is the process or order for getting something done. The word is adēlōs, uncertainly. He uses the adverb here, related to the description adēlos. It comes from the negative alpha and dēlos, clear, evident or manifest. What I want to do is clear, evident and manifest. Not just what I want to do, but how I want to do it. The goal and the plans are clear.

What I want to do must be clear, and how I intend to do it must be clear. The goals and the plans are evident. This is part of discipline, the drive of the Spirit. It is not only that I have a general desire. I know the destination and the way I am going towards it.

## The Body Brought into Subjection

Verse 27 says, “I discipline my body and bring it into subjection.” The King James says, “I keep under my body.” Then comes the second thing: “I bring it into subjection.” There are two words. Think about both: I discipline my body, and I bring it into subjection.

The first expression, hypōpiazō, describes beating the body down. It can look like training. I get myself trained, and when people train, they deal seriously with their bodies. Have you seen the different kinds of training people undertake? I saw someone walking on something like a bottle. The person's legs showed what had happened during training. When we saw the person doing it elegantly, wearing the performance clothes, it looked normal. But the person had suffered bruises to be able to do what was required.

That is training to do what you are supposed to do. But it does not seem that this is necessarily what Paul has in mind here, because he says, “I keep my body under; I bring it into subjection.” The second word, doulagōgeō, has the idea of slave-driving. It seems the entire framework he has been using is a slavery framework.

The first expression about beating someone into submission may not be about training yourself hard to acquire an ability. It may be the slave master putting someone under subjection to get him to execute something. “I discipline my body. I slave-drive myself, lest after I have preached to others, I myself should become disqualified.”

Why does he say that? Because of verse 23: we have become partakers in the same race. “Run that you may obtain.” Other people might go ahead and obtain while I am disqualified from obtaining. The reason he would fail to obtain the reward of his natural and spiritual assignments is lack of discipline.

Let us get this clear: fulfilling God's purpose rises and falls on discipline. What Paul gives is a description of discipline. After mentioning it, he starts describing it. “I run not with uncertainty. I fight not as one who beats the air.” I am very clear on my goals and my plan. Then, “I discipline my body, and I bring it into subjection.”

Is he only introducing the concept of slave-driving at that point, or is it the concept he has been working with? We can argue that he uses a slavery metaphor, a slavery description for the whole process, for how discipline works.

The master says, “You must get this done,” and makes the goal clear. Can that be part of discipline when we are speaking about certainty? Yes. Then there is subjecting yourself to the process. You cannot give an excuse; the master insists that it be done. Then he drives you through the entire process, ensuring that you do it. That too belongs to the picture.

## Pharaoh's Taskmasters and the Daily Quota

Egypt gives us a clear example of how slave masters work. When Moses went to deliver Israel, Pharaoh treated their request as evidence that they had too much ease. He set the work before them, refused help with gathering the materials and enforced their doing it. They were whipped. Let us look at Exodus 5 rather than only referring to it from memory.

Moses and Aaron told Pharaoh, “Thus says the Lord God of Israel: Let my people go, that they may hold a feast to me in the wilderness.” Pharaoh answered, “Who is the Lord, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I do not know the Lord, nor will I let Israel go.” They said that the God of the Hebrews had met with them and asked to go three days' journey into the desert to sacrifice to him.

Notice that at this point the demand is expressed as going to worship, rather than total liberation. From a critical scholarly angle, there are different arguments about this. One could consider whether the slaves initially sought permission to worship and how that related to their eventual departure. We accept the revelation, but there are also questions of reconstruction which we consider in bibliology, including discussion of the possible early Egyptian reference to Yahweh.

Pharaoh says, “Moses and Aaron, why do you take the people from their work? Get back to your labour.” The people are many, and he accuses them of making the people rest from their work. That same day he gives orders to the taskmasters and officers.

Who normally does the slave-driving? The slave master. When Paul says, “I slave-drive myself,” there is a master and a slave, but in this case he himself is both. Keep that picture in mind.

Pharaoh commands that the people no longer be given straw for making bricks. They must gather it for themselves, but the quota of bricks must not be reduced. I thought the quota was increased, but notice what it says: the same quota remains. Pharaoh calls them idle because they are asking to sacrifice to God. “Let more work be laid on the men.” The additional work may be gathering the straw, while making the same number of bricks.

You might consider whether there was already a movement among the people towards worship, with Moses arriving as deliverer amid that. There are different possible reconstructions. They had been crying to the Lord before Moses was sent. Perhaps going to worship was initially proposed as a compromise. Such questions can have some value when critical scholarship is used for reconstruction rather than simply for fault-finding or dismissal. A biblical scholar who also engages critically can ask how the account fits together without making unbelief the motive.

But the point we need here is straightforward: who sets the quota and drives the people to fulfil it? The slave master. When I say discipline, the drive of the Spirit, in this context the Spirit is the slave master.

But Paul did not mean that every disciplined athlete necessarily has the Spirit. There is discipline which is not of the Spirit. Greed can be someone's slave master. That is why we speak of mammon being a person's god. Something is driving and ruling that person: “Because of this, you must do that.” It is not necessarily anything good. The existence of a strong drive does not itself tell you that its source is the Spirit.

The taskmasters tell the people that Pharaoh will give them no straw. They must find it themselves, yet none of their work will be reduced. The goals and the process have been laid out.

The people scatter to gather stubble, and the taskmasters force them to hurry, saying, “Fulfil your work, your daily quota, as when there was straw.” There is a clear goal, and there is a clear plan. Something has been added to the process, gathering the straw, while the required quota remains.

The officers of the Israelites are beaten and asked why they have not fulfilled their task in making bricks yesterday and today as before. They appeal to Pharaoh because they have been given no straw, but are still told to make bricks. The taskmasters beat the leaders for not fulfilling their tasks.

## A Forceful Drive, with Right Goals and Plans

Think about how lightly we can treat our own assignments. After the evening prayer watch, no one has beaten you, yet you say, “I can't kill myself.” It depends. Sometimes, if you could have done it, you should have done it. Sometimes you need to change your goal and your plan. Pharaoh's goal and plan here were wicked. I also thought of showing you the account of Solomon, another instance in which that forceful discipline was not the best thing.

Slave-driving is not a gentle expression. It describes a forceful drive. In the case we are considering, where does that drive come from? The Spirit. We are considering the Spirit as taskmaster, slave driver or master. That is the picture I want you to understand in Paul's description.

The goal is not separate from that picture. The master sets a clear goal, sets a clear plan and enforces the doing. The taskmasters forced the people to hurry, telling them to fulfil their work. The drive urges execution. So the master drives the goals, the plan or order, and the execution or diligence.

## Discipline as Strength Towards and Against

Let us examine the word translated being temperate. The verb is egkrateuomai, and the noun is egkrateia. Both come from the adjective egkratēs, used in Titus 1:8. The adjective describes being in strength concerning something. The first part has the sense of “in,” en, which takes this form for pronunciation, and the second concerns strength. The idea is a state or condition of being in strength about something. Notice both parts: being in strength, and being in strength about something. We will qualify what that means. The adjective is used once, in Titus 1:8.

Titus 1:8 describes a leader as hospitable, a lover of what is good, sober-minded, just, holy and self-controlled. Discipline is a vital qualification for leadership. A leader should have developed to that point. The one who fulfils his assignment must be temperate in all things, in strength concerning all things.

One might use the word masterful, but I think a useful way to understand it is being in strength towards something or away from something. Discipline can mean strength towards or strength against. You are in strength against, or away from, unrighteous things, and in strength towards righteous things.

Because Paul describes discipline through slave-driving, I will call that strength drive. He describes someone driving you to do something: setting the goals and driving you towards them, setting the process and driving you to follow it, then driving you through the steps of doing it.

Discipline is being in strong drive about all things, either strong drive towards or strong drive against. When you should be strongly against something but are not, the drive is weak. We say you are weak in discipline. Sometimes the strong drive against something means fleeing. There are things you should be strongly against and things you should be strongly towards.

## Beginning from the Lowest Point

When you wake up to the day's work, or wake up to prayer, there should be a strong drive towards doing it. I have often said that no matter how low a believer is, you cannot be lower than having the nature of God in you. If his nature is in you, whatever happens, you can speak the word. That is the least you can do. The Spirit of faith, the Spirit of God, is in you.

Suppose you have been missing prayer and feel very low. You can still say, “I am born of God. I have discipline.” You cannot be so low that you cannot say that. Keep saying, “I am disciplined. I am disciplined to wake up at the right time.” From there, begin praying in the Spirit about that same matter. You are setting yourself up to do what you should do.

If you do not do that, what alternative do you have? Are you resigning yourself to walking in the flesh? That must be the least response you can make: “I am disciplined.” You begin with what is true of you and build that drive.

## Discipline Throughout the Christian Walk

The noun egkrateia is used four times in three verses. In one of these uses, it can describe the entire walk in the Spirit. Someone can be strong in the drive to love, having disciplined himself to walk in love. You cannot use love to describe discipline in that same sense. You can say, “I love to be disciplined,” but that is not what I mean. I am talking about relational love. Yet discipline can qualify any other gift of the Spirit. You need discipline in natural assignment, spiritual assignment and relationships.

In relationships, know where you should be strong towards people, showing up for them, and where you need to be strong in moving away or drawing boundaries. Do not be careless. You can be disciplined to be loving, kind and tender. You can train yourself to be full of tenderness and be strong in the drive to act that way. Discipline can describe the entire Christian walk.

Acts 24 gives an example. Felix came with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish, and sent for Paul to hear him concerning faith in Christ. Verse 25 says Paul reasoned about righteousness, self-control and the judgment to come. Righteousness concerns God making us right; self-control or discipline concerns our walk; judgment concerns the end. He reasoned about salvation, our walk and the end. Discipline is used to describe the Christian walk.

Galatians 5:22–23 names the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, meekness and temperance. Against such there is no law. We can consider that statement from the standpoint of love, and also from the standpoint of discipline, because discipline encompasses the expression of these things.

Second Peter 1:6 uses the noun twice. Verse 5 begins, “For this very reason, giving all diligence.” Diligence itself can be understood as part of discipline. Your faith is the starting point, and you bring diligence alongside it. Add to your faith virtue, to virtue knowledge, to knowledge self-control, and to self-control perseverance. You bring in discipline.

The verb occurs in 1 Corinthians 7:9 and 9:25. We have been studying the latter, but let us also see the former. With a right understanding of discipline, we understand that the Spirit in us is meant to be our slave master. The Lord is the Spirit. The Spirit is an expression of Christ's lordship in our lives. And this is the Spirit with whom we are one.

## The Discipline Involved in Remaining Single

First Corinthians 7:8 says to the unmarried and widows that it is good for them to remain as Paul is. Then verse 9 says, “But if they cannot exercise self-control, let them marry. For it is better to marry than to burn with passion.” Exercising self-control is the verb here. If they cannot be disciplined in this way, they should marry.

There is a kind of discipline involved in remaining single, and not everybody has it. This is why, after teaching who we are in Christ, I sometimes say that there are things in which you should not try to strain the nature of God in you. Even God's word recognizes some things as an extra burden for which you may not be qualified. Believe God and trust him for favour where you know that this is your situation.

This is not the same as gambling or taking drugs. If a person is kept away from drugs, he may go through a lot, but he cannot use what he cannot access. The same can be said of gambling, alcohol and other vices. But you cannot be separated from your sexuality in that way. That is the reality. If you do not have the grace for this kind of discipline, go and marry.

We all have discipline, but here we are speaking of a particular kind of discipline. You know where you are. You can trust God and say, “God, you see me and you see my heart. I love you. We need to solve this matter.” That is different from pretending that there is no issue.

Someone says the wedding cannot happen until years from now. What are we doing? There should, of course, be a reasonable time of courtship in which you know one another. But there is also a point at which unnecessary delay does not make sense. You cannot merely say, “We are praying,” as though that removes every consideration the passage gives.

## Do Not Turn Faith into Presumption

When Jesus was tempted to jump down, Scripture was quoted about angels having charge over him and keeping his foot from striking a stone. Jesus answered, “You shall not tempt the Lord your God.” That is relevant when someone keeps saying, “You have discipline,” while inviting you to put yourself in unnecessary temptation.

Someone says, “Come and visit me,” in a situation where you know what the temptation is. You can answer, “You shall not tempt the Lord your God.” Even the Lord whom we are describing as your master, the Spirit, should not be treated presumptuously. Do not say, “The Spirit will hold me, keep me and drive me,” while choosing to test that in a situation you should avoid.

Do not let people delay you unnecessarily in matters where you should move forward, but do not misunderstand what I am saying either. We must keep the reasonable courtship, the discernment and the actual instruction of God's word in place.

## Build the Drive Daily

The entire Christian faith can be described as a walk in discipline. Once you know God's purpose, who you are and your assignment, you know that the hidden man determines whether you fulfil that purpose. One of the first things you need to build in that hidden man, to bring alongside your faith, is discipline.

Yes, you need love, joy, peace, kindness, meekness, gentleness and the supernatural. But very primarily, you need discipline: the drive of the Spirit. You make the Spirit your master, driving your focus or goals, your order or plans, and your diligence, execution, actions and activities.

You can be strong or weak in that drive. If you build and strengthen it, it can carry you through the day and through weeks. Sometimes people come for special meetings and really build the drive of discipline. For the next few days, they are getting things done. They built it during the meeting, but then expect it to sustain itself. Afterwards, they wonder what happened and begin to feel condemned.

Build it daily. This is primary. The master drives your focus: “This is the quota you must fulfil today.” He drives the goals and the plans. When we pray that we fulfil God's purpose by discipline, we should know what must be done. The Spirit is driving what we are supposed to do, the process by which we should do it and our action in doing it.

You can look at someone who has built this and wonder whether it is possible. That person built it too. Start. Even if your starting point is lying on the bed, sunk in laziness and unwilling to stand up, start there. Say, “I am born of God. I have the discipline of the Spirit. I am strong in the drive of the Holy Ghost to do all I am to do.”

Start at that lowest point until you rise, until you are sitting up and doing what you should do. Build discipline, no matter how bad things have become. For the sake of God's purpose for your life, or what people call destiny, build discipline every day. For the sake of the assignment God has called you to, build it.

Taskmasters do not show up only when they feel like it. They are there every day to ensure that the work gets done. You cannot weaken your taskmaster, your slave driver, and expect to do what you should every day. Be strong in the drive of the Spirit to fulfil your assignment, strong towards righteousness and strong against unrighteousness.

There are times when you can tell that your drive against something is weakening. You know it. Then stay in prayer and build strength in that regard: “I am against this. I am not for this.” Build that strength in the Spirit rather than allowing the weakening to continue.

## Keep Discernment and Boundaries

I believe there is a great deal you can do by the Spirit of God in relation to sexuality. But I also believe you should do it within a reasonable time, because that is the point Paul is making. If you know you want to marry, do not try to strain the new creation and the reasonable discipline that should operate in you beyond that.

Trust God for his favour, and do not remove walking in God's word and discernment from these matters. Desperation can make you lose your boundaries, act foolishly and become permissive. Do not risk that. It is worse. Keep the instruction and the discernment together.

Walk and become stronger, or become stronger and walk, in discipline. It is the drive of the Spirit.

## The Spirit's Drive and the Flesh's Drive

I have not developed the drive of the flesh as fully as I intended. But Paul first uses discipline in a natural illustration, then applies it to us. Galatians 5:23 shows us discipline as an expression of the Spirit. There can also be a drive which comes from the flesh.

Man was ordinarily designed by God to know what is right. Otherwise, how would he become saved? If you hold that man is so totally depraved that he cannot know what is right, you will not be honest with Scripture. He can know what is right and choose to do it. But doing it from a place without redemption is itself sinful, because it is born of pride and is about self. I cannot explain that as fully as I would like here, but keep that distinction in view.

## Strong to Fulfil the Daily Assignment

I walk in discipline. I grow stronger in discipline. I am strong in the drive of the Spirit towards righteousness, and strong in the drive of the Spirit against unrighteousness. I am strong in the drive of the Holy Ghost to fulfil my natural and spiritual assignments.

Every day I am getting stronger in the drive of the Spirit, causing me to walk in all I am to do that day. I fulfil my daily quota. Every day I fulfil my daily quota in my spiritual assignment under the rulership of the Holy Ghost.

Your spiritual assignment: what you say you will do, do it every day by the Spirit of God. Your natural assignment: what you have set out to do, do it every day. Set the right plan. Have the right goals. Go after the day as you have laid it out by God's Spirit.

You are born of God. No matter how low you are, you can walk in faith for discipline. Start getting back on your feet and going after it.
