# Leadership in the Body of Christ

## The Body of Christ in God's Purpose

We're looking at leadership in the body of Christ. I want to start by showing that what we call the body of Christ is one of the descriptions of what God wants in his purpose. If I say God's purpose, you might answer in its most accurate form that God's purpose is sonship. Then, if I ask what that means, it means being his child and his heir. God's purpose is a family. One of the ways that purpose is described is as the body of Christ.

Go to Ephesians 1:9: “Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he purposed in himself.” God has made known the mystery of his will, what he wants, according to his good pleasure, what he likes, which he purposed in himself. What God wants and likes, he purposes in himself. The next verse says, “That in the dispensation of the fullness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ.” He is showing us the result of what he had on his mind, the end towards which it was directed.

In this case, the reference to the fullness of times is because he knew there would be sin and he would have to do redemption. Reading from Ephesians 1:7 gives us a better picture: “In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace; which he made to abound toward us in all wisdom and prudence.” In Christ we have redemption, and he has made his grace abound towards us, having made known the mystery of his will according to the good pleasure he purposed in himself.

He did this so that in the dispensation of the fullness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven and which are on earth, in him. What God's purpose was headed towards is this gathering of people in Christ. Redemption has now accomplished that. In redemption, he has done all he has done based on what was on his mind, so that he can gather us together in Christ.

## Everything Summed Up in Christ the Head

Notice the expression in Ephesians 1:10, “gather together in one.” The word is anakephalaioō. I hope I'm saying the word correctly. I am looking at the parts of the word, ana, up or again, and the part connected with the head. The idea I am pointing out is things adding up, everything being brought together in one point, summed up under one head.

If you pay attention to that use, you see a body with a head, where every other part is summed up in the head. Paul makes reference to that head being Christ in Ephesians 1:22: “And he put all things under his feet, and gave him to be head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fullness of him that filleth all in all.” What are the things added up in Christ? His body is added up in him.

When Ephesians 1:10 speaks of gathering all things together in Christ, both which are in heaven and on earth, it is showing that God was seeking the point where he would bring about the body of Christ, where everything would be added up in Christ. That is the point I want you to see. He was seeking to bring about his family, to bring about the church, to bring about the body of Christ. Everything is added up in Christ.

See another reference in Colossians 1:18: “He is the head of the body, the church, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence.” Jesus is called the head of the body, the church. When we talk about the body of Christ, what are we talking about? The church. The church, or the body of Christ, is God's destination in his purpose. God wanted to bring about the church because that is his family. What God wanted is a family that is his.

## A Household and a Dwelling Place

In Ephesians, you see the church described again in the sense of a family. Ephesians 2:19 helps us understand it as God's purpose: “Now therefore you are no longer strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints, and members of the household of God; having been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief cornerstone.”

Another way of talking about Jesus as chief cornerstone is that he is the head of the corner. Everything is added up in him. It is like the entire building being brought together at the head. The cornerstone is the stone to which everything is joined. We see this concept again: “In whom the whole building, being fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord; in whom you also are being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit.”

We can call it the body of Christ, the body where everything ends up in Christ. We can call it God's family. This is what God was heading towards. In the fullness of times he gathers together in one; he sums up in one head all things in Christ. Perhaps “sum up in one head” helps you see the thought. The head is Christ, and everything he is summing up is his body. The body of Christ is God's purpose, a community sharing his life and nature.

One of the best ways to understand the idea of the body of Christ is as a community sharing the same reality. There is a way you can talk about a body where every part is different, and another way where you are saying all the parts share the same reality. Both pictures are used for the church. The body can explain different functions and responsibilities, and it can also explain the sameness we have.

For example, if you talk about a body of lawyers, you are using body in the sense that they all share the identity of lawyers. A body of doctors, perhaps the Nigerian Medical Association, or a body of lawyers, the Nigerian Bar Association, is a community sharing the same identity. The term body of Christ refers to the church, which can be used to describe God's purpose as a family, a community sharing the same reality.

## The Family in Heaven and on Earth

If you have followed everything I have said about the body of Christ so far, almost all the things I have pointed out are a spiritual reality. “That in the dispensation of the fullness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven and which are on earth, in him.” It is a spiritual community. In Ephesians 2, we are no longer strangers and foreigners. We are fellow citizens with the saints, members of God's household, built on Christ, making up his dwelling place.

Notice that he says both which are in heaven and on earth. In that sense, you may include those who have passed from the body, whose engagement is strictly heavenly and no longer earthly, corporeal or physical. Ephesians 3:14–15 speaks of the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom the whole family in heaven and on earth is named. There is a family which ends up in Christ and is in heaven and on earth. Heaven and earth can show the distinction of those who are no longer involved on earth but only in heaven. Yet he is speaking about one community.

This idea of the church can also be seen in Hebrews 12:22: “But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, to an innumerable company of angels, to the general assembly and church of the firstborn who are registered in heaven, to God the Judge of all, to the spirits of just men made perfect.”

Notice that where we have come is the general assembly and church of the firstborn. We are all part of this church, these called-out ones, ekklēsia, of the firstborn. We are all headed up in Christ. He is the head of the body, the church, as Colossians 1:18 says. In this same place, we have also come to the spirits of just men made perfect. Hebrews 11 had already established that these people were just by faith but were perfected with us at the resurrection. Without us they could not be made perfect.

The Lord Jesus, at the resurrection, brought about his body, God's family, a community sharing the life and nature of God. He became the kind after which all of us are. We share his life and nature based on the work he has done for us, the redeeming God made incarnate for the work of redemption. One of the vital descriptions of God's purpose is the body of Christ, the church, the family in heaven and on earth, that dwelling place of God.

## “I Will Build My Church”

This spiritual sense of the church can be seen in the teaching of the Lord Jesus. Matthew 16:18 says, “I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.” Jesus spoke about building his church. This is what Ephesians 1:10 was speaking about: bringing about a gathering of people who are God's family through redemption. By redemption he builds his church.

“I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.” Death will not be able to stop it, death in all its ramifications. Death is the authority behind the first death, the second death and Hades. The authority of Hades, the gates of Hades, the grounds on which people enter it, will not prevail against his church. We are a people completely brought out of death, saved from death. Hades has nothing on us. The second death has nothing on us.

The Lord Jesus has built his church, that community, that body of his. Death did not prevail against it and cannot prevail against it. I know people like to use “not prevailing against it” as an ongoing fact on the earth, and there is nothing wrong with believing that as well. We have his life, and death in all its ramifications, including persecution and the evils of this world, cannot prevail against the church. But the primary truth there is the direct work he did. Death and its power did not and cannot prevail against us.

## The Church as a Natural Community

The interesting truth is that the body of Christ is not only a spiritual community. It is also an institution, an organization and a natural community. When he speaks about the church in Matthew 16, we see the spiritual community. By Matthew 18, we begin to see the church spoken of as a natural community, organization or institution.

Someone says, “The church is not an institution.” These are English words. The church is first a spiritual community which then leads to a natural community, organization or institution. Someone says, “I don't like organized religion; I just do spirituality.” What do you want, disorganized religion? Sometimes people do not know precisely what they have a problem with. They are trying to identify something, but the words they are using may not help them identify it.

There is a simplistic way of thinking where you use one brush to paint everything. You see one thing in one place, call it organized religion, and paint everything with that brush. “Churches are just full of hypocrites. Don't mind them. Pastors, everybody is hypocritical.” They are human beings like you, growing spiritually, just as you may sometimes be hypocritical. We have all kinds of people in the church. That is the nature of it. Everybody needs to be perfected and trained; everybody needs to grow. One more imperfect person is not going to ruin the church.

The church is a natural community, and the church as a natural community was instituted by the Lord. That is why I use institution. Jesus laid down how things were to be. He did not only say he would build a spiritual community, which he did and which people enter by faith. He also instituted a natural community. Matthew 16 uses church more on the spiritual side. Matthew 18 shows its use on the natural side.

## Reconciliation, Forgiveness and Restored Fellowship

Matthew 18:15 says, “Moreover, if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother. But if he will not hear, take with you one or two more, that by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established.”

When you are trying to reconcile with someone, people often say that we forgive before people repent, whether they repent or not. That is true. But there is another sense of forgiveness which requires repentance. In fact, the reason you can go and tell the person his fault is that, on one hand, you have already forgiven him. You are not holding anything against him. You can tell him, “You were wrong about this, and you should repent.” But the forgiveness which restores fellowship requires him to repent.

That is similar to what Paul was talking about in 1 Corinthians 5. If someone takes his father's wife, you may have no grudge against him. In that sense you have forgiven him. Yet there is a kind of forgiveness towards him which requires his repentance, which you have not given and should not give without repentance. Otherwise, you become the one in sin.

Even if the person refuses to repent, you still love him. When the Lord says to count him as a heathen or a tax collector, you still have the responsibility Matthew 5 gives you towards an enemy: love him, bless him, pray for him and do good to him. You know that the relationship is not right, but you still love the person. We cannot be in fellowship as though nothing happened when there is something from which you need to repent.

There may be an offence which you have brought up, and the person remains rebellious. You have forgiven him, and you love him, but the relationship is not right because he ought to repent. Unconditional fellowship or acceptance is not in the Bible, and it is dangerous. We must know what walking in love means. Some people think love is docility, accepting everything. Then they complain that God is putting them through it. That is not true. Walking in love does not stop justice.

## Love Does Not Remove Justice or Discipline

If they arrest a terrorist who has caused serious levels of harm and evil, I think he should face capital punishment. Some people will argue about capital punishment, but I think justice needs to be meted out. That is the government's job towards him. It does not mean I do not love him. I love him; I wish he were saved. I wish he were not going to be killed. I wish he did not have to go through that. But having done it, even if he repents, they have to do what they have to do. Even he should accept the consequence. It is necessary as a deterrent, as retribution, and for everything it is meant to accomplish.

You can love someone who is abusive and still seek justice. Do not let anyone gaslight you. You can love the person and allow him to go through justice. Love for other people may also require that. You love this person, and you also love the people who could suffer from his actions. Let him go through the process so that the needed change can happen or the same situation can be prevented from happening to others. That too is love. You can love your neighbour enough to think some people are better off behind bars or facing the full consequence of the justice due to them.

Train your heart rightly so you are not confused or fooled. You need to know what love is in action, instead of misinterpreting its expression. Love does not take away discipline. It does not take away displeasure. Love has a way of expressing displeasure. Do not mix those things up.

The Lord says that if someone offends you, do not simply say it is nothing. Talk about it. Bring it up. If he refuses to hear, perhaps arguing about it, take one or two others and bring it up. If he still will not hear, tell it to the authority he should hear. If the person continues refusing, you leave him to the consequences of his action.

## Church Authority in Matthew 18

The Lord mentions telling it to the church. He intends church authority here, because he begins speaking about binding and loosing, which concerns that authority. Church leadership is in view. Very clearly, the church here is not merely some spiritual community. You have to tell it to the church, a natural community.

The instruction has a progression: first between you and your brother alone, then with one or two others, and then, “If he refuses to hear them, tell it to the church.” If he refuses to hear the church, “let him be to you like a heathen and a tax collector.” There is a real community which can hear the matter, and there is leadership in that community.

He says “the church,” which they understood. It would be applicable to the synagogue to which they belonged. They could go to the leadership and say, “This is what is happening. I have tried to resolve it. I have taken one or two others, but this is where things stand.” They tell it to the church.

Then he shows that what he is saying applies to his church. There was a foreshadowing, although it did not yet reflect everything he intended. Verse 19 says, “Again I say to you that if two of you agree on earth concerning anything that they ask, it will be done for them by my Father in heaven.” Then verse 20: “For where two or three are gathered together in my name, I am there in the midst of them.”

Even this gathering was meant to be in the light of his redemptive role, around the truth of the redeeming God. The choice of “in my name” and “I am there in the midst of them” shows that what he is saying applies to his church. There is the body of Christ as a spiritual community, and there is the body of Christ as a natural community instituted by the Lord.

Matthew 16 foreshadows the founding of the church. Matthew 18 foreshadows its institution as a natural community. The founding concerns there being this community at all. Now where does he institute it? Go to Matthew 28.

## The Commission to Make Disciples

“All authority is given to me in heaven and on earth. Go therefore and make disciples.” Notice that he does not say, “All authority is given to me, therefore go and cast out demons.” This text is not primarily about believers' authority. It is about ecclesial authority, the authority of the church.

Whenever this issue of authority in heaven and on earth has appeared in this material, it has been relative to the church. In Matthew 16, after “I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it,” verse 19 says, “I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven. Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.” Keys, authority in heaven and on earth.

In Matthew 18, he tells them to take the matter to the church. If the person refuses to hear the church, he is to be treated as a heathen or tax collector. Then he speaks of what is bound on earth being bound in heaven, and what is loosed on earth being loosed in heaven, in the light of the church he has just mentioned.

So when he says in Matthew 28, “All authority is given to me in heaven and on earth; go therefore,” he is talking to his apostles. Verse 16 says that the eleven went away into Galilee, to the mountain Jesus had appointed for them. This authority would extend to Matthias later, making the twelve. When they saw him, they worshipped, although some doubted. He came and said, “All authority is given to me. Go therefore.” It is his authority, and they are to go in the light of it.

I know we may draw implications for believers' authority from this subsequently, but what is directly in view is ecclesial authority, the authority of the church or church leadership. “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations.” Here we see the Lord instituting the natural community of the spiritual church with the commission of discipleship.

Spiritually, we are already God's family, whether or not we are physically gathered and whether or not there is a natural community present. Wherever we meet, we recognize that we share the same faith in Christ. We are happy to see one another, recognize one another as our own and greet one another with a holy kiss. That relationship is already there because we are God's family. But the Lord did not only found a spiritual community. He instituted a natural community, and he instituted it on the premise of discipleship. “Go and make disciples.” That is the commission around which he instituted this natural community.

## The Natural Community in the Book of Acts

We see this in action in Acts 2. After the preaching on the day of Pentecost, three thousand people were saved. They received the word and were baptized. Verse 42 says, “They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers.” We see the church in action, a natural community centred on the apostles' teaching and discipleship.

From verse 46, the same people continued daily with one accord in the temple and broke bread from house to house. This community continued in the temple; this community continued from house to house. They ate their food with gladness and simplicity of heart, praising God and having favour with all the people. “And the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved.” He calls that community the church.

The community which continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine, fellowship, breaking of bread and prayers, meeting in the temple and in houses, was called the church. The Lord instituted this natural community on the premise of discipleship. The body of Christ is a family, but it is also an institution and organization based on the mission or commission of discipleship. That commission has a mission. There is something he wants the church to accomplish.

There is the spiritual community founded by the Lord through redemption, and the natural community instituted by the Lord on the premise of discipleship. Acts 9:31 shows several such communities: “The churches throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and were edified. And walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, they were multiplied.” There were several communities spread across those places.

A believer is not only a member of a spiritual community. He also ought to be a member of a natural community where he receives discipleship and walks in its effect. He is walking in God's purpose and carrying out the mission of the church. The church has a mission, and a believer ought to be there, receiving discipleship and walking in what it produces.

## Leadership Authority for Edification

Leadership is one of the major features, if not the primary feature, of the body of Christ as a natural community or institution. Everywhere we have seen the church spoken of, this issue of authority has arisen. Matthew 16 and Matthew 18 both speak of ecclesial authority, an authority expected to be exercised by leadership. Later passages refer to the authority leadership has in the name of Jesus. This is leadership authority which the Lord Jesus gave at the inception of the church.

Paul refers to that authority in 2 Corinthians 13:10. When the Lord instituted the church as a natural community, he instituted it with leadership authority. Paul says, “Therefore I write these things being absent, lest being present I should use sharpness, according to the authority which the Lord has given me for edification and not for destruction.”

We have seen in teaching about the expression of anger in love that sharpness can be used, but it is not the primary way we function. First there is a settled displeasure about what is wrong, and we explain persuasively. We say, “This is not okay. This is why it is not okay.” Then there is corrective reminding: “I told you this was not right. Why are you doing it again? Don't do it again.” The tone is beginning to change, but you are still reminding the person correctively.

Sharp rebuke goes further. “Why are you being so recalcitrant? What is wrong with your heart? Why are you stiff-necked?” You see expressions such as stiff-necked, hardened or hypocritical. Jesus used hypocrite. That is sharp. It goes beyond merely saying, “I told you that was not right.” Yet even in sharpness, be measured. People may appeal to a biblical use of “fool,” but that was not something to be said every day.

Paul says he could use sharpness according to the authority the Lord gave him for edification, not destruction. Do not use it to destroy people so that they lose themselves in the process. The church has authority to edify. It may use sharpness in speaking and correction, but the purpose is to build up.

First Corinthians 4:21 says, “What do you want? Shall I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of gentleness?” You can see that the default expression of love is gentleness rather than the rod. There is an authority church leadership has to correct, and it must be handled according to its purpose.

## Ecclesial Authority Has a Defined Scope

Second Corinthians 10:8 says, “Even if I should boast somewhat more about our authority, which the Lord gave us for edification and not for your destruction, I shall not be ashamed.” I am showing you ecclesial authority. Notice that he is not simply saying the Lord gave this to every believer. There is believers' authority, as when Scripture says these signs will follow those who believe. But there is also ecclesial authority, and we must be aware of it.

Verse 14 says, “For we are not overextending ourselves, as though our authority did not extend to you, for it was to you that we came with the gospel of Christ.” The supplied wording about authority is helping to show the thought. Those who bring the gospel to us, and whom we receive for discipleship, have a discipleship or ecclesial authority in relation to us. If someone preaches the gospel to you, you believe and you submit to that person for discipleship, he has ecclesial authority over you.

We must define that authority. It is not authority over your whole life, to tell you everything you must do. It is ecclesial authority pertaining to the order of the church, and you are supposed to submit to that. Questions of submission and accountability need their own teaching, but our focus here is leadership in the body of Christ.

One thing is clear: among the primary features of the church as a natural community instituted by the Lord is leadership. Its leaders have authority given by the Lord. They have ecclesial authority. Now we must notice that this leadership provision is established for all generations.

## Leadership Is Part of the Design of the Body

The Lord has already instituted that authority across time. It is a matter of order. We may look at when he gave it to those people in Matthew 28 and say, “He gave it to the twelve, and that is it.” But it was planned for all time. Look at Ephesians 4:7: “To each one of us grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift.” How many of us? Each of us. When we find out what the gift of Christ is, we realize grace is given according to the measure of that gift.

What is the gift of Christ? Verse 8 says, “When he ascended on high, he led captivity captive, and gave gifts to men.” He is speaking about people, believers, being given as gifts to the world, to humanity. He gave gifts to men. Verse 9 asks what “he ascended” means, except that he first descended into the lower parts of the earth. “He who descended is also the one who ascended far above all the heavens, that he might fill all things.”

He is giving us an Exodus picture. They passed through the sea, described as the lower parts of the earth, and then came out and went onto the mountain. He ascended that he might fill all things. Then he gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers. What did Jesus give to men? Apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers. And he gave grace to each of us, as verse 7 says, according to the measure of Christ's gift.

In Jesus' design of his body, anybody who is part of his body has grace according to his measure of functioning as Christ's gift. There is a design of the body where each member is meant to function in leadership. That gift described as apostle, prophet, evangelist, pastor and teacher is leadership. Apostles are people sent; prophets are spokespersons; evangelists announce or herald the message; pastors and teachers shepherd by teaching.

The gift Christ gave is leadership, described with five or four terms. We cannot go into all the detail in this teaching; we will probably do that in level four. But instead of seeing those words as distinct terms for different people, they are descriptive terms for every leader in the church. Each of us has grace according to our own measure of this leadership gift.

What is the gift Christ gave to the world? Leadership gifts: apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers. Each of us has grace according to our own measure of that. When you carry out the work of the church, preach the gospel to people and they believe, you then have ecclesial authority to lead them in discipleship in God's purpose. This authority is built into the church. It is not only handed out from time to time.

“To each one of us grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift.” If we understand leadership as merely a discretionary thing the Lord does from time to time, we do not have a whole picture. There is a place to discuss discretion and election, but one thing is clear: leadership is built into the body of Christ. Each person in the body has grace according to the measure of the leadership gift he exercises as he carries out the mandate of the church to the world.

There is an expectation in God's design for everybody to partake of his own grace according to the measure of leadership in which he functions. When someone in the church functions in his leadership gift and perfects you, that causes you to do the work of ministry. Then, by the grace in you, you can end up perfecting others. That is the design of the church. Every member is designed to function with the leadership gift according to his own measure.

## Psalm 68 and the Responsibility Given to God's People

Ephesians 4:8 quotes Psalm 68. Let's look at that briefly; we will expand on it in level four. The point we want to establish is that God has an expectation that every member of the body can end up a leader. He has planned for it by making grace available for the measure of the leadership gift in which you function. There is leadership grace available for your measure in the body of Christ.

Remember where I started: Jesus instituted the natural community, the church, and gave leadership or ecclesial authority. This is not believers' authority. It is leadership authority people can enter into. We are now seeing that this leadership authority and grace are planned for the entire body to function in, because of the nature of the mission of the church.

Psalm 68:17 says, “The chariots of God are twenty thousand, even thousands of thousands; the Lord is among them as in Sinai, in the holy place.” Read the preceding material and you see the Exodus of the Israelites. They have come out through the sea and arrived at Mount Sinai. The ministry of angels has accompanied them. The angels are there in their multitudes.

This is like the description of the church in Hebrews 12:22–24, where there is an innumerable company of angels. They are among God's people in the holy place. Then Psalm 68 says, “You have ascended on high, you have led captivity captive; you have received gifts among men.” Here it is speaking about God's role. He has ascended, led them on, led captivity captive and received gifts among men.

There is a discussion to be had about “captivity captive.” You can see it as the former captors now displayed as captives in victory. I have also leaned towards seeing those who were in captivity becoming his captives. But I think that latter argument is weaker in the light of a case that has been made. I want to explain that better, and I did not prepare that explanation for today.

He says, “You have received gifts among men, even the rebellious, that the Lord God might dwell there.” The word “from” which some wording supplies is not the point I am making. He has received these people as gifts and brought them out. They are to be gifts among humanity, even among the rebellious, so that the Lord might dwell there. They are supposed to take the work of the Lord among every people.

Then he says, “Blessed be the Lord, who daily loads us.” Notice the supplied expression “with benefits.” The word about loading is a word of burden or responsibility. Blessed be the Lord who gives us responsibilities, the God of our salvation. When he brought them out of Egypt and brought them to Sinai, he gave them the responsibility of being kings and priests. “You shall be a kingdom of priests.” He gave them leadership responsibility.

When Ephesians 4 says each of us has grace, it is similar to what was said about the Israelites. Each now had responsibility as kings and priests towards the world, even among the rebellious, to ensure that God would dwell there, that his sanctuary would be among those people. That is the picture we are seeing.

Is there leadership grace for everybody in the body of Christ today? Yes, according to each person's measure of the leadership responsibility or leadership gift Christ gives to the world. There is grace for everybody according to that measure. In the Great Commission there is the potential for everyone to end up a spiritual leader. Leadership is available in the body of Christ. There is the potential for everyone to end up a spiritual leader.

## Desiring Leadership and Being Qualified

We can take this further and say that spiritual leadership is a goal we ought to have in our spiritual assignment. Let me say a goal, not the whole goal. In our spiritual assignment, our goal is to do everything God wants us to do. But spiritual leadership is a vital goal within that assignment. If I faithfully do God's will for my life, I ought to end up functioning as a spiritual leader. It is something I can desire and expect.

This is why 1 Timothy 3:1 says the desire for oversight is a good one. There is nothing wrong with wanting to carry out God's will so faithfully that you function as a leader. There is nothing wrong with functioning at further levels of leadership when you want to push forward God's agenda, purpose and will, carrying out the mission of the church.

“This is a faithful saying: if a man desires the position of a bishop, he desires a good work.” The expression for that position speaks of oversight or leadership. Then the passage says, “A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, temperate, sober-minded, of good behaviour, hospitable, able to teach.” That man desires a good work, but he must be qualified.

Leadership is a possibility for every member of the body of Christ as he carries out his spiritual assignment or the work of ministry. This leadership is meant to unfold through discipleship, the discipleship that makes one qualified, and through God's orchestrations. It is meant to unfold by discipleship which makes one qualified, as well as by God's orchestrations.

I will bring this teaching towards its close with two vital points. Every believer ought to desire and expect leadership in the church. I ought to expect that I am supposed to end up a leader in the church. If I am being perfected as a saint, it will get to the point where I grow to be able to function as a leader. Every believer ought to expect and desire leadership in the church.

## Every Part Doing Its Share

In Ephesians 4, the perfecting of the saints leads to the work of ministry, which leads to the edifying of the body of Christ. When I receive the ministry of the church in discipleship, it leads me to do the work of ministry. As I do the work of ministry, there is an increase in the body, and I can then lead others.

Look at verse 16: “From whom the whole body, joined and knit together by what every joint supplies, according to the effective working by which every part does its share, causes growth of the body for the edifying of itself in love.” The body of Christ is knit together by the supply of ligaments. How do those ligaments get supplied? According to the effective working by which every part does its share.

Do you have your own measure in the body of Christ? Every part has a share to do. When we do the work of ministry, we do our own share of the work God has allotted to us, our inheritance, our assignment in ministry. By doing that there is an effective working, which is the grace of God. The grace operates as each part does its share.

When I function in the grace of God and do the work of ministry, more people are joined to the body of Christ. People are joined by this supply of ligaments, which comes from the grace by which I do my share. This causes the growth of the body and its edification in love. So I should expect discipleship. I should expect discipleship in the church as I continue in God's purpose for my life.

But should leadership itself become my focus? No. My focus should be discipleship. If I know God has a spiritual assignment for me, I should focus on being discipled so that I can carry out and fulfil that assignment. Any believer who is going to walk in God's purpose and faithfully carry out his spiritual assignment needs to receive discipleship. He needs to receive it in the local church.

## Do Not Enter Leadership Before You Are Ready

If you are not qualified and put yourself into leadership, you will have to face what follows. In fact, if you know you are not qualified and someone offers you leadership, you should reject it and tell them why. Tell them the specific reason you are not qualified.

This does not mean people should reject leadership indiscriminately. That is not right. It is something you should desire and expect. But suppose you know you could end up walking in immoral ways which become a snare to the church or to people. You should tell them, “I have this tendency. I cannot do this yet.” If you accepted the responsibility and then find yourself doing such things, be the first to say, “I don't think I am prepared. Let me step away from this.”

Perhaps you know you may steal the money. Perhaps you may abuse the women or exploit people sexually. Whatever the particular danger, some things are especially serious in spiritual leadership: financial exploitation, sexual exploitation and the exploitation of people's purpose, lives and time. Time is a way of describing life. You do not exploit people's time, their finances or their sexuality. These are very important matters.

There are people who are not qualified yet. You can believe you will become qualified later; you can desire leadership. But if you are not qualified now, hold on. Trust God and say, “I know you have called me to this. Perhaps because of my past, I am not yet ready or prepared. I am not capable of doing this properly yet.” Then allow the Lord to mould, shape and train you.

You can come back when you are ready, prepared for the Master, a vessel useful for every good work. But jumping in too early, trying to become a spiritual leader at all costs, is dangerous. It is worse when you have no accountability. If you know you are not capable, do not play around with that.

As I often say, the devil you are playing with is not playing with you. If he comes for you, the whole situation may look like an orchestration. It is not good. You will not like what has happened, and afterwards you may ask yourself whether it was worth it. You could have been patient. You could simply have done what was right.

This is very important. There is leadership in the body of Christ. You have leadership grace; you have your own measure. Be faithful to your measure. Receive discipleship. Unfold and grow and fulfil God's purpose.
