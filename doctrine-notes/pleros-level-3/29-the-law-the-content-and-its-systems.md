# The Law: The Content and Its Systems

## Why This Teaching Matters

We are looking at the law, the content and its systems.

One truth in God's word we cannot afford to ignore is what the law really means and what its implications are. If we do not understand the law and its implications, it can affect us adversely. Not understanding it does not mean you will not become a victim of its negative system. It is better to be prepared against the potential of the negative system of the law than to know about the law vaguely and say, "I only know Jesus. I only know Christ. I only know redemption. I only know new creation, and that is enough."

You can fall into the trap of the Galatians if you are not prepared against it, if you are not armed with clarity. Paul had to start teaching them about the law, about what it means, about some of its implications, about the whole scope of how they should be thinking about it. It is as though if he had taught some of that earlier, it would have served as a defense against them falling into the trap they did.

This was a serious concern in the early church. Before the church had to start dealing with Gnostics, one of the first major problems was Judaizers, legalists. It revolved around this issue.

## The Law as Content and Systems

When we say "the law," what exactly do we mean?

That word is an open word. In our minds, we often immediately think of the law of Moses. But really, it could be law of anything. The law just means law. It can mean teaching. It can mean instruction. It can mean what you have been told to do. It can mean guideline. There are laws in nations, laws in communities, laws in churches, laws in organizations.

So when we say "the law," what law are we talking about? Beyond saying the law of Moses, we may even be saying the law of God. That is why I called it the content and its systems.

Which law are we talking about? What is its content? And even when we have identified its content, that still does not resolve all the issues. Because despite one content, there can be different systems of that same content. That is very important.

## Galatians and the Danger of Another System

Look at Galatians 1.

Paul says, "I marvel that you are turning away so soon from him that called you in the grace of Christ to another gospel." That phrase, "the grace of Christ," already tells you about a particular system. He says they are turning away from something gracious, something benevolent, something to do with what someone has done for you freely, into another gospel.

They were not necessarily saying Jesus is not the Christ. That was not the issue. So what was going on? What was that subtle and important issue we must be aware of?

Paul says, even if we, or an angel from heaven, preach another gospel, reject it. Even if someone you trust has stature, if they posit a different message, reject it.

The issue can involve the same content and yet produce a different message, a different system.

Then in Galatians 5:1 Paul says, "Stand fast in the liberty wherewith Christ has made us free, and do not be entangled again with the yoke of bondage." What he is calling a yoke of bondage there is the law, but we must be careful. The law is not always spoken of negatively. The issue is not that the law as content is bad. The issue is that the law can be spoken of as a system in operation.

That is why in Galatians 3 he can say, "Is the law against the promises of God? Certainly not." Then in verse 24, "The law was our tutor to bring us to Christ." So the law had a role. But now that Christ has come, your attitude to the law must change.

In Galatians 5:2-4 he says, if you become circumcised, Christ profits you nothing. He testifies that everyone who becomes circumcised is a debtor to keep the whole law. He says, "You have become estranged from Christ, you who attempt to be justified by the law."

There is a way "the law" is being spoken of there with a negative connotation, and yet Paul is still clear elsewhere that the law is not bad in itself.

## Romans 7: The Law Is Good, Yet There Is a Problem

Romans 7 presents the same challenge.

Some preachers are dedicated to saying the law is bad, and they ignore texts that speak positively about the law. But if the law were bad, how could the same law be fulfilled in us?

There are texts that speak very positively about the law, and there are also texts that seem to cast the law in a very bad light. The same person can speak of it as something good, and then in another place speak as though it is bondage. So what is going on?

Paul says in Romans 7:5, "When we were in the flesh, the sinful passions that were aroused by the law were at work in our members to bear fruit to death." Then in verse 6 he says, "But now we have been delivered from the law."

That is a very strong statement. Delivered from the law. Set free from the law. It sounds severe. Yet immediately he has to clarify. Verse 7: "What shall we say then? Is the law sin? Certainly not." He says he would not have known sin except through the law. "You shall not covet" made coveting clear.

Then from verse 9 onward he says, "I was alive once without the law, but when the commandment came, sin revived and I died." Notice how he moves between law and commandment. The commandment was supposed to bring life, but he found it brought death. Why? Not because the commandment itself was evil, but because sin took occasion by the commandment.

So in verse 12 he says, "Therefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good." Then verse 14: "For we know that the law is spiritual."

So the law is holy. The law is just. The law is good. The law is spiritual. It is of God. It is righteous. That means if we see texts where the law is painted negatively, we must know that what is in view there is not simply the content of the law, but a particular system at play.

This is why it is important to understand the law fully. The content and the systems of the law are always around us. We need to know what the law is, and what its systems are.

## What Really Is the Law?

The first question we have to answer is: what really is the law?

The best text to answer this question is probably the book of Proverbs, because Proverbs is a book dedicated to teaching the law to one's child. In Deuteronomy 6 they were told to teach the law to their children. Proverbs gives us the mindset of people who took that assignment seriously.

Before we go there, look at Romans 5. Paul says that sin was in the world before the law, and then says death reigned from Adam to Moses. So there is a uniqueness to the way "law" is used regarding Moses. Yet the words law, commandment, statutes, instruction were used before Moses as well. So there is both a general reality and a special Mosaic turning point.

Now look at Deuteronomy 6. In Deuteronomy 5 the Ten Commandments are given. Then in chapter 6 Moses begins to teach from them. "Now this is the commandment, and these are the statutes and judgments which the Lord your God has commanded to teach you." Then comes, "Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God, the Lord is one." Then they are told to teach these things to their sons and their grandsons.

So by the time we get to Proverbs, we are watching someone obey that instruction. He will even tell you that his own father and mother taught him, and now he is teaching the next generation.

## Proverbs: The Law as Truth, Wisdom, and Life

In Proverbs 1:8 the word Torah is used: "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother."

Then Proverbs 3:1 says, "My son, do not forget my law, but let your heart keep my commands."

Proverbs 4:1 says, "Hear, my children, the instruction of a father, and give attention to know understanding, for I give you good doctrine: do not forsake my law." Then he says, "When I was my father's son... he also taught me."

Now go back to Proverbs 1 and notice everything that leads up to "do not forsake the law of your mother."

"The proverbs of Solomon... to know wisdom and instruction, to perceive the words of understanding, to receive the instruction of wisdom, justice, judgment, and equity, to give prudence to the simple, to the young man knowledge and discretion... The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge... My son, hear the instruction of your father and do not forsake the law of your mother."

So what is he doing when he teaches the law? He is teaching so they can know wisdom, instruction, understanding, justice, judgment, equity, prudence, knowledge, discretion. If the result of the law is wisdom and understanding, then what is the law?

The law is the truth.

That is the answer. The law is God's truth.

Proverbs 3 confirms it. "My son, do not forget my law... let not mercy and truth forsake you. Bind them around your neck." That language of binding around the neck is the same language used for the law. So the law is being spoken of as truth.

Proverbs 23:23 makes it even clearer: "Buy the truth and do not sell wisdom and instruction and understanding." If buying the truth gives you wisdom, instruction, and understanding, then at the root of wisdom is truth. So when Proverbs 1 says the law is being taught so that you may gain wisdom and understanding, what is being given? Truth.

So what is the law? The law is the truth. The truth of God.

Proverbs 4 says, "I give you good doctrine; do not forsake my law." Then, "Get wisdom, get understanding." If they were teaching the law and telling the child to get wisdom, what were they teaching? Truth.

Proverbs 6:20 says, "My son, keep your father's command, and do not forsake the law of your mother." Then verse 23 says, "For the commandment is a lamp, and the law a light. Reproofs of instruction are the way of life."

The law is a light. It is how God speaks to people on the earth by his word. If you put God's word on your heart, it will guide you. When you roam, it will lead you. When you sleep, it will keep you. When you awake, it will speak to you.

And one of the first things Proverbs shows the law saving you from is bad influence and sexual folly. Proverbs 1 says, "If sinners entice you, do not consent." Proverbs 6 goes into the seductress. Why? Because one of the first things God's word does is shape your environment and your influences. If you are in the wrong environment, you are already in trouble. Do not put yourself in compromising positions and then claim to be standing on God's word. The law on the heart will make you careful with your environment.

Proverbs 7 says, "Keep my commands and live... write them on the tablet of your heart. Say to wisdom, 'You are my sister.'" Again, wisdom and understanding. And we already know that at the root is truth.

So what is the law? In all honesty, the law is the truth.

There is nothing wrong with the truth. The issue is that the truth can have different systems depending on how it is used.

Proverbs 13:14 says, "The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to turn one away from the snares of death." Proverbs 28 says those who forsake the law praise the wicked, and whoever keeps the law is a discerning son. Proverbs 31:26 says, "On her tongue is the law of kindness."

So ordinarily, as far as the law is concerned, it is a content. It is the truth. Yet that same content can be used in different systems.

## Commandments in Proverbs

The word commandment in Proverbs works the same way.

Proverbs 2:1 says, "My son, if you receive my words, and treasure my commands within you... then you will understand the fear of the Lord and find the knowledge of God." So his words and his commands lead to wisdom, understanding, discernment, and the knowledge of God. That is truth.

Proverbs 4:4 says, "Let your heart retain my words; keep my commands and live."

Proverbs 10:8 says, "The wise in heart will receive commands, but a prating fool will fall." But you must not take a verse like that and use it manipulatively. The entire content of the book tells you what those commands are: commands of truth. The hearer is not supposed to throw away discernment. He must check whether what is being said is aligned with truth.

Proverbs 19:16 says, "He who keeps the commandment keeps his soul, but he who is careless of his ways will die."

So again, do not joke with living by the truth.

## The Law and the Tree of Life

Can we say the law is the tree of life?

Proverbs 3:13 says, "Happy is the man who finds wisdom... She is a tree of life to those who take hold of her." We know wisdom is rooted in truth. We know the law teaches truth and gives wisdom. So in that sense, yes, we can say the law is the tree of life.

At the beginning, did God command man? Yes. Can we therefore call that the law? Yes. The law is what someone instructs, commands, directs, guides, and teaches.

So when Genesis 2 says God took the man and commanded him, that is law. Therefore, at the beginning, man rejected the law. Man rejected the truth. Man rejected God's purpose.

## The Law Before Moses

Now we can argue clearly that there was law before Moses.

The first part is law at origin. The second major point is law with Abraham.

Look at Genesis 26:5. God says of Abraham, "Because Abraham obeyed my voice and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws." So the language of commandments and laws is there before Moses.

Can we say Abraham believed and walked in the truth? Yes. That is what it means.

Then Genesis 18:19: "I have known him, in order that he may command his children and his household after him, that they keep the way of the Lord, to do righteousness and justice." That sounds like Proverbs. It is the same movement: righteousness, justice, truth, instruction.

So there was law before Moses. The issue of the law was always present, even before Moses.

You even see the word law used before Sinai in Exodus. In Exodus 12 there is "one law" concerning the Passover. In Exodus 13 the Lord's law is to be in their mouth as a memorial of redemption. In Exodus 16 God says he will test them whether they will walk in his law or not. In Exodus 18 Moses says he makes the people know God's statutes and laws. All that is before Sinai.

So the content of the law was already there before the Sinai covenant.

## What Happened at Moses?

If the law was already there, what exactly happened at Moses that made it such a turning point? What was the issue with the law upon Moses?

It was not a different content. Deuteronomy was still teaching the same truths. Proverbs is still teaching the same truths. The content is not the issue.

The issue is that at Moses there was a covenant, an arrangement.

Never before was an entire people bound to be regarded as a people of the law in that way. Normally, it is your obeying the law that makes you a person of the law. But now a people were elected to be a people of the law, to enter into an agreement to be regarded that way.

They were entering into an agreement to reflect God's purpose and plan. They were to depict sonship, worship, priesthood, inheritance, the people of God, the reality of redemption. God wanted a people through whom his purpose and plan could be shown.

Romans 9 says of Israel that to them pertain the adoption, the glory, the covenants, and so on. They were chosen to reflect sonship. "Israel is my son, my firstborn" in Exodus is not saying that every Israelite was automatically a true son in the redemptive sense. It means they were chosen as the people through whom those realities would be depicted because through them the Christ would come.

So what is the old covenant? It is an agreement between God and Israel for Israel to reflect the reality of God's purpose and plan as his people.

That is not God's primary agreement with man. God's primary agreement with man is redemption: I will redeem you, and you must believe. That is the new covenant in its basic redemptive principle. He had that with Noah. He had that with Abraham. The Sinai arrangement was a special arrangement for depiction.

## Exodus 19: The Agreement to Reflect

Exodus 19 makes this very clear.

God says, "You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself. Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, then you shall be a special treasure to me above all people... and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."

That was an exclusive agreement. They would be the people regarded as God's people among the nations. They were agreeing to reflect God's purpose. They were agreeing that the whole nation would carry that role.

This was not the normal pattern of how God relates with people. Normally, individuals hear truth, believe, and walk in it. Here, an entire people were taken into a covenantal arrangement to represent something.

They should still have known that this was not the final reality. It was special. It was tutorship. It was depiction.

## The Lion Illustration

Think of it with a strange illustration.

Imagine someone comes and says to a whole nation, "For one month, all of you must reflect a kingdom of lions." Let us say there is even an injection that could turn you into an actual lion. But the agreement does not require the injection. The agreement simply says you must behave as lions, reflect lions, function like lions.

Common sense might tell you that if you can actually become one, that is better. But the covenant itself only demands that you reflect it.

That is what happened with Israel. The covenant demanded that they reflect God's truth, reflect God's people, reflect righteousness, reflect priesthood, reflect sonship. It did not itself necessitate faith, even though if you paid attention to the content of what was being taught, it should have led you to believe.

So someone could actually be a person of that truth and walk in it by faith, or someone could simply be trying to comply with what they had agreed to reflect.

## Two Systems From One Content

That is the key point.

From one content, because of that agreement, two systems can arise:

1. A faith system.
2. A compliance system.

One person hears the truth, believes, and walks in it.

Another person hears the truth and thinks only in terms of compliance: "This is what we must do because this is who we are supposed to be."

Before that agreement, if you heard God's law, the issue was simply whether you would believe and live by it. But from Sinai, a pressure entered: we must be like this; we must reflect this; we must comply.

That is the turning point.

## Abraham and the Faith System

Galatians 3 confirms that Abraham was in a faith system relative to the law.

"Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness." "Those who are of faith are sons of Abraham." "The Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel to Abraham beforehand."

So Abraham heard God's word and believed. That is the law functioning in a faith system.

Normally, that is how the law should function. You hear God's truth, believe it, and then the righteous expressions come.

But from the agreement with Israel, another system became common: not first believing and embodying, but simply trying to comply.

The agreement itself did not necessitate faith. It required reflection. Yet if you listened carefully to the content, it should have dawned on you that you ought to believe and then walk.

So again, there is a faith system and a compliance system.

## Hebrews 11 and Expressions of Faith

Hebrews 11 shows that even before Christ's coming, people had expressions of faith.

Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, all these people acted in righteousness because they believed in the redeeming God. They aligned themselves with what was right because of faith, not because they were trying to satisfy covenantal compliance.

That is important. They were not doing righteous things with the intention of merely complying. Their actions were born of faith.

Now, could someone before Moses have heard God's law and still adopted a compliance mentality? Yes, potentially. That means the negative system is a world issue, not merely a Jewish issue. The potential exists in this world for a person to hear truth and think, "I just have to comply," instead of, "I believe, and I now express."

So Sinai brought that issue into clearer form, but the potential is a systemic human issue.

## The Negative System of the Law

Upon Moses, we see the clearest entrance of what we may call a negative system to the content of the law.

The ordinary system of the content of the law is a faith system, and it is positive.

1 Timothy 1 is very helpful here. In Ephesus they were again dealing with Judaizers.

Paul says, "Charge some that they teach no other doctrine." Then verse 5: "Now the purpose of the commandment is love from a pure heart, from a good conscience, and from sincere faith."

That is what the law in its content was meant to produce. Love from a pure heart, good conscience, and sincere faith. It was meant to produce people walking in love because they had believed in the redeeming God.

The first part of the law was faith in Yahweh, the redeeming God. The latter part was walking in love and righteousness.

But if instead of that you teach people a pressure system, a compliance system, a legalistic system, you lead them toward hypocrisy, frustration, and possibly rebellion.

Paul says in verse 8, "We know that the law is good if one uses it lawfully." Then he says the law is not made for a righteous person, but for the lawless, the insubordinate, and so on.

The best way to understand that is that the compliance system of the law is not what governs the righteous. The content of the law is absolutely needed even for the unbeliever to be saved, because the content is truth. But that law as a compliance regime is not for the righteous person. A person who is truly of faith does not function by that system.

This is why it is vital to distinguish the law as content from the law as system.

## Galatians 3: Works of Law and the Curse

Galatians 3 says, "As many as are of the works of the law are under the curse."

That idea of works there includes compliance. Some people narrow it to Jewish identity markers only, but it is broader than that. It includes identity compliance, behavioral compliance, customary compliance, practical compliance. The whole orientation is compliance righteousness.

If you are under works of law, you are under the curse because you can never meet the demand. You can never be righteous by self-righteousness. Faith-righteousness has already been made right and then expresses righteousness. Compliance-righteousness keeps trying to meet an end it can never attain.

That is why he says, "The just shall live by faith. Yet the law is not of faith; the man who does them shall live by them." In that compliance sense, he must keep doing, keep complying, keep trying to arrive. But he never arrives.

Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law. That means all those demands that you cannot meet, all those consequences the law exposes about your condition, Christ has borne and dealt with them. In him righteousness is granted at the start, so that righteousness can now be expressed.

The promise to Abraham came first. Then four hundred and thirty years later came the law in that covenantal form. The later covenant could not cancel the earlier promise of redemptive righteousness by faith.

So what purpose then did the law serve? It was added because of transgressions, till the Seed should come. That must be read in the light of the tutorship role. God wanted to depict his purpose through a people, knowing that not all of them would be of faith. Therefore a compliance structure would expose what he wanted and also expose transgression. It would function as a tutor.

Paul says, "The law was our tutor to bring us to Christ."

## The Old Covenant Was Not the People's Idea

It is important to say this clearly: the old covenant was not merely the people's idea.

Sometimes people speak as though God wanted one thing, and the people spoiled it by saying, "All that the Lord has said we will do." But reading the text carefully, we cannot make that claim. God himself proposed the arrangement. He wanted a people through whom his purpose would be depicted. Through them Christ would come. Through them adoption, glory, priesthood, worship, inheritance, and covenantal realities would be displayed in shadow form.

So God did not wrong them by giving them the law. Sin was already reigning in the world. Even without the law, sin would still lead people into sin. Under the law, sin works through the law. Without the law, sin still works. In both cases, the need for redemption becomes clear.

The law simply gave a clearer tutorship and depiction.

## The Problem of Teaching Compliance After Christ

What is truly wrong is when someone who should now be standing in redemption is put back under the negative system of the law.

That is what Paul is fighting. Not the truth of God. Not righteousness. Not wisdom. Not obedience as the fruit of faith. He is fighting the pressure system, the self-righteous system, the compliance system.

That is why he says, "Stand fast in the liberty wherewith Christ has made us free, and do not be entangled again with the yoke of bondage."

Can believers get entangled with that yoke again? Absolutely. Many do. They live under pressure to comply rather than standing in what Christ has fulfilled and walking in it.

This can even happen while people are preaching grace. You can preach grace with a compliance system. That is the danger.

## Redemption and the Law

What did redemption do to the law?

There are many senses in which we can answer that.

One, all the demands of the compliance system are met in Christ. The law shows what is right, but we could not do it. Christ has dealt with that.

Two, the law exposes the consequences of sin, the condition of man, the handwriting of requirements against us. Christ dealt with that condition. It is not that he did something special only for the law; rather, he dealt with the sin and death that the law was exposing.

Three, the distinctions the law maintained in its tutoring role have reached their purpose in Christ. The representational, depictive role attached to Israel has reached its end in actuality. What the old order was depicting has now arrived in Christ. So the pedagogical distinction has served its purpose.

So when people say Christ is the end of the law, we must handle that carefully. He is not the end of God's truth. He is the fulfillment of what the law was aiming at, exposing, and tutoring toward. The compliance system has no claim over the believer. The content reaches fulfillment in Christ and in those who are in him.

Romans 8 says what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending his own Son. He condemned sin in the flesh "that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit."

That is crucial: fulfilled in us.

## The Law in the Epistles and in Our Lives Today

How then should we think of the law today?

As to the compliance system, it is spoken against. We have no business returning to it.

As to the content, the law is fulfilled in us. So we now speak of the law as something fulfilled in our nature and expressed in our walk.

Romans 8 begins, "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus... For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free from the law of sin and death." The righteous requirement of the law is fulfilled in us.

Romans 3 helps again. Paul asks, "By what law is boasting excluded? By the law of works? No, but by the law of faith." That means we can even speak of systems this way. There is a law of works, a compliance mode. There is a law of faith, the proper mode for the believer.

So if I am under any law now, I am under the law toward Christ. That is Paul's language in 1 Corinthians 9. He says that to those under the law he became as one under the law, to those without law as one without law, though he himself was not without law toward God but under law toward Christ.

That means: I am not under the compliance system, but I am not lawless. I am under the law toward Christ. I am in a faith system of the law. The content of God's truth is fulfilled in me, and I stay in faith to walk it out.

So the law in our lives today is this: the content of the law is fulfilled in us, and we are under the law of faith.

## The Practical Warning for Today

This is why when anything tries to bring us into identity compliance, behavioral compliance, customary compliance, practical compliance, we must be very careful.

Sometimes people have genuine concerns about practices and customs, but they begin to use legalistic tones. There is a tone that brings back what Paul fought against. He would not use that tone.

You can speak about practices in their value. You can point out what is right. You can instruct. You can correct. But do not use an ungodly tone for righteous things.

Do not bring people back under yokes of bondage.

## The Law of Faith in Parenting, Pastoring, and Practice

This applies practically in everyday life.

It can happen in parenting. It can happen in pastoring. Very commonly.

Someone may reject the compliance system in one area and use it in another. They may refuse legalism in doctrine, but use legalism in parenting. Or use it in pastoring. But you have to stay with the faith system.

Surround people with faith and love. That is how they do right. That is the righteous thing to do. Point out the wrong, correct it, instruct how to get to the right, but you cannot force the right by the volume of your voice.

If you move into the compliance system, you will see hypocrisy, stiffness, hardness of heart, frustration, rebellion, depression. That system has been tried. It has been tested. It is not the best.

Stay with the faith system. It may require patience. But it is the right way.

If someone wants change in an area, take it that it may take time. If someone is overweight and wants to lose the weight, miracles can happen, but ordinarily it will take time. You settle it: this thing will go off. You keep at it.

Same with growth. Same with transformation. Same with discipleship. Same with pastoring. Same with parenting.

Stay with the faith system.

## Final Confession and Wrap-Up

So the law: the content and its systems.

The law is God's truth. That is the content.

And with respect to systems, we have seen a faith system and a compliance system.

The compliance system is not for us. The content of the law is fulfilled in us. We are under the law of faith.

Say it:

The content of the law is fulfilled in me. I am under the law of faith. I am not under any compliance system of the law. The content of the law is fulfilled in me. I am righteous now. I have the righteous nature in Christ. The content of the law is fulfilled in me. I am under the law of faith. I am not under the compliance system of the law.

That is the practical application for our lives.

Stay with the faith system. Leave the compliance system. It is not for you. Nobody can handle it. Stay with the faith system. It is okay. No matter what, stay there.
