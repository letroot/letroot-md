# The Law: The Content and Its Systems

## Why This Teaching Matters

One truth in God's word we cannot afford to ignore is what the law really means and what its implications are. If we do not understand the law and its implications, it can affect us adversely. Not understanding it does not stop someone from becoming a victim of its negative system.

You can say, "I only want to know Jesus. I only want to know redemption and new creation." But if you are not armed with clarity about the law, you can fall into the same trap as the Galatians. Paul had to begin teaching them about the law, what it means, and how they should think about it, because they had already started slipping.

In Galatians 1:6 Paul says, "I marvel that you are turning away so soon from him that called you in the grace of Christ unto another gospel." He is speaking about a subtle issue. They were not necessarily denying that Jesus is the Christ. Yet something had shifted them away from the grace, benevolence, and liberty of Christ.

That is why in Galatians 5:1 he says, "Stand fast in the liberty wherewith Christ has made us free, and do not be entangled again with the yoke of bondage." Then he speaks about circumcision and being a debtor to keep the whole law. So there is a way "the law" is being spoken of that carries a negative connotation in that context. Yet in Galatians 3:21 he can still say, "Is the law then against the promises of God? Certainly not."

So we must distinguish carefully. The law is not always being spoken of in the same way. Sometimes the issue is the content. Sometimes the issue is the system in which that content is functioning.

## What We Mean by "The Law"

The law is an open word. In itself, "law" can mean teaching, instruction, command, guideline, what someone says should be done. There are laws of nations, communities, organizations, churches. So when we say "the law," what law are we speaking of?

That is why I called this teaching "the law: the content and its systems." The content matters, because we must ask what the law actually contains. But the content does not settle every issue, because the same content can operate in different systems.

This is why in the New Testament the same word can be used positively and negatively. Romans 7 says things that sound severe: "delivered from the law," "the sinful passions aroused by the law," "the commandment which was to bring life I found to bring death." Yet the same chapter says, "the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good," and again, "the law is spiritual."

So the law is not simply bad. The issue is more careful than that.

## Proverbs Shows What the Law Is

To answer the question, "What is the law?" Proverbs is one of the best places to go, because Proverbs is like an expository training book. It is someone teaching the law to a child, in line with Deuteronomy 6, where Israel was told to teach the commandments to their children.

Deuteronomy 6 begins with Moses saying, "This is the commandment, and these are the statutes and judgments which the Lord your God has commanded to teach you." Then: "Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength." These words are to be in their hearts, and they are to teach them diligently to their children.

So when Proverbs speaks in father-to-son language, it is functioning within that assignment.

Proverbs 1:8 says, "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother." Proverbs 3:1 says, "My son, do not forget my law, but let your heart keep my commands." Proverbs 4 says, "I give you good doctrine; do not forsake my law," and then Solomon says his own father taught him and told him to keep the commandments and live.

Now look at what Proverbs says the law is doing. Proverbs 1 opens by saying its purpose is to give wisdom, instruction, understanding, justice, judgment, equity, prudence, knowledge, discretion. Then it says, "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge," and immediately after that, "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother."

So what is the law giving? Wisdom, instruction, understanding. And Proverbs 23:23 says, "Buy the truth and do not sell it, also wisdom and instruction and understanding." That means at the root of wisdom, instruction, and understanding is truth.

So what is the law? The law is God's truth.

Proverbs 3 confirms it: "Let not mercy and truth forsake you; bind them around your neck." That language matches Proverbs 1, where the father's instruction and the mother's law are what become an ornament around the neck. So law, truth, wisdom, instruction, and understanding are being tied together.

Proverbs 6 says, "My son, keep your father's command, and do not forsake the law of your mother. Bind them continually upon your heart... For the commandment is a lamp, and the law a light." That is how God speaks to people on the earth: by his word. If his law is in your heart, it leads you. When you sleep it keeps you; when you awake it speaks with you.

That is why Proverbs keeps showing the law saving you from bad influence and sexual corruption. Proverbs 1 says, "If sinners entice you, do not consent." Proverbs 6 says the law keeps you from the flattering tongue of the immoral woman. Proverbs 7 says, "Keep my commands and live." The point is that the word of truth protects your environment, your choices, and your path.

If you have God's word in your heart, it will guide even ordinary life. It will tell you what to do next, where to pay attention, what relationship to handle carefully, what assignment to develop. The law is not just abstract material. It is light.

Proverbs 13:14 says, "The law of the wise is a fountain of life." Proverbs 3:18 says wisdom is a tree of life. If wisdom comes by truth, and the law gives wisdom because the law is teaching truth, then in that sense we can say the law is tree-of-life in content. The law is God's truth, and there is nothing wrong with the truth.

## The Law Existed Before Moses

If the law is God's truth, then the law did not start with Moses.

At origin, God commanded man. In Genesis 2 God took the man and gave him command. If law means God's instruction, command, direction, and truth, then there was law at the beginning. Man rejected the law. Man rejected the truth. Man rejected God's purpose.

The law was also present with Abraham. Genesis 26:5 says Abraham "obeyed my voice and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws." That is before Sinai. Genesis 18:19 says God knew Abraham so that he would command his children and household after him to keep the way of the Lord, to do righteousness and justice. That sounds very much like the concern of Proverbs.

So the law before Moses is real. The law as God's truth did not begin at Sinai. Anyone who was of faith was, in that sense, walking in the law.

Even in Exodus before Sinai, the words law, statutes, and commandments are already being used. In Exodus 12 there is "one law" concerning Passover. In Exodus 13 the Lord's law is to be in their mouth. In Exodus 16 God says he will test whether they will walk in his law or not. In Exodus 18 Moses says he makes the people know the statutes of God and his laws. So the content of the law is already there before the covenant at Sinai.

## What Changed with Moses

If the law already existed before Moses, then what exactly changed with Moses?

The issue was not mainly a new content. The issue was a covenantal arrangement.

At Sinai, a people were bound in an agreement to be regarded as a people of the law, a people who would reflect God's purpose and plan. Exodus 19 says, "If you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, then you shall be a special treasure to me above all people... and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."

Romans 9 explains the role of Israel in that arrangement: to them pertained the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the service of God, and the promises. They were chosen to depict God's redemptive purpose, worship, priesthood, inheritance, and sonship. "Israel is my son, my firstborn," in that representational sense.

So the old covenant was an agreement between God and Israel for Israel to reflect his purpose and plan as his people. That does not mean a person could not truly be of faith within Israel. Of course a person could. But the arrangement introduced a national obligation to reflect what God wanted.

This is where the pressure appears.

Before that arrangement, if a person heard God's law, the question was simply whether he would believe and walk in it. But once an entire nation entered a covenant to be that people, a second possibility became very pronounced: not just faith in the content, but compliance to the demand.

That is where the distinction between systems becomes critical.

## The Two Systems: Faith and Compliance

The same content can operate in two systems.

There is a faith system of the law. A person hears God's truth, believes the redeeming God, and then expresses righteousness from faith. Abraham is the pattern. Galatians 3 says Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness. Those who are of faith are blessed with believing Abraham.

Hebrews 11 shows this repeatedly. Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, and others acted by faith. They were not doing righteous things with the mindset of trying to satisfy an external compliance arrangement. They believed God, and righteousness was expressed.

Then there is what we can call the compliance system of the law. Because of the Sinai agreement, people could take the same truth and approach it as a demand they must keep in order to maintain a status. The issue becomes, "We are Israelites. This is who we must be. We must comply."

So from one content, two systems can arise: someone genuinely believing and embodying the truth, or someone trying to comply with what he has been told must be reflected.

That is why I said the law in itself is not the problem. The truth is not the problem. The problem is what system is now at work around that content.

## Why the Apostles Speak Negatively About "the Law"

Now we can understand why the New Testament sometimes speaks so severely.

Romans 7 can say we were delivered from the law, that sinful passions were aroused by the law, and that the commandment that was unto life was found unto death. Yet the same chapter insists that the law is holy, just, good, and spiritual.

What is going on? The same law is good in content, but in the context of flesh and sin it can become the site where a negative system operates.

That is why Galatians warns so sharply. Galatians 3 says, "As many as are of the works of the law are under the curse." The issue there is not simply that the commandments contained no truth. The issue is the system of compliance. If righteousness is being sought through works of law, then the demand never ends and the mark is never met. "Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all things written in the book of the law to do them."

Faith righteousness begins with being made right and then expressing righteousness. Compliance righteousness keeps trying to become right by performance and never reaches the goal.

So when Galatians says, "the law is not of faith," it is speaking in that sense. The man who does them shall live by them. It is the ongoing pressure of compliance. By contrast, "the just shall live by faith."

This is also why Paul says in Galatians 5 not to be entangled again with the yoke of bondage. Yes, a believer can become entangled again in that mindset. Many believers live under pressure to comply rather than standing in what Christ has fulfilled and then walking from faith.

## The Law Is Good If It Is Used Lawfully

1 Timothy 1 is a key text here. Paul tells Timothy to stop people from teaching another doctrine, giving heed to myths and genealogies, and causing disputes. Then he says, "The purpose of the commandment is love from a pure heart, from a good conscience, and from sincere faith."

That is the aim of the law in content. The law was meant to teach God's truth in such a way that it produces love, purity, good conscience, and sincere faith. The first part centers on faith in Yahweh the redeeming God; the outworking is love and righteousness.

Then Paul says, "We know that the law is good if one uses it lawfully." So the law is good. But it must be used rightly.

When he goes on to say the law is not made for a righteous person but for the lawless and insubordinate and so on, the best sense there is that the law as a compliance system is not what governs the righteous. A person who is truly of faith does not relate to the law that way.

So the apostles are not against the truth of God. They are against the misuse of that truth in a self-righteous, legalistic, compliance-based system.

## Why the Law Was Added

Galatians 3 asks, "What purpose then does the law serve?" and answers, "It was added because of transgressions till the seed should come." That must be read in line with the tutoring role of the law.

The law was not given because God suddenly realized the world was getting too sinful and decided to impose rules just to restrain people. The law was given as part of God's purpose to depict and tutor. Through Israel God was showing what his people, worship, priesthood, inheritance, and purpose would look like until the Seed came.

So "because of transgressions" makes sense within that tutoring arrangement. If God is going to depict his purpose through a people, and not all of that people are actually of faith, then the same content will expose transgression, provoke the issue, and show clearly what is wrong and what is needed.

The law guarded, tutored, and pointed. It did not cancel the earlier promise. The promise to Abraham came first. Redemption by promise, by faith, by Christ, was already established. The law that came 430 years later could not annul that. So the old covenant arrangement had its role, but the earlier redemptive promise remained the controlling reality.

That is why Paul says the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith.

## What Redemption Did to the Law

There are several senses in which redemption affects the law.

First, all the demands that a compliance system exposes are met in Christ. What we could not meet, Christ has fulfilled. What the law revealed about our condition under sin, Christ has dealt with.

Second, the consequences that the law exposes have been dealt with in Christ. The law was revealing the problem; Christ dealt with the problem itself.

Third, the distinctions the law maintained in its tutoring role have reached their purpose in Christ. The representational, depictive role attached to Israel has reached its end in the actual fulfillment.

So when people say Christ is the end of the law, we must handle that carefully. He is not the end of God's truth. He is the fulfillment of what the law was aiming at, exposing, and tutoring toward. The compliance system has no claim over the believer. The content reaches fulfillment in Christ and in those who are in him.

Romans 8 says what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending his own Son. He condemned sin in the flesh "that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit."

That is the believer's present stance. The content of the law is fulfilled in us. We are not under a law of works but under a law of faith. First Corinthians 9 says Paul is not without law toward God, but under law toward Christ. Romans 3 contrasts the law of works with the law of faith.

So the believer is not lawless, and he is not legalistic. He stands in a faith system under Christ. He walks from fulfilled righteousness, not toward earned righteousness.

## Practical Warning

Believers can still be entangled again with the yoke of bondage. A church can preach grace and still function by compliance. A person can talk about the new creation and still operate a framework that can never produce new-creation living.

This shows up in legalistic tones, identity compliance, behavioral pressure, and forcing righteousness rather than teaching truth and calling people to faith. The teaching applies this strongly to parenting and pastoring. The compliance system produces pressure, hypocrisy, rebellion, depression, and hardness. The faith system points out the wrong, instructs toward the right, and calls people into transformation.

Even good practices can be pressed with a wrong spirit. So the warning is not only against false doctrine in the abstract, but against any way of handling truth that turns it into bondage.

## The Main Conclusion

The law, in content, is God's truth. That content existed before Moses. At Moses, a covenantal arrangement with Israel introduced a setting in which the same content could now be handled in two systems: faith or compliance.

The proper system is faith. The negative system is compliance. Christ has fulfilled the law in us and brought us into the law of faith.

So the believer's confession is simple: the content of the law is fulfilled in me. I am under the law of faith, not the compliance system of the law.
