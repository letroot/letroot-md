## The perfect man and the work of the church

[0:00]

We are looking at the hidden man. We have looked at the different realities we have in the newness of life, but now we are looking at the hidden man of the heart.

The most emphatic message of the Christian faith is who we are in Christ, or the newness of life. In Ephesians 4:11, Jesus gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ. Those are not three separate things the leaders should do. The leaders perfect the saints, which leads the saints to do the work of ministry, and that builds up the body.

Verse 13 tells us what that perfecting is unto: the unity of the faith, the knowledge of the Son of God, the perfect man, the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ. The first two descriptions concern knowledge. But what man is to be brought to perfection? It is not your physical build. The truth of God’s word should make you take your health seriously, but it does not mean everybody will have the same physical stature.

Neither is it your spirit that is being improved. You cannot develop the nature of your spirit beyond what it is. You can grow, because you are not just spirit, but the nature you have is not what is growing. Even in speaking of Jesus growing in wisdom and stature in Luke 2:52, we have reason to consider spiritual stature, although physical stature may also be intended. The church is not bringing us to Jesus’s physical stature.

## One spirit with the Lord

[5:51]

The church cannot bring us to the nature of Christ in our spirit. The Lord himself has done that in redemption. First Corinthians 6:17 says that he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with him. Your spirit is in a complete, perfect and divine state. To suggest that this nature needs improvement would be like suggesting that the Spirit of the Lord needs improvement.

Romans 8:15–16 says we have received the Spirit of sonship, whereby we cry, “Abba, Father,” and that the Spirit bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God. They give the same testimony because the Spirit of God has brought our spirit to the same nature. That does not give us God’s status of being self-existent. Having the fullness of divinity in us does not make us God. But neither should “You are not God” be used to speak low of the new creation.

We need to stir the saints to engage better in natural affairs. There was a period of the church’s dominance, the Catholic phase, when believers were making major strides in the natural. Subsequently, the emphasis on sanctification and making heaven could leave people with little regard for natural engagement. Being an heir of God is being an heir of the world. Some should enter governance, and all should carry out civic duties to make their own part of the world better. That can include exercising civil rights and protesting.

[9:59 — The opening wording about zealots is incomplete in the captions.]

The church’s mission itself is not a protest movement. Yet what it teaches can equip people to improve society, including defending the rights of women or minorities. There is a need for that correction, but some efforts move into greed and covetousness, making everything about pleasure and convenience rather than responsibility and natural assignment.

We must also preserve the uniqueness of Christ. We cannot redeem ourselves. Only God who became man could do that. When we say we are as Christ is by nature, that is one thing; speaking about him in a way that loses the redemptive distinction is another.

## Complete in Christ without overstating or understating it

[12:08]

Colossians 2:9 says that in Christ dwells the fullness of the Godhead bodily, and then says we are complete in him, the head of all principality and power. There is both his uniqueness and an identification reality: what he exemplified as God become man, he gives us the privilege to partake of. We must say this carefully.

Ephesians 1:23 calls the church his fullness, and Ephesians 3 speaks of being filled with all the fullness of God, there in relation to growth. We are not God by status, but we must not speak down on the new creation. God himself has spoken highly of the new creation; he has not overhyped it. We should neither think higher nor lower of ourselves than we ought.

Second Peter 1:4 says we are partakers of the divine nature. The point is that we have that nature in common, as we speak of common humanity. Our spirit cannot be made better than this complete, perfect and divine state. Salvation has brought us there at the instance of faith.

First John 3:23–24 speaks of believing on the name of his Son Jesus Christ and loving one another. He who keeps his commandments abides in God, and God in him; we know he abides in us by the Spirit he has given us. God is in us and we are in him. Do not undo that truth in the attempt to appear humble. There is such a thing as false humility, and Colossians 2 speaks against it.

When you walk in the flesh, knowing who you are should stir you to say, “How can I do that?” Paul could ask, “Don’t you know who you are?” When you say you are not God, also say who you are: one spirit with the Lord, a new creation, separated from death and darkness, with the life of God in you. The world already has the contrast between light and darkness, truth and falsehood.

## The new man and the question of conduct

[20:33]

If Ephesians 4 is speaking neither of the body nor of improving our spirit’s nature, what part of us is to become the perfect man?

Ephesians 4:20–24 says we have learned Christ and have been taught the truth in Jesus: put off the old man concerning former conduct, be renewed in the spirit of the mind, and put on the new man created after God in righteousness and holiness of truth. These people are already new creations. Yet they are being told to put off and put on in a particular regard. The mind is central to this.

Second Corinthians 5:17 says that if any man is in Christ, he is a new creation. Old things have passed away; all things have become new. It is already done. How then can a new creation do things belonging to the old? And how can an old man seemingly do things belonging to the new? Romans 6:6 likewise says the old man has been crucified with Christ, so that we should no longer be slaves of sin. The teaching is meant to show us how to walk in what has happened.

## Outward man, inward man, spirit and soul

[26:07]

Second Corinthians 4:16 says that although our outward man is perishing, our inward man is being renewed day by day. One grows old; the other stays fresh and unchanging. A useful way to understand man is therefore inward and outward. If you use the categories inside and outside, you have exhausted the subject within that particular framework, although other frameworks can describe other aspects.

The outward man here is the body. The following verses speak of present affliction, the eternal weight of glory, and looking at things unseen rather than only things seen. Faith in redemption includes realities not visible to our eyes. In chapter 5, our earthly house or tent can be destroyed, but we have a building from God, eternal in the heavens: a glorified body.

The body is called a house, a tent, clothing and a habitation. It is a covering for the core of the person. That is not to condemn matter as sinful. [30:10 — The caption’s wording is uncertain; the apparent contrast is with a Gnostic view of matter.] We do not desire merely to be unclothed but further clothed, that mortality may be swallowed up by life. Immortality brings the body into the state intended in God’s purpose.

First Thessalonians 5:23 speaks of being sanctified wholly and of the whole spirit, soul and body being preserved blameless. It is often used to say man is spirit, mind and body. But we should not simply assume that “soul” means “mind.” “Soul” commonly refers to the whole person, a living being, and the Old Testament word nephesh can even be used for animals. “Bless the Lord, O my soul” is a person addressing himself; it does not establish a separate mind commanding another mind.

There is an argument that this text points to the spiritual core of the person and the body, or that man is spirit and body made alive: soul as life, breath or animation. [32:08–34:18 — Some Greek terms and wording in this explanation are unclear in the captions.] The use of soul need not mean mind. It can refer to life present in spirit and body, or to man’s life being at its core in the spirit.

James 2:26 says that the body without the spirit is dead, as faith without works is dead. Again we have body and spirit. A useful framing is that man is body with a brain and spirit with a mind. The outward man is body with a brain; the inward man is spirit with a mind. That is the biblical revelation we are examining, beyond observation and reason alone.

## The aspects of the mind

[38:08]

The mind deserves attention because the reasons for what a person does are found there. In our taxonomy, we identify seven aspects of the mind. Luke 24:44–45 says Jesus opened the disciples’ understanding so they might comprehend the Scriptures. The word translated understanding is nous, a word for mind. He taught them and made them reason on the word of God. Reasoning, or consideration, includes thinking, perceiving, examining and observing.

Luke 16 gives the account of Lazarus and the rich man. It is presented as an actual account, with a named person, rather than merely as a parable. That gives weight to the details we are considering. Lazarus desired the crumbs from the rich man’s table. Jesus condemns selfish wealth management; we must not turn that into condemnation of wealth creation or all personal wealth consumption. With liberty to create and consume wealth comes the need to speak strongly about righteous management. Neither greed nor the opposite extreme of poverty vows is the answer.

The beggar died and was carried to Abraham’s bosom. The rich man died and was buried, yet was in torment in Hades. His body was buried; his spirit was in Hades. Again we see the twofold description. His spirit is described with eyes, a tongue and movement, parallel to the outward man. Angels too are ministering spirits described in human form, although they do not have bodies in the ordinary sense.

The rich man recognized Lazarus, spoke, and remembered his brothers. Abraham told him, “Remember.” That is memory without the physical brain. There is reasoning as they converse and memory as he recalls his earthly life. What enters memory is not necessarily what you continue to believe. I can remember arguments I once believed, and even where the discussions took place, while no longer agreeing with them. [48:36 — The description of the earlier belief is unclear.]

Another aspect is will: the ability to decide. Then conviction: what you agree or disagree with. I first called conviction the third aspect; let me correct that order: will is third and conviction fourth. The rich man’s conviction mattered to where he was, just as with Abraham and Lazarus.

The fifth aspect is conscience, or consciousness. Titus 1:13–15 warns against Jewish fables and commandments that turn people from truth. To the pure all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure; even their mind and conscience are defiled. Here conscience specifies the aspect of mind being discussed. It is not saying their memory itself is defiled. The “and” can be argued to be explanatory in this context. [52:14–52:22 — The Greek grammatical discussion includes unclear wording.]

## Conscience as mental perception

[52:46]

Conscience concerns the mental perception that accompanies physical perception. The Greek explanation in the captions is partly unclear, but the teaching distinguishes perceiving physically from perceiving mentally alongside it.

Two people can feel the same cold weather and have different mental responses: one likes it and another dislikes it. Two people can see a dog, while one is affectionate and another’s past experiences make him fearful. Someone sees a red cloth tied to an object and recognizes an intended diabolical use; another sees attractive cultural decoration. The physical object is the same, but their consciousness differs.

There is reasoning, memory, will, conviction and conscience. Believing in the heart involves conviction on the mind. Conscience is the mental perception through which we view things. Someone can always think life is unfair, or see something good as bad. That is the force of the statement in Titus: the defiled person perceives through defiled lenses.

Your conscience has been shaped by your environment, subject to your free will. Before you could discern and decide, your background was already influencing it. Once you reach the age of rationality, you have responsibility for changing it.

Consider being offered twenty million naira and choosing between starting a business in Nigeria or relocating. Your choice will be affected by your consciousness of Nigeria and of other countries. Do not have a delusional optimism about either. Nigeria has flaws and can be better; moving abroad does not mean someone meets you at the airplane with money to start your life. Speak to people who will tell you the realities, including expenses.

This does not mean moving is wrong. Some may need to move for God’s purpose, personal preference, health or other reasons. People’s circumstances and thresholds differ. The claim being made is that, given money upfront, the odds of doing well with it in Nigeria may differ from moving without money. [1:00:28–1:01:09 — Parts of the comparison are unclear in the captions.] Let your conscience be right so you can make a sound decision. A conscience that assumes every prosperous person is doing something wrong can itself limit your life.

The last two aspects are emotions and judgments. Both are affected by conscience. People can judge a person by hairstyle or dress because of associations they have learned. Those associations may seem natural to them, yet a particular judgment can be wrong. Understand how this operates rather than pretending it does not happen.

The aspect of the mind often described as a man is conscience or consciousness, because it shapes your person. That is the part we are calling the hidden man. Fables and superstition can corrupt it until a spider or cockroach becomes a cause for fear. Guard your heart.

## Taking responsibility for the conscience

[1:04:41]

Acts 24:15–16 speaks of hope in the resurrection and of exercising oneself to have a conscience without offence toward God and man. Paul puts responsibility for the state of his conscience on himself. Your background has influenced it, but it is now your responsibility.

All of us have had our conscience affected by the flesh in some area. You may become harsh, or too permissive. There is a sense in which people reproduce harmful patterns they saw in earlier generations, even while condemning them. When I speak of that, I am not saying there is a demon possessing you or something in your father’s hand that needs a ritual to break it. It happens by influence. You walk away from it by renewing your mind, refusing wrong influences and putting your mind on truth.

Someone whose mother was permissive can learn to tolerate people taking advantage of her, including in relationships. If you do not set boundaries, even good people can cross them unknowingly. Complaining about a pattern is not the same as deliberately deciding against it. Predatory people look for vulnerability. [1:08:25–1:08:46 — Some wording about boundaries is unclear.] Do not dismiss your conscience: it affects your life and your fulfillment of God’s purpose.

First Peter 3 begins with wives’ conduct toward their husbands. Family patterns of fighting can influence people too; recognize them and deliberately work on them. Merely condemning a parent’s bad character does not remove what you have absorbed. But you can change. In church we see people become diligent instead of lazy, orderly instead of disorderly. That is the power of God’s Spirit and word.

## Adorning the hidden person of the heart

[1:12:19]

Peter says that husbands who do not obey the word may be won by their wives’ conduct as they observe chaste conduct with fear. A person’s character can repel someone from the gospel or draw attention to it. Your life should be a testimony. The message must still be preached; conduct alone does not save, but it calls attention to the message.

Peter then says not to make adornment merely outward—arranging the hair, wearing gold or fine apparel—but the hidden person of the heart. Your spirit is already in a complete, perfect and divine state. This concerns the hidden person of the mind, your conscience. You are to adorn that part of you.

That is the same putting off and putting on in Ephesians 4. You put who you are in Christ onto your mind, and put off who you used to be and what belongs to the world. [1:15:24 — A caption says “from your hidden man,” although the preceding explanation says “from your spirit man”; the phrase is uncertain.] This is real work in the Christian faith.

I think perhaps church time should be divided half to prayer and half to teaching, because people can hear much and still fail to put it on. That is a suggestion, not an announced decision. Daily devotion already gives substantial time for this. If you take it seriously and engage, there is no way you will not change. It is work, not magic.

Esteem can affect your natural assignment. You can be very good at something and still charge less because your background has shaped your sense of what you deserve. [1:16:45–1:17:19 — The personal billing example is partly unclear.] This is different from senseless pricing, and different from a deliberate company strategy to stay within a particular price or margin. Sometimes the issue really is a mind that feels undeserving of good.

Adorn the hidden person with a gentle and quiet spirit. There is your spirit man, your outward man, and this hidden or inner man of the mind.

## The spirit of the mind and strengthening the inner man

[1:19:04]

Ephesians 4 calls this the spirit of the mind: an unseen part that produces visible effects. As wind has effects although you cannot see its source, consciousness expresses itself in conduct. It can also be called the soul of the mind in the sense of animating what we see.

Look honestly at its state. Is there anger, hatred, offence, carelessness, permissiveness or a lack of boundaries? [1:20:21 — The caption gives “in discipline”; the intended contrast is uncertain.] Address what is there. You can also become unconcerned about the supernatural and rely entirely on observation and reason. I am not speaking against reason or logic; I am speaking for wholesomeness.

Be renewed in the spirit of your mind and put on the new man created after God in righteousness and holiness. This is not merely reasoning, memory or conviction. The consciousness must be affected. Confessing “I am born of God” puts that truth on the mind; words can affect how people act.

Ephesians 3:14 onward speaks of being strengthened with might by the Spirit in the inner man, that Christ may dwell in the heart by faith. [1:22:41 — Part of the quotation before “strengthened” is missing or garbled.] Paul is praying for believers in whom Christ already dwells. This is something happening to the hidden man of the heart, the inner man, the spirit of the mind: strength passing from the Spirit to that inner man.

I am born of God. I have the life and nature of God. I am a partaker of the divine nature, one spirit with the Lord. The fullness of Christ dwells in me. I am the righteousness of God, a love being in Christ and a possessor of God’s power. I am disciplined. I know how to set boundaries righteously and lovingly. I am not permissive; I am not rude.

Put off harmful things you have absorbed. When speaking of tendencies among women or men, I am not saying those things are inherently female or male. Social patterns develop over time; leadership can make harshness more likely, while other positions can have other tendencies. [1:25:23–1:25:41 — Parts of this comparison are unclear.] Watch what you have absorbed.

Put on the new man in your hidden man. Adorn that hidden man of the heart. Strengthen that inner man. That is how we fulfill God’s purpose.
