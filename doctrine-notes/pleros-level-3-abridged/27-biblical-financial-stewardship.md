# Biblical Financial Stewardship

## Biblical Truth Embraces Natural Truth

Father, I'm so glad that I am your child and a part of your family. In this meeting, gathered with my siblings, I receive of your word light, truth, revelation, and growth by the knowledge of Christ and his work in all its fullness, and by the knowledge of his fullness that's in me, in the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.

Let's turn to Ephesians chapter 4. We are looking at biblical financial stewardship. When we use that word “biblical,” we are saying biblical truth that embraces natural truth. There's truth that can be known by observation and reason, as well as truth that is revelatory. We're talking about written revelation in this case. There's also supernatural revelatory truth: God told you this supernaturally. That one is the lowest level. We're not looking at my opinion or idea on financial stewardship. We are looking at the truth known by observation, reason, and the Bible. I'm making the assumption, which I would have to prove, that God's word has something to say about financial stewardship.

## The Church Is Given to Teach the Truth That Produces Growth

In Ephesians 4:13, Jesus gave the church because he wants us to grow. Imagine someone saying, “All that Christ has done, he has done. There's nothing added. You don't need to grow into it.” Of course, there's nothing added in nature, but you need to grow. There's another vital detail: he gave the church because he wants us to know the truth, and that truth is going to make us grow.

It's by that truth that we're going to have spiritual growth to fulfill his purpose. In verse 13, we are to come to the unity of the faith, wholesome truth, a wholesome message, the knowledge of the Son of God, a perfect man, the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ. He's talking about information. He defines that information in verse 15 as truth: “But speaking the truth in love.” He wants us to grow into Christ in all things.

The way the word was used in the Greek is supposed to be “being truthful in love.” If you are to be truthful, what are you saying? The truth. The church has been given a truth-speaking mandate. The church is to speak the truth to you.

## The Church's Example and the Believer's Civic Responsibility

Notice he didn't say the church is supposed to speak the truth to power. Sometimes people try to say that. It's not true. How many powers did Jesus speak the truth to? The power he was speaking the truth to was spiritual, because he's functioning as a spiritual authority. How many did Paul speak the truth to?

People say, “They are supposed to speak the truth to power. That's what the church is supposed to do,” and start citing Elijah. Why didn't you start with Jesus and the apostles? “Elijah spoke the truth to power.” Does Israel look like a normal nation to you, or a church? All of them are considered a church. It was a nation in the civic sense, they were citizens, and yet it was a spiritual people, a church, a kingdom of priests. Of course, we hope that's how the new earth will be. If you miss that fact, there's a problem.

Even someone functioning nationally was not just doing that. He was functioning in a spiritual role. Then you see them have to speak the truth to the person. You can say, “This man of God is missing it.” If he's missing it, like Peter, that's the power he's speaking to, like Paul speaking to him. You don't really see them speaking to powers like that. Do you even see them confronting kings of other nations like that? It was Israel. When Jonah went there, he just went to preach; the king repented on his own. It wasn't the way they would go and confront Jeroboam. Nathan confronted David. You can't be doing that to other kings. There's a context: the church.

The church is supposed to be an example to civic authority. In Ephesians chapter 3, God has set us forth so that the principalities and powers in heavenly places can see his wisdom. Authorities and rulership in the natural, the secular, see that people can exist like this. They are not allowing ethnicity, gender, or class to divide them. They are walking in generosity amongst themselves.

In people's individual space, they can speak up. The church can speak against injustice; that is not wrong. But the church is not called to be speaking to power the way people are saying it. It is to train believers. They can go and engage in that, and they should. Civic responsibility is part of God's purpose.

In fact, when we outline God's purpose, we should make it five things so that it is more wholesome: a child of God in fellowship with God, walking in his nature and character, natural assignment, spiritual assignment, and civic responsibility. We really should be emphasizing our civic responsibility in God's purpose.

Your nature is of truth; you are meant to walk in it. You see that in John's letters, particularly 2 John and 3 John: “I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in the truth.”

## Greed Is a Primary Concern in Scripture

Before “You have not so learned Christ,” Ephesians 4:17 tells us not to walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their mind, having their understanding darkened, alienated from the life of God because of their ignorance. Our mind should be different from theirs. They don't know who God wanted them to be. You do, based on the redemption accomplished in Christ that you have received.

The other two don't specify particular conduct. Lack of restraint with what? It could be any appetite. Discipline is one of the core things that defines the believer. But he adds “with greediness.” It's like when Jesus said you can't serve two masters. You cannot serve God and mammon. This issue of finances is that primary.

When we say biblical financial stewardship, we want to see what Bible truth submits about finances.

## What We Mean by Finances

By finances, we mean matters pertaining to money, value, wealth, and resources. Before there was money, this still applied. You create value, and people began exchanging value by barter. Human beings have always had to deal with wealth and resources. Today all our value is defined with money.

What does the Bible say about natural value being produced, exchanged, consumed, accumulated, and stored? The Bible does have commentary on it, and places a high premium on it. “You have not so learned Christ.” I've not learned lasciviousness, uncleanness, and greed. I've learned the new creation, righteousness and holiness of truth. The truth gives me a different perspective to finances.

## Labor, Good Work, and Natural Welfare

On the heels of what the church teaches us, Ephesians 4:25 says, “Put away lying. Let every one of you speak truth.” There are things you change about character and financial stewardship because of what the word teaches, your new nature in Christ, and what you have been taught in that light.

Ephesians 4:28: “Let him that stole steal no more, but rather let him labor, working with his hands that which is good.” He's correcting what you can do and how you should do it. He's not just saying work, but do it laboriously, with maximum productivity. Whether a believer is taking risks financially or is in a financially or career-stable state, he must still be maximally productive. Do what is righteous, what is of life, what does not destroy people.

“That he may have something to give to him that is in need.” The biblical position is that you should have natural welfare covering not just needs but basic wants. He wants you to have in a way that you can still give.

People can take this to say, “That's abundance,” and push you into the greed Scripture is trying to correct. Natural welfare means your needs and basic wants are met. Someone can have a car and not be in what people call abundance. I'm for abundance. I'm against putting people under pressure the word of God did not put them under.

It's their choice if they want to be productive to that end. But pressure to have natural well-being is godly. You must work to eat and take care of your family, 1 Timothy 5:8. We are supposed to preach you into natural well-being and welfare, and when you are struggling, be generous towards you until that point. There is biblical pressure for that, but not to create a pressure the Bible doesn't give.

Some of them, before they got there, might not be your examples of being generous. I don't even think that one is generous; that's an investment lifestyle, sowing your way up. These are problems people have been put under before. That's not the biblical position on the matter.

The target of your productivity is natural well-being, that you may have to give. I say basic wants because human wants are insatiable. You can decide, “I want more out of life,” and be productive for that. Fine. That's not a pressure God is putting on you.

Some are willing for the ambition, the time, strain, and stress. Some want something calmer and more stable, and that's good, as long as their needs and basic wants are met, such that they can give. “Abundance” is an open-ended word. I'm for abundance as personal volition. Greed is bad. Using the Bible to preach greed in the name of Jesus is bad.

## The Steward Must Give an Account

Luke chapter 16 is probably the most difficult parable. People sometimes handle it in ways that don't reflect what the Lord intended. The real teaching is: don't live your life for now; live for the future, the aftermath. Almost like, live for retirement. But he's going to draw other lessons from it.

We're using it for financial stewardship, though it's really for life and purpose stewardship. Steward your life concerned about the future. In it he makes serious statements showing that financial stewardship reflects how seriously you take your life.

Luke 16:1: “There was a certain rich man who had a steward,” a manager, someone put in charge. “An accusation was brought to him that this man was wasting his goods.” He was wasteful. The master did not commend his wastefulness. He commended how he handled it when he knew he was in trouble. Later the Lord condemned the wastefulness.

The word for wasting, diaskorpizō, means dissipating, squandering. In the real meaning of the parable, the guy was wasting his life.

“Give an account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward.” Imagine wasting your life, and at the end the Lord calls you to account for your use of it. Your use of finances is part of what will be judged.

## The Unjust Steward Planned for the Future

Verse 3: “Then the steward said within himself, ‘What shall I do? For my master is taking the stewardship away from me. I cannot dig; I am ashamed to beg. I have resolved what to do, that when I am put out of the stewardship, they may receive me into their houses.’”

He needed a plan for the aftermath. He called his master's debtors. One owed a hundred measures of oil. He said, “Take your bill, sit down and write fifty.” He was garnering favor, planning for the future.

I know some people say he's teaching us discounts. Forget all that. Those are not the issues. The guy is a bad guy as far as the story is going. First, he's a bad guy for wasting. Second, he's a bad guy for cooking the books. Then he said to another, “How much do you owe?” He said, “A hundred measures of wheat.” He said, “Take your bill and write eighty.” Someone says it's bad debt, he's recovering money. Forget all those things. That's not what's going on here.

“The master commended...” If you're not careful, you miss the point. He knows he has done a bad thing, but he's commenting, “Guy, you're wise.” Someone can cheat you, and you're like, “Guy, you tried.” The master commended the unjust steward because he had dealt shrewdly. That word is phronimōs, “wisely.” This is bad wisdom, but he has done something there: he thought about the future. He wasn't foolish for the moment.

“The sons of this world are more shrewd in their generation than the sons of light.” They think about the future more than the sons of light, with an eternal perspective, think about their account, judgment, and afterlife. That's what he commended.

Verse 9: “Make friends for yourselves by unrighteous mammon.” If you think he's teaching us to use money to buy people to be our friends, you've missed the point.

“That when you fail, they may receive you into an everlasting home.” He's saying, “Plan for eternity.” Nobody in the story is leaving for an eternal home. He's using the comparison of the aftermath, bringing in “everlasting” to talk about what he wants to talk about. Do what they are doing, planning for the future. Do it for yourself.

## Faithfulness in Finances and in the Age to Come

Verse 10: “He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in what is much.” At this point, is he still commending the steward? No. “He who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in what is much. Therefore, if you have not been faithful in unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?”

Faithfulness in unrighteous mammon is saying with finances, because mammon is money, a Chaldee word for money or wealth. But really he's saying, “If you have not been faithful with this particular age, you will not be committed true riches, the age to come.” He's calling this entire age, this phase, unrighteous mammon. You have not been faithful. But the fact that he still chose to say unrighteous mammon, is he still teaching that you need to be faithful in your finances? Yes.

“If you have not been faithful in what is another man's, who will give you what is your own? No servant can serve two masters. Either he will hate the one and love the other, or else he will be loyal to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.” All of these are conclusions he's drawing from one parable. That guy was not a steward of his master. He was a steward of himself, of money. He was serving money, serving himself. He wasn't about his master. He's condemning him in that regard.

He's condemning his unfaithfulness and the fact that he will go unrewarded in the future. He's commending that he planned for the future. Verse 14 says the Pharisees, lovers of money, heard these things and derided him. Steward your life for eternity. Be faithful with this life for the age to come. The way you manage natural resources is proof of how you manage your life.

## A Steward Serves God Rather Than Self

Either your disposition to life is that you are a steward, or your disposition to life is that you are lord. That guy was his own master. He was disguising that he had a master. He was not concerned about whoever he was under. He was running his life his own way. He was his own master.

Biblical financial stewardship: being a steward is that you are under someone. You are serving under someone. The man was supposedly serving under someone but was not. People in this world are like that. They are creatures; they are under the Creator. But the way they live and manage their life, as well as their resources and finances, they don't really care about who they're under. They are serving themselves. That's called serving mammon, serving oneself.

Have you made the commitment to be a steward? “God made me for his purpose. I'm doing what he called me to do. I'm not living for self, for things, money, greed, or covetousness. I'm living for God. I know he wants my good, and I'm living for him.”

## Financial Productivity in Our Natural Assignment Under God

The first thing in biblical financial stewardship is financial productivity in our natural assignment under God. “I am a steward. God gave me this thing. He assigned it to me.” I didn't say natural pursuit; that can be your personal thing. I said natural assignment. If I don't see what I'm doing as what God gave me, I'm my own person. That's serving mammon, though it looks like serving self. Look at your natural assignment that produces finances as given by God to be productive with. It calls for maximum productivity.

I want you to go to Colossians 3:22. “Bondservants, obey in all things your masters according to the flesh, not with eyeservice, as men-pleasers, but in sincerity of heart, serving God.” That means be faithful to your work. Obey them; what they said to do, the KPIs they gave you, get it done. You know that it is what God himself allotted to you to do.

Verse 23: “And whatever you do, do it heartily,” out of your soul or consciousness, “as to the Lord and not to men.” Know that it was given to you by the Lord. Obey them, but reckon that it's the Lord who gave it to you through them to do those things. That's his own allotment or assignment to you. “As to the Lord and not to men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ.” You are going to receive the reward of what he has assigned to you, the inheritance, for you serve the Lord Christ.

“He who does wrong will be repaid for what he has done, and there is no partiality.” Look at chapter 4, verse 1: “Masters, give your bondservants what is just and fair, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.” Everybody is supposed to be under the rulership or lordship of Christ. We know our natural assignment God assigned to us is our choice to choose from, from where you are starting or things that come to you to what you want to do. It's assigned to you by God, but he gives you your free will to decide, because he has given you the intelligence, your mental and physical ability, to be able to do what he has allotted to you in your natural assignment. Are you financially productive with what is in your hands, with the natural assignment allotted to you by God? That's the first thing when it comes to biblical financial stewardship. Maximum productivity is instructed. We must be diligent.

## Diligence, Orderly Plans, and Natural Success

If walking in the Spirit, walking in truth, walking in the law is going to bring favor before God and men, and one of the things in walking in the law is walking in discipline or diligence, what would diligence do that can be a fruit of favor? Proverbs talks about things diligence will do that are fruits of favor. One of them is natural success. When you are walking in the favor of God, which is walking in the Spirit, walking in things in the Spirit including discipline and diligence, it is predicted in God's word to bring natural success in your natural assignment.

The law of the Lord is not against abundance, wealth, and prosperity by productivity with God's favor. It doesn't put an undue burden on people for that. It expects our productivity to give us natural well-being or welfare.

Proverbs 10:4: “He who has a slack hand becomes poor.” That's someone who is not walking in the law; he's walking in foolishness. “But the hand of the diligent makes rich.” Proverbs 12:24: “The hand of the diligent will rule, but the lazy man will be put to forced labor.” Proverbs 12:27: “The lazy man does not roast what he took in hunting, but diligence is a man's precious possession.” Proverbs 13:4: “The soul of a lazy man desires, and has nothing; but the soul of the diligent shall be made rich.”

Proverbs 21:5: “The plans of the diligent surely lead to plenty, but those of everyone who is hasty, surely to poverty.” Part of diligence is plans and order. The Spirit drives us to be focused on our goals, orderly in our plans, and diligent in execution. Notice that the hasty have plans too.

Is God's word against prosperity and wealth? The answer is no. Proverbs 22:29: “Do you see a man who excels in his work? He will stand before kings; he will not stand before unknown men.” Proverbs 27:23: “Be diligent to know the state of your flocks, and attend to your herds; for riches are not forever.” They are not automatic. “Nor does a crown endure to all generations.” It's not inherent in riches to just remain. They are sustained by productivity.

“When the hay is removed, and the tender grass shows itself, and the herbs of the mountains are gathered in,” the lambs provide clothing. It's still talking about productivity. If you don't see yourself in God's purpose with an assignment from him, you'll walk in greed and covetousness, a lover of self and money, living for self. That's ungodly.

## Financial Prudence Accounts for the Resources You Earn

The second thing in biblical financial stewardship is financial prudence. The steward in Luke 16 was squandering his master's goods. When you are productive in what God gave you to do, how are you managing the resources? If you can't account for all the money you've made this year, that's a problem.

I should have a record, an Excel sheet or an app, where I account for every penny. “This went to transport, this to item seven, this to data.” By the time you see where everything has gone, you'll be repenting.

You can account for money: “This went to transport.” You know where you should be walking and where you should take transport. You know you should reduce Bolt. You know when you need to start saving to buy a car because, from what you have spent, it is no longer making sense. Financial prudence. He was squandering the goods.

The other hand is: you have not been faithful here in this world; how can they trust you with the age to come? If you are not faithful in money, how can you be faithful in purpose? If you are not faithful in this life as unrighteous mammon, how can you be faithful in the age to come as true riches? That's why I'm saying there are those two thoughts in that single verse. If you cannot handle faithfulness with finances, financial stewardship, how can you have life or purpose stewardship? If you cannot have life or purpose stewardship, how can you be trusted with the age to come?

“If you have not been faithful in what is another man's, who will give you what is your own?” People unfaithful as employees usually mess up even a business. If someone must always follow up, “Have you done this? Have you seen the client?” who will tell you to follow up when you have your own business? If you don't fix it, there's a problem.

## Financial Intelligence Makes Resources Productive

Financial prudence is two things: intelligence and discipline. We can say character, but let's say discipline, because character can be bigger. Stealing is character; that's not what we're talking about here. Financial intelligence with what you have earned: how are you intelligently allocating it?

It's like when the Lord told the parable of the talents. How could you have gone to bury it? Money should not be allowed to waste. Money should be productive. Instead of burying it, let me put it in a bank so I can earn interest. You are a wasteful person; you're not even thinking about productivity. Why did you go and bury it? There is financial intelligence intended there. You are supposed to allow money to be productive, either actively productive or passively productive. That's what they call active and passive income.

He was telling him, even if you could not do active income, to go and buy and sell, produce directly yourself and earn, why didn't you make it earn passively? There's also a point where you are actively earning and you have stretched yourself in your active earnings, and you start doing passive earnings. That's also financial intelligence. The Lord was referencing natural truth on finances. Much of the references in Luke 16 and the parable of the talents are not revelatory truths. It's expected that you have that financial intelligence with resources.

Any money that enters your hand will rise and fall to the level of your financial prudence. If you don't know how to manage ten million, it'll find its way back to your level of prudence. Even one billion. I understand a billion dollars shouldn't be that easy, but lack of financial prudence is unrighteousness, walking in the flesh, limiting God's purpose. Handling finances affects your life in this age and the age to come.

No matter how much someone should correct you on a subject like this, if you don't go and fix it, it isn't going to change. It's like anger. “Anger is not good.” It's true. But go and fix it. Fix it from the root. It's not going to change by knowledge. It doesn't change by feeling. It changes by putting in the work.

## Righteous Allocation and Disciplined Spending

Let's define financial prudence. We have said it is intelligence and discipline. Let's call it righteous allocation of resources. Don't be wasteful. That means there's a right way to have used the resources. Don't squander it. There's a right way to use it. Once the resource entered, you should have used it rightly. Squandering is the opposite of allocation. Instead of saying, “I'm deliberately allocating,” you are dissipating. That's part of the definition of the word: to dissipate, to rout, to separate, to squander. The idea should have been allocation, a righteous allocation.

That's why I said any money you get that you don't spend entirely immediately righteously, you will end up spending later unrighteously. “This one should go here. This one should go here.” The one that's supposed to go where you cannot touch it again should go where you cannot touch it again. The one you're supposed to give to church, give it immediately. The one that's supposed to do this or that, if you don't spend it righteously immediately, you end up spending it unrighteously later.

Financial prudence is intelligent or righteous allocation. You are doing it righteously. By observation and reason you should be able to tell rationally and intelligently, “This should go here.” The priorities: care for my family, feeding, accommodation, transportation. I've mentioned giving. Finances are generally around about seven things: giving, feeding, accommodation, transportation, investment, debt, and savings.

Not everything should be invested. You might need savings because of what they call an emergency fund. You might have personal debt that is reasonable. Maybe you wanted to buy major things as a family or in your house, and you say, “I don't want to buy it at once,” or, “I can't afford to buy it at once, but I'm going to pay over time.” It can be part of your budget. You might have debts based on business. That's a different ball game. The business should pay for it.

This is natural truth that the Bible endorses. You plan it. Financial prudence is righteous, intelligent, rational allocation of resources and the discipline to do so. Discipline means being driven to get it done, focused, allocating in an orderly manner, and actually executing it.

## Giving Is Never Investing to Get a Return

Giving is never investing. You don't give to get. You give; it's gone. The only thing you get is the reward before God. If God's orchestration brings favor because you're walking in the Spirit through giving, that cannot be your intention. It shouldn't be a testimony, so you don't cause a problem.

It can so happen that that part of your walking in the Spirit can aid certain orchestrations as well. For example, they told us that the alms of Cornelius came up to God as a memorial. His walking in the Spirit, somewhere, somehow, that involved giving, also found itself attracting favor, in terms of the orchestration of the ministry of angels. It can happen, but it cannot be your intention. Otherwise, that's the problem. We don't have that he was thinking like that. If that's your intention, you're already in a mess. Get those kinds of things out of your heart. It's not good for your spiritual health.

## Giving as Commitment and Church Order

When it comes to giving in financial prudence, be aware: giving is a matter of commitment of a percentage that is reasonably expected of you for the furtherance of God's kingdom and mission on earth. In the tithe principle seen with Israel, we saw that people practiced a tithe principle. That means we can go beyond the tithe legal obligation and find a principle in it of giving. We find such a principle of percentage giving in the New Testament text, 1 Corinthians 16:1–2.

Look at 1 Corinthians 16:1: “Now concerning the collection for the saints, as I have given orders to the churches of Galatia, so you must do also.” Can a church have an order on giving? It can. “On the first day of the week let each one of you lay something aside, storing up as he may prosper.” According to what he has made, he should lay something aside. That's like percentage-wise, according to how he has prospered, “that there will be no collections when I come.”

Different churches can have different approaches to giving and their own church order. Can a church have an order on giving? Yes. We have an order on giving. Our church pledge order is at least ten percent. You can do more. It's not a matter of saying it's the law, or it's tithe, or things will be tight for you. It's our church order.

First, giving should never be a matter of investment: “I am giving to get.” Remove that from your heart. Second, giving should primarily be a matter of commitment. It's commitment to God's mission, usually on a percentage basis, and it should also be under church order. This text was not just about general generosity. It was a commitment thing. There were orders. As you prosper, lay something aside. It can be different at different times. Some might have more or less, and they still give that percentage as they have prospered. It's primarily a matter of commitment.

## Generosity Within a Prudent Allocation

Giving can be generosity. Ephesians 4:28: “That he may have to give to him that is in need.” It isn't necessarily commitment. It can be commitment; it depends. But let's put it under generosity, beyond your commitment to giving in church. You can have that in your budget. Righteous means aligned with truth, observation, and reasoning. Allocate righteously and spend with discipline. Savings can cover capital things like a car or house, with a portion for emergencies.

Not that from the place you're supposed to have allotted the money, you go back and say, “Let's remove that money. Come on, let's just remove fifty thousand. We cannot kill ourselves. Let's buy pizza and ice cream.”

## Consumption and Enjoyment Follow Prudence

The third thing is financial consumption and enjoyment. You are productive, prudent, and eventually you enjoy. Notice the order. It doesn't start with enjoyment. Starting with enjoyment is like being the unjust steward. God wants us to enjoy what we have. Consumption and enjoyment follow prudence.

First Corinthians 7:30: “Those who weep as though they did not weep, those who rejoice as though they did not rejoice, those who buy as though they did not possess.” You have the right to rejoice and buy. Don't get caught up holding everything as though it is you. That's worship of mammon.

Enjoy this world as a steward. What is in this world that you actually own like that? When you are gone, you are gone. Someone else will have to take it over. Of course, you should also allocate that righteously, to be faithful to pass it on to those you are responsible for.

## Greed and Covetousness Are Living for Self

I want you to see 1 Timothy chapter 6. We must be careful of greed and covetousness. It's a core warning. The only solution to greed and covetousness is biblical financial stewardship. What really are greed and covetousness? Sometimes people always say the issue is drawing a line, having a restraint, what you will not do, at what point you're going to stop. They say the antidote is that, meaning greed and covetousness are lack of restraint. There might be a point if you look at Ephesians 4:19, where you don't have restraint.

But greed and covetousness are actually living for self. That's the real thing that now leads to lack of restraint, because you don't have any need for prudence when you're living for self. All you're living for is yourself, money, status, or people's applause. You'll always keep going after things for that reason. But you can be productive and end up wealthy because you're doing it as a steward.

Greed or covetousness is more about living for self. You are so consumed and concerned about self rather than your role as a steward under God. You don't see your natural pursuit as an assignment. You see it as your own personal means of enrichment. Sometimes you might get competitive about it.

First Timothy 6:6: “Godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain that we can carry nothing out.” The restraint is that you're a steward managing resources. After managing them, you can consume and enjoy them.

“Having food and clothing, with these we shall be content.” What do we call that in this case? Natural needs and basic wants. If you read only this text, you might think it is a vow of poverty, if you don't go and read Ephesians 4:28, where he is expecting that you work such that you can give. He's actually saying you should go for your needs and basic wants.

## God Gives Us Things to Enjoy

First Timothy 6:17 shows he's not against excess wealth: “Command those who are rich in this present age not to be haughty, nor to trust in uncertain riches but in the living God, who gives us all things to enjoy.” God made pleasure: food, sex, buying things that meet your taste. He's not against pleasure.

But living for pleasure is being dead while living. First Timothy 5:6: “She who lives in pleasure is dead while she lives.” That means the person is living for pleasure; that's what the person is all about. God is not against you enjoying, but he's against sinful enjoyment. Pleasure is to be enjoyed, and it's God who made pleasure. It's ungodly pleasure that is not meant to be enjoyed.

Look at Hebrews 11:24–25: “By faith Moses, when he came of age, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin.” If it was godly pleasure, should he have the right to enjoy it? Yes. That ungodly pleasure of an oppressive people, of stealing or corruption, you should rather choose to suffer than enjoy those pleasures. But godly pleasure, where you have worked and have been productive to meet your needs and wants, you have the right to enjoy it.

Buy, but not as possessing. Buy good clothes, good shoes, nice rides if you can afford them. Don't let anybody blackmail you. It's okay to enjoy your wealth if you've been productive in your natural assignment under God, prudent, and have given in commitment and generosity.

## Giving Can Also Be Sacrificial

In Acts chapter 2, people sold their assets. That's sacrifice, not just generosity. Situations can call for sacrifice. God can call you to reason that this is righteous, beyond your normal commitment or generosity. Giving can be commitment, generosity, and sacrifice.

Sacrifice is not every day, because if it's like that, we will start sacrificing for you. But God can call you to give extra. Maybe there's something going on. There are times when churches have certain huge steps to take. Maybe they want to get a building or land; there will be times for sacrifice. Maybe there's a huge economic situation, and we need to meet people's needs. People will have to give sacrificially in the communal nature of the church.

Giving is commitment, generosity, and can be sacrificial. That's Bible. Giving is never investment. Don't give to get. Forget about it. It's possible the favor of God can work alongside that act of being sacrificial, but it is not supposed to be intended. We don't have that it was intended for that. It's a righteous act. When you're entertaining strangers, don't intend to do it so that you receive angels. Do it for conscience's sake. But the favor of God, which is also by the ministry of angels, can work via that. That is the righteous disposition on the matter.

## Enjoy Your Resources as a Faithful Steward

Financial productivity in our natural assignment, financial prudence, then consumption and enjoyment. You can buy from your resources, not just needs. “Those who buy as though they did not possess” cannot be talking about needs. Of course you expect them to meet their needs. “As though they did not possess” is assets, or enjoyment. Go for a holiday or vacation. Enjoy the godly pleasures of righteousness, not the passing pleasures of sin. Look good, eat good. But this follows prudence. If you're not generous and consume unrighteously, it will consume you at the end of the age, and if you're not careful, in this age too.

In prudence, your major concerns are needs and basic wants, those basic things. But as you are working, you might have so many resources that there's extra. You can enjoy. You can go for holidays. You can get better things. You can have more than one car; you can have four. You have not sinned. It might be that you want a different one for different things, and you're still a financial steward. It's possible in a godly and righteous manner.

Some numbers make you ask, “Is this still normal?” Fifty houses, not rented, just empty for when you visit. You haven't used them productively to give to others, and there are homeless people. Some things make no sense. If it's business, that's different; you can use the proceeds for generosity. At some point ask, “What is the goal?” Someone says their collection is assets. Maybe they want to liquidate it at a time.

Financial productivity, prudence, then consumption and enjoyment. It's your right; don't let anybody blackmail you. We should be faithful financial stewards under God. I'm a faithful financial steward under God.
