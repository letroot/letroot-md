# Knowing God in Truth

## Why Knowing God Is Foundational

We're looking at knowing God in truth. Welcome to level three. In this level, we'll be taking more liberty in explanation to make things further clear. You have to tax yourself a bit more to be able to walk in his purpose.

Knowing God in truth takes up theology proper, the first part of Bible doctrine. Before we talk about redemption, we are talking about a world created by God. If we cannot establish as a matter of truth the reality of God, his existence, his being, there is nothing else that we hold in the Christian faith. Christianity won't even exist at all.

I know that the reality of God is not the core of Christianity, because there are other faiths that hold to the truth of God. But it behooves us as believers to know why exactly we believe in God. What is the reason why we hold that there is God? There's a way we believe in God that people of other faiths can also believe, and a way we believe in God that people outside the Christian faith would not.

## The Gospel's Claim to Be Truth

Ephesians 1:13 says, ‘In him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth.’ The ‘him’ is the Lord Jesus. We believed on Jesus after hearing the message of truth, which he calls the gospel of our salvation. ‘In whom also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise.’

At this point we are seeing the gospel make an epic claim to being the truth. As we have already seen in earlier teachings, it's not enough to claim to be the truth. It's more important that you prove to be the truth. When we first use that word truth, we must explain. Truth means the actual state of things. This is not an assumption. This is not something dogmatic. You are saying that is how things actually are.

Truth means the actual state of things. How is truth proven? This is both a biblical and logical submission: truth is proven by observation and reason. It's not restricted to the Christian faith. There's also a place for truth by revelation.

## What Observation Can Tell Us and Why Revelation Matters

There are some truth questions we can answer simply by observation and reason. If we ask about this chair here, the black chairs that are here, and someone should ask, “What is that?” that's a truth question. They say, “That is a chair.” The actual reality of that form of matter we are seeing here is a chair, as we reckon it, as we intend it as human beings.

We could ask, ‘How was it made? What was it made from?’ Someone can look at the chair and talk about the leather, foam, and iron. Someone who has practice can tell where it was joined and how it came together. We can answer what and how by observation and reason, without appealing to revelation. We can also answer when, where, and by whom, if there are things to observe, such as a maker's label.

But there's one question that is difficult to answer by observation and reason: why was it made? You ask, “Why was the chair made?” We can probably guess for commercial purposes. But someone can make something, and you might not know that he was trying to use it for some other reasons.

Do you know how they smuggle drugs in some countries? They might seem like they are importing another product. It could be furniture, and they have stacks of drugs stuffed in different places. Sometimes it's clothes, and they stuff it in between the clothes. In your mind you might say, “I'm very sure they made this for commercial purposes,” and you could be absolutely wrong. It could be that the person was using it for some other purpose that wasn't merely commercial. The person is trying to smuggle something, but your own observation tells you, “I think it's this.”

Revelation is a core part of why, unlike what. “What is this?” Observation and reason. Why is a different ball game. Why usually requires the person who is the actor, the maker, the creator, the one taking the action, to tell you why exactly he's doing what he's doing.

We sometimes see people try to say everything is revelation, and so they try to establish a dogmatic base. That's usually a problem, particularly when you're trying to address an audience that has not already accepted that same dogmatic position you are starting from, that doesn't hold that presupposition. You must be able to start from the base, as God expects you: truth proven by observation and reason, before you get on to revelation.

If we are looking at knowing God in truth, and we are saying God exists as a matter of truth, then we are saying God can be shown to exist as a matter of observation and reason. There is also an understanding of God that goes beyond observation and reason. There is knowing God by revelation.

There is knowing my wife by observation and reason, and knowing her by revelation: her goals, dreams, aspirations, things she has to reveal to me. I can know her name, where she's from, her parents, and her accomplishments. It's another thing to know what she reveals. Some people's knowledge of God stops with the way they know the president of their country: they know that he is, but don't know him personally.

Knowing God in truth, as we are going to see, is layered. There's knowing God by observable, reasonable truth. There is knowing God by revelatory truth. There's even more, as we are going to see, in an experiential sense.

## Romans 1 Explains the Claim

Romans 1 would have to be our first point of reference in our understanding of God in truth. While Ephesians 1:13 has told us that the gospel is the truth, Romans 1 explains how the gospel shows itself to be the truth. Ephesians 1:13 simply makes an assertion, while Romans 1:16 through to verse 21 makes more than a claim; it gives the explanation.

From Romans 1:16, Paul says, “I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation to everyone who believes. For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith, as it is written, ‘The just shall live by faith.’” When you believe the gospel, we see how God makes right in the gospel. He brings people from sin, death, and darkness into light, life, and righteousness. That is how he makes right.

But something else is revealed in the gospel. Romans 1:18: “For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and all unrighteousness of men who suppress the truth in unrighteousness.” On the one hand, when people believe we see the righteousness of God. On the other hand, when people don't believe we see the wrath of God. What are those people who don't believe described as doing? They are suppressing the truth in unrighteousness.

When you say someone is suppressing the truth, you are even making an inference that the person knows better. It's like saying someone is suppressing evidence, which is a crime in any nation where the rule of law exists. If you are suppressing evidence, you are aware but now deliberately suppressing it. There's a problem that you are not bringing it out, or you are distorting it. He's making a claim that there's something they are aware of but are denying; they are in denial of it.

## What Is Clear to the Mind Through the Cosmos

It says, “Because what may be known of God...” The first thing they know in this truth is the reality of God. That implies that the first claim of the Christian faith is the reality of God, the reality of God's existence. “What may be known of God is manifest in them.” He's talking about in their heart, their mind. When it says in them, it cannot mean in their lungs, liver, or kidney. In them is specifically speaking of their mind.

That word manifest means it's clear in them; it's obvious in them. “For God has shown it to them.” The second thing is observation. The first is that it is clear on their mind, because it has been shown to them. It's clear to their eyes. By observation and reason they know something, but they are suppressing it.

Verse 20: “For since the creation of the world...” He's about to give further explanation; that's why you are seeing the word for. “For since the creation of the world his invisible attributes are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead.” He's showing us how God has shown it to them and how it became manifest in them.

Paul is using creation and the things that are made. But if we want to stay neutral before asserting, you will say, “For as long as the cosmos has existed, the invisible attributes of God are clearly seen, being understood by the very cosmos itself, even God's eternal power and Godhead.” The invisible attributes that are clearly seen are two things: his eternal power and Godhead.

Since the cosmos has existed, the invisible things of God are clearly seen, being understood by that very cosmos, the things that are made. What are those things we know? His eternal power and Godhead. The first one is very key, and actually answers the question that there is God: his eternal power. The second explains the nature of God. That second one is the word theiotēs, divinity, from the adjective theios, divine. We can know the nature of God. That's the second part.

Paul is simply making a case for God's existence from two core arguments. One is the finitude of the cosmos, which we can call the cosmological argument. A world that is finite must have been caused. As long as we see a world like this that must have been caused because it's finite, it must have had a start and must have been caused; then there is God. The second argument is for God's existence from contingency. I'll explain why those two things are what Paul is pointing to.

## Have They Not Heard? The Message of the Cosmos

Go to Romans 10, where Paul tells us that this same gospel that reveals the reality of God has always been preached, even before Christ came. Verse 13 says, “For whoever calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.” But he now wants to show us how we get to that point where we can call on the name of the Lord, or confess the Lord.

He says, “How shall they call on him that they have not believed? How shall they believe on him that they have not heard?” Before we can call on him, we must first have believed in our hearts. Before we can speak with our mouth, we must have believed in our heart. But before we can believe, we must first have heard. “How shall they hear without a preacher? How shall they preach except they be sent?” Then he says, “As it is written, ‘How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the gospel of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!’”

He brings up a common rebuttal people always make. People still ask that question today: what about people who have not heard? Verse 16: “But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, ‘Lord, who has believed our report?’ So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.” This is where he brings up the rebuttal, verse 18: “But I say, have they not heard?”

There are layers to the answer. The first one is that God would always ensure people hear the gospel. The second is that it is your responsibility to ensure people hear the gospel. The third is that they will be judged according to the evidence available to them.

The answer the gospel gives in this instance is much like the third one: people will be judged according to the evidence available to them. That is to say, the evidence of the gospel is available to everybody, even if at the lowest level.

Romans 10:18: ‘But I say, have they not heard? Yes indeed. Their sound has gone out into all the earth, their words to the ends of the world.’ This is not talking about people. He's talking about the sound and words of the cosmos. God's message in the cosmos has gone into the world.

In Psalm 19:1, the psalmist, David, says, “The heavens declare the glory of God.” What are the heavens? Part of the cosmos. “The firmament shows his handiwork.” It says, “Day unto day,” which is a reality or a phenomenon of the cosmos, “utters speech; night unto night reveals knowledge. There is no speech or language where their voice is not heard.” The voice of what? The cosmos. Then it says, “Their line...” That word line actually is sound, which I think we saw in Romans 10. “Their line has gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.”

The message being communicated by the cosmos, it says, has reached every place. I know there are times when people want to use this text to talk about spiritual realities, but let me be clear: that's not the primary use. There's room for such secondary use that we know does exist in Scripture, but in this case it would be an abuse of the primary use of that text, like we saw Paul using this in Romans 10.

## Finitude and the Question of an Infinite Past

Day unto day utters speech. There is today, there will be tomorrow, there was yesterday. Think about it. If there's yesterday, day unto day, and we keep counting backwards, if there is no end to counting backward, if there's an infinite past, then there cannot be today, because what would have allowed us to get to today? There's still a day counting backwards, and another one still counting backwards. There would be no room for you to get to today.

There would even be no room for you to get to the day that is counting backwards now, because several other days are still counting backwards that will never allow you to get to that day. We can speak about infinity in theoretical terms, like for numbers. You can keep having minus one, minus two, minus three. But of actual things, an infinite past is questionable.

I know there are arguments that can be made on another level for it, where we're talking about a block time. But even a block time does not really permit such an infinite past. Let's leave that. Sometimes on this premise people will argue that the finitude argument can be weak, but I think it still holds its own very well.

This is where the question always comes: they say it is not a logical contradiction that there can be an infinite past, because there can be infinite things. But it's a metaphysical implausibility. We cannot reckon that that can happen when you will not reach today. The reason some will argue, although I still think it goes deeper than that, that it's not a logical contradiction is that all things are possible to God. He can as well create an infinite past. But all things is also in context. You can't say it should include telling himself not to be. As long as it's not a logical contradiction.

## Contingency and a Self-Existent Cause

The second argument it always makes is the contingency one. We see that in verse 1 when it says the heavens declare the glory of the Lord, the firmament his handiwork. The idea of saying it must have been made is not necessarily pointing to time. “It must have been made” is saying it is not something that exists by necessity. If something is contingent, not necessary, we must ask the question: how did it get there?

The second is contingency: our reason requires us, whenever we encounter anything that is contingent, to ask how it got there and to provide the most plausible answer. Only if it fails do we have the right to reject it. If we ask, “How come there's a contingent world?” it means there must be something powerful enough, with the power to bring it forth. Something self-existent, necessary, non-contingent has the power to bring it forth. An eternal power must be a cause of a contingent world. An entire cosmos that is contingent requires an eternal power.

Our first reckoning of God is a matter of philosophical or metaphysical truth. Paul says everybody can know, because God has shown it to them and it is manifest in them.

For many people it happens almost intuitively. They might not be able to spell out the explanation, but the sense that, “How could this world be without a cause?” that leads to the reckoning of divinity happens very intuitively. Being unable to set it out with premises and a conclusion does not mean they are not going through the same process. They arrive at recognizing God through the world's finite and contingent nature. It happens to every human being.

## God's Nature as Life and Light

The issue of God's nature is also why the Christian faith stands in opposition to idealistic worldviews like panentheism, pantheism, animism, very common with Eastern religions and the like. When we look at the nature of divinity, we are arguing that it is what is primary or first, in existence generally, not just in this created world. Whatever nature or reality is first or primary must be the nature of God, since he is the first and primary thing that brought forth everything else.

If you happen to have light and darkness in any possible world, and you ask what would originally be of the self-existent, the answer would be whatever is first, which would be light, because that's who he is by design, by necessity. We can tell not only the reality of God, that he exists, but also the nature of God.

His nature is often described with the word light or the word life. We can say it is described with light conceptually and with life substantially, even though life can also be described in a couple of ways. People can argue it as being and non-being. That's another level we don't want to stress ourselves with. We can describe it just with life and death. Conceptually, very often you'll see it described with light and darkness; substantially, with life and death. God's nature is light. God's nature is life, which is what would be called good, what would be called righteous.

## Recognizing God and Then Suppressing the Truth

“Even his eternal power and Godhead, so that they are without excuse. Because although they knew God...” This is what I want to show you. Never be fooled into thinking one can be an atheist or an agnostic without first the recognition of God. It is upon recognizing that that you can still argue. You have the liberty to argue that the evidence is insufficient. But it can never be the case that it is without that recognition at all.

Verse 21 addresses those who will still reject or suppress that truth: “Because although they knew God,” by this means he has told us, “they did not glorify him as God, nor were they thankful.” Despite recognizing that there has to be a source and a cause, instead of holding that cause and source in a separate place, they will simply merge it with the world itself, as you will see: “It's just nature. It just is like that. How do we have a contingent world? We don't know. There's just a contingent world. Maybe it's brute fact.”

Although they knew God, they did not glorify him as God, nor were they thankful, “but they became futile in their thoughts,” empty in their thoughts, “and their foolish hearts were darkened. Professing to be wise,” by their further arguments, “they became fools, and changed the glory of the incorruptible God into an image made like...” They are merging what should be separate, life and light without any corruption, darkness, or evil, with creation. They changed the glory of the incorruptible God into an image made like corruptible man, birds, four-footed animals, and creeping things.

Let me be fair to say that panentheism can also fall here very much so, because this seems to be pointing to idolatry. Animism, where people now worship things, for example the god of the water, is often extremely related philosophically with pantheism or panentheism. The moment you hold pantheism or panentheism, God is everything or God is in everything, you won't be able to separate animistic worship and practice very soon, or sometimes animistic worship and practice will derive from it later on.

We cannot accept naturalistic or materialistic belief systems: “It's just a natural and material world.” Neither can we accept idealistic belief systems. In this case by idealistic I mean merging God and the world. I know there's another use of idealism, that mind is the only thing and every other thing is just a production of mind. That's not the sense I'm using it here. Paul did this in Acts 17, but we have looked at it through Romans 1, Romans 10, and Psalm 19.

## Revelation Must Rest on Observable, Reasonable Truth

If I know God in truth beyond metaphysical truth, how else do I know him? In this case we are going to say we also know God by theological, or preferably revelatory, truth. Now that we have established that there is God, what does he tell us about himself?

The challenge at this juncture, when we talk about revelatory truth, is that if revelatory truth is not established on reasonable, observable truth, then it is merely being dogmatic. The Christian faith has told us that there is God, that his nature is like this, that he is a Creator separate from the creation. There are many belief systems that accept that God is Creator, separate from creation. In fact, the two dominant beliefs or religions in the world hold God to be Creator in a way that is other than the world: the Christian faith and Islam. But they don't believe the same thing.

What then is the distinction? How do we know which theological framework to accept? It is one thing that both share a philosophical, metaphysical framework about God. In fact, some of the arguments that are in God's word, but also propounded by some Muslim scholars, are sometimes described after the Muslim scholars today. The argument for finitude is more popularly called today, in its syllogistic presentation, the kalam cosmological argument. Kalam there is an Islamic framework. I forgot what century the scholars did that, but it was popularized in this century by the Christian apologist William Lane Craig. They share that same framework, but they don't share the same theological framework.

## Redemption, Jesus, and the Authority of Scripture

We are in a world that needs redemption, a redemption that only God can do. If corruption has set in, death has set in, darkness has set in, it is the original life and light that needs to enter into the corrupted state to fix it.

It's not as if you can wash off the corruption and it goes. You still don't have what you need. You can't wash out darkness; what you need is light. You can't wash away death. You can't throw away death; what you need is life. You can't just throw away corruption; what you need is incorruption, which is life.

It's beyond just saying, “God is merciful, God is this, God is that,” but you are not speaking from a redemptive worldview. If redemption is not at the core, what you are saying is not existential truth as far as this existence is concerned that we can observe and reason on.

Even more than talking about this redemptive worldview, we find a historical fulfillment strongly validated by a resurrection claim that has not been disproved. All you have to do to disprove a resurrection claim is show that the person so resurrected is still dead. By the resurrection of the person of the Lord Jesus, this is the force and the strength of the Christian worldview.

Before the Lord Jesus, this worldview, this redemptive worldview which is God's truth across all people in all places, was still true, and you should still hold on to it. But today it has a greater force in the fulfillment of that redemption in the person of Christ Jesus. It is on that note that it is with the person of Jesus we are going to accept whatever revelation we hold today.

The Lord Jesus pointed us to the Scriptures of the prophets, what we call the Old Testament text, as God's revelation, and also gave the authority for truth and revelation to his apostles, who now make up, by God's orchestration, the New Testament text. We recognize that the Bible is the authoritative document for the truth and revelation of God from the Lord Jesus. From the Lord Jesus, the fulfillment of that metaphysical, philosophical truth, we now know the source of theological, revelatory truth.

From the document we now see to be the source of revelatory truth, what do we know? What does God tell us about himself? There are already many things we know about God. We know his self-existence. We know he is life and light by nature. But there is something we need to know from him: why did you create the world? What do you want? What do you like? What are you about? What gets to you? What are your interests?

## God's Good Pleasure Is Sonship

Look at Ephesians 1:9. I like the way he says it, showing us God's revelation: “Having made known to us the mystery of his will.” He's the kind of person who wants to reveal, and he has now revealed to us. “Having made known to us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he purposed in himself.”

Beyond knowing him as Creator, as good, righteous, life, light, beyond knowing him as Redeemer, which observation and reason can lead to, we now know him by his good pleasure. He wants to be Father. We now know him as Father. In the first sense we know him as God, that is Creator and Redeemer. In the second sense, by his revelation, we know him as Father.

Look at Ephesians 1:5: “Having predestined us to adoption as sons,” or sonship, “by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will.” Because of this good pleasure of his will, which he has revealed to us, he predestined us to sonship. He predestined salvation and redemption by Jesus to sonship, implying that sonship must have been the good pleasure and will. It is his goal. That's why he predestined us to it.

If all you know about God is just that calculated, reasoned knowledge, as beautiful and important as that is, giving you stability, you don't know him in a personal way. You start to know him in a personal way when you know that he made you to be his child, that is also his heir. The way you interact now changes. You start to cry out in a different way.

## Abba, Father: Children and Heirs

Romans 8:15 says, “You did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but the Spirit of adoption whereby we cry, ‘Abba, Father.’” When you receive the Spirit of his purpose, sonship, the Spirit of what he wants, what he likes, you cry, “Abba, Father.” You come into that reality of engaging with him as his own very child.

This is not an adopted child. It is a natural cry. This is not something that is developed later. You cry, “Abba, Father,” like the child naturally over time will get to start to call his parents “Dada,” “Mama,” and later “Dad,” “Mom.” It just so happens, because of course they are playing with him, “Daddy loves you,” all of this. Your cry says, “Abba, Father.”

Verse 16 says this Spirit bears witness with our spirit. They have the same evidence that we are children of God. If children, then heirs. I don't only relate with him as my Father whose child I am, having his nature, but also as a Father who has given me an inheritance.

The inheritance we have received from the Father includes spiritual assignment and a natural assignment: responsibilities, an allotment of things to do. An inheritance can include many things, just as someone might receive a car and other things as an inheritance.

## A Personal, Experiential Walk with God

Thirdly, we know God by personal and experiential truth. After he has spoken to us and revealed himself to us, he calls us to know him by personal and experiential truth. If your knowledge of God does not include experiences, you are missing the plot. He made you, and this is his purpose, sonship. The whole point of it is for you to have a walk with him.

Depending on what standpoint you ask, if you ask some people, “What is God's purpose?” from certain Jewish theological or philosophical frameworks, they will say the halakhah, which is the walk. But it depends on what they now mean. Some can mean the walk in the covenant, walk in the law, or the walk with God is the purpose. That's why consistently you see Abraham walked with God, Noah walked with God, Enoch walked with God and he was no more.

If you don't have a deepening experience with him, so much so that your walk with him can get to be described as a friendship, something is wrong. Friendship cannot be theoretical. Friendship cannot just be about the knowledge that this is what someone wants. Friendship is about a walk, familiarity, engagement, interaction.

If I know God in truth, I must know things about him in an experiential way, things I have observed from my walking with him, not things I've only observed from my thinking on him. “I know if God speaks to me like this...” These are now experiential things. I know how he speaks to me. I know how I discern his voice. I know what I'm supposed to do next in a given situation. I know how I engage with him, how I interact with him. I know if I see something like this, then it's like that. I need to have that deepened experiential relationship with him.

Experiential truth is truth. You have many experiential truths in your life. You woke up this morning, you got dressed. These are experiential truths. Nobody has experienced them with you. It's difficult for us to even know by observation and reason because we're not there. But they are experiential truths: how you felt this morning, that you knew God was displeased with you in a particular situation, or that he was very pleased with you about something.

There's a theoretical truth that you know God loves you. There's the experiential truth that you know God loves you. You know your husband or wife loves you, and there are moments when things happen and you feel that. We are called to know him experientially.

## The Trinity as Revelatory Truth

I want to show you a number of texts. Look at John 16 first, before I take you to the core text on this. John 16:23, the Lord Jesus speaking: “In that day you will ask me nothing.” They had been used to engaging the Lord. When there was a situation, they would cry out to him, “Lord, carest thou not that we perish? Save us. We are in this situation.” He said, “In that day you will ask me nothing.” That's not because he's not God.

I forgot to mention another revelatory truth. There are things we know about God by revelatory truth, and one of such is the Trinity. I was about to reference it now; that's why it came up. Is the Trinity a truth you can arrive at by observation and reason on the cosmos? Absolutely not.

In fact, by observation and reason you are likely to take a very non-pluralistic conclusion about the origin and source of the world, which is still the Christian view, still non-pluralistic. But let me start that way. When you get into theological truth in Scripture, and what is truly one is now having pluralistic realities, questions are going to arise in your heart. This is a matter of revelatory truth, necessarily so. It's something we accept as a matter of revelation, not something we expect to be resolved logically by reason.

Why do we have to accept that revelation? Because as far as the authoritative document for truth shows, this is a necessary reality: there is the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost. You might want to say there was just only one, and then they started becoming distinct later. If you read the text properly, you will not have that. There's that eternal reality of three persons. It's not a subsequent reality. While the role we are seeing being carried out, that redemptive role, became obvious later, it is only revealing what has eternally been, based on the statements we see being made.

Very importantly, this is neither tritheism, because we are not told that there are three gods we are worshiping, nor is it modalism. It's just the reality of one that is also three, three that is also one. Generally, if you try to over-explain it, you will be moving one way or the other. It is to be accepted as a matter of revelation. You start moving either into modalism or into tritheism.

Of course, I don't subscribe in any way, shape, or form to the reality of Jesus not being God. That would be a very big problem. If he's not God, there's a problem, because he's part of the cosmos; he's not the Creator. That cosmos was already dead. Why is he not dead? If he was created anew, why didn't they just create a new cosmos altogether?

Only God in his reality as life and light can redeem. The Redeemer must necessarily be God, not using a part of the cosmos. Why did he not use the entire cosmos to save the cosmos? He can do what he likes. He should have used all of us once and for all, so we don't have to worship anybody. He is the Redeemer who saved us. That's why he should be worshiped. It's God himself, rather. He is the Redeemer who saved us. If there's any question to that, there's a very big problem.

Anything like modalism, saying it is a form of God that saved, or a mode of God that saved, it is not God that saved, it is a form or mode, the substance needed for salvation is lacking in that state. Let's leave that. It is a matter of revelation, and is to be so accepted because it is presented in Scripture.

## The Father Himself Loves You

Back to John 16, where he says, “You will ask me nothing.” You will now ask the Father. “Whatever you ask the Father in my name, he will give it to you. Until now you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, and your joy will be full.”

Look at verse 25: “These things I have spoken to you in figurative language, but the time is coming when I will no longer speak to you in figurative language, but I will tell you plainly about the Father.” Now verse 26, where I'm taking you: “In that day you will ask in my name, and I do not say that I shall pray the Father for you.”

“I do not say that I shall pray the Father for you, for the Father himself loves you.” Very important. “Because you have loved me, and have believed that I came forth from God.” The Father himself loves me. There's an experiential walk with God. There's knowing God by experiential truth. This is a direct interaction with God, including Jesus as God.

Look at John 17:23: “I in them, and you in me.” Talking about our union with God, having his nature and his life, it is often described that way. He's not talking about an ontological being in the same place. That's not it. He's talking about our union with him: we share the same nature and life.

“I in them, and you in me, that they may be made perfect in one, and that the world may know that you have sent me, and have loved them as you have loved me.” When they see his disciples having the nature of God, they will know that Jesus was sent. They also see that you have loved my disciples as you have loved me.

Look at verse 24: “Father, I desire that they whom you gave to me may be with me where I am, that they may behold your glory which you have given unto me; for you loved me before the foundation of the world.” That's why I must hold to an eternal Trinity, not a Trinity that developed later. Anything developed later is modalism. It cannot be eternal.

## The Father and Son Make Their Home with Us

John 14:21: “He who has my commandments and keeps them,” which is faith in him, “it is he who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and manifest myself to him.” That's God being made manifest to us. He only said “myself” in verse 21, but you'll see him expand it. That's why I'm saying what he was talking about in John 16 is that we won't see a mediator role in interaction with God, including him.

In verse 22, Judas, not Iscariot, said to him, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us, and not to the world?” Jesus answered and said to him, “If anyone loves me and keeps my word, my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.” Para, with him, not in him. With him.

They can know God by observation and reason. They can even hear about the revelation. It is when they are saved that they have that experience. The Father and the Son have made their home with us, and we are now worshiping God directly via the mediation Christ has done, not an ongoing mediation to interact with God.

## Walking Daily as His Child and Heir

I know his purpose is sonship: to walk in fellowship with him, to walk in his nature and character, to carry out assignment. My life is to be spent walking with him. Doing my assignment is a matter of walking with God. I'm called to walk with him, to worship him, to live for him, to serve him, to be his child. That's what life is now all about.

There's a way you can live for self, idolatry, that you have no reference to the Creator as to your existence. There's a way you are now living in true worship, where the Creator is constantly referenced in the way you live your life. You have your devotion, your walk in his nature and character, and your walk in the assignment he has given you. That is what we have been called to.

I know God in truth. I know him by observation and reason. I know him by revelation. I know him personally and experientially. I walk with him daily in all the truth. I know God in truth, and I walk in his truth all the days of my life.

Just worship him. Thank him. The Father himself loves you. He loves you as he loves the Lord. How would you think about yourself? Imagine what that should mean. Just like he said to the Lord, “This is my beloved Son.” Imagine what that would do to you, to your heart, to your esteem.

The Father himself loves me, and he loves me even as he loves the Lord. This is maximum possible love. You can't be seeking validation somewhere else. You can't be looking for acceptance somewhere else. For goodness' sake, the Father himself loves you. He loves you as he loves the Lord Jesus. The Father and the Son have made their home with me. I now worship forever.
