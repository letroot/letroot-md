## Spiritual growth in God’s purpose

[0:00]

We are looking at the decision of a local church. One necessity of God’s purpose is spiritual growth, and therefore discipleship. This would be true even apart from the need for redemption. God’s purpose is sonship: he wants us as his children and his heirs. As children, we are to grow.

First Peter 2:1–2 tells us to lay aside malice, hypocrisy, guile, envy and evil speaking, and to desire the milk of the word as newborn babes, so that we may grow. First John 3:9 speaks of being born of God and having his seed. Ephesians 4:13–15 speaks of coming to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, no longer remaining children tossed about by doctrine, but growing up into him in all things.

Where growth is expected, there must be a ministry of growth. Naturally, when a child is born, we expect growth and therefore parenting. A child may grow despite lacking proper parenting, but that is not the intended or best situation. In the spiritual context, we call this discipleship.

Ephesians 4:11–12 says Jesus gave the leaders of the church for the perfecting of the saints. The growth described afterward happens through that ministry. Perfecting is how growth is ministered to the saints.

## Prudence about the ministry of growth

[5:44]

Lack of parenting can cause harm, but parenting itself can also be harmful. Likewise, discipleship can be traumatic. That needs fuller discussion, although it is not our main focus here.

Do not confuse prudence with fear. Recognizing an unpleasant possibility and knowing how to avoid it is not expecting the worst. Ignoring the possibility is simplicity in the sense of gullibility. Predators thrive on people not knowing better, not recognizing what is happening and not being able to stand their ground. Be equipped to identify what is wrong.

That caution does not remove the expectation of discipleship. It tells us to recognize that the ministry of growth must be carried out rightly.

## Perfecting and the disciple’s teacher

[8:03]

The word for perfecting in Ephesians 4 has a related verb used in other passages. First Corinthians 1:10 speaks of being perfectly joined together in the same mind and judgment. There is teaching and training so that the people are not divided in their thinking.

Luke 6:39–40 asks whether the blind can lead the blind without both falling into a ditch. It then says a disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher. The point is not merely an adjective describing someone as perfect; it is that someone has been trained or perfected. The teacher ministers growth to the disciple.

God’s purpose requires growth; growth is ministered by perfecting; this occurs through discipleship. That is why the decision of a local church begins with spiritual growth, not merely with choosing an institution you like.

I would even suggest, as theological deduction or speculation, that the structure for ministering growth has an enduring place in God’s purpose. We need spiritual nourishment, both for initial maturity and ongoing strengthening, just as we need food physically. That is a suggestion about the implications of the purpose, not a separately demonstrated account of every future arrangement.

## Salvation and discipleship in the local church

[14:25]

At his resurrection, Jesus spoke about discipleship in the great commission. Matthew 28:18 onward says all authority is his, and sends his followers to make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching them to observe what he commanded.

Discipleship begins with salvation. Mark 16 records the command to preach the gospel to every creature, with belief and baptism in the statement about salvation. People must first enter God’s purpose through salvation, then continue being taught. The instruction to make disciples includes that beginning.

We see it in Acts 2. People believed and continued steadfastly in the apostles’ doctrine, fellowship, breaking of bread and prayers. As they continued together, the community is called the church. [18:48 — The caption says “verse 26,” but the passage being read appears to be Acts 2:46–47.]

I am speaking here of the birth of the local church as a natural community, distinguishing it from the church’s reality at the resurrection. The instruction to disciple produces this community for discipleship. Ephesians 4 likewise describes leaders ministering growth to the saints so they can walk in God’s purpose.

## The primary and natural choice

[21:13]

The primary and natural decision of a local church occurs through the means by which you receive saving faith. Someone preaches the gospel, you are convinced of the truth of redemption in Christ, and you believe. What do you do next? The person guides you to continue learning, ordinarily in the church through which that message reached you.

You have already begun receiving discipleship through what brought you to salvation. Continuing with that community is the natural next step. You believe what was said, and now want to know how to walk in it and fulfill God’s purpose.

For someone born into a Christian family, that may be the church their parents attended. They heard the gospel while growing up and, at the age of rationality when they needed salvation, received it and continued. For another person, it may be a different church through which someone preached to them.

Why is that the natural choice? The basis is not merely that this institution happened to be first. It is that you believed its message was true. The decision may not look like a separate, dramatic church choice, but it rests on persuasion about the truth.

## Truth is the basis of both decisions

[25:22]

Ephesians 1:13 says, “In whom you trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation,” and speaks of believing and being sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise. You believed because you were persuaded that the gospel was true, not because you were forced or because someone offered an unrelated personal benefit.

That same persuasion leads you to continue with those who brought you the truth. “If this is true, I want to continue in it.” Romans 1:18–20 likewise treats the issue as truth made known, which people can suppress in unrighteousness.

Second Thessalonians 2:13–15 connects salvation with sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth. It then tells the brethren to stand fast and hold what they were taught, whether by word or epistle. Belief leads naturally into continuing in the teaching.

So the sequence is clear: God’s purpose calls for growth; growth calls for discipleship; entry into that purpose is through salvation; and the natural community in which to continue is the one through which you received that truth. The basis of the commitment is truth.

## The possibility of another decision

[31:13]

Is another decision of a local church possible? This is a general guide, not a case for one particular church, including Fullness of Christ Church. I want to examine the grounds honestly and as impartially as possible.

The first possibility is that the present church drifts from the truth. Suppose, as an extreme hypothetical example, that the church through which you believed in salvation begins saying there is no salvation. Your commitment was to the truth you believed. If you still believe it, you cannot make loyalty to the institution more important than that truth.

Loyalty is not merely sticking with something; that can be stubbornness. Faithfulness rests on persuasion. People share loyalty because of shared conviction. In church, that begins with belief in God and redemption, and then the mission pursued together on that basis.

Examining this possibility is prudence, not fear. Scripture itself gives hypothetical cases so that believers will know what to do if they arise.

## When spiritual leadership drifts

[35:00]

Galatians 1:6–9 expresses astonishment that people are turning from the gospel they received to a different gospel. Paul says that even if he and his fellow ministers, or an angel from heaven, preached another gospel, that person should be accursed or rejected.

Before we settle all the details of the deviation involved, one point is already plain: there is a ground for rejecting spiritual leadership. Paul includes himself. If there is a ground for rejecting Paul, there is a ground for rejecting me. You cannot be so committed to a leader that you have no category for rejecting what is wrong.

That establishes one possibility of another decision: a present church drifting from the truth. In my opinion, people often remain where they are, so this may be a rarer ground in practice. But it is possible, and we should recognize it rather than refuse to consider it.

Where someone was raised around a church but had not believed, then receives salvation through another ministry, the ordinary response is to continue where they received the gospel, all things being equal. Being familiar with another church from childhood does not by itself overturn that. But geography introduces another possibility.

## Geographical distance

[39:41]

Distance from the present church can require another decision. There may, however, be a community aligned with the same church and leadership in the new location. Then you may simply join that community rather than change your underlying commitment.

We can see related churches across locations in the apostolic period. Someone moving from Corinth to Ephesus could find communities connected with Paul’s ministry. Peter and James also wrote to dispersed people across locations. There was greater similarity among churches then than we see today, but there were still differences of setting and composition.

A Jewish diaspora community could differ from a more mixed Pauline community. [41:21–41:37 — Parts of that comparison are unclear in the captions.] Even within a communion claiming substantial sameness, people sometimes prefer a particular congregation because of its practice.

A well-taught Protestant should recognize all who have faith in Christ as sharing the communion of that faith, across denominational labels: non-denominational, Reformed, Presbyterian, Baptist, Roman Catholic or Orthodox. Our differences do not mean that everyone outside our immediate group is outside Christ.

When geography requires a choice, the present church has a natural priority if a suitable aligned community is available. Otherwise, look for a community matching your persuasion of truth. In some circumstances, extending the present church’s work into the new location may also be possible.

## Further light in God’s truth

[43:20]

The third possibility is further light in God’s truth. This requires care. The basis of salvation and church commitment is truth; the church is meant to be a community of truth.

First Timothy 3:14–15 calls the church of the living God the pillar and ground of the truth. This is a vocation, a responsibility. Paul writes so Timothy will know how to conduct himself in it. It is not a claim that every church automatically fulfills that responsibility by its nature. Nor does every error mean it immediately ceases to be a church: Paul still addressed the Galatians as churches while correcting their drift.

A church must ensure that its people are founded on and upheld by truth. That explains why a departure from truth can change commitment. But what about receiving a fuller understanding than you previously had?

Sometimes this happens as people raised in a church mature and begin examining the faith for themselves. They want to own their conviction. Sometimes it happens later through encountering teaching they had not understood before. Can further light affect their discipleship commitment?

## Apollos and the disciples at Ephesus

[47:48]

Acts 18:24 onward describes Apollos as eloquent, mighty in the Scriptures, instructed in the way of the Lord and fervent in spirit. He taught accurately, although he knew only John’s baptism. Aquila and Priscilla took him aside and explained the way of God more accurately.

Here is someone already taught and already ministering who receives further understanding. He then went to Achaia, helped believers and publicly showed from Scripture that Jesus is the Christ. Paul later says, “I planted, Apollos watered.” We can see Apollos becoming more aligned with Paul’s ministry. I am describing a shift or tilt in his commitment, while acknowledging that he is a minister and may seem a distinctive case.

Acts 19 gives another example. Paul met disciples at Ephesus and asked whether they had received the Holy Ghost when they believed. He explained John’s baptism and its direction toward the one coming after John, Jesus Christ. They were baptized in Jesus’s name, and after Paul laid hands on them, they spoke with tongues and prophesied.

I would argue that they likely already had redemptive faith without identifying its fulfillment in the person of Jesus. People had faith in God’s redemption before John, and before John pointed out Jesus. Their situation can be described as a community still functioning with a pre-redemption understanding when redemption had already been accomplished. That is an argument about their condition, not something to state without qualification. [51:55–53:24 — Some wording in this explanation is unclear.]

Further light changed their understanding and affected commitment. With Apollos the connection to Paul’s ministry becomes visible; with the Ephesian disciples we likewise see a real change. Is there teaching beyond the narratives that bears on this responsibility?

## Knowledge, preparation and judgment

[54:31]

Luke 12:47–48 speaks of the servant who knew his master’s will but did not prepare and do it, and the servant who did not know yet did things deserving judgment. Knowledge affects responsibility. The one who knew is beaten with many stripes; the one who did not know with few.

The preceding discussion responds to Peter asking whether Jesus speaks only to them or to all. Jesus describes the faithful servant who does the assigned work, and the servant who mistreats others while assuming the master delays. I understand verses 47–48 as explaining that judgment, not introducing an entirely unrelated category.

There is an interpretive question over “unbelieving” and “unfaithful” in the preceding statement. I favor “unfaithful” there, while acknowledging the other wording and the argument about suffering loss. [57:57–1:00:09 — Several phrases in this explanation are garbled in the captions, so the exact comparison of loss, judgment and damnation cannot be fully recovered.]

I do not want this teaching turned into the whole debate about eternal salvation. People on both sides can misrepresent one another and use proud or hostile labels. Disagreeing with a particular position does not automatically make someone a legalist or a heretic. There may be a later teaching devoted to that question; I am not resolving the entire dispute here.

The point we need is that the servant knew, yet did not prepare and do. The word “prepare” connects with being equipped for the work. Second Timothy 2:20–21 speaks of vessels in a great house and of cleansing oneself to become a vessel of honor, sanctified, useful to the master and prepared for every good work.

Second Timothy 3:16–17 says Scripture is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction and righteousness, so the man of God is complete and thoroughly equipped for every good work. Preparation comes through discipleship by the Scriptures, and this is ministered in the local church. [1:02:30–1:02:58 — The Greek pronunciation and part of the initial reference are uncertain; the passage is subsequently identified as 2 Timothy 2:20–21.]

Your knowledge of God’s will therefore bears on the preparation you should receive. If further truth has become clear to you, you should prepare according to it. You cannot refuse to do so on the grounds that others are also believers but do not act on that knowledge. Their responsibility is measured in light of what they know; yours is affected by what you know.

## Responsibility for what we have received

[1:06:06]

God’s word gives direction for the decision of a church. The primary choice is ordinarily where you receive salvation. Its basis is truth, which also explains the possibilities of another choice: drift from truth, geographical distance or further light in truth.

When God’s orchestration brings you to more understanding, it is for the benefit of others as well. If you grow through that truth, you can bear, help, strengthen and equip more people. Refusing to align yourself with it can leave others without the help you could have brought.

Luke 12 says that to whom much is given, much is required. What have you done with the knowledge given to you? Have you committed to the training, perfecting and preparation through which you can do God’s will?

The conclusion will differ for different people. One may recognize that the right preparation is where he already is. Another may recognize a need for a different decision. The burden is to prepare in line with what you know of God’s will so that you can fulfill it better, not to make everyone arrive at the same institutional destination.

## Making the decision in righteousness and love

[1:09:48]

Knowing what decision to make is not enough; make it with the character of God’s Spirit, in righteousness and love.

Do not stay out of hypocrisy: you know your persuasion is no longer aligned, but you remain merely for certain benefits. Do not stay through manipulation either. Being manipulated does not remove the responsibility we have just read about. Align with your conviction in faithfulness to God.

Yet make the decision with honor. People have helped you according to what they knew. A parent’s mistake does not erase all the good the parent has done. Similarly, receiving more understanding is no reason to speak down on those who taught you, insult them or become ungrateful. They are responsible for what they know; you are responsible for what you now know.

You can explain that your conviction differs. In some circumstances you have the relationship and position to bring further understanding to the leadership. They may receive it, and the place may remain one where you can be prepared according to that truth. If you can bring help without strife and division, staying can be appropriate.

But if attempting that will create rancor, division or rebellion, do not make yourself a source of it. Not everyone is in a position to bring that help. There is a way to leave peaceably and honorably, acknowledging the good you received and explaining your reasons.

## Time, discussion and the different circumstances

[1:13:19]

Where manipulation exists, making the decision may still be difficult. Ensure that, on your own side, you act righteously. Communicate and allow reasonable time. These are brothers in the church of Jesus Christ; giving them an opportunity to hear you and discuss the matter may lead to help, a clearer understanding, or their reception of what you have seen.

If it remains necessary to leave, do it properly. This is particularly the case of further light. If a church is abandoning the truth, the question of waiting may be different, except perhaps for discussion aimed at helping it return. Geographical relocation is different again, and an aligned congregation may already be available.

Do not remain bound by hypocrisy or manipulation, but do not leave in a way that creates needless strife. Help if you can; otherwise leave with peace and honor.

## Clarity of conviction and strength to act

[1:15:21]

Sometimes the biblical and rational decision is clear, but friendship, benefits or other emotional attachments make it difficult. You may have clarity of conviction without strength of conviction. Give yourself time prayerfully to gain the strength to act in line with what you know.

Nothing about this choice should be forceful. Make it wisely, rationally, thoroughly and patiently. If you know what you ought to do but lack the strength, take time—perhaps days or weeks—to bring yourself to a place where you can make the decision righteously.

Truth is the paramount basis, even when geography is a factor. The choice matters because it affects your walk in God’s purpose. Judgment is individual, yet your individual decision joins you to a collective influence. In the letters to the seven churches, Jesus speaks to communities whose members have subscribed to that influence. A church’s tolerance of indiscipline, or its approach to work, can shape its people. How well the community reflects God’s truth, character and purpose affects your own reflection of them.

## Prayer and the Spirit of truth

[1:17:42]

Is there a place for prayer and the Lord’s leading? Absolutely. But the Spirit who leads is the Spirit of truth. He will not lead you aside from what has been made known in the word. Do not try to twist his arm against written truth, any more than asking for another way of salvation while refusing what he has already said about Christ.

You can pray about the choice, seek confirmation and ask whether there is another detail you need to know. The Spirit may give that, or there may be no additional detail beyond what you have rightly judged. As with a marital decision, having judged by the word and considered the natural situation, you may simply proceed when there is nothing further to add.

Be wise, discerning and righteous in choosing a local church, because it affects your walk in and fulfillment of God’s purpose. I wish anyone at that crossroads well in making this important decision. Thank you.
