# What is the value of one hour of a startup founder's time?

## Summary

Jason Cohen argues that a startup founder should value their time at roughly $1000 per hour, not because that is their market wage today, but because startup work is deferred, risky, and paid only if the company survives. The point is not literal hourly billing; it is a forcing function for better decisions. Once you internalize that your time is extremely expensive, you stop spending it on low-leverage tasks and start protecting it for prioritization, customers, product-market fit, and the highest-value work only you can do.

## Key Insights

- **Founder time has venture-risk pricing.** If a consultant would charge $150/hour cash now, startup work should be valued far higher because payment is delayed for years and may never come at all. The upside must compensate for time delay, uncertainty, and high failure odds.
- **The $1000/hour frame is about [[Prioritization]], not math.** The real lesson is to use a very high internal rate so that low-leverage work becomes obviously irrational.
- **Delegation is not a luxury; it is a survival strategy.** If your time is truly expensive, then a [[Personal Assistant]], bookkeeper, CPA, cleaner, or other support becomes economically sensible far earlier than most founders assume.
- **Small wins are often too cheap to deserve founder attention.** You cannot afford to spend prime hours on projects that produce only incremental improvements when the business still needs breakthrough clarity on product, market, and growth.
- **[[Product-Market Fit]] work is worth the founder's best energy.** Talking to customers, selling, figuring out what to build, and improving conversion or retention belong squarely in the founder's zone of importance.
- **Your job is to stay inside the intersection of excellence and importance.** Founder work should cluster around the things you do unusually well that also matter disproportionately to company outcomes.

## Coach's Corner

**On Time as a Strategic Asset:**

This is less about feeling busy and more about respecting leverage. Founders leak time everywhere: admin, tiny optimizations, inbox churn, side quests, perfectionist product tweaks. The $1000/hour lens is brutal in a good way because it forces one question: "Should I really be the one doing this?"

**On Your Current Founder Transition:**

As you move from developer to founder, this is one of the hardest identity shifts. Coding feels productive because it is concrete and emotionally safe. But some of the highest-value founder work looks messier: customer calls, positioning, writing, pricing, sales, hiring, and decision-making under ambiguity. Those are often the true $1000/hour tasks.

**On Delegation and Systems:**

If something can be done competently by someone else, by automation, or by a lightweight process, it probably should be. Not because you're above it, but because the business is more fragile than your ego. Every hour spent reconciling receipts or cleaning up avoidable workflow chaos is an hour not spent increasing the odds of survival.

**On Focus and Self-Discipline:**

The deeper challenge is not knowing this principle. It's obeying it. You'll still be tempted by tasks that create quick dopamine. The discipline is to protect time for things that compound slowly but matter massively: customer understanding, retention, sales conversations, and strategic clarity.

## Action Items

- [ ] **Run a $1000/hour audit**: Review the last 7 days and mark which tasks truly required founder-level attention.
- [ ] **Eliminate or delegate one recurring low-leverage task**: Pick one admin or operational task that should no longer live on your plate.
- [ ] **Protect high-value blocks**: Reserve focused time this week for customer calls, positioning work, or PMF-related decisions.
- [ ] **Write your founder-only list**: Define the 3-5 activities that only you should be doing right now.
