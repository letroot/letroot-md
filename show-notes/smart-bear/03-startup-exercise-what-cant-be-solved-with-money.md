# Startup Exercise: What can't be solved with money?

## Summary

Jason Cohen argues that the most valuable parts of a startup are often the ones money cannot fix. Capital can accelerate execution once you know what to do, but it cannot buy judgment, learning speed, customer understanding, or the ability to attract great people. His core exercise is to separate the parts of the business that are improved by funding from the parts that still require intelligence, adaptation, and hard-earned clarity. That distinction matters both for fundraising and for strategy, because investors fund acceleration, not the discovery of basic competence.

## Key Insights

- **Money accelerates [[Execution]], not [[Exploration]].** Cash helps you write more code, run more campaigns, survive longer, and hire support around a known bottleneck. It does not tell you what to build, what copy converts, what customers actually value, or how to think well under uncertainty.
- **[[Team Building]] is mostly not solved by money.** Great people are scarce and usually have options. Money can help close a hire after you find the right person, but it does not create that person or make them easier to discover.
- **Most [[Marketing]] can be bought, but [[Authority]] cannot.** Ad spend can buy attention, experiments, and distribution. It cannot buy trust, earned audience, or the kind of authority that makes people listen voluntarily.
- **Capital is best used on already-identified constraints.** If a team knows exactly what is broken and money is the only reason it is still broken, that is a strong fundraising case. If the real problem is confusion, lack of product clarity, or missing customer understanding, money will mostly magnify waste.
- **Investors want proof that you can do what money cannot buy.** The real signal is not whether you can repeat [[Lean Startup]] slogans; it is whether you can demonstrate learning, adaptation, pricing insight, customer understanding, and evidence-backed iteration.
- **Self-awareness is part of fundability.** If there are important things money cannot solve and you have not mastered them yet, the right move is to surface that openly and show a credible plan rather than pretend cash will solve everything.

## Coach's Corner

**On Fundraising Readiness:**

This is a sharp filter for founder honesty. If you ever raise, you need to be able to say: "Here is what money unlocks, and here is what we already know how to do without it." Investors are not funding your hope that you'll become more thoughtful after wiring money. They are funding a machine that already learns, already adapts, and already understands its customer.

**On Your Current Stage as a SaaS Founder:**

For an early-stage founder, this is especially important because it's easy to fantasize that more runway would fix everything. Usually it wouldn't. If positioning is fuzzy, if the ICP is vague, if retention is weak, if you still haven't heard the pain in the customer's own language, more money mostly buys a longer path to the same confusion. That's uncomfortable, but useful.

**On Strategy Under Constraint:**

Constraints can be a gift if they force you to learn the non-purchasable parts of the business. You can validate demand without a big budget. You can sharpen messaging without paid acquisition. You can build authority by publishing clear thinking. You can learn from customer calls. Those skills compound. A better-funded competitor can outspend you on execution, but if they cannot learn faster or think better, they are not actually safer.

**On What to Prove Before Scaling:**

Before you try to scale anything, prove that you can diagnose truth. Can you explain why customers buy? Why they hesitate? What changed after each iteration? Which parts of growth came from spend and which came from insight? Those are founder muscles. Money is fuel. Fuel does not steer.

## Action Items

- [ ] **Separate your bottlenecks**: List what in your startup would genuinely improve with capital versus what still requires customer learning, positioning clarity, or strategic judgment.
- [ ] **Audit your current weak spots**: Identify one important problem you may be falsely labeling as a money problem when it is really an understanding problem.
- [ ] **Gather proof of learning**: Document examples of how customer feedback changed your product, messaging, pricing, or roadmap.
- [ ] **Define the fundraising story**: If you raised tomorrow, write the exact 3-5 uses of funds that are blocked only by money and not by uncertainty.
