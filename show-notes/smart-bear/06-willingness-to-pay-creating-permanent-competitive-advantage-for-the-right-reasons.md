# Willingness-to-pay: Creating permanent competitive advantage for the right reasons

## Summary

Jason Cohen reframes willingness-to-pay as three fundamentally different forces: [[Love]], [[Utility]], and [[Coercion]]. Traditional economics treats all payment as equivalent proof of value, but Cohen argues that the reason customers pay matters enormously for strategy, growth, retention, and ethics. Love creates allyship and advocacy, Utility creates fair exchange and steady expansion, while Coercion creates adversarial customers who stay only until a better exit appears. The strongest long-term companies increase willingness-to-pay by making customers genuinely happier to buy, then share that value rather than merely extracting it.

## Key Insights

- **Not all [[Willingness to Pay]] is equal.** A customer paying you does not automatically mean they love you, value you cleanly, or chose freely. Payment can come from admiration, usefulness, or lack of alternatives, and those produce radically different strategic outcomes.
- **[[Love]] is the strongest form of competitive advantage.** Mission, reciprocity, exceptional design, quality, transparency, community, and authentic cultural values create customers who root for your success, recommend you publicly, and tolerate modest price increases.
- **[[Utility]] is solid but less magnetic.** Convenience, simplicity, risk reduction, integration, familiarity, and system-of-record value create fair exchange. Customers stay because the product is useful and switching is costly or annoying, but they are not necessarily evangelists.
- **[[Coercion]] creates retention that is structurally fragile.** Lock-in, monopoly dynamics, bundle stuffing, predatory pricing, and policy-driven captivity may preserve revenue temporarily, but they turn customers into allies of your future competitors.
- **Growth quality depends on why customers pay.** Love creates repeat purchases, word of mouth, and non-linear growth. Utility grows accounts and supports expansion revenue. Coercion poisons brand, makes renewals dangerous, and leaves you exposed to disruption.
- **Profit is healthiest when you first increase customer value, then split it.** The right sequence is: create more willingness-to-pay through real value, then decide how much of that value to capture versus leave as consumer surplus.
- **Even a purely financial investor should prefer Love over Coercion.** A company whose customers advocate for it is more likely to grow efficiently and resist competition than one whose customers are waiting to escape.

## Coach's Corner

**On Building the Right Kind of Moat:**

This is one of the more important strategy pieces in the set because it helps you ask not just "Can we charge?" but "Why are they paying us?" A lot of SaaS founders accidentally build weak moats through inconvenience, internal process lock-in, or tool sprawl. That can look like retention on a dashboard while hiding resentment underneath.

**On What You Actually Want From Customers:**

The goal is not merely to trap customers long enough to hit a revenue milestone. The goal is to create customers who are glad you exist. If someone recommends your product with their own reputation on the line, that is a very different asset from someone who renews because switching feels painful.

**On Product and Brand Strategy:**

Love is not fluff. It can come from design taste, trust, humane support, authentic mission, clarity, reliability, community, and a product experience that feels like care. For an early-stage founder, that means brand and product quality are not separate. The way you write onboarding emails, the way support feels, the values you signal, and the corners you refuse to cut all shape willingness-to-pay.

**On Revenue With Integrity:**

A strong business captures value because it created value. That is the healthy frame for pricing. If your monetization depends on confusion, captivity, or exploitative switching costs, you're building a company that invites rebellion. If it depends on usefulness plus genuine customer goodwill, you are building something more durable and more worth becoming.

## Action Items

- [ ] **Classify your current value**: For each reason customers stay, label it as Love, Utility, or Coercion.
- [ ] **Strengthen one Love driver**: Improve one area like onboarding, support, design quality, transparency, or community that makes customers genuinely glad to buy from you.
- [ ] **Find hidden coercion**: Identify any retention you currently benefit from that comes from friction, confusion, or switching pain rather than delight.
- [ ] **Review pricing through value creation**: Ask whether your price reflects real new value delivered or just your ability to capture a stuck customer.
