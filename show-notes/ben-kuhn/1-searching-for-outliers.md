# Searching for Outliers

## Summary
[[Ben Kuhn]] argues that most important outcomes in life -- jobs, hires, relationships, startups, blog posts -- follow a [[Heavy-Tailed Distribution]], where a tiny fraction of outcomes vastly outperform the rest. Because heavy-tailed distributions are deeply unintuitive, people systematically under-sample, misjudge how good an outlier could be, and get demoralized by repeated "failures" that are actually just the cost of searching for a rare win. He lays out a practical process for sampling well: draw lots of samples, know what a great outcome looks like, filter for "maybe amazing" rather than "probably good," and iterate quickly using fast feedback.

## Key Insights
*   **[[Heavy-Tailed Distribution]]**: A distribution where the top few percent of outcomes are a large multiple of the median outcome (income, startup valuations, blog post virality). Contrast with light-tailed distributions (height, 100m sprint times) where outliers barely differ from average.
*   **[[The Pareto Principle]]**: Roughly 80% of outcomes come from 20% of causes -- the same skew shows up in jobs, hiring, VC returns, and citations. Reused concept: this same 80-20 idea already appears in your notes on pricing and marketing.
*   **Draw More Samples**: In a heavy-tailed world, every additional sample (date, candidate, blog post, pitch) meaningfully raises your odds of hitting an outlier. Optimizing one sample is usually worse than drawing a second one.
*   **Filter for "Maybe Amazing," Not "Probably Good"**: The traits that make something *pretty good* are often different from the traits that make it a potential outlier. Y Combinator ignores idea quality and filters for founder quality because bad-sounding ideas are disproportionately likely to be outliers.
*   **Ruling In vs. Ruling Out**: Filtering by easy, legible negatives (a resume gap, a "bad" industry, poor small talk) is a poor use of a limited "filtering budget." It's better to filter for the few traits that are actually correlated with outlier outcomes, even if they're harder to observe.
*   **Abundance Mindset & the Settling Trap**: People chronically overestimate how good their current job/partner/hire is, and underestimate what else is out there, because they don't know what a true outlier looks like. Ask people who've found outliers what those outliers actually looked like.
*   **Fast Feedback Loops**: When you can cheaply and quickly evaluate whether a sample is an outlier (A/B test results, blog engagement), you can iterate on your sampling strategy fast. Watch out for [[Goodhart's Law]] when the feedback metric is only a proxy for what you actually care about.

## Coach's Corner
**On Marketing & Removing Friction:** Your landing pages, ad copy, and outbound messages are all samples from a heavy-tailed distribution of outcomes. Most will flop. Don't over-optimize a single landing page for weeks -- ship variant after variant and let the heavy tail do the work. The winner will not look like a 10% improvement on the loser; it'll look like a different animal entirely.

**On Hiring for Your SaaS:** When you eventually hire, resist the urge to filter for "safe, well-rounded" candidates. The people who become true outliers on a team often have a rough edge that a conventional interview process screens out. Filter hard for the 1-2 traits that actually predict outlier performance in your context (ownership, shipping speed, customer empathy), and be much more lenient on everything else.

**On Productivity & Winning the Morning:** Applying "draw more samples" to your own habits means running many small, cheap experiments on your morning routine, your work blocks, your outreach scripts, rather than agonizing over the "perfect" system. Most experiments will be forgettable. A few will change your trajectory.

**On Life & the Abundance Mindset:** If your current path (job, market, cofounder situation) feels merely "good," don't assume that's as good as it gets. Ask founders you respect what their outlier moments looked like before they happened -- you likely have miscalibrated intuitions for what a 99th-percentile outcome even looks like.

## Action Items
*   [ ] **Increase your sampling rate**: Pick one heavy-tailed area of your business (cold outreach, content, pricing experiments) and commit to shipping 3x more samples this month instead of polishing existing ones.
*   [ ] **Write your "outlier profile"**: Before your next big decision (hire, cofounder, market bet), write down what a 99th-percentile outcome would actually look like, so you can recognize it when it shows up.
*   [ ] **Audit your filters**: List the criteria you currently use to say no to opportunities/candidates/ideas. Flag any that filter for "legible negatives" rather than true outlier-correlated traits.
*   [ ] **Research [[Goodhart's Law]]**: Look into how proxy metrics (MRR growth, follower count) can distort your product decisions if optimized too directly, and identify one metric in your business at risk of this.
