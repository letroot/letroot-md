## Our Supernatural Nature in Christ

In this episode, we continue our study on our supernatural nature in Christ. Our supernatural nature in Christ. Now, we've already seen that one of the things we have as an internal reality of the new life in Christ is a supernatural nature. We've begun to make the case. We have seen in a more awesome way that the new life we have includes external realities as well as internal ones.

And some of the external realities we saw included things like healing, preservation, and favor in the newness of life. Then we began to see the internal realities, such as righteousness, love, and now supernatural nature in Christ.

Our study on supernatural nature began by making the submission that every reality of the new life is powered by the Spirit. We did see it when we were looking at every one of those realities: how healing is powered by the Spirit, our preservation is powered by the Spirit alongside the ministry of angels, our favor is powered by the Spirit alongside the ministry of angels, how we have righteousness because we are one spirit with the Lord, and how we have love because of the indwelling of eternal life, the seed of God, the love of God, and the Spirit of God in us.

## The New Life Powered by the Spirit

So when it tells us that as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we should walk in the newness of life, Romans 6:4, Romans 7:6 can attest to that claim that that new life is powered by the Spirit because it tells us he has called us to walk in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the letter.

Another text I want to see in that regard is how Romans 8:11 tells us that the Lord Jesus was raised by the Spirit of God that is also going to raise us up from the dead at his coming. So these details are very important for us.

Now with that in mind, the question we're asking is, what else can we do by the Spirit? And one of the things we see we can do is we can do supernatural works, miraculous works by the Spirit.

## The Works in John 14

John chapter 14 and verse 11 is where we have been seeing that from. It says, "Believe me that I'm in the Father and the Father in me, or else believe for the sake of the works themselves."

You could also see John chapter 14:10, the earlier verse, where he said, "Do you not believe I'm in the Father and the Father in me? The words that I speak to you I do not speak of my own authority, but the Father who dwells in me does the works."

So he's calling his utterance supernatural as well. But he starts from, "The words I speak is not on my authority, but the Father in me does the works." Then he adds, "Believe me that I'm in the Father, the Father in me, or else believe for the sake of the works themselves."

So he's making a distinction about himself, even though he's also saying by what he will do we share in the same reality of the divine nature. But he's pointing out that he's the Christ and he's revealing and unveiling the Father, and we should believe it from the message he's preaching, but also from the evidence given by the works.

And here we must be careful to submit that the works in question are miraculous works. We have tried to look at some texts to that effect. We saw John chapter 6. Let's go back there, then we would look at a number of other texts that show consistently in John that the use of works in this manner is for miraculous works. So it's not works like our character. It's works referencing miraculous or supernatural works, things that are beyond natural human ability.

## Signs and Works in John 6

Now in John chapter 6 and in verse 26, the Lord answered them and said, "Most assuredly I say to you, you seek me not because you saw the signs." And this clearly is about miraculous signs. He had multiplied loaves and fishes. All those events have just happened earlier. So he said, "You seek me not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of the loaves and were filled. Do not labor for the food which perishes."

So he was expecting they would have seen the signs and now they believe what the sign was pointing to, his person. But they were only concerned about the satisfaction the sign gave them and not about the thing, in this case the person, that the sign was pointing to.

It says, "Do not labor for the food which perishes, but for the food which endures to everlasting life, which the Son of Man will give you, because God the Father set his seal on him," again pointing out that he's the Christ.

Now in verse 28 it said, then they said to him, "What shall we do that we may work the works of God?" So you can see the context here is about miraculous signs, and they say, "What shall we work, the works of God?" And rather than answer in the way they're expecting, which is probably, "What can we do to also do the miraculous?" he said, "The work of God is that you believe in him whom he sent." So you believe in redemption in Christ.

Therefore they said to him, "What sign will you perform then, that we may see it and believe you? What work will you do? Our fathers ate manna in the desert, as is written, he gave them bread from heaven to eat."

So we can see here, as we saw in the last episode, the use of works is for miraculous works, supernatural signs.

## Nicodemus and the Confirmation of Signs

## The Man Born Blind and the Works of God

Also in John chapter 9, we see the same issue with works come up. So here John 14 refers not to character but supernatural works.

In John chapter 9 and in verse one downwards, the Lord saw a man that was blind from birth, and his disciples said, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" And Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but that the works of God should be revealed in him."

And he has really not answered any question here. I know there can be arguments about the punctuation and kind of, it's allowed, it's arguable, but the Lord, even if we leave it this way, the Lord has not put any blame on God here. He has simply said that the reason why, he has simply said it's not this man or his parents that are responsible for this, but this is an instance for the works of God to be revealed in him.

He said, "I must work the works of him who sent me while it is day, for the night cometh when no man can work. As long as I'm in the world, I am the light of the world."

There can be arguments made about punctuation and that Jesus responded, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned," and we can have a period or a full stop there. "But that the works of God should be revealed in him, I must work the works of him that sent me while it is day, for the night cometh when no one can work." That's a possible, plausible, and probably preferable reading.

But notwithstanding that reading, the conclusion from the entire scope of the scripture's submission should stand. It is not God making the man sick so that the works will be done. But nobody's to be blamed. This is a matter of sin and death when we understand the position of the scriptures entirely, but yet an opportunity for the works of God to be revealed.

So you can see here again, when the Lord uses works or the word works is used, it is for miraculous works.

## Works That Bear Witness

Later on from this same incident, reading downwards, chapter 10, the Lord is going to talk about supernatural works again. John 10:25, "I told you, and you do not believe. The works I do in my Father's name, they bear witness of me."

So it's one thing that he has a message, and which part of that message was pointing to himself. He wasn't just talking about redemption generally, but also saying it's been fulfilled in me. But also he has the signs backing him up.

In verse 34, the Lord answered them, "Is it not written in your law, you are gods? If he called them gods to whom the word of God came, and the scripture cannot be broken, do you say of him whom the Father sanctified and sent to the world, you are blaspheming, because I said I'm the Son of God? If I do not do the works of my Father," he says, "do not believe me. But if I do, though you do not believe me, believe the works that you may know and believe that the Father is in me and I in him."

And some of the sense in which he's saying that is exclusive to him, even though there's an extended sense where he's going to be inclusive of us.

## The Father in Him and the Divine Nature

So back to John chapter 14, he says again, just as he had been saying earlier, "Believe in me that I'm in the Father, Father is in me, or else believe me for the sake of the works themselves."

Now this reality of the Father in him and in the Father is someone who has a nature that is divine in the way we can now say today we are partakers by nature, right in our humanity. This was the first time a human being was embodying the fullness of divinity, as Colossians 2:9 says.

So people had had the Spirit with them, even his disciples also partook of that reality, the Spirit being with them, but for the first time God himself, in becoming a man, embodies what it means for divinity to be fully resident in humanity. Colossians 2:9, "In him dwells the fullness of the Godhead," or the fullness of divinity bodily. And then he says, "You are complete in him," which is now an extended reality to us.

So he has set aside his divine privileges. Yet for the first time we are finding this distinctive reality in humanity where a human person embodies divinity fully bodily. And he's saying, yes, I'm that Christ. I'm different. God become man to reflect this, and I'm going to bring you into that as well, as was my intention from the beginning.

So let's leave those touchy issues and move further. So one thing is clear: John 14:11, when he speaks about works here, is nothing other than miraculous works and supernatural signs. Nothing else. It's not character. It's not his teaching. It's not the work of ministry. It's not any other thing than miraculous works and supernatural signs attesting to his person and his message.

## The Spirit Given to Do the Works

So when he then tells us by extension, "Most assuredly I say to you, he who believes in me," the redemption he would accomplish, "the works I do, he will do also, and greater works than these shall he do because I go unto my Father," he's telling us we do miraculous and supernatural signs because he goes to the Father.

What will happen when he goes to the Father? John 14:16-17, he will give us the Spirit. John 14:26, he will give us the Spirit. John 15:26, he will give us the Spirit. But most clearly John 16:7, he will give us the Spirit again.

Now I read John 16:7. "Nevertheless, I tell you the truth, it is to your advantage that I go away. For if I do not go away, the Helper or Comforter will not come to you. But if I depart, I would send him to you."

He will give us the Comforter, the Helper, the Spirit to be in us. The manner in which he is going to be sent to us is that he would be in us. That is John 14:16 and 17. "I'll pray the Father, who will give you another Comforter or Helper, to abide with you forever, the Spirit of truth whom the world cannot receive, for they do not see him, but he is with you and shall dwell in you."

So that's an important detail. We have the Spirit of God in us regeneratively because of the redemption Christ has accomplished and the fact that he has sent his Spirit to us, and therefore we can do the works he did and greater, speaking of miraculous works.
