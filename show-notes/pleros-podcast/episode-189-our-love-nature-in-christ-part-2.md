# Our Love Nature in Christ (Part 2)

## The Internal Realities of the Newness of Life

We are continuing our new study on our love nature in Christ. And as we've said, the reason why we are looking at this subject matter is because of our continuation on the newness of life. One of the internal realities of newness of life is the love nature. Recall that the reason we are looking at the newness of life itself is because we saw that that's how we grow spiritually to fulfill God's purpose.

If we're going to fulfill God's purpose for our lives, it comes down to spiritual growth, giving us the capacity to bear the burdens of purpose. And with that in mind, we then need to find what exactly are we supposed to grow spiritually on, what are we supposed to feed on for our spiritual growth. And we can sum up everything we need for growth in a term, the newness of life. We could also sum it up as the new creation, that is, this individual that lives in this new life externally and that has this new life internally.

So we're using the term newness of life so that we can effectively capture the external and internal realities of this life. We've been studying everything we have in the newness of life. We studied the external realities of the new life we have in consummation of God's plan. One of the realities of the new life is immortality, another is incorruption.

Immortality of our bodies and incorruption of matter are some of the realities of this new life, and these are external realities if we are not talking about things internal to us like our character, our person, and so on. Today, those realities, immortality of our body and the incorruption of matter, are not available to us yet because they'll be affected at the end by the Lord Jesus. However, we could walk in what is available today of those realities such as healing and preservation. So we've already seen healing in the newness of life, we have also seen preservation in the newness of life, and one more thing we saw that is available to us in this new life externally is favor in the newness of life.

## From Death to Life in Christ

Now our attention has switched to the internal realities of the new life. In fact, we said that when Paul was speaking in Romans and in chapter 6 about walking in the newness of life, he was more concerned about the internal realities of the newness of life. This is notwithstanding the fact that there are indeed external realities of the new life. We made a very strong case for that when we studied healing, preservation, and favor, but his focus in Romans 6 was on the internal realities, the fact that our nature and our character has changed.

And when we follow through the trail of this life we have in us, we see that it includes the love nature being in us. In Romans 6 and in verse 4, "Therefore we were buried together with him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we should walk in the newness of life." So there's a new life we have. Verse 5 says, "For if we were united together in the likeness of his death... certainly we shall be in the likeness of his resurrection."

Knowing this, that the old man was crucified with him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin. You could see here that in us there was this old man that was our nature before internally. There was this body of sin, the same entity being described. The old man, the body of sin, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

So there was this status we had as slaves of sin. We were bound to sin. You could also say we're debtors to sin, we're bound to just act in sin or walk in sin. You will see later he's going to say we're slaves of uncleanness and lawlessness. All of those things are saying the same thing.

With that in mind, what has happened to us internally? He says we are now dead to sin and we are alive to God. So before on the inside we were dead, now we are alive. We now have the life of God. We are no longer alienated from the life of God.

Very similar to Ephesians 2:1, "You were dead in trespasses and sins in which you once walked according to the course of this world." By the time he gets to verse 4, he says, "But God who is rich in mercy because of the great love with which he has loved us," when we were dead in trespasses and sins, made us alive. So we were once dead, now we are alive. Just like Ephesians 5:8 will say, "You were once darkness, now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light." Something has changed on the inside of us.

## The Love of God Abides in the Believer

One of the texts that shows us both the external and internal reality of the new life is 1 John chapter 3. And this particular text helps us to see that the love nature is part of the internal reality of the new life. We ran through that text the last time. We'll go through it, and we're going to then make another emphasis that will lead us to the next point we need to make, because we have to answer the question, what exactly is love?

Remember we saw that when we were looking at righteousness, we said, what is righteousness then, and we answered that. But we need to answer the question, what is love? I ask you, what does it mean to love? You might say it's common sense, I mean it's intuitive to love, but really what does it mean? In fact, if you check a dictionary for the definition of love, you find out that the definition is circular. That is, you keep going in circles. It might say love is to have affection, then you go and check affection, then affection is to care about something, then go and check care, and they might end up even using the word affection again.

That's the challenge with some words. They are already banking on your intuitive understanding of what it is. But we're going to see why that word is used and what the word means, love, and what love really is, so that we can have a good appreciation of when we say we have the love nature of God in us.

In 1 John 3 and in verse 13, John says, "Do not marvel, my brethren, if the world hates you. For we know that we have passed from death to life... because we love the brethren." It says he that does not love his brother abides in death. So there's this reign and domain of death, it's like being in a place of death. Then verse 15 says, "Whoever hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him."

By this we know love, because he laid down his life for us. We also ought to lay down our lives for the brethren. But whoever has this world's goods and sees his brother in need and shuts up his heart of compassion from him, how does the love of God abide in him? So that's a rhetorical question. One who is born of God, the love of God abides in him, making it impossible for him to hate or to murder someone. When you are born of God, eternal life abides in you. The love of God abides in you.

We saw that as well in 1 John 4:7. "Beloved, let us love one another for love is of God... and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God." Once you believed in him, you are born of him, you have his nature, then you love. He that does not love does not know God. For God is love.

And in this the love of God was manifested towards us, that God sent his only begotten Son into the world that we might live through him. In this is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation of our sins. Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another.

I want you to pay attention to verse 12. "No one has seen God at any time. If we love one another, God abides in us, and his love has been perfected in us." By this we know that we abide in him and he in us, because he has given us his Spirit. So it is by God's Spirit in us, his life in us, God in us, that we are able to love. There is such a thing as a love nature.

## Love and Righteousness Belong Together

You could say eternal life is to have the love nature. You could also say eternal life is to have the righteous nature, and you'll be right because you speak about righteousness and love in the same breath. First John 3:10 says, "In this the children of God and the children of the devil are manifest." One of the ways to know those who are children of God and those who are not is to look at their conduct. It says it becomes obvious by their conduct.

"Whoever does not practice righteousness is not of God, nor is he who does not love his brother." Notice righteousness and love used in the same breath. The way he uses it there is as though he's distinguishing them even though they are very much connected, but it is to make a point that there's an emphasis of love in righteousness that requires us to speak of love in a distinct way, if I may say it that way, or to put a special emphasis on love.

So there's an emphasis of love in righteousness that requires us to distinctly emphasize love. We need to point out what love is at the core of this issue of righteousness. Love is at the very center of our nature in Christ. In Colossians chapter 3 and verse 14, Paul says, "But above all things put on love, which is the bond of perfection."

Above all things, put on love, which is the bond of perfection. Love puts together everything about who we are in Christ. It is shown here that love is at the center of who we are in Christ. We have a righteous nature, we have a love nature, and this emphasis of love as binding everything that we are supposed to do, as defining everything that we are supposed to do, comes up quite often.

## Love Fulfills the Law

One of the texts that expresses the central nature of love is Romans chapter 13, where Paul was speaking about the law and showing love as the fulfillment of the law. Romans 13:8 says, "Owe no one anything except to love one another, for he who loves another has fulfilled the law." The commandments, "You shall not commit adultery, you shall not murder, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness, you shall not covet," and if there is any other commandment, are all summed up in this saying, namely, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."

Love does no harm to a neighbor. Therefore love is the fulfillment of the law. So he sums up the law as love. And interestingly in Romans 8, he has summed up the Spirit of God in us as righteousness in us, the righteous requirement of the law fulfilled in us. But here he is summing up the law as love.

Romans 8 and verse 2 says, "The law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free from the law of sin and death. For what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh on account of sin. He condemned sin in the flesh that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk after the flesh but according to the Spirit."

Very important. So the believer has the righteous requirement of the law fulfilled in him. And we are seeing here that love is the fulfillment of law. So love is abiding in him. The ability, the nature of love, is abiding in him.

There are other texts to see here, and I'll just show us one because our time has run on us, which will be Matthew chapter 7, where the Lord sums up the law in a particular way. "Therefore, whatever you want men to do to you, do also to them, for this is the law and the prophets." There are questions to ask here. What about people who are psychopaths, or even people that love pain and suffering? Are we saying that whatever they want men to do to them, they should do it to others?

That will be reading the text without the mind you are supposed to read it with, which is a presupposition that everybody wants something to be done to them, and that's what they should do to others. And we're going to get into it in the next episode as well, something we have already kind of seen in righteousness. We'll tie it up together, but something is clear. Love is the summation of the law, and it is to do good towards men as we would even desire good be done to us.

For now, we wrap it up there. Remember, you have a love nature. God bless you. Thank you. Fulfill God's purpose and see you on the next one.
