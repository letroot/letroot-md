# Morning Digest - 2026-07-12

## Main Thought
Trust ideas, people, and systems more when they are exposed to consequences. Taleb's point in *Skin in the Game* is not only moral; it is practical. Reality teaches differently when error has a cost. A person who can be wrong forever without paying for it becomes dangerous, even if they sound polished.

The clean test is: who carries the downside? If someone keeps the upside while customers, employees, taxpayers, contractors, or future-you absorb the damage, the structure is fragile and ethically suspect.

## For You As A Founder
Do not build a company that wins applause from builders but loses contact with customers. Peer praise, elegant decks, smart opinions, and clean narratives are useful only if reality still has veto power. Revenue, retention, support tickets, churn conversations, referrals, and failed demos are closer to truth.

Today, look for one place where your business has insulated you from consequences. Are you overpromising to customers? Letting a weak product experience hide behind better marketing? Listening to advisors who lose nothing if you follow bad advice? The founder's advantage is not sounding right. It is arranging your work so reality corrects you early enough to adapt.

## For You As A Young Man
Skin in the game is a cure for performance. It asks you to take risks that make you more useful: sell, build, apologize, publish, ask directly, keep promises, and let your work be judged. Avoid risk theater, where you get adrenaline or status without responsibility.

Maturity means wanting upside without hiding from downside. If you want authority, carry responsibility. If you want confidence, expose your ideas to reality. If you want respect, let your actions cost you something before they reward you.
